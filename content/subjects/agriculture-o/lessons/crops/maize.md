## Introduction

Maize is the staple food of Zimbabwe. On a communal farm near Gokwe or a commercial farm near Chinhoyi, the maize crop decides whether a family has sadza for the year and cash for school fees. It is also stock feed, a source of income through the Grain Marketing Board (GMB) and private buyers, and the crop most often used in exam questions on crop production.

Both boards examine maize in detail. ZIMSEC asks you to grow one cereal and Cambridge asks for one crop of local importance "in detail": requirements, soil preparation, sowing, cultivars, seed rate and spacing, fertiliser, pests, weeds, diseases, maturity, harvesting, yield, storage and records. You should already know about soil fertility and fertilisers.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Hybrid maize"}
Seed produced by crossing selected inbred parent lines. The first-generation (F1) plants are uniform and high-yielding, but seed saved from them gives uneven, lower-yielding plants, so new seed must be bought every season.
:::

:::definition{term="Open-pollinated variety (OPV)"}
A cultivar that is allowed to pollinate freely within itself. Seed can be saved and replanted for a few seasons without a large fall in yield, but its yield potential is usually lower than that of a good hybrid.
:::

:::definition{term="Plant population"}
The number of plants per hectare, set by the spacing between rows, the spacing within the row and the number of plants per station.
:::

:::definition{term="Physiological maturity"}
The stage when grain has reached its maximum dry mass; in maize a dark layer (the black layer) forms where each kernel joins the cob.
:::

## Requirements of the crop

| Factor | Requirement (typical) |
| --- | --- |
| Rainfall | Well distributed rain through the season; the crop is most sensitive to drought at tasselling and silking |
| Temperature | Warm days and nights; maize is killed by frost |
| Soil | Deep, well-drained, fertile loams or sandy loams with good organic matter |
| Soil pH | Slightly acid to neutral; lime acid soils |
| Natural regions | Mainly II and III; in IV and V maize often fails in dry years and small grains are safer |

## Land preparation and planting

Plough or dig the land after harvest or early in the dry season to bury crop residues and weeds, then harrow to a medium tilth before planting. Under Pfumvudza/Intwasa the farmer instead digs planting basins and keeps a mulch on the surface.

**Choosing a cultivar.** Seed companies sell hybrids in maturity groups. **Early (short-season)** cultivars mature quickly and suit drier regions and late planting. **Medium and late (long-season)** cultivars need a longer, wetter season and give the highest yields in region II. Choose for the region, the planting date, disease resistance (for example to maize streak virus) and the market.

**Planting time.** Plant with the first effective rains, usually in November, when the soil is wet to a good depth. Planting late shortens the season, so yields fall. Dry planting just before the rains is practised on large farms so that the crop starts as soon as it rains.

**Seed rate, spacing and population.** A typical seed rate is about **25 kg/ha**. Common spacings are 90 cm × 30 cm or 75 cm × 25 cm, with **one plant per station** after thinning. Wider rows suit drier areas where plants compete for water; closer spacing suits well-watered, fertile land. Seed is placed about 5 cm deep in moist soil.

:::worked-solution[Plant population and seed for Rudo's field]
Rudo plants 1.8 ha at 90 cm × 30 cm, one plant per station. Assume a thousand-seed mass of 320 g and 90% germination.

1. Area per station = 0.9 m × 0.3 m = 0.27 m².
2. Plants per hectare = 10,000 m² ÷ 0.27 m² = 37,037 plants/ha.
3. For the field: 37,037 × 1.8 = 66,667 plants.
4. Seeds needed per ha at 90% germination = 37,037 ÷ 0.90 = 41,152 seeds.
5. Mass of seed = 41,152 × 320 g ÷ 1,000 = 13,169 g, about 13.2 kg/ha, or about 23.7 kg for the field.
6. This is the minimum. Farmers sow extra (often two seeds per station, then thin) to allow for pests, poor seed and gaps, which is why the recommended rate is nearer 25 kg/ha.
:::

## Fertilising maize

Maize is a **heavy feeder**, especially of nitrogen.

1. **Lime** acid soils well before planting.
2. **Basal dressing** at planting: a compound such as Compound D (7:14:7) at about 300-400 kg/ha, placed in the planting furrow or hole a few centimetres from the seed, never touching it. Phosphorus feeds early roots; potassium strengthens stems.
3. **Top dressing**: ammonium nitrate (34.5% N) at about 200-300 kg/ha, often split, for example at about 4-6 weeks (knee height) and before tasselling. Apply it on moist soil beside the plants, not on wet leaves.
4. **Manure or compost** improves structure and nutrient supply on smallholder fields.

:::worked-solution[Fertiliser for Rudo's 1.8 ha]
Rudo applies Compound D at 400 kg/ha and ammonium nitrate at 240 kg/ha in two equal splits. Fertiliser comes in 50 kg bags.

1. Compound D: 400 × 1.8 = 720 kg; 720 ÷ 50 = 14.4, so she buys **15 bags** (always round bags up).
2. Per station: 400,000 g ÷ 37,037 stations = **10.8 g** of Compound D.
3. Ammonium nitrate: 240 × 1.8 = 432 kg; 432 ÷ 50 = 8.64, so **9 bags**; each split is 216 kg.
4. Nitrogen per hectare: Compound D gives 400 × 7 ÷ 100 = 28 kg; AN gives 240 × 34.5 ÷ 100 = 82.8 kg; total **110.8 kg N/ha**.
:::

## Weeds, pests and diseases

**Weeds** compete most in the first six to eight weeks, so keep the crop clean early by hoeing, ox-drawn cultivation, mulching or a selective pre-emergence herbicide. Witchweed (*Striga*) is a parasitic weed of maize on poor sandy soils.

| Problem | Cause | Signs | Control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maize stalk borer | Larva of a moth (*Busseola fusca*) | Rows of holes in young leaves ("windowing"), dead heart, tunnels in stems, broken stems | Destroy old stalks, early planting, insecticide granules in the funnel |
| Fall armyworm | Larva of a moth (*Spodoptera frugiperda*), first reported in southern Africa in 2016-2017 | Ragged holes in leaves, sawdust-like droppings (frass) in the funnel, damaged cobs | Scout fields weekly, hand-pick, early uniform planting, rotate, approved insecticides when the threshold is reached |
| Maize streak virus | Virus spread by leafhoppers | Narrow yellow streaks along the leaves, stunting | Resistant cultivars, early planting, control grass weeds |
| Grey leaf spot | Fungus | Long, narrow, grey-brown rectangular spots between the veins | Resistant cultivars, rotation, bury residues |

Storage pests include grain weevils and the larger grain borer.

:::example{title="Diagnosing a problem in the field"}
Tatenda finds young maize plants with rows of small holes across the leaves; in some plants the central shoot is dead and pulls out easily. Splitting a stem shows a pinkish-grey caterpillar inside a tunnel. These signs point to **stalk borer**, not fall armyworm (which feeds openly in the funnel and leaves heaps of frass). He should treat the funnels of the young plants, and after harvest destroy or plough in the stalks, where the larvae survive the dry season.
:::

## Maturity, harvesting and storage

Signs of maturity: leaves and husks dry and turn brown, cobs droop, grains are hard and dented and the **black layer** has formed. The crop is then left to dry in the field or picked and dried on racks.

1. Pick the cobs, remove the husks and dry them further in a crib or on a clean floor.
2. **Shell** the grain by hand or with a sheller.
3. Dry to a safe moisture content (about 12.5%) so that moulds and insects cannot multiply.
4. Clean the store, treat grain with an approved storage protectant or use hermetic (airtight) bags, and keep bags off the floor on pallets.
5. Sell surplus grain to the GMB or another buyer.

:::worked-solution[Yield per hectare]
Rudo harvests 9,450 kg of dry grain from 1.8 ha.

1. Yield = 9,450 ÷ 1.8 = 5,250 kg/ha.
2. In tonnes: 5,250 ÷ 1,000 = **5.25 t/ha**; in 50 kg bags: 9,450 ÷ 50 = 189 bags in total.
:::

**Records** (planting date, cultivar, seed, fertiliser and chemicals used with dates and costs, rainfall, labour, yield and sales) let the farmer calculate gross margins and improve the next season.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Forgetting to convert centimetres to metres"}
90 cm × 30 cm is 0.9 m × 0.3 m = 0.27 m², not 2,700 m². Plants per ha = 10,000 ÷ 0.27.
:::

:::mistake{title="Rounding bags down"}
14.4 bags means the farmer must buy 15. Rounding down leaves part of the field unfertilised.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saving hybrid seed"}
Seed from a hybrid crop gives a mixture of weaker plants. Hybrid seed must be bought every season.
:::

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Maize needs warm, frost-free weather, well-distributed rain and deep, fertile, well-drained soil; it suits natural regions II and III best.
- Choose hybrid or OPV cultivars by maturity group, region and disease resistance; buy hybrid seed every season.
- Plant with the first effective rains at about 25 kg/ha, for example 90 cm × 30 cm, one plant per station.
- Basal compound at planting, ammonium nitrate top dressing in splits; keep weeds down for the first six to eight weeks.
- Control stalk borer, fall armyworm, maize streak virus and grey leaf spot; harvest after the black layer forms, dry to about 12.5% moisture and store safely.
:::
