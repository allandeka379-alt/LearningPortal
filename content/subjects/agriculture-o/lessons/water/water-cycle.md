## Introduction

The water that falls on a maize field near Chinhoyi in January may have evaporated from the Indian Ocean only days before. Some of it soaks into the soil and is taken up by the crop; some runs into the nearest stream and on to a dam; some sinks deep underground and may be pumped up years later from a borehole to water cattle in the dry season. This endless movement of water between the sea, the air and the land is the **water cycle**.

Every source of water a farmer uses, from rain to rivers to boreholes, is part of this cycle, and farming practices change how it behaves on the land. Examination questions ask you to *describe* the stages of the cycle, to *explain* its significance for agriculture and to *describe* groundwater resources and how they are reached.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Water cycle"}
The continuous movement of water between the oceans, the atmosphere and the land through evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff and groundwater flow.
:::

:::definition{term="Infiltration"}
The entry of water into the soil through its surface.
:::

:::definition{term="Groundwater"}
Water held underground in the pores and cracks of soil and rock below the water table.
:::

:::definition{term="Aquifer"}
A layer of permeable rock or sediment (for example sandstone, gravel or fractured rock) that stores groundwater and allows it to flow, so that it can supply wells and boreholes.
:::

:::definition{term="Water table"}
The upper surface of the saturated zone: below it, all pores are full of water.
:::

## The stages of the water cycle

1. **Evaporation:** the sun's heat changes water from oceans, lakes, dams, rivers and moist soil into water vapour.
2. **Transpiration:** plants lose water vapour from their leaves, mainly through the stomata. Evaporation and transpiration together are called **evapotranspiration**.
3. **Condensation:** as moist air rises it cools, and the water vapour condenses on tiny particles to form clouds.
4. **Precipitation:** droplets in clouds join until they are heavy enough to fall as rain, or as hail in thunderstorms.
5. **Interception:** some rain is caught on leaves and evaporates again before it reaches the ground.
6. **Infiltration and percolation:** rain soaks into the soil (infiltration) and moves down through it (percolation). Some is held as soil water for plants.
7. **Surface runoff:** rain that cannot infiltrate flows over the surface into streams, rivers and dams.
8. **Groundwater flow:** water that percolates below the root zone reaches the saturated zone and moves slowly through aquifers, feeding springs, rivers and wetlands, and eventually returning to the sea.

| Store | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Oceans | The largest store of water on Earth |
| Atmosphere | Water vapour and clouds |
| Surface water | Lakes, dams, rivers, wetlands (vleis) |
| Soil water | Water held between field capacity and the wilting point |
| Groundwater | Aquifers below the water table |

## Significance of the water cycle for agriculture

- **Rainfall** supplies almost all the water for rain-fed crops and pastures.
- **Infiltration** refills soil water for crops and recharges groundwater; runoff fills rivers and dams used for irrigation and livestock.
- **Evapotranspiration** is the crop's water use; it decides how much irrigation water is needed and how quickly soils dry out.
- **Groundwater** keeps springs and some rivers flowing in the dry season and supplies wells and boreholes.
- **Land use changes the cycle:** forests and grass cover increase interception and infiltration. Clearing trees, overgrazing and bare soil increase runoff, so floods are higher, soil is eroded and less water is stored for the dry season.

![Bar chart of where 250 cubic metres of rain from a 25 mm storm on 1 hectare goes: runoff 50 m3 (20%), infiltration 175 m3 (70%) and evaporation 25 m3 (10%), which add up to 250 m3.](media:diagrams/rain-partition-bar-chart.png "Figure: where the 250 m3 of rain from a 25 mm storm on 1 ha goes")

:::worked-solution[Where does the rain go?]
A storm drops $25\ \text{mm}$ of rain on a $1\ \text{ha}$ field. Measurements suggest that 20% runs off, 70% infiltrates and 10% evaporates soon after falling.

1. $1\ \text{mm}$ of rain on $1\ \text{m}^2$ gives $1\ \text{L}$. Area $= 10\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
2. Volume of rain $= 10\,000 \times 25 = 250\,000\ \text{L} = 250\ \text{m}^3$.
3. Runoff $= 20\%$ of $250 = 50\ \text{m}^3$.
4. Infiltration $= 70\%$ of $250 = 175\ \text{m}^3$, which refills soil water and may recharge groundwater.
5. Evaporation $= 10\%$ of $250 = 25\ \text{m}^3$. Check: $50 + 175 + 25 = 250\ \text{m}^3$.
:::

## Groundwater resources

### The underground zones

Below the surface lies an **unsaturated zone**, where pores contain both air and water. Deeper down is the **saturated zone**, where every pore is full; its top is the **water table**. The water table rises in the rainy season as water percolates down (recharge) and falls in the dry season or when too much water is pumped out.

An **aquifer** is a permeable layer within the saturated zone that can supply useful amounts of water. Clay and solid, unfractured granite are poor aquifers; sands, gravels, sandstone and fractured or weathered rock are better.

### Reaching groundwater

| Source | Description | Points to note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring | Groundwater flows out naturally where the water table meets the ground surface, often at the foot of a slope | Protect the eye of the spring from animals and pollution |
| Hand-dug well | A wide hole dug by hand to below the water table, lined with bricks or concrete rings | Shallow, so it may dry up when the water table falls; easily polluted unless covered |
| Borehole | A narrow hole drilled by machine, often tens of metres deep, lined with casing and fitted with a hand pump, motor pump or solar pump | Reaches deeper, more reliable water; costly to drill |

:::example{title="A well that dries up"}
Chipo's family well near Gweru held water at $6.5\ \text{m}$ below the surface in March, but by October the water level had fallen to $9.0\ \text{m}$, below the bottom of the well. The water table fell $9.0 - 6.5 = 2.5\ \text{m}$ through the dry season, as there was no recharge while water was still being drawn and plants and evaporation removed soil water. Deepening the well or drilling a borehole into the aquifer would give a more reliable supply; protecting the catchment above so more rain infiltrates would help recharge.
:::

### Protecting groundwater

- Site wells and boreholes **uphill** of, and well away from, pit latrines, kraals and dip tanks.
- **Cover** wells and build a raised concrete apron so dirty water drains away.
- Avoid **over-pumping**, which lowers the water table for everyone.
- Use fertilisers and pesticides carefully so that nitrates and chemicals do not leach down.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing evaporation and transpiration"}
Evaporation is from water surfaces and soil; transpiration is water vapour lost **through plants**, mainly from the stomata.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking groundwater is an underground lake"}
Most groundwater fills the pores and cracks of rock and sediment. It moves slowly through the aquifer; it is not usually a cave of open water.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting recharge"}
Groundwater is refilled only by water that infiltrates and percolates down. Practices that increase runoff reduce recharge.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Describe the cycle"}
Describe the stages in order and use the correct terms: evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, percolation and groundwater flow. Say what drives each step (the sun's heat, cooling of rising air, gravity).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Significance"}
For *explain the significance*, link each stage to a farming need: rainfall for crops, runoff for dams, infiltration for soil water and groundwater recharge, evapotranspiration for irrigation needs.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Water moves continuously between oceans, air and land by evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff and groundwater flow.
- Evapotranspiration is a crop's water use; infiltration refills soil water and recharges groundwater.
- Vegetation increases infiltration; bare and overgrazed land increases runoff, floods and erosion.
- An aquifer stores and transmits groundwater; the water table is the top of the saturated zone.
- Springs, wells and boreholes tap groundwater; protect them from pollution and over-pumping.
:::
