## Introduction

At a vegetable irrigation scheme near Mutare, a farmer opens a valve and water rushes from a buried main line through a hydrant into a row of sprinklers. Further along, another farmer's tomatoes are fed by black drip lines connected through a filter. If a washer is missing, a filter is blocked or a pipe has been left to bake in the sun for years, the system leaks, waters unevenly and costs money.

ZIMSEC Form 4 candidates are expected to *identify* irrigation equipment, *describe* its structure and *state* or *explain* its function, and to know how the equipment is assembled and maintained. Pumps are covered in a separate lesson on appropriate technology.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Main line"}
The large pipe that carries water from the pump or source to the field.
:::

:::definition{term="Lateral"}
A smaller pipe branching from the main or sub-main that carries water along the crop rows to the sprinklers or emitters.
:::

:::definition{term="Emitter (dripper)"}
A small device on a drip line that releases water slowly, at a steady rate in litres per hour, to the root zone of a plant.
:::

:::definition{term="Riser"}
A short vertical pipe that lifts a sprinkler above the crop so that the spray is not blocked by leaves.
:::

## Pipes

| Pipe | Properties | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PVC (rigid plastic) | Light, smooth, does not rust; becomes brittle in sunlight, so it is buried | Buried main and sub-main lines |
| Polyethylene (black, flexible) | Flexible, resists sunlight better, easily joined with fittings | Laterals and drip lines |
| Aluminium (with quick couplings) | Light and strong; easily carried and joined | Portable sprinkler laterals |
| Galvanised steel | Strong and rigid; can rust when the zinc coating wears | Risers, pump connections |
| Lay-flat hose | Flat when empty; rolls up for storage | Temporary supply lines |

The system is arranged like a tree: the **main line** feeds **sub-mains**, which feed the **laterals** in the field.

## Fittings

- **Couplings (connectors)**: join two pipes end to end.
- **Elbows (bends)**: change the direction of a pipe, usually by 90° or 45°.
- **Tees**: make a branch, for example where a lateral leaves the main.
- **Reducers**: join a larger pipe to a smaller one.
- **End caps (end plugs)**: close the end of a pipe or lateral; on drip lines they are removed to flush the line.
- **Washers and seals (rubber rings)**: make joints watertight.

## Valves and gauges

| Device | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Gate valve or ball valve | Turns the flow on and off, or controls it |
| Hydrant | Outlet on a buried main to which a portable lateral is connected |
| Non-return (check) valve | Lets water flow one way only, protecting the pump from backflow |
| Air-release valve | Lets trapped air out of pipes, so water flows freely and pipes are not damaged |
| Pressure regulator | Keeps pressure steady at the level the sprinklers or drippers need |
| Pressure gauge | Shows the water pressure, so faults such as leaks or blockages are noticed |

## Sprinklers

The common **impact (rotary) sprinkler** has a **nozzle** that forms a jet, a **swinging arm** on a **spring** that is struck by the jet and knocks the sprinkler round a little at a time, and a **bearing** on which the body turns. It is screwed onto a **riser** on the lateral. Sprinklers are set out so their circles **overlap**, giving even watering. **Mini-sprinklers** and **micro-jets** give small, gentle sprays for orchards and nurseries.

:::worked-solution[Application rate of a sprinkler system]
Sprinklers are spaced $12\ \text{m}$ apart along laterals that are $12\ \text{m}$ apart. Each sprinkler discharges $1\,440\ \text{L/h}$.

1. Area watered by one sprinkler $= 12 \times 12 = 144\ \text{m}^2$.
2. Application rate $= 1\,440 \div 144 = 10\ \text{L/m}^2$ per hour $= 10\ \text{mm/h}$ (since $1\ \text{L/m}^2 = 1\ \text{mm}$).
3. Time to apply $25\ \text{mm}$ $= 25 \div 10 = 2.5\ \text{h}$.
4. If the soil can take in water only at, say, $8\ \text{mm/h}$, water would run off: the farmer should use smaller nozzles or wider spacing.
:::

## Drip equipment

- **Filter** at the head of the system: a **screen (mesh)** filter, a **disc** filter or a **sand (media)** filter for dirty water. It removes silt, sand and organic matter that would block the emitters.
- **Fertiliser injector** (for example a venturi), which draws dissolved fertiliser into the water (fertigation).
- **Pressure regulator**, because drip works at low pressure.
- **Drip lines or drip tape**, laid along each row, with **emitters** spaced to suit the crop; **pressure-compensating** emitters give the same flow along a long or sloping line.
- **Flushing points** (end caps or valves) at the end of each line.

:::worked-solution[Water delivered by a drip system]
A plot has 20 drip lines, each $50\ \text{m}$ long, with an emitter every $0.5\ \text{m}$. Each emitter delivers $2\ \text{L/h}$.

1. Emitters per line $= 50 \div 0.5 = 100$.
2. Flow per line $= 100 \times 2 = 200\ \text{L/h}$.
3. Flow for the plot $= 20 \times 200 = 4\,000\ \text{L/h}$.
4. The pump and filter must supply at least $4\,000\ \text{L/h}$ at the right pressure, or the plot must be watered in sections.
:::

## Surface irrigation equipment

- **Siphon tubes**: curved plastic or aluminium tubes. The tube is filled with water, one end is placed in the canal and the other in the furrow; water then flows over the canal bank because the canal water level is higher than the outlet.
- **Gated pipes**: pipes with small sliding gates (outlets) spaced to match the furrows; opening a gate releases water into its furrow.
- **Canal structures** such as division boxes and checks control the water level and direct the flow.

## Assembling and caring for equipment

**Assembly**

1. Lay the main and sub-mains first, from the source towards the field, and fit valves and hydrants.
2. Join pipes with the correct fittings and washers so every joint is watertight.
3. **Flush** the pipes with water before fitting sprinklers or emitters, to remove soil and debris.
4. Fit the laterals, risers, sprinklers or drip lines; open the valve slowly and check pressure and leaks.

**Care and maintenance**

- Clean filters regularly, and **flush drip lines** by opening the end caps.
- Check sprinklers turn freely; replace worn nozzles, springs and washers.
- Repair leaks promptly; replace cracked pipes.
- Keep plastic pipes out of strong sun when not in use and protect lines from rodents, livestock and machinery.
- Drain portable pipes after use and store them on racks under cover.
- Treat drip lines with a recommended cleaning solution if algae or salt deposits build up.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing a hydrant with a valve"}
A **hydrant** is an outlet on a buried main where a lateral is connected; a **valve** controls flow anywhere in the system. A hydrant usually contains a valve.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving out the filter"}
Without a filter a drip system blocks within weeks. The filter is essential, not optional.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Identify, describe, state function"}
For each item give its **name**, a short **description** (material, shape) and its **function**: "Non-return valve: a valve that lets water flow one way only, protecting the pump from water flowing back."
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Maintenance answers"}
Give specific actions linked to a part: "clean the screen filter", "flush the laterals", "replace worn sprinkler washers". Vague answers such as "look after it" earn nothing.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Water flows from the main line through sub-mains to laterals; PVC is buried, polyethylene suits laterals and drip lines, aluminium suits portable laterals.
- Fittings (couplings, elbows, tees, reducers, end caps) join and direct pipes; valves, hydrants, gauges and regulators control flow and pressure.
- Impact sprinklers on risers spray overlapping circles; application rate (mm/h) = discharge (L/h) ÷ area per sprinkler (m²).
- Drip systems need a filter, regulator, drip lines and emitters; siphons and gated pipes feed furrows.
- Flush before use, clean filters, fix leaks, replace worn parts and store pipes out of the sun.
:::
