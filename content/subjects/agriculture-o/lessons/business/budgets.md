## Introduction

Before the rains Tatenda sits with his mother at their farm near Marondera. They can plant all 3 ha to maize as usual, or put one hectare into soya beans, which a buyer has offered to contract. Seed, fertiliser and labour must be paid for before any crop is sold, and the family's savings are limited. Which choice is likely to leave them better off, and can they afford it?

A **budget** answers questions like this **before** money is spent. A profit and loss account looks back at what happened; a budget looks forward at what is expected. ZIMSEC asks you to *explain* the importance of budgeting, *identify* sources of information, *calculate* gross margins and *prepare* partial and whole-farm budgets.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Budget"}
A plan, written in figures, of the expected income and costs of a farm or an enterprise for a future period.
:::

:::definition{term="Variable costs"}
Costs that change with the size of an enterprise and can be linked to it directly, such as seed, fertiliser, chemicals, feed, casual labour, transport and packaging.
:::

:::definition{term="Fixed costs"}
Costs that do not change with the size of an enterprise in the short term and are shared by the whole farm, such as permanent wages, rent, depreciation of buildings and machinery, insurance and interest on loans.
:::

:::definition{term="Gross output"}
The value of everything an enterprise produces: yield × price, including produce used at home.
:::

:::definition{term="Gross margin"}
Gross output of an enterprise minus its variable costs, usually given per hectare (crops) or per head (livestock).
:::

## Why budget?

- **Plan**: decide which enterprises to run and how big each should be.
- **Compare** alternatives before committing land, money and labour.
- **Check cash needs**: know when money must be spent and whether savings or a loan are needed.
- **Obtain credit**: lenders and contract buyers ask for a budget.
- **Control**: compare actual results with the budget during the season and act early.
- **Reduce risk**: test what happens if yields or prices are lower than hoped.

## Sources of information

- The farm's own **records** from previous seasons (yields, inputs, costs).
- **AGRITEX** extension officers and published **farm management handbooks**.
- **Input suppliers'** price lists; **buyers'** and marketing boards' price notices (for example the GMB for grain).
- **Banks**, contract companies, research stations and **other farmers**.

## Calculating a gross margin

$$\text{gross margin} = \text{gross output} - \text{variable costs}$$

Fixed costs are left out because they are shared by the whole farm and would be paid whichever crop is grown; this makes gross margins useful for **comparing enterprises**.

:::worked-solution[Gross margin for 2 ha of maize]
Tatenda expects 4.5 t/ha from 2 ha, so 9 t in total. Assume the grain price is US\$290 per tonne and these variable costs for the 2 ha:

| Variable cost | US\$ |
| --- | --- |
| Seed | 110 |
| Basal fertiliser | 330 |
| Top dressing | 250 |
| Chemicals | 90 |
| Hired labour | 160 |
| Transport | 70 |
| Bags | 40 |
| **Total** | **1,050** |

1. Gross output $= 9 \times 290 =$ US\$2,610.
2. Variable costs $=$ US\$1,050.
3. Gross margin $=$ US\$2,610 $-$ US\$1,050 $=$ US\$1,560 for 2 ha.
4. Gross margin per hectare $= 1\,560 \div 2 =$ **US\$780 per ha**.
:::

![Bar split of the 2 ha maize gross output of US\$2,610 (9 t at US\$290): variable costs of US\$1,050 (seed 110, basal fertiliser 330, top dressing 250, chemicals 90, hired labour 160, transport 70, bags 40) leave a gross margin of US\$1,560, which is US\$780 per hectare.](media:diagrams/maize-gross-margin-bar.png "Figure: where the US\$2,610 gross output of 2 ha of maize goes, leaving a gross margin of US\$1,560")

For livestock the gross margin is usually given **per head** (per cow, per ewe, per 100 layers), so herds of different sizes can be compared.

## Partial budgets

A **partial budget** tests a **change** to part of the farm (a new crop on one field, buying a machine, changing a feed) by looking only at what the change alters.

| Gains | Losses |
| --- | --- |
| A. Extra income from the change | C. Extra costs caused by the change |
| B. Costs saved by the change | D. Income lost because of the change |

$$\text{net change} = (A + B) - (C + D)$$

If the result is **positive** the change should raise profit; if **negative**, it should not be made on these figures.

:::worked-solution[Partial budget: one hectare of soya beans instead of maize]
Assume soya beans would yield 2.2 t/ha at a contract price of US\$480 per tonne with variable costs of US\$410 per ha, and that 1 ha of maize gives 4.5 t at US\$290 per tonne with variable costs of US\$525.

1. A, extra income: soya $= 2.2 \times 480 =$ US\$1,056.
2. B, costs saved: maize variable costs $=$ US\$525.
3. C, extra costs: soya variable costs $=$ US\$410.
4. D, income lost: maize $= 4.5 \times 290 =$ US\$1,305.
5. Gains $= 1\,056 + 525 =$ US\$1,581; losses $= 410 + 1\,305 =$ US\$1,715.
6. Net change $= 1\,581 - 1\,715 =$ **$-$US\$134**: on these figures the change would lower profit by US\$134.
:::

![Partial budget for 1 ha of soya beans instead of maize: gains are extra income US\$1,056 plus maize costs saved US\$525, a total of US\$1,581; losses are extra soya costs US\$410 plus maize income lost US\$1,305, a total of US\$1,715; the net change is minus US\$134 so the change would lower profit.](media:diagrams/soya-partial-budget-bars.png "Figure: gains and losses of replacing 1 ha of maize by soya beans (net change minus US\$134)")

The budget is not the whole decision: a soya crop also adds nitrogen to the soil for next season's maize and spreads the risk, benefits that are hard to put into figures.

## Whole-farm budgets

A **whole-farm budget** covers every enterprise. It is prepared when starting a farm, making a major change, or applying for a large loan.

1. List the planned enterprises and their sizes.
2. Work out the gross margin of each.
3. Add the gross margins to get the **total gross margin**.
4. Subtract the farm's **fixed costs** to get the expected **net farm income** (profit).

:::example{title="Whole-farm budget for a small mixed farm"}
Assume the farm expects gross margins of US\$1,560 from maize, US\$640 from broilers and US\$900 from vegetables, and fixed costs of US\$1,450 (a permanent worker, repairs and depreciation).

- Total gross margin $= 1\,560 + 640 + 900 =$ US\$3,100.
- Net farm income $= 3\,100 - 1\,450 =$ **US\$1,650**.
- If the family needs more than this to live on, it must enlarge or change an enterprise, or cut fixed costs.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Subtracting fixed costs in a gross margin"}
A gross margin subtracts **variable** costs only. Fixed costs come off the total gross margin in a whole-farm budget.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the income lost in a partial budget"}
Replacing maize with soya beans loses the maize income. Leaving out column D makes almost any change look profitable.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing totals and per-hectare figures"}
State whether a figure is for the whole field or per hectare, and divide by the area when asked for a gross margin per hectare.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Set out calculations"}
Write gross output (yield × price), list variable costs with a total, then subtract. Label each line and give units (US\$, US\$ per ha).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Explain, then judge"}
After a partial budget, state whether the change is worthwhile and add one factor the figures leave out (risk, soil fertility, labour peaks).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A budget is a forward plan in figures; it helps plan, compare, check cash needs, get credit and control.
- Information comes from records, extension officers, handbooks, suppliers, buyers, banks and other farmers.
- Variable costs change with enterprise size; fixed costs are shared by the whole farm.
- Gross margin = gross output − variable costs, per ha or per head.
- Partial budget: (extra income + costs saved) − (extra costs + income lost).
- Whole-farm budget: total gross margin − fixed costs = net farm income.
:::
