## Introduction

When Cyclone Idai struck eastern Zimbabwe in March 2019, farmers around Chimanimani and Chipinge lost crops, livestock, soil and homes within a few days. Most farm losses are less dramatic: a dry spell at tasselling, a hail storm, a disease outbreak in the broiler house, a fall in the price of tomatoes or the theft of a few goats. Farming is a business carried out in the open air, with living things, for markets the farmer does not control.

Farmers cannot remove risk, but they can **reduce its effects** and make **careful decisions**. ZIMSEC asks you to *distinguish* risk from uncertainty, *outline* the risks in agriculture, *explain* ways of minimising them, and *explain* the importance and steps of decision making; Cambridge asks you to explain decision making based on economic factors.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Risk"}
A situation in which the possible outcomes, and roughly how likely each is, can be estimated from past experience or records, so the chance of loss can be planned for and often insured.
:::

:::definition{term="Uncertainty"}
A situation in which the possible outcomes or their likelihood cannot be known or estimated, so it cannot be calculated or insured.
:::

:::definition{term="Diversification"}
Running several different enterprises so that a loss in one is balanced by income from the others.
:::

:::definition{term="Decision making"}
Choosing, after considering the alternatives, the course of action most likely to achieve the farmer's goals.
:::

## Risk and uncertainty compared

| Risk | Uncertainty |
| --- | --- |
| Outcomes and their likelihood can be estimated | Outcomes or their likelihood cannot be estimated |
| Based on past records (for example how often hail has hit the area) | No useful past record (a new disease, a sudden policy change) |
| Can usually be insured | Cannot be insured |
| Example: in a dry region, a crop failure in some years out of ten | Example: a new pest arriving from another continent, a sudden market collapse |

## Risks farmers face

- **Weather and climate**: drought and dry spells, floods, hail, frost, strong winds, cyclones.
- **Pests and diseases** of crops and livestock, including new ones such as fall armyworm.
- **Market and price risks**: produce prices fall at harvest; input prices rise; buyers fail to pay.
- **Fire and theft** of crops, livestock and equipment.
- **Policy and institutional risks**: changes in regulations, import rules, support schemes or land matters.
- **Human risks**: illness, injury or death of the farmer or workers.
- **Financial risks**: rising interest rates, loans that cannot be repaid.
- **Technology risks**: a new variety or machine that does not perform as expected.

## Reducing the effects of risk

| Method | How it reduces risk | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diversification | Losses in one enterprise are offset by others | Maize, groundnuts, goats and chickens on one farm |
| Insurance | An insurer pays out after a covered loss in return for a premium | Hail insurance on tobacco, livestock insurance |
| Irrigation and water harvesting | Reduces dependence on rainfall | Drip-irrigated vegetables, a small dam |
| Drought-tolerant crops and early varieties | Survive or escape dry spells | Sorghum and millets in dry areas; early-maturing maize |
| Contracts | Fix the price or guarantee a buyer before planting | Contract farming of soya beans or tobacco |
| Savings and reserves | Carry the family and farm through a bad season | Cash savings, stored grain, fodder reserves |
| Good husbandry | Prevents losses | Vaccination, dipping, IPM, conservation agriculture, firebreaks |
| Security | Prevents theft | Branding, kraaling animals at night, locked stores |
| Information | Better forecasts and prices lead to better decisions | Seasonal forecasts, AGRITEX advice, market prices |
| Flexibility | Allows a quick change of plan | Planting a short-season crop if rains start late |

:::worked-solution[Comparing the riskiness of two crops]
A farmer's records show these gross margins (US\$ per ha) for five seasons:

| Season | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maize | 600 | 750 | 200 | 680 | 720 |
| Sorghum | 420 | 460 | 380 | 450 | 440 |

1. Mean for maize $= (600 + 750 + 200 + 680 + 720) \div 5 = 2\,950 \div 5 =$ US\$590 per ha.
2. Mean for sorghum $= (420 + 460 + 380 + 450 + 440) \div 5 = 2\,150 \div 5 =$ US\$430 per ha.
3. Range for maize $= 750 - 200 =$ US\$550; range for sorghum $= 460 - 380 =$ US\$80.
4. Maize earns more on average but varies much more (season 3 was a drought year). A farmer with no savings may prefer some sorghum to make sure of a minimum income: a choice between **higher returns** and **lower risk**.
:::

## Why decision making matters

Every season a farmer decides what to grow, how much, when to plant, which inputs to buy, when and where to sell, whether to borrow. Good decisions:

- make the best use of **scarce resources** (land, labour, capital and management);
- raise **profit** and reduce **losses**;
- allow the farm to **respond to change** in weather, prices and policy;
- help the farmer reach the family's **goals** (food security, income, school fees).

## Economic factors in decisions

- **Costs** of inputs and **prices** of produce, and when they are paid and received.
- **Expected returns**: yields, gross margins and profit.
- **Capital and credit** available, and the interest cost of borrowing.
- **Opportunity cost** of land, labour and money.
- **Markets**: whether a buyer exists, distance and transport costs.
- **Risk**: how variable the returns are.
- **Diminishing returns**: how much input is worth using.

## Steps in making a farm decision

1. **Identify** the problem or opportunity.
2. **Collect information** (records, extension officers, prices, other farmers).
3. **List the alternatives**.
4. **Analyse** each alternative: costs, returns, risks (budgets, gross margins).
5. **Choose** the best alternative.
6. **Implement** the decision.
7. **Evaluate** the results, accept responsibility, and learn for next time.

:::example{title="A decision step by step"}
Farai's broilers near Bulawayo sell slowly in January when customers have little money after the holidays.

1. Problem: unsold birds eat feed and lose value in January.
2. Information: past sales records show slow January sales; a school and a restaurant want regular birds.
3. Alternatives: skip a January batch; sell to the school and restaurant on contract; slaughter and freeze.
4. Analysis: contracts give a steady market at a fixed price; freezing needs electricity and equipment.
5. Choice: supply the school and restaurant on contract, with a smaller January batch.
6. Implement, then 7. evaluate: compare January profit with last year's.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using risk and uncertainty as the same word"}
Risk can be estimated and insured; uncertainty cannot. Give an example of each.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing risks without saying how to reduce them"}
For each risk named, give a matching method: drought → irrigation or drought-tolerant crops; price falls → contracts or storage.
:::

:::mistake{title="Stopping at 'choose the best'"}
The decision process ends with implementing **and evaluating** the decision.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Explain a method"}
Say how it works, not just its name: "insurance: the farmer pays a premium, and if hail destroys the crop the insurer pays compensation, so the farmer can still repay loans".
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Steps in order"}
Learn the steps in order; marks are often given for the correct sequence.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Risk: outcomes and likelihood can be estimated and often insured. Uncertainty: they cannot.
- Risks: weather, pests and diseases, prices and markets, fire and theft, policy, illness, finance, technology.
- Reduce effects by diversification, insurance, irrigation, drought-tolerant crops, contracts, savings, good husbandry, security, information and flexibility.
- Good decisions make the best use of scarce resources and raise profit.
- Steps: identify, collect information, list alternatives, analyse, choose, implement, evaluate.
:::
