## Introduction

Nyasha's school ran a batch of 100 broilers near Gweru. When the birds were sold the class had more cash in the tin than at the start and declared the project a success. But had they counted the chicks, all the feed, the vaccines, the electricity for the brooder and the transport to market? A **profit and loss account** puts every item of income against every item of expenditure for a period, so the true result can be seen.

ZIMSEC asks you to *explain* the functions of a profit and loss account, *identify* the income and expenditure of a farm enterprise and *prepare* a profit and loss account. Your coursework project ends with one.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Enterprise"}
A single activity on a farm that produces something for sale or use, such as a broiler batch, a maize crop or a dairy herd.
:::

:::definition{term="Income (revenue)"}
The value of everything an enterprise produces in a period: sales, produce used at home or given away, and any increase in the value of stock.
:::

:::definition{term="Expenditure (expenses)"}
The cost of everything used to produce the output in that period, such as seed, feed, fertiliser, labour, transport and repairs.
:::

:::definition{term="Profit and loss account"}
A statement that lists the income and expenditure of an enterprise or farm over a period and shows the difference as a profit (income greater) or a loss (expenditure greater).
:::

:::definition{term="Valuation"}
The value of the stock (animals, feed, produce in store) on hand at the start (opening valuation) or end (closing valuation) of the period.
:::

## Income and expenditure of an enterprise

| Income | Expenditure |
| --- | --- |
| Sales of produce (crops, eggs, milk, animals) | Seed, seedlings, chicks or young stock bought |
| Value of produce eaten at home or given away | Feed, fertiliser, manure bought and chemicals |
| Closing valuation of stock still on hand | Opening valuation of stock at the start |
| By-products sold (manure, hides) | Hired labour and wages |
| | Veterinary costs: vaccines and medicines |
| | Fuel, electricity, transport, packaging, repairs, rent and interest |

## Functions of a profit and loss account

- Shows whether the enterprise made a **profit or a loss** in the period.
- Shows **how much** profit, so the farmer can compare enterprises and seasons.
- Shows which **costs are largest**, so savings can be found.
- Provides evidence for **loans**, partners and tax.
- Helps in **planning** and budgeting for the next period.

## Preparing the account

1. Choose the **period** (a season, a batch or a year) and the enterprise.
2. List every item of **income** with its value, including home use and the closing valuation.
3. List every item of **expenditure**, including the opening valuation.
4. Total each side.
5. **Profit** = total income − total expenditure. If expenditure is larger the result is a **loss**.

A common layout puts expenditure on the left and income on the right; the profit is entered on the expenditure side so both sides balance.

:::worked-solution[Profit and loss account for a broiler batch]
The class bought 100 day-old chicks at US\$0.90 each. Six birds died. The other 94 were sold at US\$6.50 each. Other costs: 8 bags of feed at US\$28 a bag, vaccines US\$8, electricity for brooding US\$25, transport US\$15 and litter US\$6.

| Expenditure | US\$ | Income | US\$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chicks (100 × 0.90) | 90 | Sales (94 × 6.50) | 611 |
| Feed (8 × 28) | 224 | | |
| Vaccines | 8 | | |
| Electricity | 25 | | |
| Transport | 15 | | |
| Litter | 6 | | |
| Total expenditure | 368 | Total income | 611 |
| **Profit** | **243** | | |

1. Income $= 94 \times 6.50 =$ US\$611.
2. Expenditure $= 90 + 224 + 8 + 25 + 15 + 6 =$ US\$368.
3. Profit $=$ US\$611 $-$ US\$368 $=$ **US\$243**.
4. Feed is by far the largest cost (US\$224 of US\$368), so feeding efficiency matters most for profit.
:::

### Including valuations

When stock is held over from one period to the next, it must be valued:

$$\text{profit} = (\text{sales} + \text{other income} + \text{closing valuation}) - (\text{opening valuation} + \text{expenses})$$

:::worked-solution[A rabbit project with valuations]
At the start of the year a club's rabbits were valued at US\$60. During the year it sold rabbits for US\$140 and spent US\$70 on feed and US\$10 on veterinary care. At the end of the year the rabbits on hand were valued at US\$95.

1. Income $=$ sales $+$ closing valuation $=$ US\$140 $+$ US\$95 $=$ US\$235.
2. Expenditure $=$ opening valuation $+$ expenses $=$ US\$60 $+$ US\$70 $+$ US\$10 $=$ US\$140.
3. Profit $=$ US\$235 $-$ US\$140 $=$ **US\$95**.
4. Without the valuations (US\$140 sales against US\$80 costs) the profit would be wrongly shown as US\$60, because the growth of the herd from US\$60 to US\$95 is part of what the club produced.
:::

:::example{title="Profit is not the same as cash"}
Tatenda's maize project sold grain for US\$400 and paid US\$310 in costs, so his cash rose by US\$90. But the family also ate maize worth US\$50 from the project, and he still owes US\$30 to a neighbour for ploughing.

- Income should include the US\$50 eaten at home: US\$450.
- Expenditure should include the US\$30 still owed: US\$340.
- Profit is US\$110, not the US\$90 increase in cash.
:::

## Using the account to improve the enterprise

A profit and loss account is most useful when it is compared: with the budget made before the period, with the same enterprise last season, and with other enterprises on the farm. Useful questions to ask are:

- **Which cost is largest?** In broilers it is usually feed, so feeder design, feed wastage and the price paid for feed deserve most attention.
- **Was output lower than planned?** High deaths, poor yields or low prices each point to a different problem.
- **Would a change pay?** A partial budget can then test the change before it is made.
- **Is the profit worth the work?** Compare the profit with the labour and money the enterprise ties up, and with what they could earn elsewhere.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Leaving out costs that were not paid in cash"}
Unpaid bills, family produce used and the opening valuation of stock all belong in the account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Counting birds that died as sales"}
Only birds actually sold earn income. Deaths raise the cost per bird sold.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling a negative result a profit"}
If expenditure exceeds income, write the result as a **loss** of that amount.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Layout earns marks"}
Use two columns with headings, list items with their workings (94 × 6.50), total both sides, and label the result clearly as profit or loss with US\$.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Explain the result"}
After calculating, comment: which cost was largest and one way to raise profit (reduce mortality, buy feed in bulk, sell at a better time).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Income: sales, produce used at home or given away, closing valuation.
- Expenditure: inputs, labour, veterinary costs, fuel, transport, repairs, opening valuation.
- Profit = total income − total expenditure; a negative result is a loss.
- The account shows profit or loss, compares enterprises, shows the largest costs and supports loans and planning.
- Profit is not the same as the change in cash.
:::
