## Introduction

Rudo adds a first bag of ammonium nitrate to a hectare of maize near Chinhoyi and the yield jumps. A second bag helps, but less. By the fifth bag she can hardly see a difference, and a sixth bag might even scorch the crop. The same happens when more and more workers are put on a small garden: at first the work goes faster, but soon they get in each other's way.

This pattern is so common that economists call it a **law**. It tells a farmer that the highest yield is **not** the most profitable one. ZIMSEC asks you to *explain* and *interpret* the law and *describe* its implications; Cambridge asks you to *explain* diminishing returns.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Law of diminishing returns"}
When more and more units of a variable input are added to a fixed input, the extra output from each additional unit of the variable input will eventually fall.
:::

:::definition{term="Fixed input"}
An input whose quantity cannot be changed in the short run, such as the area of land, a building or a machine.
:::

:::definition{term="Variable input"}
An input whose quantity the farmer can change, such as fertiliser, feed, seed, water or labour.
:::

:::definition{term="Total product (TP)"}
The total output obtained from a given amount of the variable input.
:::

:::definition{term="Average product (AP)"}
Output per unit of variable input: $\text{AP} = \dfrac{\text{TP}}{\text{units of input}}$.
:::

:::definition{term="Marginal product (MP)"}
The extra output from one more unit of the variable input: $\text{MP} = \dfrac{\text{change in TP}}{\text{change in input}}$.
:::

## Reading an input-output table

:::worked-solution[Workers on a one-hectare vegetable garden]
The land (1 ha) is fixed; the number of workers is varied (example figures).

| Workers | Total product (kg) | Marginal product (kg) | Average product (kg per worker) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | | |
| 1 | 400 | 400 | 400 |
| 2 | 1,000 | 600 | 500 |
| 3 | 1,500 | 500 | 500 |
| 4 | 1,800 | 300 | 450 |
| 5 | 1,900 | 100 | 380 |
| 6 | 1,850 | $-50$ | 308.3 |

1. MP for the 2nd worker $= 1\,000 - 400 = 600$ kg; for the 3rd $= 1\,500 - 1\,000 = 500$ kg.
2. AP for 4 workers $= 1\,800 \div 4 = 450$ kg per worker.
3. MP **rises** for the first two workers (they share tasks), then **falls** from the third: diminishing returns set in after the second worker.
4. TP is greatest with 5 workers (1,900 kg). The 6th worker has a **negative** MP: total output falls because workers crowd the plot and damage plants.
:::

## The graph in words

If total product is plotted against input:

- The **TP curve** rises steeply at first, then more and more gently, reaches a peak, and finally turns down.
- The **MP curve** rises at first, reaches a peak, then falls, crossing zero exactly where TP is at its maximum, and becomes negative after that.
- The **AP curve** rises and falls more gently; MP cuts AP at the highest point of AP.

### Three stages of production

| Stage | What happens | Should the farmer stop here? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I: increasing returns | MP rising and above AP; each extra unit adds more than the last | No: adding input makes every unit more productive |
| II: diminishing returns | MP falling but still positive; TP still rising | Yes: the best level lies somewhere in this stage |
| III: negative returns | MP negative; TP falling | Never: extra input lowers output and wastes money |

## Why the law matters: the most profitable level of input

A farmer should keep adding units of an input **as long as the value of the extra output is greater than the cost of the extra unit**. The best level is reached when the value of the marginal product has fallen to about the cost of the input.

$$\text{value of marginal product} = \text{MP} \times \text{price of output}$$

:::worked-solution[How many bags of top dressing pay?]
On one hectare of maize, extra bags of nitrogen fertiliser give these yields (example figures). Assume maize sells at US\$0.30 per kg and a bag of fertiliser costs US\$35.

| Bags per ha | Yield (kg/ha) | MP (kg) | Value of MP (US\$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 2,000 | | |
| 1 | 2,600 | 600 | 180 |
| 2 | 3,100 | 500 | 150 |
| 3 | 3,450 | 350 | 105 |
| 4 | 3,600 | 150 | 45 |
| 5 | 3,650 | 50 | 15 |

1. Value of MP of the 4th bag $= 150 \times 0.30 =$ US\$45, more than its cost of US\$35, so the 4th bag **pays**.
2. Value of MP of the 5th bag $= 50 \times 0.30 =$ US\$15, less than US\$35, so the 5th bag **loses** US\$20.
3. The most profitable rate is **4 bags per hectare**, even though the highest yield comes with 5 bags.
:::

If the price of maize rises or the price of fertiliser falls, the profitable rate moves **up**; if fertiliser becomes dearer or maize cheaper, it moves **down**.

## Implications for farm decisions

- **Fertiliser and manure**: follow recommended rates; heavy extra doses give little extra yield and can scorch crops, cause lodging, leach and pollute water.
- **Feed**: extra feed raises growth or milk at first, but each extra kilogram gives less; overfeeding wastes money and makes animals too fat.
- **Labour**: too many workers on a small area get in each other's way.
- **Seed and plant population**: beyond the best population, plants compete for water, light and nutrients and yield per plant falls.
- **Irrigation and chemicals**: extra water or sprays beyond the need waste money and can cause waterlogging, leaching or residues.
- The law applies because one input is **fixed**: to raise output a lot, the farmer may need more land, a bigger building or better technology (a new variety), not just more of the variable input.

:::example{title="Explaining the law with stock feed"}
A dairy farmer near Marondera raises the dairy meal given to each cow step by step. Milk rises quickly at first, then each extra kilogram of meal adds less milk. The cow's capacity to digest and turn feed into milk is the fixed factor. The farmer stops raising the meal when the extra milk is worth less than the extra meal.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying total output falls as soon as returns diminish"}
Under diminishing returns **total** product is still rising; it is the **extra** output from each unit (MP) that falls.
:::

:::mistake{title="Aiming for maximum yield"}
Maximum yield is where MP is zero. The most profitable level is lower, where the value of MP equals the cost of the input.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the fixed factor"}
The law only applies when at least one input (land, a building, the animal's capacity) is held fixed.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Calculating from a table"}
Show MP as the difference between successive totals and AP as total ÷ units of input. Say at which unit MP begins to fall: that is where diminishing returns set in.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Describing a graph"}
Use words like "rises steeply", "levels off", "reaches a maximum at" and "falls", with the input values at which each happens.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Adding more of a variable input to a fixed input eventually gives smaller extra output: diminishing returns.
- TP is total output; AP = TP ÷ input; MP = change in TP ÷ change in input.
- Stages: increasing returns (I), diminishing returns (II, where the best level lies), negative returns (III, never).
- Most profitable input level: keep adding while MP × output price is greater than the cost of the extra unit.
- Applies to fertiliser, feed, labour, seed rate, water and chemicals; maximum yield is not maximum profit.
:::
