## Introduction

Every October, farmers queue at agro-dealers in Chinhoyi and Marondera to buy fertiliser for the coming maize season. Fertiliser is often the largest cash cost of growing a crop, so it pays to buy the right type, the right quantity, and to apply it at the right time and place. A farmer who buys two bags too few cannot finish the field; one who buys two too many has tied up money that could have bought seed.

Examiners ask you to *distinguish* straight from compound fertilisers, *interpret* a fertiliser ratio, *describe* methods of application, *distinguish* basal from top dressing and, above all, *calculate* fertiliser quantities. Calculations appear in almost every paper, so practise them until they are automatic.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Inorganic (artificial) fertiliser"}
A manufactured or mined chemical compound that supplies one or more plant nutrients in a concentrated, mostly soluble form.
:::

:::definition{term="Straight fertiliser"}
A fertiliser that supplies only one of the three primary nutrients (N, P or K), for example ammonium nitrate (nitrogen only).
:::

:::definition{term="Compound fertiliser"}
A fertiliser that supplies two or more of the primary nutrients in each granule, in a fixed ratio, for example Compound D.
:::

:::definition{term="Fertiliser ratio (analysis)"}
Three numbers giving the percentage by mass of nitrogen (N), phosphate (as $\text{P}_2\text{O}_5$) and potash (as $\text{K}_2\text{O}$) in a fertiliser, in that order.
:::

:::definition{term="Basal dressing"}
Fertiliser applied at or just before planting, usually supplying phosphorus and potassium with some nitrogen, to support early root growth.
:::

:::definition{term="Top dressing"}
Fertiliser, usually nitrogen, applied to a growing crop after it has emerged.
:::

## Common fertilisers

| Fertiliser | Type | Typical analysis (assume) | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ammonium nitrate (AN) | Straight N | 34.5% N | Top dressing cereals |
| Urea | Straight N | 46% N | Top dressing; loses ammonia if left on the surface |
| Ammonium sulfate | Straight N (with sulfur) | 21% N | Top dressing; strongly acidifying |
| Single superphosphate (SSP) | Straight P (with calcium and sulfur) | about 18-20% $\text{P}_2\text{O}_5$ | Basal for legumes and pastures |
| Muriate of potash (potassium chloride) | Straight K | 60% $\text{K}_2\text{O}$ | Basal, where potassium is short |
| Compound D | Compound N:P:K | 7:14:7 | Basal for maize and many other crops |

A ratio of **7:14:7** means that every 100 kg of the fertiliser contains 7 kg N, 14 kg $\text{P}_2\text{O}_5$ and 7 kg $\text{K}_2\text{O}$. So a $50\ \text{kg}$ bag holds $50 \times \frac{7}{100} = 3.5\ \text{kg N}$, $50 \times \frac{14}{100} = 7\ \text{kg}$ of $\text{P}_2\text{O}_5$ and $3.5\ \text{kg}$ of $\text{K}_2\text{O}$. The other 36 kg is mostly the rest of the chemical compounds and filler.

## Methods of application

| Method | How it is done | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broadcasting | Spread evenly over the whole surface by hand or spreader, often worked in | Closely spaced crops (wheat, pastures) and lime |
| Banding | Placed in a band along the row, a few centimetres to the side of and below the seed | Row crops; phosphorus near young roots |
| Placement (spot or station) | A measured amount (for example a cup or bottle-top) placed in each planting hole or beside each station | Planting basins and hand planting |
| Side dressing | Placed beside the rows of a growing crop and covered | Top dressing maize and vegetables |
| Foliar feeding | A dilute solution sprayed on the leaves | Trace elements; quick correction |
| Fertigation | Dissolved in irrigation water | Drip-irrigated vegetables |

## Basal and top dressing

- **Basal dressing** supplies **phosphorus** (roots, early growth) and **potassium**, which move little in the soil, so they are put near the seed at planting. Keep it a few centimetres from the seed: concentrated fertiliser touching a seed can **burn** it by drawing water out.
- **Top dressing** supplies **nitrogen**, which is soluble and easily leached. It is applied when the crop can use it, after emergence, and often **split**: for maize, for example, part when the plants are about knee-high and the rest a few weeks later. Apply it to moist soil, beside (not on) the plants, and not on wet leaves, which it can scorch.

## Fertiliser calculations

Use these steps every time:

1. Convert the area to **hectares** ($1\ \text{ha} = 10\,000\ \text{m}^2$).
2. Fertiliser needed (kg) $=$ rate (kg/ha) $\times$ area (ha).
3. Bags $=$ kg $\div$ mass of one bag, **rounded up** to a whole bag.
4. For grams per station: kg/ha $\times 1000 \div$ plants (stations) per hectare.

:::worked-solution[Bags of basal fertiliser for a field]
A farmer plans to apply Compound D at $350\ \text{kg/ha}$ to $2.5\ \text{ha}$ of maize. Fertiliser is sold in $50\ \text{kg}$ bags.

1. Fertiliser needed $= 350 \times 2.5 = 875\ \text{kg}$.
2. Bags $= 875 \div 50 = 17.5$.
3. Half a bag cannot be bought, so round **up**: the farmer buys **18 bags**.
:::

:::worked-solution[Grams of fertiliser per planting station]
Maize is planted at $90\ \text{cm} \times 30\ \text{cm}$, one plant per station. The basal rate is $350\ \text{kg/ha}$.

1. Area per station $= 0.9 \times 0.3 = 0.27\ \text{m}^2$.
2. Stations per hectare $= 10\,000 \div 0.27 = 37\,037$ (to the nearest whole station).
3. Grams per station $= \frac{350 \times 1000}{37\,037} = 9.45\ \text{g}$.
4. In practice the farmer finds a small container that holds about 9-10 g and uses one level measure per station.
:::

:::worked-solution[Ammonium nitrate to supply a nitrogen target]
A crop needs $80\ \text{kg}$ of nitrogen per hectare from ammonium nitrate (assume 34.5% N).

1. Each kilogram of ammonium nitrate supplies $0.345\ \text{kg N}$.
2. Ammonium nitrate needed $= 80 \div 0.345 = 231.9\ \text{kg/ha}$ (to one decimal place).
3. Check: $231.9 \times 0.345 = 80.0\ \text{kg N}$.
:::

## Storing and handling fertiliser

- Store bags in a **dry**, roofed, well-ventilated shed, stacked on pallets off the floor and away from walls, because fertiliser absorbs moisture and cakes.
- Keep fertiliser away from **seed, food, animal feed and pesticides**.
- Ammonium nitrate is an **oxidising** substance: keep it away from fuels, oil, straw and naked flames.
- Wear gloves, wash hands after handling and keep children and animals away.

:::example{title="Choosing the method for the situation"}
Thandeka grows maize in planting basins under conservation agriculture. Broadcasting would feed the weeds between the basins, so she uses **placement**: a measured cap of Compound D in each basin at planting, covered with soil before the seed goes in. Six weeks later she side-dresses a measured cap of ammonium nitrate beside each plant on moist soil, so almost all of the fertiliser reaches the crop.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Forgetting to convert to hectares"}
A rate in kg/ha multiplied by an area in m² gives an answer 10,000 times too large. Convert first: $4\,500\ \text{m}^2 = 0.45\ \text{ha}$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Rounding bags down"}
$17.5$ bags means the farmer must buy **18**. Rounding down leaves part of the field unfertilised.
:::

:::mistake{title="Multiplying by the percentage when you should divide"}
To find the **product** needed for a nutrient target, **divide** by the fraction of nutrient (80 ÷ 0.345). Multiplying gives the nutrient **in** a mass of product.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Show every step"}
Write the formula, substitute with units, and state the final answer with units and rounding ("18 bags, rounded up"). Method marks are given even if the final figure slips.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Describe and explain"}
For "distinguish basal from top dressing", give **when**, **what nutrient** and **why** for each. For application methods, state how it is done and one crop it suits.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Straight fertilisers supply one primary nutrient; compound fertilisers supply two or more in a fixed ratio.
- A ratio such as 7:14:7 gives % N, % $\text{P}_2\text{O}_5$ and % $\text{K}_2\text{O}$.
- Methods: broadcasting, banding, placement, side dressing, foliar feeding and fertigation.
- Basal dressing (P and K) goes on at planting; nitrogen top dressing goes on the growing crop, often split.
- Fertiliser (kg) = rate × area (ha); bags are rounded up; g per station = kg/ha × 1000 ÷ stations per ha.
:::
