## Introduction

Look across the farmland of the Zimbabwean highveld and you will see long, curving lines of grass running across the slopes. These are **contour ridges**, built to catch runoff before it can gather speed and carry soil away. Below them, broad strips of grass lead water gently down to the stream. Together with good farming practices, these structures have protected a great deal of land for many decades.

Soil conservation means keeping soil in place and in good condition. It combines **biological methods**, which use plants and management to protect the soil surface, with **mechanical methods**, which use structures to control the flow of water. Examination questions ask you to *describe* methods for arable and grazing land, to *explain* how a structure works, to *describe* how one is laid out or maintained and to *suggest* how an eroded area can be reclaimed.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Soil conservation"}
The protection of soil from erosion and loss of fertility, so that it remains productive for future generations.
:::

:::definition{term="Contour"}
An imaginary line joining points of equal height on a slope. Working along the contour means working across the slope, never up and down it.
:::

:::definition{term="Contour ridge"}
A channel dug across a slope on a very gentle gradient, with the excavated soil thrown downhill to form a bank. It intercepts runoff and leads it slowly to a waterway.
:::

:::definition{term="Vertical interval"}
The difference in height between two neighbouring contour ridges. The horizontal distance between them depends on the vertical interval and the slope.
:::

## Biological methods

These methods protect the surface from raindrops and wind, slow runoff and bind soil with roots.

- **Cover crops** such as velvet beans, cowpeas or pumpkins grow fast and spread over the ground.
- **Mulching** with crop residues or grass absorbs raindrop impact, slows runoff and adds organic matter.
- **Contour cultivation**: ploughing, planting and weeding along the contour, so each furrow and row holds water back.
- **Strip cropping**: strips of a dense crop or grass alternate across the slope with strips of a row crop such as maize, so soil washed from one strip is trapped by the next.
- **Grass strips**: permanent strips of grass, for example vetiver, along the contour trap soil and slow water.
- **Crop rotation and intercropping** with legumes and grass leys keep the ground covered for longer and build organic matter.
- **Early planting and good fertility** give a crop canopy that covers the soil before the heaviest storms.
- **Afforestation**: planting trees on steep slopes, hilltops and gully sides.
- **Controlled grazing** on grazing land: keeping stocking rates within the carrying capacity, rotational grazing, resting paddocks so grass recovers, and avoiding burning.

## Mechanical methods

| Structure | Where and how | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Storm drain (diversion drain) | A large channel and bank at the top of a field | Intercepts runoff from land higher up and diverts it to a waterway before it reaches the field |
| Contour ridge | Channel and bank across the slope at regular spacings, on a very gentle gradient | Breaks the slope into short sections, catching runoff before it gathers speed |
| Grass waterway | A broad, shallow, grassed channel running downslope | Carries water from ridges and drains safely to a stream without cutting a gully |
| Terraces | Level or nearly level steps cut into a steep slope, with walls of stones or grass | Allow crops on steep land; water soaks in on each step |
| Gabions | Wire-mesh baskets filled with stones | Stabilise gully heads, river banks and spillways; water passes through slowly |
| Check dams | Low walls of stones, brushwood or sandbags built across a gully | Slow the flow, trap silt and let the gully fill up and grass over |

### Laying out contour ridges

1. Build a **storm drain** above the field first, so runoff from higher land is kept out.
2. Measure the **slope** and choose the vertical interval; the horizontal spacing follows from it (steeper slopes need closer ridges).
3. **Peg the line** across the slope with a line level, an A-frame or a surveyor's level, so that it falls very gently towards the waterway. Smooth out sharp bends.
4. **Dig the channel** along the pegs and throw the soil **downhill** to form the bank; compact the bank.
5. **Plant grass** on the bank and in the outlet to the waterway.

The design figures (channel size, gradient and spacing for each slope) come from AGRITEX design tables; examination questions give the figures you need.

:::worked-solution[Spacing contour ridges]
A field rises $2\ \text{m}$ over a horizontal distance of $50\ \text{m}$ and is $200\ \text{m}$ long down the slope. The extension officer recommends a vertical interval of $1.0\ \text{m}$.

1. Slope $= \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{horizontal distance}} \times 100 = \frac{2}{50} \times 100 = 4\%$.
2. Horizontal spacing $= \frac{\text{vertical interval}}{\text{slope}} \times 100 = \frac{1.0}{4} \times 100 = 25\ \text{m}$.
3. Number of ridges on the field $= \frac{200}{25} = 8$ (the first ridge one spacing below the storm drain).
4. On a steeper slope, the same vertical interval gives a smaller spacing, so more ridges are needed.
:::

:::worked-solution[Fall along a channel]
Assume a ridge channel $375\ \text{m}$ long is designed with a gradient of 1 in 250 towards the waterway.

1. The channel falls $1\ \text{m}$ for every $250\ \text{m}$ of length.
2. Total fall $= \frac{375}{250} = 1.5\ \text{m}$ from its far end to the waterway.
3. Such a gentle fall lets water flow slowly, so it does not scour the channel.
:::

### Maintenance

- Remove **silt** from channels after the rains; repair breaks in banks at once.
- Keep **grass** on banks and waterways healthy; never plough or plant crops in waterways.
- Keep **livestock** off banks and waterways, or provide proper crossing places.
- Check **outlets** after each heavy storm and fill small gullies before they grow.

## Reclaiming eroded land

:::example{title="Reclaiming a gully on a school farm near Gweru"}
A gully 2 m deep is growing back towards the school's maize field.

1. **Stop the water**: build a storm drain above the gully head to divert runoff to a grassed waterway.
2. **Stabilise the head**: place a gabion or pack stones at the head so it stops cutting back.
3. **Build check dams** of stones or brushwood across the gully every few metres to slow water and trap silt.
4. **Revegetate**: plant grass runners and trees on the sides and on the trapped silt.
5. **Fence it off** from livestock until the cover is established, and slope the sides gently where possible.
:::

On overgrazed land, reclamation means **reducing stock numbers**, **resting** the area, **reseeding** grass, filling small gullies and building check dams where needed.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Throwing soil uphill"}
The soil from a contour ridge channel is thrown **downhill** to form the bank. A bank above the channel would not hold the water.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ridges that are dead level or too steep"}
A ridge with no fall holds water that may break the bank; one with too much fall becomes a gully. It needs a very gentle fall to a waterway.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the waterway"}
Contour ridges and storm drains must empty into a grassed waterway. Without one, the collected water cuts a new gully.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Biological or mechanical?"}
Questions often ask for both kinds. Biological methods use plants and management (mulch, cover crops, grass strips, controlled grazing); mechanical methods are structures (ridges, drains, terraces, gabions, check dams).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Describe and explain"}
To *describe* a structure, say where it is built and what it is made of. To *explain* how it conserves soil, say what it does to runoff: intercepts it, slows it, spreads it or lets it soak in.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Biological methods: cover crops, mulch, contour cultivation, strip cropping, grass strips, rotation, afforestation, controlled grazing.
- Mechanical methods: storm drains, contour ridges, grass waterways, terraces, gabions and check dams.
- Contour ridges are pegged across the slope with a gentle fall, soil thrown downhill, and emptied into a grassed waterway.
- Horizontal spacing = vertical interval ÷ slope % × 100; steeper slopes need closer ridges.
- Maintain structures by de-silting, repairing and keeping grass cover; reclaim gullies with diversion, check dams, gabions and replanting.
:::
