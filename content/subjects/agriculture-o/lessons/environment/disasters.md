## Introduction

In March 2019, **Cyclone Idai** brought violent winds and torrential rain to eastern Zimbabwe. In Chimanimani and Chipinge districts, rivers burst their banks, landslides buried fields and homesteads, and roads and bridges were swept away. Farmers lost crops, livestock, stored grain and years of work in a few days.

A **natural disaster** is a natural event that causes serious damage to people, property and livelihoods. Farmers cannot prevent disasters, but they can **reduce the risk** by preparing in advance and limiting the damage when a disaster strikes. This topic is examined in ZIMSEC Paper 1 and Paper 2: you must **explain the effects** of each disaster on agriculture and **outline precautionary measures**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Natural disaster"}
A natural event, such as a flood, cyclone, drought or fire, that causes serious loss of life, property, crops, livestock or livelihoods.
:::

:::definition{term="Disaster risk management"}
Planning and action to reduce the chance of a disaster causing harm, to prepare for it, to respond when it happens and to recover afterwards.
:::

:::definition{term="Fire guard"}
A strip of land cleared of all burnable material, by slashing, ploughing or controlled burning, to stop a veld fire from spreading.
:::

:::definition{term="Insurance"}
An agreement in which a farmer pays a regular sum (the premium) to an insurer, who pays compensation if the insured crop, animal or property is lost or damaged by a named risk.
:::

:::definition{term="Early warning"}
Advance information, such as a weather forecast or cyclone alert, that gives farmers time to protect lives, animals and property.
:::

## Effects of natural disasters on agriculture

| Disaster | Main effects on crops | Main effects on livestock and property |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hailstorm | Shreds leaves, breaks stems, bruises and knocks off fruit; leaf crops such as tobacco and vegetables can be ruined in minutes | Injures or kills poultry and young animals in the open; damages roofs and greenhouses |
| Flood | Waterlogs or washes away crops; deposits silt and sand on fields; leaches nutrients | Drowns animals; destroys fences, pens, stores, roads and bridges; spreads water-borne diseases |
| Cyclone | Very strong winds flatten crops and orchards; torrential rain causes floods and landslides | Destroys buildings and infrastructure; drowns or buries livestock; cuts off markets and supplies |
| Whirlwind | Flattens crops along a narrow track | Lifts roofs from houses, barns and poultry houses |
| Veld fire | Burns standing crops, stored hay, woodlots and orchards | Destroys grazing, fences and buildings; kills or injures animals and wildlife; bare soil then erodes |
| Drought | Wilting, poor pollination, crop failure | Shortage of grazing and drinking water; loss of condition and deaths; forced sales at low prices |
| Heat wave | Wilting, flower and fruit drop, sun scald | Heat stress: poultry and pigs may die; milk and egg production fall |

Disasters also have **wider effects**: food shortages, loss of income, debt, selling of assets such as cattle, and outbreaks of disease among people and animals.

## Precautionary measures

### Weather forecasts and early warning

Farmers should follow **weather forecasts** and **seasonal forecasts** issued by the national meteorological services and passed on by radio, mobile phones and AGRITEX extension officers. Forecasts help farmers decide **what and when to plant**, when to harvest and when to move animals and property to safety.

### Disaster preparedness

- Have a **plan**: where animals will be moved in a flood, who does what, where seed and documents are kept.
- Store seed, grain and feed in **raised, secure** buildings away from flood-prone land.
- Keep **emergency supplies**: water, feed, veterinary drugs, fuel.
- Do not build homes, stores or pens on **flood plains** or below unstable slopes.

### Conservation structures

**Contour ridges, storm drains, grassed waterways, gabions** and **dams** slow and guide runoff, reducing flood and erosion damage. Keeping **vegetation on slopes and stream banks** reduces landslides and siltation.

### Fire guards and fire control

- Make **fire guards** around the farm boundary, buildings, woodlots, haystacks and grazing paddocks **before the dry season**.
- Keep **firefighting tools** (beaters, water, knapsack sprayers) ready.
- Never leave fires unattended; teach the community about the dangers of careless burning.

:::worked-solution[Area cleared for a fire guard]
A farmer makes a fire guard 10 m wide along all four sides of a paddock that measures 600 m by 400 m. Ignore the small overlaps at the corners.

1. Length of the guard = perimeter $= 2 \times (600 + 400) = 2\,000\ \text{m}$.
2. Area cleared $\approx 2\,000 \times 10 = 20\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
3. In hectares: $20\,000 \div 10\,000 = 2\ \text{ha}$.
4. The guard must be cleared or burnt under control before the grass dries out, and kept clear through the dry season.
:::

### Insurance

Insurance spreads the cost of a disaster: many farmers each pay a small premium, and those who suffer losses are compensated. Crops such as tobacco are often insured against **hail** and **fire**.

:::worked-solution[Hail insurance for tobacco]
Chipo insures 4 ha of tobacco against hail at US\$2,500 per hectare. The premium is 4% of the sum insured. A storm damages 30% of the crop.

1. Sum insured $= 4 \times 2\,500 =$ US\$10,000.
2. Premium $= \dfrac{4}{100} \times 10\,000 =$ US\$400.
3. Compensation for 30% damage $= \dfrac{30}{100} \times 10\,000 =$ US\$3,000.
4. Paying US\$400 protected Chipo from a US\$3,000 loss. In a season with no hail, the US\$400 is a cost, but it buys security.
:::

### Drought-tolerant crops and other drought measures

Growing **sorghum, millets, cowpeas and early-maturing varieties**, harvesting water, conserving soil moisture, keeping **feed reserves** (hay, silage, crop residues) and **destocking early** in a drought all reduce losses.

### Protecting livestock and crops from heat and storms

Provide **shade, water and ventilation** during heat waves; house poultry in well-ventilated buildings; use **hail nets** over high-value crops; and plant **windbreaks** to reduce wind damage.

:::example{title="Lessons from a cyclone"}
After Cyclone Idai, communities in Chimanimani rebuilt with lessons in mind: homes and stores were moved away from river banks and the foot of unstable slopes; vegetation was replanted on slopes to hold the soil; and early-warning messages through radio and phones were taken seriously in later storm seasons. These are examples of **disaster risk reduction**: acting before the next event, not only responding after it.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing effects without saying how"}
"Floods affect crops" earns nothing. Write "floods waterlog the soil, so roots lack oxygen and the crop dies" or "floods wash away topsoil and crops".
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying insurance prevents disasters"}
Insurance does not prevent damage; it **compensates** the farmer for losses, so the farm can recover.
:::

:::mistake{title="Making fire guards after the fires start"}
Fire guards must be prepared **before** the grass dries out at the start of the dry season.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "**explain** the effects of a disaster", give the effect on crops, on livestock and on property or income, each with its cause. For "**outline** precautionary measures", match each measure to the disaster it reduces: fire guards → veld fires; forecasts and moving animals → floods and cyclones; drought-tolerant crops → drought.
:::

:::exam-tip
Insurance calculations are percentages of the sum insured. Show the sum insured, the premium and any compensation, with US\$ signs.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Hail, floods, cyclones, whirlwinds, veld fires, drought and heat waves damage crops, livestock, buildings and incomes.
- Cyclone Idai (March 2019) caused floods and landslides in eastern Zimbabwe, especially Chimanimani and Chipinge.
- Weather forecasts and early warning give time to protect lives, animals and property.
- Preparedness, conservation structures and fire guards reduce damage; insurance compensates losses.
- Drought-tolerant crops, water harvesting, feed reserves and early destocking reduce drought losses.
:::
