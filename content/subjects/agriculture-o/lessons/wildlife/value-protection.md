## Introduction

Near Hwange National Park, Thandeka's family earns part of its income from wildlife: her brother guides tourists, her mother sells crafts at the lodge, and the community receives a share of hunting fees for local projects. Yet the same elephants that bring tourists also trample the family's maize, and a lion once killed two of their cattle.

**Wildlife** means the wild animals and plants of an area. It is a valuable resource for farmers and the nation, but it must be **used sustainably** and **managed** where it conflicts with people. ZIMSEC Forms 1 and 2 ask you to **explain** the value of wildlife, **identify** flora and fauna, **classify** animals by feeding habit, **name** the big five, **explain** sustainable use and **describe** how dangerous and problem animals are dealt with.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Wildlife"}
The wild (undomesticated) animals and plants that live naturally in an area.
:::

:::definition{term="Flora and fauna"}
Flora means the plants of an area; fauna means its animals.
:::

:::definition{term="Herbivore"}
An animal that feeds only on plants: **grazers** eat grass, **browsers** eat leaves, twigs and shoots of trees and shrubs.
:::

:::definition{term="Carnivore"}
An animal that feeds mainly on the flesh of other animals.
:::

:::definition{term="Omnivore"}
An animal that eats both plant and animal food.
:::

:::definition{term="Sustainable utilisation"}
Using wildlife for human benefit at a rate and in a way that does not reduce populations or damage habitats, so that the resource lasts for future generations.
:::

:::definition{term="Problem animal"}
A wild animal that repeatedly damages crops or property, kills livestock or threatens people.
:::

## The value of wildlife

| Type of value | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Socio-economic | Tourism income and jobs (guides, lodges, crafts); foreign currency; game meat; hides and skins; hunting fees; income for communities through programmes such as CAMPFIRE; game ranching and crocodile farming |
| Cultural | Animals as **totems** (for example shumba, the lion; nzou, the elephant; soko, the monkey); taboos that protect animals and sacred places; traditional medicines from plants; songs, stories and art |
| Ecological | Each species has a role in **food chains**; predators control herbivore numbers; herbivores and fire shape vegetation; insects and birds **pollinate** flowers; animals **disperse seeds**; scavengers and decomposers recycle nutrients; diversity keeps ecosystems stable |

## Flora and fauna of Zimbabwe

- **Flora:** woodland trees (msasa, mopane, mukwa, baobab), grasses of the veld, aloes, cycads, orchids, wild fruits (mazhanje, mauyu from the baobab) and medicinal plants.
- **Fauna:** large mammals (elephant, buffalo, giraffe, zebra, lion, leopard, cheetah, wild dog, hippo, rhinoceros, kudu, sable, impala), primates (baboons, monkeys), reptiles (crocodiles, pythons, tortoises), birds (fish eagles, hornbills, ground hornbills, vultures), fish (tigerfish, bream) and countless insects.

## Classifying animals by feeding habit

| Feeding group | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Herbivores: grazers | Zebra, buffalo, white rhinoceros, hippo, wildebeest, sable |
| Herbivores: browsers | Giraffe, kudu, black rhinoceros, duiker |
| Herbivores: mixed feeders | Elephant, impala (graze in the wet season and browse in the dry season) |
| Carnivores | Lion, leopard, cheetah, wild dog, crocodile, jackal; hyenas both hunt and scavenge |
| Omnivores | Baboon, bushpig, many monkeys |

:::example{title="Classifying animals from a game count"}
A ranger's notebook records: "kudu feeding on mopane leaves; zebra on short grass; a baboon troop eating fruit and insects; wild dogs chasing impala."

- Kudu: **herbivore (browser)**, eating leaves of trees.
- Zebra: **herbivore (grazer)**, eating grass.
- Baboon: **omnivore**, eating fruit and insects.
- Wild dog: **carnivore**, hunting impala.
- Impala: **herbivore (mixed feeder)**.
:::

## The big five

The **big five** are the **lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhinoceros**. The name comes from hunters, for whom they were the most dangerous animals to hunt on foot. Today they are the animals tourists most want to see, so they bring in large amounts of tourism income. Note that the hippo and cheetah are **not** in the big five.

## Sustainable utilisation of wildlife

1. **Tourism:** photographic safaris, game drives and walking safaris earn income without killing animals.
2. **Game ranching:** wild animals are kept on private or communal land for tourism, live sales, meat and hunting; indigenous animals are adapted to dry conditions and local diseases.
3. **Controlled (trophy) hunting:** a limited number of animals, set by a **quota**, is hunted each year; high fees support conservation and communities.
4. **Culling and cropping:** removing animals when numbers exceed the **carrying capacity** of the habitat, with the meat used.
5. **Farming wild species:** crocodiles and ostriches are farmed for skins, meat and eggs.

:::worked-solution[Is a wildlife population growing or falling?]
A conservancy counted 240 impala in one year and 300 three years later; its sable fell from 64 to 48.

1. Impala change $= 300 - 240 = +60$; percentage $= \dfrac{60}{240} \times 100 = +25\%$.
2. Sable change $= 48 - 64 = -16$; percentage $= \dfrac{-16}{64} \times 100 = -25\%$.
3. The impala population can support a small **hunting or cropping quota**; the sable population is falling, so hunting of sable should stop and the cause (poaching, disease, poor habitat) should be investigated.
:::

## Specially protected plants and animals

Some species are **specially protected** by law because they are rare, endangered or heavily poached. Examples are both species of **rhinoceros** and the **pangolin** among animals, and **cycads** among plants. Hunting, capturing, possessing or trading them without a permit is a serious offence.

## Dangerous and problem animals

| Animal | Typical problem |
| --- | --- |
| Elephant | Raids crops, breaks fences, granaries and water pipes; can kill people |
| Lion, leopard, hyena | Kill cattle, goats and sheep |
| Crocodile, hippo | Attack people at rivers; hippos raid crops at night |
| Baboon, monkey, bushpig | Raid crops and gardens |
| Buffalo | Can be aggressive; can spread diseases such as foot-and-mouth disease to cattle |
| Snakes | Bite people and livestock |

**Ways of dealing with them:**

- **Report** problem animals to the wildlife authority (Zimbabwe Parks and Wildlife Management Authority) or the rural district council, which may capture and relocate, scare or, as a last resort, destroy the animal. People must not kill protected animals themselves.
- **Barriers:** strong, predator-proof kraals for livestock at night; electric or chilli-treated fences; secure granaries.
- **Guarding and scaring:** watch towers, noise, fires and chilli smoke to drive elephants away from fields.
- **Behaviour:** avoid rivers where crocodiles live, fetch water at safe points, herd livestock by day.
- **Land-use planning:** keep fields away from wildlife corridors and park boundaries where possible.

:::example{title="An elephant raiding fields"}
Every night in March, an elephant bull enters fields near Hwange. The village responds by (1) **reporting** to the wildlife authority, (2) building **chilli fences** and lighting fires at the field edges, (3) **guarding** from a raised platform with noise-makers, and (4) planning next season's fields **closer together**, away from the elephants' path to water.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Putting the hippo or cheetah in the big five"}
The big five are lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhinoceros only.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying hyenas are only scavengers"}
Hyenas hunt much of their own food as well as scavenging; they are carnivores.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying farmers may kill any problem animal"}
Problem animals are dealt with through the wildlife authority. Killing protected animals without authority is illegal.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "**explain** the value of wildlife", cover socio-economic, cultural **and** ecological value with an example of each. For "**classify**", state the group and the reason (what the animal eats).
:::

:::exam-tip
For "**describe** ways of dealing with problem animals", give practical measures matched to the animal: kraals against predators, fences and chilli against elephants, avoiding rivers against crocodiles.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Wildlife has socio-economic (tourism, meat, hunting fees, jobs), cultural (totems, taboos, medicines) and ecological (food chains, pollination, seed dispersal, nutrient cycling) value.
- Herbivores (grazers, browsers, mixed feeders) eat plants; carnivores eat flesh; omnivores eat both.
- The big five are lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhinoceros.
- Sustainable use includes tourism, game ranching, quota-based hunting, culling and farming crocodiles and ostriches.
- Rhinos, pangolins and cycads are examples of specially protected species; problem animals are handled through the wildlife authority, barriers, guarding and land-use planning.
:::
