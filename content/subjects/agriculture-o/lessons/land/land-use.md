## Introduction

Stand on a hill above a rural ward near Mutare and you will see a patchwork: fields of maize near the homesteads, cattle grazing on the slopes, a strip of woodland along the ridge, a dam with a few fish ponds below it, and a school and clinic beside the road. Every piece of land has been put to a **use**, and the choice of use is not random.

Land is limited. It cannot be made bigger, and the population that depends on it keeps growing. Farmers and planners must decide which use suits each piece of land best, given its slope, climate and soil. Examiners ask you to **describe** forms of land use, **explain** what limits them and **suggest** how land can be used more efficiently.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Land use"}
The purpose for which an area of land is used, for example growing crops, grazing livestock, forestry, wildlife conservation, fish farming or settlement.
:::

:::definition{term="Limiting factor (of land use)"}
A feature of the land or its surroundings, such as steep slope, low rainfall or shallow soil, that prevents or restricts a particular use.
:::

:::definition{term="Aquaculture"}
The farming of fish and other water organisms in ponds, tanks, dams or cages under controlled conditions.
:::

:::definition{term="Protected area"}
Land set aside by law mainly to conserve wildlife, plants, scenery or water catchments, where farming and settlement are restricted; national parks are an example.
:::

:::definition{term="Land pressure"}
The situation in which too many people or animals depend on too little land, so that the land is overused and degraded.
:::

## Forms of land use

| Land use | What it involves | Typical land |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crop production | Field crops, vegetables, orchards and plantations | Gentle slopes, deep fertile soils, adequate rain or irrigation |
| Livestock production | Grazing cattle, goats and sheep; housed pigs and poultry | Grassland and veld too dry, steep or shallow for crops |
| Forestry | Natural woodland and planted timber (pine, eucalyptus) | High-rainfall slopes; land too steep to plough |
| Wildlife management | National parks, safari areas, game ranching | Dry, hot, remote or tsetse-infested land |
| Aquaculture | Fish ponds, fish cages in dams | Land near reliable water with soils that hold water |
| Settlement and services | Homes, schools, roads, markets, towns | Well-drained, gently sloping land near water and roads |
| Protected areas | National parks, forest reserves, water catchments | Land of special ecological or scenic value |

In Zimbabwe, examples of protected areas include **Hwange, Mana Pools, Gonarezhou and Matobo National Parks**, as well as forest reserves and the catchment areas of major dams. Farming and settlement are restricted there so that wildlife, forests and water supplies are conserved.

## Factors that limit land use

### Topography

- **Steep slopes** erode easily when cleared, and tractors cannot work them safely; they are better left under forest or permanent pasture.
- **Low-lying, flat land** near rivers may be **waterlogged** or flood, which limits crops unless it is drained.
- **Altitude** affects temperature: high land is cool and may have frost, while low valleys are hot.

### Climate

- **Rainfall** decides whether crops can be grown without irrigation and which crops will survive. Dry areas suit livestock, wildlife and drought-tolerant crops.
- **Temperature** limits crops: tea and apples need cool conditions, while cotton and sugar cane need heat. **Frost** kills tender crops in cold hollows.
- **Wind** damages tall crops and dries soils; strong winds speed up erosion of bare land.

### Soil

- **Shallow or stony soils** hold little water and few roots, so they suit grazing or forestry rather than crops.
- **Infertile, acidic or salty soils** give low yields unless they are improved with fertiliser, lime or drainage.
- **Poorly drained clay soils** become waterlogged; **sandy soils** dry out fast and lose nutrients by leaching.

### Other environmental and human factors

- **Water supply**: a dam, river or borehole allows irrigation, livestock watering and fish farming.
- **Pests and diseases**: tsetse flies, which spread trypanosomiasis, have historically limited cattle keeping in some low-lying valleys.
- **Access and markets**: land far from roads and markets is used less intensively.
- **Law and tenure**: protected areas, and who owns or controls the land, restrict what may be done on it.

:::example{title="Choosing uses for a hillside farm"}
Rudo's farm near Chimanimani has a steep, stony ridge, gently sloping red soils in the middle and a wet vlei (seasonally waterlogged valley) at the bottom.

- **Ridge:** keep under trees (woodland or a woodlot); ploughing would cause severe erosion.
- **Gentle slopes:** crops on contour ridges, because the soil is deep and can be cultivated safely.
- **Vlei:** grazing in the dry season, or a fish pond; ploughing it would damage a wetland that feeds the stream.

Each use matches what the land can sustain without being degraded.
:::

## Population growth and land pressure

As the population grows, each household gets a smaller share of the same land.

:::worked-solution[Land per household in a growing village]
A village has 480 ha of arable land. There were 120 households in an earlier survey and 200 households now.

1. Earlier land per household $= 480 \div 120 = 4.0\ \text{ha}$.
2. Land per household now $= 480 \div 200 = 2.4\ \text{ha}$.
3. Change $= 2.4 - 4.0 = -1.6\ \text{ha}$.
4. Percentage change $= \dfrac{-1.6}{4.0} \times 100 = -40\%$: each household now has 40% less arable land.
:::

**Effects of land pressure:**

1. Fields are divided into small plots (**land fragmentation**) that are hard to farm efficiently.
2. Fallow periods are shortened or abandoned, so soil fertility falls.
3. Steep slopes, stream banks and wetlands are cultivated, causing **erosion and siltation**.
4. Too many animals graze too little land (**overgrazing**), leaving bare soil.
5. Woodland is cleared for fields and firewood (**deforestation**).

**Making land use more efficient:**

- **Intensify** production on good land: improved seed, fertiliser, irrigation, conservation agriculture.
- **Plan** farms and wards so that each piece of land is used for what suits it (see physical farm planning).
- Keep livestock numbers within the **carrying capacity** of the grazing.
- Protect slopes, wetlands and catchments; plant woodlots for firewood.
- Create **non-farm jobs** so that fewer people depend directly on the land.

:::worked-solution[Share of each land use in a ward]
A ward of 12,000 ha has 3,600 ha of crop land, 5,400 ha of grazing, 1,800 ha of woodland, 720 ha of settlement and roads, and 480 ha of steep hills and rock.

| Land use | Area (ha) | Share (%) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crop land | 3,600 | 30 |
| Grazing land | 5,400 | 45 |
| Woodland | 1,800 | 15 |
| Settlement and roads | 720 | 6 |
| Steep hills and rock | 480 | 4 |

1. Total area $= 3\,600 + 5\,400 + 1\,800 + 720 + 480 = 12\,000\ \text{ha}$.
2. Share of crop land $= \dfrac{3\,600}{12\,000} \times 100 = 30\%$; the other shares are found in the same way.
3. Check: $30 + 45 + 15 + 6 + 4 = 100\%$.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing land uses without the limiting factor"}
"Explain why the land is used for forestry" needs the reason: "the slope is too steep to cultivate without severe erosion", not just "it is a forest area".
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating climate and soil as the same factor"}
Rainfall, temperature and wind are **climatic** factors; depth, texture, fertility and drainage are **soil** factors. Keep them separate when a question asks for one type.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking population growth increases the land available"}
Land is fixed. More people means less land per person, unless production per hectare rises.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "**describe** the forms of land use", name the use and say what it involves. For "**explain** how topography limits land use", give the feature and its effect: "steep slopes → erosion and no mechanisation → forestry instead of crops".
:::

:::exam-tip
Calculations on land per person or percentage shares are simple divisions: show the division, the answer and the unit (ha or %). Check that percentage shares add up to 100.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Land is used for crops, livestock, forestry, wildlife, aquaculture, settlement and protected areas.
- Topography (slope, drainage, altitude), climate (rain, temperature, frost, wind) and soil (depth, fertility, drainage) limit what each piece of land can be used for.
- Water supply, pests such as tsetse fly, access to markets and the law also limit land use.
- Population growth reduces land per person and leads to fragmentation, overgrazing, deforestation and erosion.
- Efficient land use means intensifying on good land, matching each area to a suitable use and conserving slopes, wetlands and catchments.
:::
