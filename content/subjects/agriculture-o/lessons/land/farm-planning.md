## Introduction

When Farai took over a bare 40 ha plot near Chinhoyi, he could have built his house anywhere, ploughed wherever was easiest and let his cattle wander. Instead, with an AGRITEX officer, he walked the land, noted where the soil was deep, where the slope was steep, where water collected and where the road ran, and drew a **farm plan** before he cleared a single tree.

**Physical farm planning** means deciding where each part of the farm should go, so that land is used for what it suits, work is easy, and soil and water are conserved. Examiners ask you to **discuss the importance** of planning, **state the factors** considered and **describe or sketch a farm layout**. Because you cannot draw in this course, you will learn to describe a layout in words and as a table, which is also how you should plan a sketch.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Physical farm planning"}
Deciding how a farm's land is divided and where fields, paddocks, buildings, roads, water supplies and conservation works will be placed.
:::

:::definition{term="Farm layout"}
The arrangement of the parts of a farm on the ground, shown on a plan (sketch map) drawn roughly to scale with a key and north arrow.
:::

:::definition{term="Paddock"}
A fenced area of grazing land; dividing grazing into paddocks allows rotational grazing.
:::

:::definition{term="Arable land"}
Land suitable for cultivation, usually deep, well-drained soils on gentle slopes.
:::

## Why plan a farm?

1. **Each piece of land is used for what it suits**: crops on the best soils, grazing on poorer or steeper land, trees on the steepest slopes.
2. **Soil and water are conserved**: contour ridges, waterways and storm drains are placed before fields are opened.
3. **Work is efficient**: short distances between the homestead, fields, water and roads save time, fuel and labour.
4. **Livestock are controlled**: fenced paddocks stop animals damaging crops and allow rotational grazing.
5. **Development can be phased**: the farmer knows what to build first and can budget for it.
6. **Records and future changes** are easier with a plan to refer to.

## Factors considered in planning

| Factor | What the planner checks | Effect on the plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soil | Depth, texture, drainage, fertility | Deep, well-drained soils become arable fields; shallow or stony soils become grazing or woodland |
| Slope (topography) | Gentle, moderate, steep; vleis | Crops on gentle slopes with contour ridges; steep land under trees or grass; vleis protected |
| Water sources | Rivers, springs, boreholes, dam sites | Homestead, garden, livestock pens and irrigated land near water |
| Roads and access | Main road, farm tracks | Buildings near the access road; tracks along boundaries or contours |
| Markets | Distance to buyers | Perishable crops (vegetables, milk) need quick access to a road |
| Natural region and climate | Rainfall, frost, prevailing wind | Choice of enterprises; pens downwind of the house; frost hollows avoided for tender crops |
| Existing features | Trees, rock outcrops, old buildings, boundaries | Use what is there; keep useful trees as windbreaks |

## Placing each part of the farm

- **Homestead and farm buildings:** on slightly **higher, well-drained** ground, central to the farm, near water and the access road. Stores and sheds close to the house for security.
- **Livestock pens, piggery and poultry houses:** near the homestead for supervision and security, but **downwind** of the house so smells and flies blow away, and **downslope of water sources** so effluent does not pollute them.
- **Arable fields:** on **deep soils and gentle slopes**, protected by **contour ridges** and grassed waterways. Fields of similar size make a crop rotation easier.
- **Vegetable garden and orchard:** close to the **water supply** and the house, because they need frequent watering and attention.
- **Paddocks (grazing):** on land too steep, shallow or wet for crops, divided by fences, each with access to **water**.
- **Woodlot:** on **steep slopes, ridges or rocky land**, or as a **windbreak** on the side of the prevailing wind.
- **Dam or weir:** across a valley where the walls of the valley are close together and the soil holds water.
- **Roads and tracks:** along **boundaries or on the contour**, not straight down slopes, where they would become gullies.
- **Fire guards:** cleared strips along boundaries and around buildings, woodlots and grazing.

:::example{title="A layout described in words"}
Farai's 40 ha plot slopes gently from a ridge in the north down to a stream in the south. The main road runs along the eastern boundary, and the prevailing wind blows from the east.

| Area | Position on the plan | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Woodlot (3 ha) | Steep stony ridge, north | Too steep to plough; trees prevent erosion and supply poles |
| Homestead and stores | Higher ground in the centre-east, beside the road | Well drained, central, easy access |
| Pig and poultry houses | West of the homestead | Downwind, so smells and flies blow away from the house |
| Arable fields (four of equal size) | Gentle slopes in the centre, on contour ridges | Deep soils; equal fields suit a four-course rotation |
| Garden and orchard | Near the borehole, south of the homestead | Need regular water and attention |
| Paddocks | Lower slopes near the stream | Grazing with water; poorer soils |
| Vlei and stream bank | South boundary, fenced off | Protects the wetland and prevents siltation |

On a sketch, each area would be drawn roughly to scale, labelled, and explained in a **key**, with a **north arrow** and the **contours or direction of slope** shown.
:::

## Using measurements in planning

:::worked-solution[Areas on a farm plan]
A rectangular farm measures 800 m by 500 m. The planner sets aside an arable block of 500 m by 300 m to be divided into four equal fields.

1. Area of the farm $= 800 \times 500 = 400\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
2. In hectares: $400\,000 \div 10\,000 = 40\ \text{ha}$ (1 ha $= 10\,000\ \text{m}^2$).
3. Arable block $= 500 \times 300 = 150\,000\ \text{m}^2 = 15\ \text{ha}$.
4. Each of four equal fields $= 15 \div 4 = 3.75\ \text{ha}$.
5. Land left for buildings, paddocks, woodlot and roads $= 40 - 15 = 25\ \text{ha}$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Dividing grazing into paddocks]
The planner allows 24 ha of grazing, to be split into four equal paddocks for rotational grazing.

1. Area of each paddock $= 24 \div 4 = 6\ \text{ha}$.
2. The cattle graze one paddock while the other three rest and regrow; each paddock is grazed for about a quarter of the time.
3. Each paddock must reach the water point, so the paddocks are arranged around a central trough or along the stream.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Putting the pig sty upwind of the house"}
Smells and flies follow the wind. Pens go **downwind** of the homestead.
:::

:::mistake{title="Running roads straight down the slope"}
Water rushes down such roads and turns them into gullies. Roads follow the **contour or the boundaries**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting to convert m² to ha"}
Divide square metres by **10,000** to get hectares: 150,000 m² is 15 ha, not 150 ha.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In a "**sketch a farm layout**" question, label every part, add a key, a north arrow and the direction of slope or prevailing wind, and give a reason for each position. Marks come from sensible placing *with reasons*, not from artistic skill.
:::

:::exam-tip
For "**discuss the importance** of farm planning", give several points and explain each: "fields placed on contour-ridged gentle slopes reduce erosion, so fertility is maintained".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Physical farm planning places fields, paddocks, buildings, roads, water and conservation works where they suit the land.
- Planners consider soil, slope, water sources, roads, markets, natural region, frost and prevailing wind.
- Buildings go on higher, well-drained ground near water and the road; pens downwind; fields on gentle slopes with deep soil; woodlots on steep land; paddocks on poorer land with water.
- Roads follow boundaries or contours; wetlands and stream banks are protected; fire guards surround key areas.
- Areas on plans are calculated in m² and converted to hectares (÷ 10,000).
:::
