## Introduction

The agriculture club at a school near Marondera wants a woodlot to supply firewood for the school kitchen and poles for a new poultry house, so that pupils no longer cut trees from the surrounding hills. The club cannot simply buy trees: it must **raise seedlings in a nursery**, **plant them out** at the right time and **look after them** for years. The same skills let farmers grow fruit trees, fodder trees and windbreaks on their own land.

ZIMSEC asks you to **describe** the choice of a nursery site, **explain** how seedlings are raised and managed, **describe** how a plantation is established and managed, and **discuss** agroforestry. Practical steps must be given in a sensible order.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Tree nursery"}
A place where tree seedlings are raised and cared for until they are strong enough to be planted out in the field.
:::

:::definition{term="Pricking out"}
Transferring young seedlings from a seedbed into individual pots or polythene tubes, usually when they have their first true leaves.
:::

:::definition{term="Hardening off"}
Gradually reducing watering and shade in the weeks before planting out, so that seedlings become tough enough to survive field conditions.
:::

:::definition{term="Woodlot"}
A small area of land planted with trees, usually fast-growing species, to supply firewood, poles or timber.
:::

:::definition{term="Thinning"}
Removing some trees from a plantation so that the remaining trees have more space, light, water and nutrients to grow larger.
:::

:::definition{term="Pruning (of plantation trees)"}
Cutting off the lower branches of trees to produce straight, knot-free timber and to reduce the risk of fire climbing into the crowns.
:::

:::definition{term="Agroforestry"}
Deliberately growing trees together with crops and/or livestock on the same piece of land, so that they interact to benefit the farmer.
:::

## Choosing a nursery site

A good nursery site is:

1. **close to a reliable water supply**, because seedlings are watered every day;
2. **level or gently sloping and well drained**, so beds do not flood or wash away;
3. **sheltered from strong wind** but in the open, **away from large trees** whose roots and shade compete with the seedlings;
4. **near the homestead or school** for supervision and security, and near a **road** for moving seedlings;
5. on **good soil** for filling pots, or close to a source of topsoil, sand and compost;
6. **fenced** against livestock and wild animals, and **not in a frost hollow**.

## Raising seedlings

1. **Collect seed** from healthy, well-formed mother trees, or buy certified seed.
2. **Pre-treat** hard-coated seed (for example many acacias) by soaking in hot water or nicking the coat (scarification) so water can enter and germination is faster and more even.
3. **Prepare the potting mixture**, for example topsoil, sand and well-rotted compost or manure, and fill polythene tubes or pots. Stand them upright in beds.
4. **Sow** small seeds (such as eucalyptus) thinly in a seedbed and **prick out** the seedlings into tubes; sow large seeds **directly** into the tubes.
5. **Water** gently, morning and evening, and give **shade** (grass or shade netting) to young seedlings.
6. **Weed** the pots and paths, and watch for pests and diseases. **Damping-off**, a fungal disease that makes seedlings collapse at soil level, is reduced by not overwatering and by using clean potting mixture.
7. **Root prune** by moving the tubes regularly so roots do not grow into the ground.
8. **Harden off** for the last few weeks: reduce water and remove the shade.

:::worked-solution[How well did the seed germinate?]
The club sowed 1,500 seeds and 1,260 germinated.

1. Germination percentage $= \dfrac{1\,260}{1\,500} \times 100 = 84\%$.
2. If the club needs 1,320 healthy seedlings, and about 90% of germinated seedlings survive pricking out, it must sow at least $1\,320 \div 0.84 \div 0.90 \approx 1\,746$ seeds; in practice it would sow more as a safety margin.
:::

## Establishing a woodlot or plantation

1. **Clear** the land of bush and weeds, keeping useful indigenous trees where possible, and make **fire guards** around it.
2. **Mark out** the planting positions at the chosen **spacing**.
3. **Dig pits** (pitting) well before planting, for example about 30 cm wide and deep, and refill them with topsoil, adding manure or compost.
4. **Plant** at the start of the rainy season, after good rains have wetted the soil. Remove the polythene tube without breaking the root ball, plant at the same depth as in the pot, **firm** the soil and water if possible.
5. **Protect** the young trees from livestock, termites and fire.

:::worked-solution[How many seedlings for a woodlot?]
The club's woodlot is 0.6 ha, planted at a spacing of 2.5 m between rows and 2.0 m within rows. It adds 10% extra seedlings to replace those that die (beating up).

1. Area per tree $= 2.5 \times 2.0 = 5\ \text{m}^2$.
2. Trees per hectare $= 10\,000 \div 5 = 2\,000$.
3. Trees for 0.6 ha $= 2\,000 \times 0.6 = 1\,200$.
4. With 10% extra: $1\,200 \times 1.10 = 1\,320$ seedlings.
:::

## Managing a plantation

- **Weeding:** keep a weed-free ring around each young tree until the trees shade out weeds.
- **Beating up:** replace seedlings that die in the first season.
- **Fire guards:** clear them every year before the dry season.
- **Thinning:** remove weak, crooked or diseased trees so the best trees grow bigger; thinnings give poles and firewood.
- **Pruning:** remove lower branches for knot-free timber and to keep fire on the ground.
- **Pest and disease control:** termites and some insects attack young trees.
- **Harvesting and coppicing:** many eucalyptus species regrow from the stump after cutting (coppicing), giving several harvests from one planting.

## Agroforestry

### Components

- **Trees** (woody perennials: fruit, fodder, timber and nitrogen-fixing trees);
- **Crops** (annual field crops or vegetables);
- **Livestock** (grazing or fed cut fodder).

Systems combine them: trees with crops (**agrisilviculture**), trees with livestock (**silvopastoral**), or all three (**agrosilvopastoral**).

### Practices

| Practice | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Alley cropping | Crops grown in the alleys between rows of trees or hedges; prunings are used as mulch or green manure |
| Boundary planting and live fences | Trees or thorny shrubs planted along field and farm boundaries |
| Windbreaks and shelterbelts | Rows of trees across the prevailing wind |
| Improved fallows | Fast-growing nitrogen-fixing trees planted on resting land to restore fertility |
| Fodder banks | Blocks of fodder trees cut and fed to livestock in the dry season |
| Scattered trees and home gardens | Fruit and shade trees in fields and around the homestead |
| Woodlots | Small plantations for poles and firewood on the farm |

### Benefits

1. **Soil fertility:** nitrogen-fixing trees and leaf litter add nutrients and organic matter.
2. **Soil and water conservation:** roots and litter reduce erosion and runoff.
3. **Products:** firewood, poles, fruit, fodder, medicines, honey; less need to cut natural woodland.
4. **Microclimate:** shade and windbreaks reduce evaporation and protect crops and animals.
5. **Diversified income** and lower risk.

### Problems

Trees can **compete** with crops for light, water and nutrients; they take **years** to give returns; they may harbour **pests or birds**; and they need **labour** for pruning and management.

:::example{title="Planning an agroforestry plot"}
Nyasha has a sloping 1 ha field near Gokwe with tired soil, and her goats lack feed in the dry season. She plants rows of nitrogen-fixing fodder trees along the contours, grows maize and cowpeas in the alleys, uses the prunings as mulch, and cuts some leaves for her goats in the dry season. The tree rows also slow runoff down the slope. One practice gives her **fertility, fodder and erosion control** together.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Planting out in the dry season"}
Seedlings planted before the rains die of drought. Plant at the **start of the rainy season**, after the soil is wet.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing thinning and pruning"}
**Thinning** removes whole trees; **pruning** removes branches from trees that stay.
:::

:::mistake{title="Skipping hardening off"}
Seedlings moved straight from shade and daily watering to the field suffer transplanting shock and many die.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A nursery site needs water, good drainage, shelter, security, good soil and protection from animals and frost.
- Seedlings are raised by collecting and pre-treating seed, potting, sowing, pricking out, watering, shading, weeding, root pruning and hardening off.
- A plantation is established by clearing, pitting, planting at the start of the rains and protecting the trees.
- Management includes weeding, beating up, fire guards, thinning, pruning and pest control.
- Agroforestry combines trees with crops and/or livestock (alley cropping, windbreaks, fodder banks, improved fallows), improving soil, products and income.
:::
