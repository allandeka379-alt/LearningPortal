## Introduction

Thandeka runs a small piggery near Bulawayo. A bag of commercial pig grower meal is expensive, but she grows maize and can buy soya bean cake from a local oil expresser. If she mixes the two in the right proportions she can make a ration with the protein her growers need at a lower cost. If she gets the mix wrong, her pigs grow slowly and she loses money.

A **ration** is the total amount of feed an animal receives in 24 hours. Feeding correctly means giving a ration that is **balanced** for the animal's age and stage of production, in the right **quantity**, at the right **times**. ZIMSEC (Form 4) asks you to calculate maintenance and production rations, including with the Pearson square; Cambridge asks you to outline maintenance and production rations and describe feeding practices for the age and stage of the animal.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use *maintenance ration* and *production ration*. ZIMSEC names the **Pearson square** method; Cambridge names no method, but the Pearson square is a quick way to answer any "mix two feeds" question.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Ration"}
The total feed given to one animal in 24 hours.
:::

:::definition{term="Balanced ration"}
A ration that supplies all the nutrients the animal needs, in the correct proportions and amounts, for its age, size and level of production.
:::

:::definition{term="Maintenance ration"}
The feed needed just to keep an animal alive and healthy at a constant body weight, with no production (body temperature, breathing, movement, tissue repair).
:::

:::definition{term="Production ration"}
The feed given **in addition to** the maintenance ration to support growth, fattening, milk, eggs, wool, work or the growth of a foetus.
:::

:::definition{term="Feed conversion ratio (FCR)"}
The mass of feed eaten divided by the gain in live mass (or the mass of product): $\text{FCR} = \dfrac{\text{feed eaten (kg)}}{\text{gain in mass (kg)}}$. A lower FCR means more efficient use of feed.
:::

## Matching the ration to the animal

| Animal and stage | What the ration must supply |
| --- | --- |
| Young growing stock (chicks, piglets, calves) | High protein for rapid growth; easily digested; starter feeds |
| Fattening stock (broilers, baconers, steers) | High energy with adequate protein |
| Lactating cow, sow or doe | Extra energy, protein, calcium and phosphorus for milk |
| Pregnant female in late pregnancy | Extra feed for the growing foetus ("steaming up" of dairy cows) |
| Laying hens | Protein and plenty of calcium (limestone grit or shell) for eggshells |
| Dry, mature animals | Maintenance ration, mostly roughage for ruminants |
| Working animals (oxen, donkeys) | Extra energy on working days |

:::worked-solution[Maintenance plus production for a dairy cow]
Assume a farm's rule of thumb: the cow's grazing and hay provide her maintenance ration, and she receives 1 kg of dairy meal for every 2.5 L of milk she produces.

1. A cow producing 20 L of milk a day needs $20 \div 2.5 = 8$ kg of dairy meal as her production ration.
2. A dry cow (no milk) receives no dairy meal: roughage alone covers maintenance.
3. As her yield falls later in lactation, her dairy meal is reduced in the same proportion.
:::

## The Pearson square

The Pearson square finds the proportions of two feeds that give a mixture with a **target** crude protein (CP) percentage. The target must lie **between** the CP of the two feeds.

How to set it out:

1. Write the target CP in the centre of a square.
2. Write the CP of the high-protein feed at the top left and of the low-protein feed at the bottom left.
3. Subtract **diagonally**, ignoring minus signs. The answer on the top right is the parts of the top-left feed; the answer on the bottom right is the parts of the bottom-left feed.
4. Add the parts, then change to percentages or kilograms.

:::worked-solution[A 16% protein pig grower ration]
Thandeka wants 500 kg of a 16% CP ration from maize meal (9% CP) and soya bean cake (44% CP).

| Feed and CP | Target | Parts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soya bean cake, 44% | 16% | $16 - 9 = 7$ parts soya |
| Maize meal, 9% | | $44 - 16 = 28$ parts maize |

1. Total parts $= 7 + 28 = 35$.
2. Soya $= \dfrac{7}{35} \times 100 = 20\%$; maize $= \dfrac{28}{35} \times 100 = 80\%$.
3. For 500 kg: soya $= 0.20 \times 500 = 100$ kg; maize $= 0.80 \times 500 = 400$ kg.
4. Check: $\dfrac{100 \times 44 + 400 \times 9}{500} = \dfrac{4\,400 + 3\,600}{500} = 16\%$. ✓
:::

A common error is to read the parts **across** instead of diagonally, which gives 28 parts soya and a ration far too high in protein (and cost).

In practice a farmer also adds a mineral and vitamin premix and, for layers, limestone; those small additions are ignored in the simple square.

## Calculating how much feed to buy

:::worked-solution[Feed for a month]
200 laying hens each eat about 120 g of layers' mash a day. How many 50 kg bags are needed for 30 days?

1. Feed per day $= 200 \times 120 = 24\,000$ g $= 24$ kg.
2. Feed for 30 days $= 24 \times 30 = 720$ kg.
3. Bags $= 720 \div 50 = 14.4$, so buy **15 bags** (round up: 14 bags would run out).
:::

## Feed conversion ratio

:::worked-solution[FCR of a grower pig]
A pig grows from 30 kg to 90 kg and eats 180 kg of feed.

1. Gain $= 90 - 30 = 60$ kg.
2. FCR $= 180 \div 60 = 3.0$: the pig ate 3 kg of feed for every 1 kg of gain.
:::

FCR rises (gets worse) as animals get older and heavier, when feed is wasted, when animals are sick or wormy, and in very hot or cold housing. Comparing FCR between batches shows a farmer whether feeding and management are improving.

## Good feeding practices

- Feed at **regular times** each day; animals become restless and waste feed when meals are irregular.
- Make **changes gradually** over several days, especially for ruminants, whose rumen microbes need time to adjust (a sudden switch to a lot of grain can cause bloat or acidosis).
- Give **clean water** at all times.
- Use clean feeders at the right height and fill them only partly to reduce **waste**.
- Feed according to **production**: more for high-yielding cows, less for dry cows.
- Store feed dry, off the floor and away from rats; mouldy feed can poison animals.
- Weigh animals and feed and keep **records**, so that FCR and costs can be worked out.

:::example{title="Why are the growers doing badly?"}
Sipho's grower pigs have an FCR of 4.2 when similar pigs on a neighbouring farm achieve about 3. He notices meal spilled under the feeders, a dirty water trough and pigs that have not been dewormed. Each of these raises the FCR: spilled feed is counted as eaten, thirsty pigs eat and grow less, and worms take nutrients. Adjusting the feeders, cleaning the water and deworming should improve the ratio.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Reading the Pearson square across"}
Subtract diagonally; the number opposite each feed is the parts of **that** feed.
:::

:::mistake{title="A target outside the two feeds"}
You cannot make a 50% protein mix from feeds of 9% and 44%; the target must lie between them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Rounding bags down"}
Feed bags are bought whole and rounded **up**, or the animals run short.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Set out the square"}
Draw or tabulate the square, show the two subtractions, the total parts, the percentages and then kilograms. Check your answer by working back to the target CP: examiners give a mark for a correct method even when an arithmetic slip follows.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Explain FCR"}
When asked to *explain* a high FCR, give a cause and its effect on feed eaten or growth ("worms absorb nutrients, so more feed is needed for each kilogram of gain").
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A ration is the feed for 24 hours; a balanced ration meets all needs for the animal's age and stage.
- Maintenance ration keeps weight constant; production ration is extra feed for growth, milk, eggs, work or pregnancy.
- Pearson square: subtract diagonally; parts of each feed are opposite it; target must lie between the two CP values.
- Feed quantity = animals × feed per animal per day × days; round bags up.
- FCR = feed eaten ÷ gain; lower is better.
- Feed regularly, change gradually, give clean water, avoid waste and keep records.
:::
