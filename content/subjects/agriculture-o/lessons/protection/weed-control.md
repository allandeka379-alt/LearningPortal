## Introduction

Every rainy season Farai's family near Chinhoyi spends weeks bent over hoes in their 3 ha of maize, and in a wet year the second weeding is always late. A neighbour now sprays a pre-emergence herbicide just after planting, then hoes once. Another neighbour, following Pfumvudza, keeps the soil covered with a thick mulch and finds she weeds far less. Which approach is best depends on cost, labour, the weeds present and the skills of the farmer.

This lesson covers the **methods** of weed control, the **types of herbicide** and how to **compare** methods. ZIMSEC asks you to *describe* methods, *differentiate* herbicides and *compare* effectiveness; Cambridge asks you to *explain* cultural, mechanical and chemical methods.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Herbicide"}
A chemical used to kill weeds or stop them from growing.
:::

:::definition{term="Selective herbicide"}
A herbicide that kills certain plants (for example broad-leaved weeds) without seriously harming the crop.
:::

:::definition{term="Non-selective herbicide"}
A herbicide that kills or damages all green plants it reaches, crop and weed alike.
:::

:::definition{term="Systemic (translocated) herbicide"}
A herbicide absorbed by the plant and carried in its transport system to all parts, including roots, rhizomes and tubers.
:::

:::definition{term="Contact herbicide"}
A herbicide that kills only the plant parts it touches.
:::

## Preventive control

Stopping weeds from arriving or multiplying is the cheapest control of all:

- Plant **certified, clean seed** free of weed seeds.
- **Clean** implements, vehicles and boots before moving from weedy land.
- Use **well-made compost** and manure (the heat of a good compost heap kills many weed seeds).
- **Never let weeds set seed**: remove them before they flower, including along field edges.

## Cultural control

Cultural methods help the crop compete:

- **Crop rotation** breaks the link between a weed and the crop it thrives in.
- **Correct plant population and spacing**, with **timely planting** into a clean seedbed and **good fertilisation**, so the crop canopy closes quickly and shades weeds out.
- **Mulching** and **permanent soil cover** (a principle of conservation agriculture such as Pfumvudza/Intwasa) stop light reaching weed seeds.
- **Smother crops and cover crops**, such as velvet beans or pumpkins between maize, cover the ground.
- **Intercropping** leaves less bare ground.

## Mechanical control

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hand-pulling and hoeing | Cheap in cash; precise; suits small plots | Slow and tiring; often late in wet weather; hoeing can damage crop roots and spread perennial pieces |
| Slashing or mowing | Quick; stops seeding | Weeds regrow; no root kill |
| Ox-drawn or tractor cultivators | Fast; cover large areas | Need animals or machines; miss weeds within the row; can spread rhizomes |
| Ploughing and harrowing before planting | Buries weed seedlings; prepares a clean seedbed | Exposes soil to erosion; brings buried weed seeds to the surface |

## Chemical control

Herbicides are grouped in three ways.

| Basis | Groups | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What they kill | Selective / non-selective | A selective herbicide (for example one that kills broad-leaved weeds in maize, wheat or sorghum) spares the crop; a non-selective one kills all plants, so it is used before planting, on paths and fence lines, or as a careful directed spray |
| How they move | Contact / systemic (translocated) | Contact kills what it touches, good for young annual weeds but perennials regrow; systemic moves to roots and rhizomes, so it can kill perennials such as couch grass |
| When they are applied | Pre-plant / pre-emergence / post-emergence | Pre-plant: before sowing, to kill existing weeds or mixed into the soil; pre-emergence: after sowing but before crop and weeds emerge, forming a layer in moist soil that kills germinating weeds; post-emergence: after crop and weeds have emerged, sprayed on weed leaves |

Two well-known examples: **glyphosate** is a non-selective, systemic herbicide sprayed on the leaves of growing weeds; **2,4-D** is a selective, systemic herbicide that kills many broad-leaved weeds in cereal crops but not grasses.

**Advantages of herbicides**: fast; save labour; control weeds within the row and in wet weather when hoeing is impossible; pre-emergence types keep the crop clean in its critical early weeks. **Disadvantages**: cash cost; need a sprayer, skills and protective clothing; risk of damaging the crop (wrong product or dose, spray drift onto neighbouring crops), of poisoning and of polluting water; some leave residues that harm the next crop; weeds can become **resistant** when one product is used repeatedly.

:::worked-solution[Mixing a herbicide for a knapsack sprayer]
A label (for this example) recommends 3 L of herbicide product per hectare in 250 L of spray mixture per hectare. Farai's knapsack sprayer holds 16 L.

1. Product per litre of mixture $= \dfrac{3\ \text{L}}{250\ \text{L}} = 0.012$ L per litre $= 12$ mL per litre.
2. Product per tank $= 12 \times 16 = 192$ mL.
3. He measures 192 mL with a measuring cylinder, half-fills the tank with clean water, adds the product, then tops up to 16 L.
:::

## Biological control

Insects or diseases that attack only one weed can be released against serious invasive weeds; for example, weevils that feed on water hyacinth have been used to reduce this weed on dams. Grazing animals can also keep some weeds down. Biological control is slow and suits only certain weeds.

## Controlling witchweed

Because witchweed seeds can lie dormant for years, control needs several methods together:

- **Trap crops**: legumes such as groundnuts, cowpeas and soya beans, and cotton, cause witchweed seeds to germinate, but the seedlings cannot attach to them and die ("suicidal germination").
- **Rotation** of cereals with these crops.
- **Hand-pull** witchweed before it flowers and sets seed, and destroy it.
- Improve **soil fertility** with manure and nitrogen fertiliser, which reduces attack.
- Use **tolerant or resistant varieties** where available, and clean seed.

## Comparing methods

:::worked-solution[Hand weeding against a pre-emergence herbicide]
Assume labour costs US\$4 per person-day. For one hectare of maize:

- **Hand weeding twice**: $2 \times 25$ person-days $= 50$ days; $50 \times 4 =$ **US\$200**.
- **Herbicide plus one hoeing**: herbicide 3 L at US\$12 per litre $=$ US\$36; spraying 2 days $=$ US\$8; one later hoeing of 10 days $=$ US\$40; total **US\$84**.

1. Saving per hectare $=$ US\$200 $-$ US\$84 $=$ US\$116.
2. For Farai's 3 ha: hand weeding $= 3 \times 200 =$ US\$600; herbicide programme $= 3 \times 84 =$ US\$252.
3. The herbicide programme is cheaper here, but it needs cash at planting, a sprayer and training. If family labour is free and available on time, hand weeding may still be the better choice.
:::

:::example{title="Choosing a method for couch grass"}
Rudo's garden plot is infested with couch grass. Hoeing chops the rhizomes and spreads them; a contact herbicide only burns the leaves. Before planting she sprays a **non-selective systemic** herbicide on actively growing couch grass, which carries the chemical down to the rhizomes, waits the time the label states, then plants into the cleaned land and keeps it mulched.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up selective and systemic"}
*Selective* is about **which plants** are killed; *systemic* is about **how the chemical moves** in the plant. A herbicide can be both.
:::

:::mistake{title="Pre-emergence means before the weeds emerge"}
Pre-emergence herbicides are applied after sowing but **before the crop and the weeds emerge**, not to emerged weeds.
:::

:::mistake{title="Comparing only cash costs"}
Also compare labour, timeliness, effectiveness on the weeds present and risks.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Differentiate herbicides"}
For "differentiate", write a matched pair: "a selective herbicide kills only certain plants, such as broad-leaved weeds in maize, whereas a non-selective herbicide kills all plants".
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Calculations"}
Show product per litre, then per tank, with units. Read whether the rate is per hectare of land or per litre of mixture.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Prevent: clean seed and implements, good compost, never let weeds seed.
- Cultural: rotation, spacing and timely planting, mulch and soil cover, smother crops, intercropping.
- Mechanical: hand-pulling, hoeing, slashing, cultivators, ploughing.
- Herbicides: selective or non-selective; contact or systemic; pre-plant, pre-emergence or post-emergence.
- Witchweed: trap crops, rotation, hand-pulling before seeding, fertility, resistant varieties.
- Compare methods by cost, labour, timeliness, effectiveness and risk.
:::
