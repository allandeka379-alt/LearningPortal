## Introduction

In the same district near Gweru you may see a family preparing land with hoes, a neighbour ploughing with a span of oxen, and a commercial farmer whose tractor ploughs more in a morning than the oxen manage in a week. Each farmer has chosen a level of **mechanisation** that suits the size of the farm, the money available and the labour in the family.

Cambridge asks you to explain the advantages and disadvantages of farm mechanisation. Exam questions often give a farm situation and ask you to discuss whether the farmer should buy a tractor, hire one or keep using animals, so you must be able to weigh both sides and reach a judgement.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Farm mechanisation"}
The use of machines and power sources other than human muscle, such as draught animals, tractors and engines, to carry out farm operations.
:::

:::definition{term="Draught animal power"}
The use of animals such as oxen, cows and donkeys to pull implements and carts.
:::

:::definition{term="Timeliness"}
Carrying out each farm operation (ploughing, planting, weeding, spraying, harvesting) at the best time for the crop.
:::

:::definition{term="Soil compaction"}
The pressing together of soil particles by heavy loads such as tractor wheels, which reduces pore space, aeration, drainage and root growth.
:::

## Levels of farm power

| Level | Power source | Typical tools and machines | Typical farm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hand tool technology | People | Hoes, axes, slashers, knapsack sprayers | Small plots and gardens; Pfumvudza basins |
| Animal draught technology | Oxen, cows, donkeys | Ploughs, cultivators, planters, ridgers, carts | Smallholdings and communal farms |
| Motorised (engine-powered) technology | Tractors, engines, electric motors | Tractor ploughs, disc harrows, planters, boom sprayers, combine harvesters, pumps | Commercial farms and contractors |

Many farmers use **selective mechanisation**: they mechanise the operations where it helps most (ploughing or shelling, for example) and do the rest by hand. Small **two-wheel (walking) tractors** and hired tractor services are steps between animal power and owning a large tractor.

## Advantages of mechanisation

1. **Timeliness**: land can be prepared and planted quickly after the first rains. Crops planted on time yield more, because they use the whole season and avoid late-season drought and pests.
2. **Larger areas** can be farmed by the same number of people.
3. **Less drudgery**: hard, tiring work such as ploughing and shelling is done by machines, which makes farming more attractive to young people.
4. **Labour shortages** at peak times (planting, weeding, harvesting) are overcome.
5. **Better quality work**: deeper ploughing, even planting depth and spacing, accurate spraying and quick harvesting reduce losses.
6. **Operations that are impossible by hand** become possible, such as breaking a hardpan with a subsoiler or pumping water for irrigation.

:::worked-solution[Days to prepare 20 ha]
Assume an ox span ploughs about 0.4 ha a day and a tractor about 5 ha a day. Sipho must plough 20 ha.

1. Oxen: 20 ÷ 0.4 = **50 days**.
2. Tractor: 20 ÷ 5 = **4 days**.
3. With oxen, much of the land would be planted weeks after the first rains; with a tractor, all of it can be planted on time.
:::

## Disadvantages of mechanisation

1. **High capital cost**: tractors and implements are expensive, and credit may be hard to obtain.
2. **Running costs**: fuel, oil, tyres, repairs and spare parts, often imported and priced in foreign currency.
3. **Skills** are needed to operate and maintain machines; breakdowns at peak time can be costly if spares are far away.
4. **Soil compaction** by heavy wheels, and a **plough pan** from repeated ploughing at the same depth, reduce drainage and root growth.
5. **Erosion** increases if land is over-cultivated or ploughed up and down slopes.
6. **Unemployment**: machines replace farm workers, which can cause hardship and migration to towns.
7. **Unsuitable** for very small, scattered fields, steep or rocky land and some crops.
8. **Pollution** from exhaust fumes and fuel spills.

## Animal draught compared with tractors

| Feature | Animal draught | Tractor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capital cost | Low; animals can be bred on the farm | High |
| Running costs | Feed, grazing, veterinary care | Fuel, oil, repairs, spares |
| Work rate | Slow (a fraction of a hectare a day) | Fast (several hectares a day) |
| Timeliness | Poor on large areas; animals are weak after the dry season | Good |
| Depth of ploughing | Shallow | Deeper; can subsoil |
| Soil compaction | Little | Can be serious |
| Other benefits | Manure, milk, meat and calves; animals can be sold | Can power pumps, trailers and shellers |
| Suits | Small farms, small or sloping fields | Large, level fields |

## Choosing the level of mechanisation

The right choice depends on:

- **Farm size and field shape**: large, level fields justify tractors; small, scattered or sloping fields suit animals or hand tools.
- **Capital and credit** available, and the expected return.
- **Labour**: how many workers are available and what they cost.
- **Crops and operations**: some operations (shelling, threshing, pumping) are easy to mechanise even on a small farm.
- **Hire services**: if a contractor or neighbour hires out a tractor, buying one may not be necessary.
- **Fuel, spares and repair services** nearby, and the skills of the farmer.
- **Environmental effects** such as compaction and erosion.

:::worked-solution[Own or hire a tractor?]
Assume a tractor costs US\$24,000, lasts 10 years and is then worth US\$4,000. Interest, insurance and housing cost US\$1,000 a year. Fuel, repairs and a driver cost US\$30 per hectare worked. A contractor charges US\$60 per hectare. Rudo farms 40 ha.

1. Depreciation = (US\$24,000 − US\$4,000) ÷ 10 = US\$2,000 a year.
2. Fixed costs = US\$2,000 + US\$1,000 = US\$3,000 a year.
3. Owning on 40 ha = US\$3,000 + 40 × US\$30 = **US\$4,200** a year (US\$105 per ha).
4. Hiring on 40 ha = 40 × US\$60 = **US\$2,400** a year.
5. Break-even area = fixed costs ÷ (hire charge − variable cost) = US\$3,000 ÷ (US\$60 − US\$30) = **100 ha**. Below 100 ha hiring is cheaper, unless timeliness from owning her own tractor is worth more than the difference.
:::

:::example{title="A co-operative's choice"}
A group of twelve farmers near Masvingo each farm about 3 ha. None can afford a tractor, and their oxen are weak at the end of the dry season, so planting is late. Together they buy one tractor and a plough, employ a trained driver, and plough members' fields in turn, then hire the tractor out to neighbours to cover fixed costs. They keep their oxen for weeding with cultivators and for carts, and agree to vary ploughing depth and avoid ploughing wet soil to limit compaction.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Only listing advantages"}
"Discuss" questions need advantages and disadvantages, and a conclusion that fits the farm described.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting fixed costs"}
A tractor costs money every year even when it is parked: depreciation, interest, insurance and housing. Compare total costs per hectare, not just fuel.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Explain, do not just state"}
"Mechanisation improves timeliness" earns one mark; "so crops are planted with the first rains and use the whole season, increasing yields" earns the second.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Reach a judgement"}
End a discussion with a decision linked to the facts given: farm size, money, labour, fields and hire services.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Farm power ranges from hand tools to animal draught and tractors; selective mechanisation and hiring are common middle paths.
- Advantages: timeliness, larger areas, less drudgery, labour shortages overcome, better and new operations.
- Disadvantages: capital and running costs, skills and spares, soil compaction, erosion, unemployment, unsuitable for small or steep fields, pollution.
- Animal draught is cheap and gives manure and products but is slow; tractors are fast but costly and can compact soil.
- Choose by farm size, capital, labour, crops, hire services, spares and environment; compare owning and hiring with a break-even area.
:::
