## Introduction

The ox-drawn mouldboard plough is probably the most important farm implement in Zimbabwe's communal areas. With a well-adjusted plough and a trained span of oxen, a family can prepare its fields in time for the first rains; with a blunt share, loose bolts and a badly set hitch, the same oxen struggle, the furrow wanders and the job takes twice as long.

ZIMSEC asks you to list five tillage implements, label a mouldboard plough and explain the functions of its parts (Form 1), adjust the depth and width of animal-drawn implements (Form 2) and describe routine maintenance (Form 3). Cambridge asks you to describe the use and maintenance of the mouldboard plough, cultivator, harrow, planter and ridger. Land preparation itself is covered in its own lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Implement"}
A piece of equipment pulled or driven by animals or a tractor to carry out a field operation such as ploughing, harrowing, planting or weeding.
:::

:::definition{term="Furrow slice"}
The strip of soil cut, lifted and turned over by one pass of a plough body.
:::

:::definition{term="Draught"}
The pulling force needed to move an implement through the soil.
:::

:::definition{term="Routine maintenance"}
Regular cleaning, checking, lubricating, adjusting and repairing of an implement so that it works well and lasts long.
:::

## The mouldboard plough

The parts below are described from front to back, as a labelled diagram would show them.

| Part | Position | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trek chain | Joins the yoke to the front of the plough | Transmits the pull of the animals |
| Hake (hitch or clevis) | Front end of the beam, with holes in a vertical and a horizontal row | Point of attachment for the chain; used to adjust depth (vertical) and width (horizontal) |
| Wheel | Under the front of the beam | Rides on the surface, steadies the plough and controls depth |
| Beam | The long main bar | Connects all parts and carries the pull to the body |
| Coulter (on some ploughs, mainly tractor ploughs) | Ahead of the share | Cuts the soil vertically, separating the furrow slice from the unploughed land |
| Frog | The body under the rear of the beam | Frame to which the share, mouldboard and landside are bolted |
| Share | Bottom front of the body | Its point penetrates and its sharp edge cuts the furrow slice horizontally |
| Mouldboard | Curved plate behind the share | Lifts, turns and inverts the furrow slice, burying weeds and residues |
| Landside | Flat plate on the side opposite the mouldboard | Presses against the furrow wall, resisting the sideways push of the soil and keeping the plough straight |
| Heel | Rear end of the landside | Wearing part that takes the friction at the bottom of the furrow; replaceable |
| Handles (stilts) | Rear, joined to the beam | Used by the ploughman to steer, balance and lift the plough at turns |

### Adjusting the plough

**Depth.**

1. **Wheel**: raising the wheel lets the share go deeper; lowering it makes the plough shallower.
2. **Hake**: on most ox ploughs, moving the hitch point **up** the vertical row of holes makes the plough go **deeper**, because the line of pull from the yoke becomes less steep and lifts the front of the beam less; moving it **down** makes it shallower.
3. The ploughman also presses or lifts on the handles, but a correctly set plough should run level on its own.

**Width.** Moving the hitch point sideways along the **horizontal** row of holes on the hake changes the angle at which the body meets the unploughed land, and so the **width** of the furrow slice.

A correctly adjusted plough runs level, the heel just touches the furrow bottom, the furrow slice is fully turned, and the ploughman does not have to push or lift the handles all the time.

## Other tillage implements

| Implement | Main parts | Uses | Adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Disc plough | Large concave steel discs on bearings, scrapers, frame, furrow wheel | Primary tillage on hard, stony, root-filled or sticky soils; discs roll over obstacles | Disc angle and weight for depth |
| Cultivator (ox inter-row or tractor tine cultivator) | Frame, tines with shovels or sweeps, wheel, handles, expanding lever | Weeding between crop rows, breaking surface crusts, loosening soil | Width set to the row spacing with the expanding lever; depth with the wheel |
| Spike-tooth harrow | Zigzag frame with rows of spikes | Breaks clods, levels the seedbed, kills young weeds, covers broadcast seed | Spike angle; weight |
| Disc harrow | Gangs of smaller discs | Cuts residues, breaks clods after ploughing | Angle of the gangs |
| Planter | Seed hopper, seed plate with cells, furrow opener, covering device, drive wheel; often a fertiliser hopper | Places seed at an even depth and spacing (and fertiliser beside it) | Seed plate (hole size and number), depth of opener |
| Ridger | Double mouldboard (two wings) on a beam | Forms ridges and tied ridges; earths up potatoes | Width of the wings; depth with the wheel |

:::worked-solution[Seed spacing from a planter]
Assume the drive wheel of an ox planter turns the seed plate once for every 3.6 m travelled, and the plate has 12 cells, each dropping one maize seed.

1. Seeds dropped per 3.6 m = 12.
2. Spacing in the row = 3.6 m ÷ 12 = 0.3 m = **30 cm**.
3. To plant at 25 cm, the farmer would fit a plate with more cells: 3.6 ÷ 0.25 = 14.4, so a plate with about 14 or 15 cells is needed (or the gear ratio changed).
:::

:::worked-solution[How long will an ox plough take?]
An ox plough cuts a slice 25 cm wide at 3 km/h and is actually ploughing for 70% of the time (turns and rests take the rest).

1. Theoretical rate = 0.25 m × 3,000 m/h = 750 m²/h = 0.075 ha/h.
2. Effective rate = 0.075 × 0.70 = 0.0525 ha/h.
3. Time for 1 ha = 1 ÷ 0.0525 = about **19 hours**.
:::

## Routine maintenance and storage

**After each day's work**

1. Scrape off soil and plant material; wash if the soil is sticky.
2. Check for loose or missing nuts and bolts and tighten or replace them.
3. Grease or oil moving parts: wheel bearings, disc bearings, planter mechanisms.
4. Check the share, points and heel for wear and damage.

**During the season**

1. Sharpen blunt shares by grinding or filing (a blunt share increases draught and gives a poor cut) and replace badly worn shares, points, heels and sweeps.
2. Straighten or replace bent tines and repair cracked parts.
3. Keep planter hoppers clean and dry; empty fertiliser hoppers after use, because fertiliser corrodes metal.

**At the end of the season**

1. Clean thoroughly and repair or order spare parts.
2. Coat bright working surfaces (share, mouldboard, discs) with used oil or grease to prevent rust; paint the frame.
3. Store under a roof, off the ground on blocks, out of rain and away from livestock.
4. Keep a record of repairs and parts used.

:::example{title="Fixing a plough that will not stay in the ground"}
Tatenda's plough keeps rising out of the soil and the furrow is very shallow. He finds the share worn round and blunt, the wheel set low and the chain hitched at the bottom hole of the hake. He fits a new share, raises the wheel and moves the hitch up two holes. The plough now enters the soil, runs level at the depth he wants and the oxen pull more easily because a sharp share cuts with less draught.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the landside and the mouldboard"}
The mouldboard turns the furrow slice over; the landside is the flat plate that presses against the furrow wall and keeps the plough straight.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the handles to control depth"}
Constant pressing or lifting on the handles tires the ploughman and gives uneven depth. Set the wheel and hitch correctly instead.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Label and state functions"}
When asked to label a mouldboard plough, use the exact names (share, mouldboard, landside, frog, heel, beam, hake, wheel, handles) and give one clear function for each.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Maintenance answers"}
Group your answer into daily, seasonal and storage tasks, and give a reason: "grease bearings to reduce friction and wear".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Mouldboard plough: share cuts, mouldboard turns, landside steadies, frog holds them, heel takes wear, beam joins, hake adjusts, wheel controls depth, handles steer.
- Depth is set with the wheel and the vertical hake; width with the horizontal hake.
- Disc plough for hard or stony soil; cultivator for weeding between rows; harrows to break clods and level; planter for even seed spacing; ridger for ridges.
- Planter spacing = distance per plate turn ÷ number of cells.
- Maintain by cleaning, tightening, greasing, sharpening and replacing worn parts; store clean, oiled, under cover and off the ground.
:::
