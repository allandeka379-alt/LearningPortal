## Introduction

Every school farm and smallholding near Mutare or Gweru depends on hand tools. A sharp hoe makes weeding quicker; a well-set saw cuts poles for a fowl run without binding; a clean knapsack sprayer with a working nozzle protects the crop without wasting chemical. Tools that are left dirty and in the rain rust, crack and break, and blunt or loose tools cause accidents.

Cambridge asks you to describe the use and maintenance of a saw, hammer, screwdriver, file, spanner, sprayers and hand tools for cultivation. Answers that earn full marks name the correct tool, describe how it is used safely, and give maintenance with a reason. Calibrating a knapsack sprayer is covered with farm chemicals.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Maintenance"}
Regular care of a tool (cleaning, sharpening, oiling, tightening and repairing) so that it works well, lasts long and is safe.
:::

:::definition{term="Set (of a saw)"}
The bending of alternate saw teeth slightly to the left and right, so that the cut (kerf) is wider than the blade and the saw does not stick.
:::

:::definition{term="Personal protective equipment (PPE)"}
Clothing and equipment worn to protect the user, such as gloves, boots, overalls, goggles and a face mask.
:::

## Workshop tools

| Tool | Types | Use | Safe handling | Maintenance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Saw | Crosscut (across the grain), rip (along the grain), bow saw (logs, pruning), hacksaw (metal) | Cutting timber, poles and metal | Support the work firmly; start with short strokes; keep fingers away from the line | Sharpen teeth with a file and keep them set; oil the blade; guard the teeth and hang it up |
| Hammer | Claw hammer, ball-pein, lump hammer | Driving and pulling nails; shaping metal | Grip near the end of the handle; strike squarely; check the head is tight | Tighten a loose head with a wedge; replace cracked handles; keep the face clean |
| Screwdriver | Flat (slotted) and cross-head, in several sizes | Driving and removing screws | Use the size that fills the screw head; never point it at your hand | Keep the tip square by filing or grinding; never use as a chisel or lever |
| File | Flat, half-round, round, triangular | Shaping and smoothing metal; sharpening hoes, slashers and saw teeth | Always fit a handle (the bare tang can pierce the hand); cut on the forward stroke | Clean teeth with a file card (wire brush); store apart from other tools; never use as a lever, because files are brittle |
| Spanner | Open-ended, ring, combination, socket, adjustable | Tightening and loosening nuts and bolts on implements, pumps and sprayers | Use the exact size; pull towards you rather than push | Keep clean and oiled; do not hammer on it or lengthen it with a pipe |

:::example{title="Building a gate for the goat pen"}
Tendai cuts gum poles to length with a bow saw, cuts planks across the grain with a crosscut saw and joins them with nails driven by a claw hammer. Hinges are fixed with screws, using a screwdriver whose tip fits the screw head exactly so the slot is not damaged. He tightens the bolts of the latch with a ring spanner of the correct size. Afterwards he cleans and oils the saw blades, checks that the hammer head is tight and hangs the tools on the workshop board.
:::

## Sprayers

A **knapsack sprayer** is carried on the back. Its main parts, from the tank to the nozzle, are:

1. **Tank** with a lid and a **filter (strainer)** at the opening to keep out dirt.
2. **Pump** (piston or diaphragm) worked by a **lever handle**.
3. **Pressure chamber** that keeps the pressure even.
4. **Hose** leading to the **trigger (cut-off) valve**.
5. **Lance** with the **nozzle**, which breaks the liquid into droplets of the right size and pattern.

**Using a sprayer safely**

1. Check the sprayer with clean water for leaks and a good spray pattern before adding any chemical.
2. Read the label; mix only the amount needed, outdoors and away from water sources.
3. Wear PPE: overalls, gloves, boots, goggles or a face shield and a mask as the label requires.
4. Spray in calm weather, walk at a steady pace and keep the pressure even; do not spray into the wind or near people, animals or streams.
5. Do not eat, drink or smoke while spraying; wash well afterwards.

**Maintaining a sprayer**

1. After use, rinse the tank three times with clean water and spray the rinse water over the treated crop, never into a stream or well.
2. Pump clean water through the hose, lance and nozzle.
3. Clean a blocked nozzle with a soft brush or a grass stalk: **never blow through it with your mouth** and never poke it with a wire, which damages the opening.
4. Check washers and seals and replace worn ones; oil moving parts of the pump as instructed.
5. Store empty, dry and open to the air, locked away from food, children and animals. Keep a separate sprayer for herbicides, because traces left in the tank can damage crops.

## Hand tools for cultivation

| Tool | Main uses | Maintenance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hoe | Digging, weeding, making ridges and basins | Sharpen the blade with a file; tighten or replace the handle |
| Spade | Digging, making beds and trenches, mixing compost | Clean off soil; sharpen the edge; oil the blade |
| Shovel | Moving loose soil, sand, gravel and manure | Clean and dry; oil to prevent rust |
| Digging fork | Digging heavy or stony soil, turning compost, lifting potatoes | Straighten bent tines; clean and oil |
| Rake | Levelling seedbeds, breaking small clods, collecting trash | Clean; store hanging up with the teeth facing the wall |
| Pick (pickaxe) and mattock | Breaking hard ground and rocks; digging out stumps and roots | Keep the head tight; sharpen the points |
| Slasher | Cutting grass and weeds, clearing fire guards | Sharpen with a file; keep a firm handle |

**General care**: clean off soil after every use, dry the tool, oil bare metal, sharpen cutting edges, replace split or loose handles (smooth rough handles with sandpaper and rub with linseed oil), and store tools under cover, hung up on a board where they can be counted.

**Safety**: carry sharp tools with the blade pointing down; never leave rakes or forks with the points upwards on the ground; keep a safe distance from others when swinging a pick or slasher; wear boots; and report damaged tools.

:::example{title="Choosing tools for a new garden bed"}
Chipo breaks the hard, stony ground with a pick, then digs it over with a digging fork, which goes into stony soil more easily than a spade. She mixes in compost with a spade, levels the bed and makes a fine tilth with a rake, and finally uses a hoe to draw shallow drills for the seed. When she finishes she scrapes and oils the blades and hangs the tools in the store.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using a file without a handle"}
The pointed tang can be driven into the palm. Always fit a handle before filing.
:::

:::mistake{title="Clearing a nozzle by blowing it"}
This puts pesticide in your mouth. Use a soft brush or grass stalk and wear gloves.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Describe use and maintenance"}
Name the tool, say what it is used for, then give two or three maintenance steps with a reason ("oil the blade to prevent rusting").
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Sprayer questions"}
Expect to name parts of a knapsack sprayer, state their functions and describe safe use, cleaning and storage. Always mention PPE and safe disposal of rinse water.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Saws cut wood or metal and must be kept sharp and set; hammers need tight heads; screwdrivers must fit the screw; files need handles and are brittle; spanners must be the exact size.
- A knapsack sprayer has a tank, filter, pump and lever, pressure chamber, hose, trigger valve, lance and nozzle.
- Use sprayers with PPE, in calm weather, at an even pace and pressure; rinse, clean nozzles safely and store locked away.
- Hoes, spades, shovels, forks, rakes, picks and slashers each have their own job.
- Clean, dry, oil, sharpen, repair handles and store tools under cover; carry and store them safely.
:::
