## Introduction

A sow can produce more than twenty piglets a year, and each one can reach slaughter weight in about six months. That is why a small piggery near Harare or a school unit near Chinhoyi can make good money, provided that the sow is served on time, the piglets survive their first weeks and the growing pigs turn feed into meat efficiently. Pigs eat concentrate feed, so feed is the largest cost, and poor management shows up quickly in the accounts.

ZIMSEC (Form 3) asks you to identify the breeds, describe housing systems and manage pigs (or donkeys) to maturity. Cambridge uses one non-ruminant to examine housing, nutrition and health, and the pig is a common choice. Calculations include farrowing dates, litters per year, daily gain, feed conversion ratio and dressing percentage.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Boar, sow and gilt"}
A boar is an entire male pig kept for breeding; a sow is a female that has farrowed; a gilt is a young female that has not yet farrowed.
:::

:::definition{term="Farrowing"}
The act of giving birth in pigs.
:::

:::definition{term="Weaner"}
A young pig that has just been separated from the sow.
:::

:::definition{term="Porker and baconer"}
A porker is a pig fattened to a lighter weight for fresh pork; a baconer is taken to a heavier weight for bacon and processed meat.
:::

## Breeds

| Breed | Appearance | Main features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large White | White, erect ears, long body | Prolific, good mothers, hardy; bacon and pork |
| Landrace | White, long body, ears drooping forward over the eyes | Long, lean carcass suited to bacon; good mothers |
| Duroc | Red-brown, drooping ears | Fast growth, hardy; often used as a terminal sire for crossbreds |
| Hampshire | Black with a white band over the shoulders | Lean, muscular carcass |
| Mukota (indigenous) | Small, black, hardy | Survives on poor feed and scavenging; slow growth, fatty carcass |

Most commercial farmers cross Large White and Landrace sows (for mothering ability and litter size) with a boar of a meaty breed, using **hybrid vigour** to obtain fast-growing slaughter pigs.

## Housing

Pigs cannot cool themselves well by sweating, so houses must be shaded and well ventilated, with water always available. Floors are concrete, sloped towards a drain, with a separate dunging area; pigs keep their sleeping area clean if given space.

- **Farrowing pen** with a **farrowing crate** or guard rails, which stop the sow lying on her piglets, and a warm **creep area** (with a heat lamp or straw) that only piglets can enter.
- **Weaner pens** and **grower/finisher pens**, grouping pigs of similar size.
- **Dry sow pens** or stalls and a strong **boar pen** next to the sows.

Systems range from **intensive** (all pigs housed, fed concentrates; high output, high cost) to **extensive** or free-range (pigs outdoors with simple shelters and wallows; cheaper, but slower growth and more disease and losses).

## Feeding

Pigs are omnivores with a simple stomach; they need feeds rich in energy and protein (maize, soya and fishmeal) with vitamins and minerals, plus clean water at all times.

| Stage | Feed (typical) |
| --- | --- |
| Piglets from about 1-2 weeks | Creep feed (highly digestible, high protein) |
| Weaners | Weaner meal |
| Growers and finishers | Grower meal, then finisher (porker or baconer) meal |
| Pregnant sows and gilts | Restricted sow meal so they do not get too fat |
| Lactating sows | Plenty of lactation meal and water for milk |
| Boars | Restricted boar or sow meal to keep them fit, not fat |

Kitchen waste (swill) must be **boiled** before feeding. Uncooked meat scraps can spread serious diseases such as African swine fever and foot-and-mouth disease, and many countries control or ban swill feeding for this reason.

## Breeding

Gilts are usually first served at about 7-8 months, at their second or third heat. The oestrous cycle is about **21 days** and heat lasts two to three days. Signs of heat: a red, swollen vulva, restlessness, mounting other pigs, loss of appetite, and the **standing reflex**: the sow stands rigid when the stockman presses firmly on her back. Serve her twice, about 12-24 hours apart. Gestation is about **114 days** (3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days). After weaning a sow usually returns to heat within about a week.

:::worked-solution[Farrowing date and litters per year]
Chipo's sow is served on 4 May 2026. She weans her piglets at 5 weeks (35 days) and is served again 7 days after weaning.

1. Expected farrowing date = 4 May 2026 + 114 days = **26 August 2026**.
2. Length of one breeding cycle = 114 + 35 + 7 = 156 days.
3. Litters per sow per year = 365 ÷ 156 = **2.34** (two decimal places).
4. If 10 piglets are weaned per litter: 2.34 × 10 = about **23.4** piglets weaned per sow per year.
:::

## Farrowing and care of piglets

**Signs of farrowing:** nest-building (biting and arranging bedding), restlessness, a swollen vulva and milk that can be squeezed from the teats. Wash the sow and move her into the clean farrowing pen about a week before the due date.

**Care of piglets:**

1. **At birth:** clear mucus from the nose and mouth, dip the navel in iodine and make sure every piglet suckles colostrum.
2. **Day 1:** clip the sharp **needle teeth** (eye teeth) so piglets do not injure each other or the sow's teats.
3. **About day 3:** give an **iron injection** to prevent **piglet anaemia**. Piglets are born with little iron, sow's milk contains little iron, and piglets on concrete cannot get it from soil.
4. Identify piglets by ear notching or tattooing.
5. **Castrate** males not kept for breeding in the first two weeks.
6. Offer **creep feed** from about 1-2 weeks and clean water.
7. **Wean** at about 4-8 weeks (often 5 weeks); move the sow out and leave the piglets in their pen to reduce stress.

## Fattening for pork and bacon

Growing pigs are fed to appetite or on a careful ration until they reach slaughter weight: porkers at a lighter weight (for example about 60-70 kg live) and baconers at a heavier weight (about 90-100 kg). Pigs typically dress at about 70-80%.

:::worked-solution[Performance of a baconer]
A baconer grew from 25 kg to 90 kg in 110 days and ate 195 kg of feed. Its carcass weighed 68.4 kg.

1. Live-weight gain = 90 − 25 = 65 kg.
2. Average daily gain = 65 ÷ 110 = **0.59 kg/day** (two decimal places).
3. FCR = 195 ÷ 65 = **3.0**: 3 kg of feed per kg of gain.
4. Dressing percentage = 68.4 ÷ 90 × 100 = **76%**.
:::

## Health

| Problem | Cause or sign | Prevention or control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Piglet anaemia | Iron shortage: pale, weak, slow piglets | Iron injection at about 3 days |
| Scours | Diarrhoea in young pigs | Hygiene, warmth, colostrum |
| Mange | Mites: itching, scabby skin | Spray or inject with an approved acaricide |
| Roundworms | Poor growth; liver damage | Regular deworming, clean pens |
| African swine fever | Viral, high death rate; spread by sick pigs, pork scraps, warthogs and soft ticks | Fence pigs in, boil swill, report suspected cases |
| Heat stress | Panting, distress | Shade, ventilation, water, wallows |

**Records:** a **sow card** (service dates, boar used, farrowing date, piglets born alive, weaned), weekly weights, feed used, treatments and sales. They show which sows to keep and whether the unit makes a profit.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the iron injection"}
Piglets on concrete become anaemic within weeks. Give iron at about 3 days old.
:::

:::mistake{title="Overfeeding pregnant sows"}
Fat sows have farrowing difficulties and crush more piglets. Restrict feed in pregnancy and feed generously in lactation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling a young female that has farrowed a gilt"}
A gilt has not yet farrowed. After her first litter she is a sow.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Piglet care in order"}
Give the age and the reason for each practice: teeth clipping on day 1 (injury), iron at about 3 days (anaemia), castration within two weeks (less stress), creep feed from 1-2 weeks (growth, weaning), weaning at about 5 weeks.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Calculations"}
Farrowing date: add 114 days. Litters per year: 365 ÷ (gestation + lactation + days to service). Show every step.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Breeds: Large White and Landrace (prolific, good mothers), Duroc and Hampshire (fast, lean), Mukota (indigenous, hardy).
- Houses are shaded and ventilated, with farrowing crates and a warm creep area; pigs need water at all times.
- Feed creep, weaner, grower and finisher meals; restrict pregnant sows and boars; boil swill.
- Oestrous cycle about 21 days; standing reflex; serve twice; gestation about 114 days.
- Piglets: navel, colostrum, teeth clipped on day 1, iron at about 3 days, castration within two weeks, creep feed, weaning at about 4-8 weeks.
:::
