## Introduction

Beef cattle are the backbone of farming in the drier parts of Zimbabwe. A ranch near Gwanda runs Brahman-cross cows on thousands of hectares of veld, while a smallholder near Masvingo keeps a few Mashona cows that also provide draught power and manure. In both cases the aim is to turn grass that people cannot eat into calves and beef that can be sold, as cheaply as possible and without damaging the veld.

ZIMSEC (Form 4) asks you to list exotic and indigenous breeds of cattle, sheep or goats in Zimbabwe, describe their characteristics and describe their management, including the care of young stock. Cambridge examines one named ruminant for housing, nutrition and health; beef cattle are a common choice.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Indigenous breed"}
A breed that developed locally over a long time and is adapted to local heat, diseases, ticks and grazing, for example the Mashona, Nkone and Tuli.
:::

:::definition{term="Exotic breed"}
A breed brought in from another country, for example the Brahman, Hereford and Simmental.
:::

:::definition{term="Breeding season"}
A fixed period of the year, usually about three months, when bulls are run with the cows, so that calves are born within a short calving season.
:::

:::definition{term="Pen fattening (feedlotting)"}
Keeping cattle in pens for a few months and feeding them a high-energy ration so that they gain mass quickly and reach a good slaughter grade.
:::

## Breeds

| Breed | Type and origin | Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mashona | Indigenous (Sanga type) | Small to medium size, hardy, very fertile, good mothers, tolerant of heat and ticks, survives on poor veld |
| Nkone | Indigenous (Sanga type), historically kept by the Ndebele | Medium size, hardy and fertile, well adapted to dry conditions |
| Tuli | Indigenous, developed in Zimbabwe from Sanga cattle | Yellow to golden-brown, hardy, heat tolerant, good beef shape, docile |
| Brahman | Exotic zebu (*Bos indicus*), developed in the USA | Large hump, loose skin and dewlap, drooping ears; very heat and tick tolerant; used in crossbreeding |
| Hereford | Exotic British (*Bos taurus*) | Red body with a white face; early maturing, good beef, docile; less heat tolerant |
| Simmental | Exotic Swiss | Large, red or fawn and white; fast growth; good milk for calves |

**A good beef animal** has a deep, wide, rectangular ("blocky") body, well-muscled hindquarters and loin, a short thick neck and short legs. Exotic breeds grow faster and bigger with good feeding; indigenous breeds survive drought, ticks and poor grazing better. Many farmers **crossbreed** (for example Mashona cows with a Simmental or Brahman bull) to combine hardiness with growth and gain **hybrid vigour**.

## Breeding and calving seasons

Allow about **one mature bull to 25-30 cows**. Keep bulls apart from the herd for most of the year, then run them with the cows for a **breeding season** of about three months, timed so that calves are born when the cows will soon have good grazing for milk.

:::worked-solution[Setting the breeding season]
A rancher near Beitbridge runs 120 cows. Bulls are put in on 1 January 2026 and taken out on 31 March 2026. Assume a ratio of one bull to 30 cows and a gestation of 283 days.

1. Bulls needed = 120 ÷ 30 = **4 bulls**.
2. First calves due = 1 January 2026 + 283 days = **11 October 2026**.
3. Last calves due = 31 March 2026 + 283 days = **8 January 2027**.
4. Calves arrive from about mid-October to early January, around the start of the rains, so cows are milking when new grass grows.
:::

**Advantages of a controlled breeding season:** calves of similar age that can be branded, dosed, vaccinated, weaned and sold in groups; calving can be supervised; cows' feed needs match the grazing; open (non-pregnant) cows are easy to identify and cull.

## Management practices

| Practice | How | When and why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identification | Hot-iron or freeze branding with the owner's brand; numbered ear tags; ear notches | Branding proves ownership and deters theft; tags allow individual records |
| Dehorning | Disbud calves with a hot iron or caustic paste; adults only with a saw or wire by a skilled person | Young calves; fewer injuries and bruised carcasses, easier handling |
| Castration | Rubber ring, bloodless castrator (burdizzo) or knife | Young bull calves not kept for breeding; docile steers, no unwanted mating |
| Dipping or spraying | Plunge dip tank, spray race or pour-on with an acaricide | Often weekly in the hot, wet season when ticks are many, less often in the dry season, as advised by the veterinary services; controls tick-borne diseases |
| Dosing | Oral drench or injection against internal parasites | At strategic times, for example at the start and end of the rains; better growth |
| Vaccination | Against diseases such as anthrax, blackleg, lumpy skin disease and botulism | On the veterinary schedule; prevents losses |
| Weaning | Separate calves from cows | At about 6-8 months; the cow regains condition before her next calf |
| Culling | Remove old, infertile or poor-performing animals | After pregnancy testing and weaning |

## Feeding on the veld and pen fattening

Beef cattle are usually grazed on the **veld**. In summer, green grass supplies enough energy and protein. In the dry season the grass is dry and low in protein, so cattle lose mass unless they receive **supplements**: protein or urea licks, hay, stover or silage. Veld in many areas is also short of **phosphorus**; cattle short of it chew bones and can pick up botulism, so a **phosphate lick** (with salt) is given. Keep stocking rates within the carrying capacity of the veld and rotate paddocks.

**Pen fattening.** Weaners or older steers are put into pens for about three to four months and fed a ration of energy feeds (maize or hominy chop, molasses), a protein concentrate and roughage, with clean water. Grain is introduced gradually to prevent bloat and an over-acid rumen. On arrival the cattle are dosed, dipped and vaccinated. Pen fattening finishes cattle quickly to a high slaughter grade and takes pressure off the veld in the dry season, but feed is costly, so performance must be measured.

:::worked-solution[Pen-fattening results]
A steer entered the pens at 300 kg and left 100 days later at 450 kg, having eaten 1,050 kg of ration. Its carcass weighed 252 kg.

1. Gain = 450 − 300 = 150 kg.
2. Average daily gain = 150 ÷ 100 = **1.5 kg/day**.
3. FCR = 1,050 ÷ 150 = **7**: 7 kg of ration per kg of gain.
4. Dressing percentage = 252 ÷ 450 × 100 = **56%**. Cattle typically dress at about 50-60%.
:::

**Records:** a herd register (each animal's number, breed, date of birth, sire and dam), breeding and calving dates, weaning weights, dipping, dosing and vaccination dates, sales, deaths and costs.

:::example{title="Choosing a breed for natural region IV"}
Farai farms in natural region IV near Gwanda, with long dry seasons, many ticks and poor grazing. A pure Hereford herd would grow fast in a good year but lose condition badly in drought and suffer from heat and ticks. Mashona or Tuli cows are better adapted, and crossing them with a Brahman or Simmental bull gives calves that grow faster while keeping much of their mothers' hardiness.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling Brahman an indigenous breed"}
The Brahman is an exotic zebu breed developed in the USA. Zimbabwe's indigenous breeds include the Mashona, Nkone and Tuli.
:::

:::mistake{title="Running the bull with the cows all year"}
This spreads calving over the whole year and makes group management impossible. Use a breeding season.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring the dry season"}
Dry veld grass is short of protein. Without licks and extra roughage, cattle lose much of the mass they gained in summer.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="List and describe breeds"}
For each breed give its type (indigenous or exotic) and at least two characteristics linked to the environment: "Mashona: hardy, fertile, tolerant of ticks and poor grazing".
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Management practices"}
Give the method, the time and the reason. "Calves are disbudded with a hot iron at a few weeks old to prevent injuries and bruising" scores better than "dehorning".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Indigenous breeds (Mashona, Nkone, Tuli) are hardy, fertile and adapted; exotic breeds (Brahman, Hereford, Simmental) grow faster; crossbreeding combines the two.
- Use about one bull to 25-30 cows and a breeding season of about three months, timed so calves are born near the start of the rains.
- Brand and tag, disbud and castrate young calves, dip against ticks, dose against worms, vaccinate, wean at about 6-8 months and cull poor animals.
- On veld, give protein and phosphate licks in the dry season; pen fattening finishes cattle quickly on high-energy rations.
- Measure performance: ADG, FCR and dressing percentage (typically about 50-60%).
:::
