## Introduction

On a dairy farm near Mutare every cow that fails to get pregnant on time costs the farmer weeks of lost milk. On a beef ranch near Gwanda a bull that is infertile can leave a whole herd without calves for a season. Good breeding management starts with knowing how the reproductive organs work, when a female is ready to mate and how long pregnancy lasts.

ZIMSEC (Form 4) asks you to identify the parts of the male and female reproductive systems of a ruminant and explain their functions. Cambridge asks you to describe the systems of a named farm mammal and describe fertilisation. Both boards expect you to know the signs of heat and the gestation periods of the main farm animals. Here the named animal is the cow; birth and the care of the calf are in the next lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Oestrous cycle"}
The regular cycle of changes in the reproductive organs of a female mammal from one period of heat to the next; about 21 days in the cow, sow and doe and about 17 days in the ewe.
:::

:::definition{term="Oestrus (heat)"}
The short period of the cycle when the female is willing to stand to be mated and ovulation is about to occur.
:::

:::definition{term="Fertilisation"}
The fusion of a sperm nucleus with an egg (ovum) nucleus to form a zygote, which takes place in the oviduct.
:::

:::definition{term="Gestation period"}
The length of pregnancy, from successful mating (conception) to birth.
:::

:::definition{term="Artificial insemination (AI)"}
Placing semen collected from a selected male into the reproductive tract of a female in heat, using an inseminating gun, instead of natural mating.
:::

## The male reproductive system (bull)

| Part | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Scrotum | Bag of skin that holds the testes outside the body, keeping them a few degrees cooler than the body, which sperm production needs |
| Testes (two) | Produce sperm and the male hormone testosterone |
| Epididymis | Coiled tube on each testis where sperm mature and are stored |
| Vas deferens (sperm duct) | Carries sperm from the epididymis to the urethra |
| Accessory glands (seminal vesicles, prostate, Cowper's glands) | Add fluid that nourishes and carries sperm; sperm plus fluid is semen |
| Urethra | Carries semen (and urine at other times) through the penis |
| Penis | Places semen in the female's vagina; in the bull an S-shaped bend (sigmoid flexure) straightens when it is extended |
| Sheath | Fold of skin that protects the penis |

## The female reproductive system (cow)

Described from the ovaries outward:

![Front view of the cow's reproductive tract: two ovaries, each with an oviduct where fertilisation takes place, leading to two uterine horns that join in the uterine body, then the thick-walled cervix, the vagina and the vulva.](media:diagrams/cow-reproductive-system.png "Figure: the reproductive system of the cow (schematic)")

| Part | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Ovaries (two) | Produce eggs (ova) in follicles and the hormones oestrogen and progesterone; after ovulation the follicle becomes a corpus luteum that keeps up pregnancy |
| Oviducts (Fallopian tubes) | Funnel-shaped ends catch the egg; fertilisation takes place here |
| Uterus (body and two horns) | The embryo implants and develops; the placenta forms on its wall |
| Cervix | Thick, muscular neck of the uterus; closed by a mucus plug during pregnancy, it opens at heat and at birth |
| Vagina | Receives the penis or the inseminating gun; forms the birth canal |
| Vulva | External opening; swells and reddens at heat |

## The oestrous cycle and signs of heat

A cow comes on heat about every **21 days** until she is pregnant. Heat itself is short, usually less than a day, and many heats begin at night, so the herdsman must watch the cows closely, ideally two or three times a day.

**Signs of heat in a cow:**

1. Stands still when mounted by other cows or the bull (**standing heat**), the surest sign.
2. Mounts other cows.
3. Restless, bellows, walks a lot and may stop grazing.
4. Swollen, red, moist vulva with clear, stringy mucus.
5. A sudden drop in milk yield in a dairy cow.

**Timing service.** Ovulation happens shortly after the end of standing heat, and sperm need a few hours in the tract before they can fertilise, so service is timed for the second half of heat. The common rule is: a cow seen on heat in the **morning** is served that **afternoon**; a cow seen on heat in the **afternoon** is served early **next morning**.

:::worked-solution[Predicting the next heats]
Chipo sees a Jersey heifer on standing heat on 3 March 2026 but does not serve her because she is not yet big enough.

1. Cycle length = about 21 days.
2. Next expected heat = 3 March + 21 days = **24 March 2026**.
3. The one after = 24 March + 21 days = **14 April 2026**.
4. Chipo marks these dates on the breeding calendar and watches the heifer closely for two or three days around each one.
:::

## Mating: natural service or AI

**Natural service** uses a bull running with the cows or brought to a cow on heat. It needs little skill, and the bull detects heat himself, but the bull must be fed and housed all year, may spread venereal diseases, and limits the farmer to that one bull's genes.

**Artificial insemination** uses frozen semen from proven bulls kept in liquid nitrogen. It gives access to top-quality sires, removes the cost and danger of keeping a bull, reduces the spread of venereal diseases and allows many cows to be served by one bull. However, it needs accurate heat detection, a trained inseminator and a supply of semen and liquid nitrogen.

## Fertilisation and pregnancy

At service, semen is placed in the vagina (natural service) or through the cervix into the uterus (AI). Sperm swim up through the uterus into the oviducts. If an egg has been released, one sperm fuses with it to form a **zygote**. The zygote divides as it moves down to the uterus, where the embryo **implants**. A **placenta** forms, through which food and oxygen pass from the mother's blood to the foetus and wastes pass back; the foetus floats in **amniotic fluid** that cushions it.

**Signs and tests of pregnancy:** no return to heat about 21 days after service; later, an enlarged abdomen and udder; a veterinarian can confirm pregnancy by rectal examination or a scan.

| Animal | Typical gestation | Typical oestrous cycle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cow | About 283 days (about 9 months) | About 21 days |
| Sow | About 114 days (3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days) | About 21 days |
| Ewe | About 150 days | About 17 days |
| Doe (goat) | About 150 days | About 21 days |
| Rabbit doe | About 31 days | Ovulation is induced by mating |

:::worked-solution[Expected calving date and calving interval]
Tendai's cow is served on 12 January 2026.

1. Gestation = 283 days.
2. Expected calving date = 12 January 2026 + 283 days = **22 October 2026**.
3. If she is served again 85 days after calving, the calving interval = 283 + 85 = **368 days**.
4. For one calf a year (365 days) she must conceive within 365 − 283 = **82 days** of calving.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying fertilisation happens in the uterus"}
Fertilisation takes place in the **oviduct**. The embryo then moves to the uterus and implants there.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the cycle with heat"}
The oestrous **cycle** lasts about 21 days in the cow; **heat** is the short part of it, usually less than a day, when she will stand to be mated.
:::

:::mistake{title="Counting months instead of days"}
Use 283 days, not "9 months", when calculating a calving date; months have different lengths.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Parts and functions"}
Questions often describe a part ("a coiled tube where sperm are stored") and ask you to name it (epididymis). Learn each part with one clear function, and say *why* the testes are in the scrotum (a lower temperature for sperm production).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Calculations and explanations"}
For dates, show the number of days added and give the full date. When asked to *explain* the timing of service, link it to ovulation near the end of heat and the time sperm need in the tract.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Bull: testes in the scrotum make sperm and testosterone; epididymis stores sperm; vas deferens and urethra carry them; accessory glands add fluid; the penis deposits semen.
- Cow: ovaries release eggs and hormones; oviducts are the site of fertilisation; the embryo develops in the uterus; the cervix seals the uterus during pregnancy; the vagina and vulva form the birth canal.
- The oestrous cycle is about 21 days (17 in the ewe); standing to be mounted is the surest sign of heat; serve in the second half of heat (morning seen, afternoon served).
- Natural service is simple but limits genetics; AI gives access to proven sires but needs good heat detection.
- Gestation: cow about 283 days, sow 114, ewe and doe about 150, rabbit about 31.
:::
