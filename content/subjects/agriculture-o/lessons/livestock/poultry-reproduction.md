## Introduction

Nyasha's grandmother near Chipinge keeps indigenous chickens with a cock, and her hens hatch chicks from their own eggs. Nyasha's school keeps a flock of layers with no cock at all, yet the hens lay an egg almost every day. Why do both flocks lay, and why can only one of them produce chicks? The answer lies in how the hen's reproductive system makes an egg, layer by layer, whether or not it has been fertilised.

ZIMSEC (Form 2) asks you to draw and label the reproductive systems of the cock and the hen, describe egg formation and state the functions of the parts of an egg. Because you cannot see a drawing here, learn each system as a labelled list in order; in the exam you may be asked to draw it.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Oviduct"}
The long tube of the hen, from the ovary to the cloaca, in which the yolk is fertilised (if sperm are present) and the albumen, shell membranes and shell are added.
:::

:::definition{term="Fertile egg"}
An egg laid by a hen that has mated with a cock, in which the germinal disc has been fertilised and can develop into a chick when incubated.
:::

:::definition{term="Hatchability"}
The percentage of eggs that hatch, usually expressed as a percentage of the fertile eggs set (or of all eggs set).
:::

:::definition{term="Candling"}
Examining an egg in front of a bright light in a dark room to see its contents: cracks, the air cell, blood spots, and whether an embryo is developing.
:::

## The cock's reproductive system

Described as a labelled diagram would show it:

1. **Two testes**, bean-shaped and **inside the body cavity**, along the backbone near the kidneys. They produce sperm and the male hormone. Unlike mammals, the cock has no scrotum.
2. **Vasa deferentia** (sperm ducts), two coiled tubes that carry and store sperm.
3. Each duct opens through a small **papilla** into the **cloaca**.

The cock has no true penis. During mating he mounts the hen and their cloacas touch briefly, transferring semen. Sperm can be stored in glands in the hen's oviduct and stay fertile for about one to two weeks, so a hen keeps laying fertile eggs for some days after the cock is removed. A typical flock for hatching eggs has about one cock to ten hens.

## The hen's reproductive system

In the hen only the **left ovary and left oviduct** develop; the right ones stay small and do not work.

The **ovary** looks like a bunch of grapes: it holds many ova (yolks) at different stages of growth. A mature yolk is released (**ovulation**) into the oviduct, which has five parts.

| Part of the oviduct | What it adds or does | Time spent (about) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infundibulum (funnel) | Catches the yolk; fertilisation takes place here; chalazae start to form | 15 minutes |
| Magnum | Longest part; secretes the albumen (egg white) | 3 hours |
| Isthmus | Adds the inner and outer shell membranes | 1¼ hours |
| Uterus (shell gland) | Adds water to the albumen, then the hard calcium carbonate shell and its colour | About 20 hours |
| Vagina | Adds the cuticle (bloom); pushes the egg out through the cloaca and vent | Minutes |

Altogether an egg takes about a day (roughly 24-26 hours) to form, which is why a good layer lays about one egg a day.

## Parts of the egg

| Part | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Shell | Hard calcium carbonate layer that protects the contents; thousands of tiny pores let oxygen in and carbon dioxide and water vapour out |
| Cuticle (bloom) | Thin coat that seals the pores against bacteria and slows water loss; washing removes it |
| Shell membranes (outer and inner) | Two thin membranes that keep out bacteria |
| Air cell | Space between the two membranes at the broad end; supplies air to the chick just before hatching; grows larger as the egg ages |
| Albumen (thick and thin) | Water and protein; cushions the yolk and supplies water and protein to the embryo |
| Chalazae | Twisted cords of albumen that hold the yolk in the centre of the egg |
| Vitelline membrane | Thin membrane around the yolk |
| Yolk | Food store of fat, protein, vitamins and minerals for the embryo |
| Germinal disc (blastodisc) | Small pale spot on the yolk that becomes the embryo if the egg is fertile |

:::worked-solution[Proportions of an egg]
Form 2 pupils broke a 60 g egg and weighed its parts: shell 6 g, albumen 36 g, yolk 18 g.

1. Check: 6 + 36 + 18 = 60 g.
2. Shell = 6 ÷ 60 × 100 = **10%**.
3. Albumen = 36 ÷ 60 × 100 = **60%**.
4. Yolk = 18 ÷ 60 × 100 = **30%**.
5. So the albumen is the largest part by mass; it is mostly water.
:::

## Fertile eggs and hatching

A hen lays whether or not she has mated, so eggs from a flock of layers kept without a cock are **infertile** table eggs. They are safe to eat and keep better. To hatch chicks you need a cock with the hens, clean, well-shaped eggs of medium size stored for less than about a week, and incubation for **21 days** under a broody hen or in an incubator kept at the correct temperature and humidity, with the eggs turned several times a day.

:::worked-solution[Fertility and hatchability]
Farai sets 120 eggs in an incubator. Candling on day 7 shows 108 with developing embryos; 96 chicks hatch.

1. Fertility = 108 ÷ 120 × 100 = **90%**.
2. Hatchability of fertile eggs = 96 ÷ 108 × 100 = **88.9%** (one decimal place).
3. Hatchability of all eggs set = 96 ÷ 120 × 100 = **80%**.
4. The 12 infertile eggs point to the cock (too few, too old or unhealthy); the 12 fertile eggs that did not hatch point to incubation (temperature, humidity, turning) or old, dirty eggs.
:::

**Egg defects** you should recognise: soft-shelled or shell-less eggs (often a shortage of calcium or disease), blood spots (a small blood vessel broken at ovulation), double-yolked eggs (two yolks released together, common in young hens) and misshapen eggs.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking a hen needs a cock to lay"}
Hens lay without a cock. The cock is needed only for **fertile** eggs that can hatch.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the magnum and the uterus"}
The **magnum** adds the albumen; the **uterus** (shell gland) adds the shell. The isthmus, between them, adds the shell membranes.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting the testes in a scrotum"}
In the cock the testes are inside the body cavity near the kidneys.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Drawing and labelling"}
If asked to draw the hen's system, draw one ovary like a bunch of grapes and one long oviduct divided into infundibulum, magnum, isthmus, uterus and vagina, ending at the cloaca. Label lines must touch the part and must not cross.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Functions of egg parts"}
Give one precise function for each part: "the chalazae hold the yolk in the centre", not "the chalazae protect the egg".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The cock has two internal testes and two sperm ducts opening into the cloaca; mating is by cloacal contact.
- The hen has only a left ovary and oviduct; the oviduct has five parts: infundibulum (fertilisation), magnum (albumen), isthmus (shell membranes), uterus (shell) and vagina (cuticle, laying).
- An egg takes about a day to form; hens lay without a cock, but only fertile eggs can hatch (21 days of incubation).
- Egg parts: shell and cuticle (protection, gas exchange), membranes, air cell, albumen, chalazae, yolk and germinal disc.
- Fertility = fertile ÷ set × 100; hatchability = hatched ÷ fertile (or set) × 100.
:::
