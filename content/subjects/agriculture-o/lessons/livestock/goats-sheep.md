## Introduction

Goats are found at almost every homestead in the drier parts of Zimbabwe, from Gokwe to Mwenezi. They browse bushes that cattle ignore, survive droughts that kill cattle, breed quickly and can be sold for cash when school fees are due. Sheep are fewer but are kept for meat, especially hardy hair sheep in the south and west. Small ruminants need less capital than cattle, but they suffer heavily from worms, ticks, predators and theft if they are poorly managed.

ZIMSEC (Form 4) asks you to list exotic and indigenous breeds of cattle, sheep or goats in Zimbabwe, describe their characteristics and describe their management, including the care of young stock. Cambridge accepts goats or sheep as the named ruminant for housing, nutrition and health.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Buck, doe and kid; ram, ewe and lamb"}
For goats: buck (male), doe (female), kid (young). For sheep: ram (male), ewe (female), lamb (young). Giving birth is kidding in goats and lambing in sheep.
:::

:::definition{term="Browsing and grazing"}
Browsing is eating leaves, twigs and pods of shrubs and trees, as goats prefer to do; grazing is eating grass and low plants close to the ground, as sheep and cattle do.
:::

:::definition{term="Flushing"}
Improving the feeding of does or ewes for about two to three weeks before and during mating, so that more eggs are released and more twins are born.
:::

:::definition{term="Kidding (or lambing) percentage"}
The number of young born divided by the number of females mated, multiplied by 100; it can be more than 100% when twins are common.
:::

## Breeds

| Breed | Species, type | Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matebele goat | Goat, indigenous | Large-framed local goat; hardy, good meat, many colours |
| Mashona goat | Goat, indigenous | Small, hardy, prolific; survives on poor browse |
| Boer goat | Goat, exotic (South Africa) | White body with a red-brown head; fast-growing, well-muscled meat goat |
| Kalahari Red | Goat, exotic (South Africa) | Red-brown meat goat; hardy in hot, dry areas |
| Saanen | Goat, exotic (Switzerland) | White dairy goat; high milk yield, needs good feed and shelter |
| Angora | Goat, exotic | Produces mohair |
| Sabi sheep | Sheep, indigenous | Hardy, fat-tailed hair sheep; tolerates heat and poor grazing |
| Dorper | Sheep, exotic (South Africa) | Black head and white body (or all white); fast-growing hardy meat sheep with a hair-and-wool coat that sheds |
| Merino | Sheep, exotic | Fine-wool breed; needs shearing and suits cooler, better-fed areas |

Indigenous breeds are hardy, resist heat, ticks and worms better, survive on poor feed and are good mothers, but they are small and grow slowly. Exotic breeds grow faster and bigger with good feeding. Crossing local does with a Boer buck is a common way to raise kid growth while keeping hardiness.

## Housing

Goats dislike rain and wet ground, and damp conditions cause pneumonia, foot rot and worms. A good goat house has:

- a **raised slatted floor** (about a metre off the ground), so droppings and urine fall through and the floor stays dry;
- a roof and walls that keep out rain and wind but allow ventilation;
- secure doors and walls against predators (jackals, dogs) and thieves;
- a separate pen for kids and for the buck, feed racks above floor level and water troughs.

Sheep are often kept in a kraal with a roofed, dry shelter. Both species are housed at night and herded or paddocked during the day.

## Feeding: browsing and grazing

Goats browse shrubs and trees, often standing on their hind legs, and select the most nutritious leaves and pods; sheep graze grass close to the ground. Running goats with cattle or sheep uses the veld more fully, and goats help control bush encroachment. In the dry season give crop residues, hay, tree pods and a protein lick, and always provide clean water and salt. Do not let goats overbrowse: too many goats on a small area strip the bushes and expose the soil to erosion.

## Breeding

- Does and ewes are usually first mated at about 8-12 months, when well grown.
- The oestrous cycle is about **21 days in goats** and about **17 days in sheep**; gestation is about **150 days** in both.
- Allow about one buck to 25-30 does (a similar ratio for rams and ewes) and keep the male separate except in the mating season.
- **Flush** the females before mating, and use a mating season so that kids or lambs are born when feed is plentiful.
- Twins are common in goats, so the kidding percentage is often well above 100%.

**Kidding and lambing.** Signs are like those in the cow: a full udder, a swollen vulva, restlessness and separation from the flock. Most births need no help; give assistance only for a clear problem, with clean hands and lubricant. Make sure each kid or lamb suckles colostrum within the first hours, dip the navel in iodine and keep mother and young in a dry, sheltered pen for a few days. Wean at about **3-4 months**.

:::worked-solution[Flock performance for Nyasha's goats]
Nyasha mated 40 does. 36 kidded, giving 58 kids, of which 52 were weaned.

1. Does kidding = 36 ÷ 40 × 100 = **90%**.
2. Kidding percentage = 58 ÷ 40 × 100 = **145%** (many twins).
3. Weaning percentage = 52 ÷ 40 × 100 = **130%**.
4. Kid losses = 58 − 52 = 6; 6 ÷ 58 × 100 = **10.3%** of kids born.
5. Bucks needed at about one to 25 does: 40 ÷ 25 = 1.6, so she keeps **2** bucks.
:::

## Management practices

| Practice | How and when | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identification | Ear tags, ear notches or tattoos, soon after birth | Records of breeding, growth and treatment; ownership |
| Castration | Rubber ring in the first week, or a bloodless castrator | Docile males that cannot mate unwanted females; better meat |
| Hoof trimming | Every few months, or when overgrown | Prevents lameness; overgrown hooves trap dirt and favour foot rot |
| Dipping or spraying | Regularly in the tick season | Ticks spread heartwater (bont tick), to which goats and sheep are very susceptible |
| Dosing | Against worms at strategic times and when animals show signs | Wireworm sucks blood, causing anaemia (pale eye membranes), bottle jaw and death |
| Shearing and tail docking | Wool sheep only | Clean, valuable fleece; less soiling and fly strike |
| Culling | Old, barren or poor animals | Keeps flock productivity high |

Handle goats and sheep calmly; catch them by a back leg above the hock or under the jaw, never by the fleece or the tail.

:::worked-solution[Growth of a Boer cross kid]
A Boer cross kid weighed 3.5 kg at birth and 24.5 kg at 120 days.

1. Gain = 24.5 − 3.5 = 21 kg.
2. Average daily gain = 21 ÷ 120 = **0.175 kg/day** (175 g a day).
3. Comparing this with the local kids in the same flock shows the farmer whether the Boer buck is worth his cost.
:::

## Marketing

Goats and sheep are sold live at local markets, auctions and to traders, or slaughtered for butcheries and abattoirs. Demand and prices rise before the festive season and for ceremonies, so plan the breeding season to have well-grown animals ready then. Selling by mass on a scale gives a fairer price than selling by eye. Skins, mohair and wool are by-products that add income.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the cow's cycle for ewes"}
The ewe's oestrous cycle is about 17 days, not 21. The doe goat's is about 21 days.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking a kidding percentage above 100% is an error"}
It is calculated from young born per female mated, so twins and triplets push it above 100%.
:::

:::mistake{title="Housing goats on wet earth floors"}
Damp floors cause foot rot, pneumonia and worm build-up. Use a raised slatted floor.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Indigenous breeds (Matebele and Mashona goats, Sabi sheep) are hardy; exotic breeds (Boer, Kalahari Red, Saanen, Angora, Dorper, Merino) grow faster or give milk, mohair or wool.
- Goats browse, sheep graze; house goats on raised slatted floors, dry and secure.
- Cycles: doe about 21 days, ewe about 17 days; gestation about 150 days; flush before mating; twins are common in goats.
- Identify, castrate early, trim hooves, dip against ticks (heartwater), dose against worms, shear and dock wool sheep, cull poor animals.
- Kidding % = young born ÷ females mated × 100; sell when demand is high and by mass.
:::
