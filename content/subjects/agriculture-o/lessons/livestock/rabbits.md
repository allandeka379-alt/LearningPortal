## Introduction

Tatenda started with two does and a buck in hutches behind his home in Gweru. A year later he sells rabbits to a restaurant every week, uses the droppings on his vegetable garden and sells the dried pelts to a craft group. Rabbits suit small spaces in towns and rural homes: they breed quickly, eat forage that people cannot eat and produce lean, white meat.

ZIMSEC (Forms 3 and 4) lets schools study rabbits, layers or indigenous chickens. For rabbits you must identify breeds, choose a site and design a hutch, describe feeding and the management of the young, slaughter and dress rabbits, prepare pelts for market and keep financial and production records.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Buck, doe and kit"}
A buck is a male rabbit, a doe a female rabbit and a kit (or kitten) a young rabbit.
:::

:::definition{term="Kindling"}
The act of giving birth in rabbits.
:::

:::definition{term="Hutch"}
A raised cage in which rabbits are housed, usually of wood and wire mesh, with one adult per compartment.
:::

:::definition{term="Pelt"}
The skin of a rabbit with the fur still on it, removed at slaughter and dried for sale.
:::

:::definition{term="Caecotrophy"}
The habit of rabbits of eating the soft droppings they produce, mostly at night, straight from the anus; these droppings are rich in protein and B vitamins made by microbes in the caecum.
:::

## Breeds

| Breed | Main use | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New Zealand White | Meat | White fur, pink eyes, fast growth, good mothers |
| Californian | Meat | White body with dark nose, ears, feet and tail |
| Flemish Giant | Meat | Very large, slower to mature |
| Chinchilla | Meat and fur | Grey-blue fur valued for pelts |
| Angora | Wool | Long, fine hair that is clipped or plucked |
| Rex | Fur | Short, velvety fur |

Local crossbred rabbits are hardy but grow more slowly than the meat breeds.

## Housing

**Siting the hutches.** Choose a shaded, cool, well-drained place near the house for security, sheltered from strong wind and rain, away from dogs, noise and smoke, with space for cleaning and a water supply nearby.

**Building a hutch.** Raise it on legs about 60-90 cm off the ground, with a sloping roof that overhangs to keep out rain and sun. Use wire-mesh or slatted floors so droppings fall through, and wire-mesh fronts for ventilation. Each adult needs its own compartment, for example about 90 cm long, 60 cm wide and 45 cm high for a medium-sized doe (assume these dimensions). Fit a feeder that does not tip over, a water bottle or heavy drinker and a door that closes securely. Coat the legs or stand them in tins of water to stop ants, and make it rat- and snake-proof.

## Feeding

Rabbits are herbivores with a large **caecum**, in which microbes digest fibre. Feed:

- **Forage**: good hay, grass, lucerne, sweet potato vines and other leafy greens, preferably **wilted**, never wet or mouldy.
- **Concentrates**: rabbit pellets or a mixture of crushed grain and protein meal, especially for pregnant and nursing does and growing rabbits.
- **Clean water at all times**; a nursing doe drinks a great deal.

Rabbits feed mostly at night, so give the larger feed in the evening. Change the diet gradually: sudden changes and wet or mouldy greens cause bloat and diarrhoea.

## Breeding

1. Medium breeds are first mated at about 5-6 months (does) and 6-7 months (bucks). One buck can serve about ten does.
2. Always take the **doe to the buck's hutch**. A doe defends her own hutch and may attack the buck.
3. Mating is quick; the buck falls to one side afterwards. The doe is returned to her hutch. Rabbits do not have a regular heat cycle: mating itself causes ovulation (**induced ovulation**).
4. About 10-14 days later, check for pregnancy by gently feeling the abdomen (**palpation**) for small marble-sized embryos; re-mate if she is empty.
5. Gestation is about **31 days**. Put in a **nest box** with clean, dry grass a few days before kindling; the doe pulls fur from her body to line the nest.
6. Kindling usually takes place at night. Do not disturb the doe; next day, check the nest quietly and remove any dead kits.

:::worked-solution[Planning a doe's kindling]
Rudo mates a New Zealand White doe on 2 March 2026.

1. Palpate about 10-14 days later, around 12-16 March.
2. Put in the nest box about day 27: 2 March + 27 days = **29 March 2026**.
3. Expected kindling about day 31: 2 March + 31 days = **2 April 2026**.
4. Write the dates on the hutch card so that whoever feeds the rabbits knows what to expect.
:::

## Care of the young

Kits are born naked, blind and helpless. They open their eyes at about ten days and leave the nest at about three weeks, when they start eating solid feed. Wean them at about **6-8 weeks**, sex them and separate bucks from does before they mature. Rabbits are **handled** by lifting the loose skin over the shoulders while supporting the rump; never lift a rabbit by its ears. Identify breeding stock with ear tattoos and keep a **hutch card** for each doe (mating date, kindling date, kits born, kits weaned).

:::worked-solution[Production from six does]
Assume each doe in Tatenda's unit weans 7 kits a litter and has 5 litters a year.

1. Kits weaned per doe per year = 7 × 5 = **35**.
2. For six does: 35 × 6 = **210** young rabbits a year.
3. At slaughter, a rabbit weighing 2.4 kg live gives a 1.32 kg carcass: dressing percentage = 1.32 ÷ 2.4 × 100 = **55%**.
:::

## Health

| Problem | Signs | Prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coccidiosis | Diarrhoea, pot belly, poor growth, deaths of young | Clean, dry hutches; clean feed and water; medication on advice |
| Snuffles (a bacterial respiratory disease) | Sneezing, discharge from the nose, matted front paws | Good ventilation without draughts; isolate or cull sick rabbits |
| Ear canker (mites) | Crusts in the ears, head shaking | Clean ears, treat with an approved product |
| Sore hocks | Raw, sore patches on the hind feet | Smooth mesh or a resting board on wire floors |
| Mastitis | Hot, hard, swollen teats in a nursing doe | Clean nest boxes |

## Slaughter, dressing and pelts

Rabbits for meat are usually slaughtered at about 10-12 weeks, at about 2-2.5 kg live mass. Withdraw feed for a few hours, stun the rabbit humanely and quickly, then cut the neck blood vessels and hang it by a hind leg to bleed. Cut around the hocks, slit the skin down the inside of the hind legs and peel the skin off downwards in one piece like a sock (a **cased** pelt). Remove the head and feet, eviscerate, wash and chill the carcass. A rabbit typically dresses at about 50-60%.

**Preparing pelts:** scrape off fat and flesh, pull the pelt flesh-side out over a wire or board stretcher, dry it in the shade (never in hot sun) and store it dry, away from insects, until sold to tanners or craft makers.

**Marketing:** sell to restaurants, hotels, butcheries and households (rabbit meat is lean and high in protein), sell breeding stock and pets, and sell manure for gardens.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Taking the buck to the doe"}
The doe may attack a buck placed in her hutch. Always take the doe to the buck.
:::

:::mistake{title="Lifting rabbits by the ears"}
This hurts and can injure the rabbit. Lift by the loose skin over the shoulders and support the rump.
:::

:::mistake{title="Feeding wet or mouldy greens"}
Wet or mouldy forage causes bloat and diarrhoea. Wilt fresh greens and change diets slowly.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Design a hutch"}
For "describe a suitable hutch", give each feature with its reason: raised legs (dry, safe from predators), wire floor (droppings fall through), overhanging roof (rain and sun), mesh front (ventilation), separate compartments (does fight).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Breeding sequence"}
Give the steps in order with numbers: mating age, doe to buck, palpation at 10-14 days, nest box at about day 27, kindling at about day 31, weaning at 6-8 weeks.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Meat breeds: New Zealand White, Californian, Flemish Giant; fur and wool: Chinchilla, Rex, Angora.
- Hutches are raised, shaded, ventilated, predator-proof, with wire or slatted floors and one adult per compartment.
- Feed wilted forage, pellets and clean water; rabbits ferment fibre in the caecum and eat their soft night droppings.
- Take the doe to the buck; ovulation is induced by mating; palpate at 10-14 days; nest box at about day 27; kindling at about day 31; wean at 6-8 weeks.
- Slaughter at about 10-12 weeks; dressing about 50-60%; cased pelts are stretched flesh-side out and dried in the shade.
:::
