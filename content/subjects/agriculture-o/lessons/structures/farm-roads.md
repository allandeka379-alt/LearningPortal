## Introduction

A farm near Marondera may grow an excellent crop, but if the farm road turns to mud in January, the fertiliser lorry cannot get in and the tobacco or vegetables cannot get out. A badly sited road also becomes a channel for runoff: water races down the wheel tracks, cuts a gully and carries away soil from the fields beside it.

ZIMSEC asks you in Form 2 to discuss the factors in siting a farm road, list siting equipment and describe a well-sited road; in Form 3 to describe the features of a farm road and their dimensions and repair a road; and in Form 4 to identify construction materials, describe construction and maintain farm roads.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Farm road"}
A track or road on a farm that links the homestead, fields, buildings, water points and the public road, built to carry vehicles, carts and animals in all seasons.
:::

:::definition{term="Camber"}
The curved or sloping shape of a road surface, highest along the centre line (the crown), so that rainwater runs off to the sides.
:::

:::definition{term="Mitre drain"}
A short drain that leads water out of the side drain, at an angle away from the road, onto grassed land or into a waterway, so that water does not build up along the road.
:::

:::definition{term="Culvert"}
A pipe or covered channel under a road that carries water from one side to the other at a low point or stream crossing.
:::

:::definition{term="Gradient"}
The steepness of a road, given as the vertical rise divided by the horizontal distance, as a percentage or as "1 in n".
:::

## Siting a farm road

1. **Gradient**: keep it as gentle as possible. Steep roads are hard for loaded vehicles and carts, and water running down them causes erosion. Where a slope must be climbed, the road can run diagonally across it.
2. **Ridges and watersheds**: where possible, run the road along a ridge, where water drains away on both sides and few streams have to be crossed.
3. **Soil and drainage**: choose firm, well-drained soil. Avoid vleis (wetlands), waterlogged land and black clays that become slippery and impassable when wet.
4. **Water crossings**: avoid crossing streams and waterways; where a crossing is unavoidable, cross at right angles at a narrow point with firm banks.
5. **Links and distance**: connect the homestead, fields, barns, sheds, dip tank, water points and the public road by the shortest *practical* route.
6. **Fields**: run roads along field boundaries and contours rather than cutting fields into awkward shapes.
7. **Obstacles and cost**: avoid large rocks, ant heaps and big trees where possible, and consider the cost of earthworks and the materials available nearby.

**Siting equipment**: pegs and ranging rods to mark the line, a measuring tape, a line level or an A-frame to measure slopes and find contours, and a dumpy level with a staff for accurate gradients.

:::worked-solution[Gradient of a proposed road]
Nyasha's proposed road rises 6 m over a horizontal distance of 150 m.

1. Gradient = rise ÷ run = 6 ÷ 150 = 0.04.
2. As a percentage: 0.04 × 100 = **4%**.
3. As "1 in n": 150 ÷ 6 = 25, so the gradient is **1 in 25**, a gentle slope suitable for a farm road.
:::

## Features of a well-made farm road

Described across the road from the centre outwards, as a cross-section diagram would show them:

| Feature | Description | Typical dimension (example only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carriageway (road surface) | Firm, gravelled surface used by vehicles | About 3.5-4 m wide for a single lane, wider where vehicles pass |
| Crown and camber | Centre line is highest; the surface slopes down to each side | A fall of about 1 in 20 to 1 in 25 from the crown to the edge |
| Shoulders | Firm strips beside the carriageway | About 0.5-1 m on each side |
| Side (table) drains | Open drains along both sides that collect water from the road | About 0.5 m deep, with sloping sides |
| Mitre drains | Lead water out of the side drains onto grassed land | Spaced closer together on steeper roads |
| Culverts | Pipes under the road at low points and crossings | Large enough for storm flows; with headwalls at each end |
| Drifts (causeways) | Low concrete crossings over small streams that flow only after rain | Built at stream-bed level |

Dimensions vary with the traffic, the soil and the rainfall; they are examples, not fixed rules.

:::worked-solution[Camber, gravel and mitre drains]
Tendai is building a 400 m farm road with a carriageway 4 m wide. The crown is 8 cm higher than the edges. He spreads gravel 10 cm thick, carried in a truck that holds 8 m³, and places mitre drains every 40 m.

1. Each half of the road is 4 ÷ 2 = 2 m wide; the fall is 8 cm = 0.08 m.
2. Camber = 0.08 ÷ 2 = 0.04 = **4%**, or 2 ÷ 0.08 = **1 in 25**.
3. Gravel volume = 400 m × 4 m × 0.10 m = **160 m³**.
4. Truck loads = 160 ÷ 8 = **20 loads**.
5. Mitre drain positions = 400 ÷ 40 = **10** along the road.
:::

## Materials

- **Gravel** (natural gravel or crushed stone) for the surface, with some **clay** to bind it and **sand** for fine filling.
- **Stones** to line drains and protect mitre drain outlets from erosion.
- **Concrete** for drifts and culvert headwalls; **concrete or steel pipes** for culverts.
- **Grass** planted on banks, shoulders and drain sides to hold the soil.

## Constructing a farm road

1. **Survey and peg** the route with ranging rods, pegs and a level, checking the gradient.
2. **Clear** trees, bushes, stumps, rocks and ant heaps from the road reserve.
3. **Remove the topsoil** (keep it for fields or banks) down to firm subsoil.
4. **Form the road bed**: cut and fill to the planned level and shape the crown and camber with hand tools, a grader or a drag.
5. **Dig the side drains** and the **mitre drains**, and install **culverts** and **drifts** at low points and crossings.
6. **Lay the gravel** in layers, wet it and **compact** it with a roller or by traffic.
7. **Plant grass** on banks and drain edges, and line steep drains with stones.

:::example{title="Repairing a damaged road after the rains"}
After a wet season, the road to Rudo's tobacco barns near Karoi has deep ruts down the wheel tracks, potholes, and side drains full of silt; one culvert is blocked with grass, so water has crossed the road and cut a gully. Her workers clear the culvert and side drains, fill the gully and potholes with gravel in layers, wetting and ramming each layer, and reshape the camber so that water runs off the surface again. They add two more mitre drains on the steepest section and line their outlets with stones, and Rudo stops lorries using the road when it is soaked.
:::

## Repair and maintenance

1. **Clear drains and culverts** of silt, grass and debris before and during the rains.
2. **Reshape the camber** with a grader, drag or hand tools so that water does not stand on the surface.
3. **Fill potholes and ruts** with gravel and compact them.
4. **Repair eroded drains and gullies**; line badly eroded sections with stones.
5. **Cut grass and bush** on the verges so that drivers can see and drains stay open.
6. **Restrict heavy traffic** when the road is very wet, because wheels quickly cut ruts in soft surfaces.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Running a road straight down a slope"}
Water follows the wheel tracks and cuts a gully. Run roads along ridges or diagonally across the slope, with mitre drains.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing side drains and mitre drains"}
Side drains run along the road; mitre drains lead water away from the side drains at an angle onto grassed land.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Siting factors"}
Explain each factor: "avoid vleis, because waterlogged soil is soft and vehicles get stuck". A list of words earns fewer marks than reasons.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Calculations"}
Gradient and camber = rise ÷ distance (same units); "1 in n" = distance ÷ rise. Gravel volume = length × width × thickness in metres.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Site roads on gentle gradients, along ridges, on firm well-drained soil, avoiding vleis and stream crossings, linking all farm points along field boundaries.
- Siting equipment: pegs, ranging rods, tape, line level or A-frame, dumpy level and staff.
- A good road has a gravelled carriageway with a camber, shoulders, side drains, mitre drains, culverts and drifts.
- Build by surveying, clearing, removing topsoil, shaping the camber, digging drains, fitting culverts, laying and compacting gravel and grassing banks.
- Maintain by clearing drains and culverts, reshaping the camber, filling potholes and ruts, repairing erosion and limiting traffic in wet weather.
:::
