## Introduction

A dairy farm near Marondera may need tens of thousands of litres of water every day: drinking water for the cows, water for washing the milking parlour, water for the family and water for the irrigated pasture. All of it has to be found, stored, cleaned where necessary and piped to where it is used. A broken tap or a cracked tank can waste a day's supply.

The Cambridge syllabus asks you to *list* sources of water for people, livestock and irrigation, *outline* treatment by settling and filtration, *outline* how storage dams are built to resist water pressure, and *describe* storage tanks, pipe distribution and simple plumbing such as joining pipes and fitting tap washers.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Sedimentation (settling)"}
Letting water stand still so that suspended particles such as silt sink to the bottom.
:::

:::definition{term="Filtration"}
Passing water through a porous material, such as layers of sand and gravel, to remove suspended particles and some micro-organisms.
:::

:::definition{term="Water pressure"}
The force exerted by water on each unit of area; in still water it increases with depth.
:::

:::definition{term="Head"}
The height of a water surface above the point where it is used; a greater head gives a greater pressure at the outlet.
:::

## Sources of water

| Use | Common sources |
| --- | --- |
| People (drinking, cooking, washing) | Boreholes, protected wells and springs, roof rainwater stored in tanks, piped municipal supply |
| Livestock | Rivers, dams, boreholes with troughs, wells, springs |
| Irrigation | Rivers, dams, boreholes, large tanks and reservoirs |

Water for **people** must be the cleanest: groundwater from a borehole or protected spring is usually safer than open surface water. **Livestock** need clean water too; animals drinking straight from muddy dam edges trample the banks and foul the water, so water is often piped to **troughs**.

## Treating water

1. **Screening**: a mesh over the intake keeps out leaves, fish and floating rubbish.
2. **Settling (sedimentation)**: water stands in a tank or pond so silt sinks. A **coagulant** such as alum may be added to make fine particles clump together and settle faster.
3. **Filtration**: in a **slow sand filter**, water trickles down through a layer of fine sand on top, then coarse sand, then gravel, to an outlet pipe at the bottom. Particles are trapped, and a thin living layer that forms on the sand surface feeds on many micro-organisms. When the flow slows, the top few centimetres of sand are scraped off and washed.
4. **Disinfection**: for drinking water, filtered water is chlorinated or boiled to kill remaining pathogens.

## Storage dams

The deeper the water, the greater its **pressure** on the dam wall. At twice the depth the pressure is twice as great. A dam wall must therefore be **thickest at its base**.

Steps in building a small **earth dam**:

1. **Choose the site**: a narrow part of a valley with a large catchment above it, a wide basin behind it, and an impermeable clay or rock base so water does not seep away.
2. **Dig a cut-off trench** along the line of the wall down to impermeable material, and fill it with compacted clay to stop water seeping under the wall.
3. **Build the wall** in thin layers, each moistened and **compacted**, with a **clay core** in the centre to make it watertight. The wall is much **wider at the base** than at the top, with gentle side slopes.
4. **Protect the faces**: stones (stone pitching) on the upstream face resist wave action; grass on the downstream face prevents erosion.
5. **Build a spillway** round one end of the wall, cut in firm ground, to carry flood water safely past the wall. If flood water flows over an earth wall, it can wash it away.
6. Leave **freeboard** (extra height above the full water level) and fit an outlet pipe.

Concrete and masonry dam walls follow the same rule: they are thicker at the bottom where the pressure is greatest.

## Storage tanks

Tanks are made of ferrocement, plastered brick or concrete blocks, reinforced concrete, galvanised steel or plastic. A good tank has:

- a **cover** or roof, to keep out dirt, animals and mosquitoes and reduce evaporation and algae;
- a screened **inlet**, and for roof water a **first-flush** device that discards the dirtiest first rain;
- an **overflow** pipe, so excess water does not damage the tank or its base;
- an **outlet tap** a little **above the floor**, so the sediment that settles is not drawn off;
- a **drain (scour) valve** at the very bottom, for cleaning;
- a firm base, and for gravity supply a **raised stand**, giving head.

:::worked-solution[How much does a tank hold, and for how long?]
A cylindrical tank has an inside radius of $1.5\ \text{m}$ and a water depth of $2\ \text{m}$. Assume each of 30 cows drinks about $50\ \text{L}$ a day.

1. Volume $= \pi r^2 h = \pi \times 1.5^2 \times 2 = 14.137\ \text{m}^3$ (to three decimal places).
2. In litres: $14.137 \times 1\,000 = 14\,137\ \text{L}$ (to the nearest litre; $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1\,000\ \text{L}$).
3. Daily demand $= 30 \times 50 = 1\,500\ \text{L}$.
4. A full tank lasts $14\,137 \div 1\,500 = 9.4$ days (to one decimal place).
:::

## Distributing water

Water is piped from the source or tank to troughs, taps and fields. If the tank is **higher** than the outlets, water flows by **gravity**; the higher the tank, the greater the pressure at the tap. Otherwise a pump is needed. Main pipes are larger; branches to troughs are smaller; trenches protect buried pipes from sun, animals and machinery. **Trough floats** (ball valves) shut off the supply when a trough is full.

### Joining plastic pipe

- **PVC pipe (solvent welding)**: cut the pipe square, remove burrs, clean and roughen both surfaces, apply solvent cement to the pipe end and the inside of the fitting, push together with a slight twist, and hold for a short time. Leave the joint to set before putting water through.
- **Polyethylene pipe**: push the pipe into a **compression fitting** and tighten the nut, or push it over a barbed **insert fitting** and tighten a clip.
- **Threaded steel pipe and fittings**: wrap thread seal (PTFE) tape round the thread in the direction of tightening and screw the parts together.

### Fitting a tap washer

A dripping tap usually has a worn **washer**.

1. Turn off the water supply to the tap (at the stop valve).
2. Open the tap fully to let the remaining water drain.
3. Unscrew the **headgear** (the top part of the tap) with a spanner, protecting the finish with a cloth.
4. Remove the worn washer from the **jumper** (the small plate at the bottom of the spindle); some washers are held by a small nut.
5. Fit a new washer of the correct size.
6. Reassemble the tap, turn the supply on slowly and check that the tap no longer drips.

:::example{title="Planning a supply for a poultry unit"}
Chipo's poultry unit is 200 m from her borehole. She pumps borehole water into a covered tank on a raised stand beside the house, with an overflow and a tap above the floor. From the tank a buried polyethylene pipe runs by gravity to drinkers in the houses, where float valves keep the water level steady. Because the water is from a borehole and stored covered, it stays clean.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Building a dam wall the same thickness top to bottom"}
Pressure increases with depth, so the base must be much thicker than the top.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting the tank outlet at the very bottom"}
The outlet should be a little above the floor so settled sediment is not drawn out; a separate drain valve at the bottom is used for cleaning.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Outline and describe"}
For an outline of dam construction, list the main stages in order with a reason for each: cut-off trench (stops seepage), compacted clay core (watertight), wide base (resists pressure), spillway (protects the wall).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Plumbing steps"}
Number the steps and begin with safety: turn the water off before you open a tap or cut a pipe.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Sources: boreholes, wells, springs, rivers, dams, roof rainwater and piped supplies; people need the cleanest water.
- Treat water by screening, settling (with a coagulant if needed) and slow sand filtration, then disinfect drinking water.
- Water pressure increases with depth, so dam walls are thickest at the base; earth dams need a cut-off trench, clay core, compaction, protected faces and a spillway.
- Tanks need a cover, screened inlet, overflow, outlet above the floor and a drain; raised tanks give gravity pressure.
- Join PVC with solvent cement and polyethylene with compression or insert fittings; replace tap washers with the supply turned off.
:::
