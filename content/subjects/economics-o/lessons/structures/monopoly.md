## Introduction

If you want electricity from the national grid in most countries, you can buy it from only one supplier. A town may have a single water utility, one railway operator or one company licensed to supply a particular service. When a market has only one seller, consumers cannot shop around: the seller can choose its price, and if they want the product, they must pay it. This is **monopoly**, the opposite extreme from a competitive market.

Monopoly matters because it can lead to higher prices, less choice and lower output than in a competitive market, which is why governments watch and regulate monopolies. Both boards ask you to explain the characteristics of monopoly, its advantages and disadvantages, and the effect of one firm on **price, quality, choice and profit**. Monopoly also appears as a cause of **market failure**. Cambridge 0455 does not require diagrams for this topic.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Monopoly"}
A market structure in which there is a single seller of a product with no close substitutes. In practice, a firm with a very large share of a market (a dominant firm) is often described as having monopoly power.
:::

:::definition{term="Barriers to entry"}
Obstacles that prevent new firms from entering a market and competing with the existing firm.
:::

:::definition{term="Price maker"}
A firm that can set the price of its product, because it faces no close competitors. It cannot, however, control both price and the quantity consumers buy: a higher price means fewer sales.
:::

:::definition{term="Natural monopoly"}
An industry in which one firm can supply the whole market at a lower average cost than two or more firms could, because of very large economies of scale (for example, water pipes or an electricity grid).
:::

## Characteristics of monopoly

- **One seller** (or one dominant seller) supplies the whole market.
- **No close substitutes** for the product, so consumers cannot easily switch.
- **High barriers to entry** keep other firms out, even when the monopoly earns high profits.
- The firm is a **price maker**: it chooses its price, but it faces the downward-sloping market demand curve.
- **Abnormal profits can last in the long run**, because new firms cannot enter.

## Barriers to entry

- **Legal barriers:** the government grants the sole right to supply (a licence or franchise), or a **patent** or copyright protects an invention for a number of years.
- **Economies of scale:** the existing firm is so large that its average costs are far below what a new entrant could achieve; in a natural monopoly a second set of pipes or cables would make no sense.
- **High start-up costs:** building a power station, railway or mobile network needs huge capital.
- **Control of raw materials or outlets:** the firm owns the only source of a raw material or the shops through which the product is sold.
- **Brand loyalty and heavy advertising** that new firms cannot match.
- **Anti-competitive practices:** cutting prices below cost when a rival appears, or refusing to supply retailers who stock a rival's product.

## The effect of one firm on price, quality, choice and profit

- **Price:** usually **higher** than in a competitive market. The monopoly can restrict output to push the price up, because consumers have nowhere else to go.
- **Quality:** may be **lower**, because there is no competitor to lose customers to. On the other hand, a monopoly's large profits can pay for research, which may improve quality over time.
- **Choice:** **less** choice for consumers: one supplier, often one type of product.
- **Profit:** **abnormal profits** can be earned in the long run, because barriers stop new firms from entering and competing them away.

:::worked-solution[Worked example 1: why a monopoly restricts output]
A fictional company is the only supplier of cement in a small country. Its cost per tonne, including a normal return, is US\$110 at every output. The table shows how much cement buyers would demand at different prices.

| Price (US\$ per tonne) | Quantity demanded (tonnes per month) | Total revenue (US\$) | Total cost at US\$110 a tonne (US\$) | Abnormal profit (US\$) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 110 | 14,000 | 1,540,000 | 1,540,000 | 0 |
| 120 | 13,000 | 1,560,000 | 1,430,000 | 130,000 |
| 130 | 12,000 | 1,560,000 | 1,320,000 | 240,000 |
| 140 | 10,500 | 1,470,000 | 1,155,000 | 315,000 |
| 150 | 9,000 | 1,350,000 | 990,000 | 360,000 |
| 160 | 7,000 | 1,120,000 | 770,000 | 350,000 |
| 170 | 5,000 | 850,000 | 550,000 | 300,000 |

1. Abnormal profit $= (\text{price} - 110) \times \text{quantity}$. At US\$150: $(150 - 110) \times 9{,}000 = 360{,}000$.
2. Profit is greatest (US\$360,000) at a price of **US\$150**, selling 9,000 tonnes.
3. If there were many competing suppliers, competition would push the price down to the cost of US\$110 (normal profit only), and 14,000 tonnes would be bought.
4. So the monopoly charges US\$40 a tonne more and sells 5,000 tonnes a month less than a competitive market would. Builders pay more, and some building projects do not go ahead at all.
:::

## Advantages of monopoly

- **Economies of scale:** one large firm may produce at a lower average cost than many small firms, which could allow lower prices (especially in a natural monopoly).
- **Research and development:** abnormal profits can finance new products and better methods; patents reward inventors.
- **No wasteful duplication** of expensive infrastructure, such as two sets of railway lines.
- **Stability:** a secure firm can plan long-term investment and provide stable employment.
- A state-owned or regulated monopoly can **cross-subsidise** services, for example supplying remote areas at the same price as towns.

## Disadvantages of monopoly

- **Higher prices and lower output** than in a competitive market (restricted supply).
- **Less choice** for consumers.
- **Little incentive to be efficient**: without competition, costs may rise and service may be poor.
- **Exploitation** of consumers and of suppliers, who may have only one buyer.
- **Income is transferred** from consumers to the monopoly's owners.
- **Allocative inefficiency:** price is above the cost of producing the last unit, so fewer resources go into the product than consumers want.

## Monopoly as a cause of market failure

**Market failure** occurs when the free market does not allocate resources efficiently. A monopoly restricts supply to raise its price, so the quantity produced is **less** than consumers would buy at a price that just covers costs. Too few resources are devoted to the product, and consumers pay more than in a competitive market. This is why governments use **competition policy** (investigating mergers and banning anti-competitive practices), **regulation** of prices and quality, **public ownership** of some natural monopolies, and policies that **remove barriers to entry**.

:::example{title="A natural monopoly"}
A fictional town's piped water network cost many millions of dollars to build. A second company laying its own pipes beside the first would double the cost of the network without adding much water, so one supplier can serve the town most cheaply: a natural monopoly. Because households cannot switch supplier, the government either runs the water utility itself or regulates the prices and service standards of a private one.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying a monopoly can charge any price it likes"}
A monopoly sets its price, but consumers then decide how much to buy. A higher price means fewer sales, so the monopoly cannot choose both a high price and a high quantity.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying monopoly is always bad"}
Economies of scale, research funded by profits and the avoidance of duplication can benefit consumers. Discuss both sides.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting barriers to entry"}
Abnormal profits last only because barriers keep new firms out. Always explain the barrier.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**Identify** barriers to entry from the case in the question, and **explain** how each keeps rivals out.
:::

:::exam-tip
**Analyse** the effect of a monopoly on consumers through price, quality, choice and profit, one at a time.
:::

:::exam-tip
**Discuss** whether a monopoly should be broken up or regulated: weigh higher prices and less choice against economies of scale and research, and consider the type of industry (a natural monopoly is best regulated, not broken up).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Monopoly: one seller, no close substitutes, high barriers to entry, a price maker that can keep abnormal profits in the long run.
- Barriers: legal rights and patents, economies of scale, high start-up costs, control of raw materials or outlets, brand loyalty, anti-competitive practices.
- Compared with competition: higher price, possibly lower quality, less choice, abnormal profit.
- Advantages: economies of scale, research, no duplication. Disadvantages: higher prices, restricted output, inefficiency, exploitation.
- Monopoly is a cause of market failure because it restricts supply and raises price.
:::
