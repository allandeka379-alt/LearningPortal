## Introduction

At Mbare Musika in Harare, dozens of farmers and traders sell tomatoes side by side. If one trader asks much more than the others, customers simply walk to the next stall. If tomatoes are selling at a high price and traders are clearly doing well, more farmers bring tomatoes the following week. No single seller controls the price. This is a **competitive market**.

The number of firms in a market, and how easily new firms can join, is called the **market structure**. It affects the prices consumers pay, the quality and choice of goods, and the profits firms earn. Both boards ask you to explain the characteristics, advantages and disadvantages of competitive markets and the effect of many firms on **price, quality, choice and profit**. ZIMSEC (Form 3) also asks for the features of **perfect competition**, an extreme form of competitive market. Cambridge 0455 does not require diagrams or the theory of perfect competition for this topic.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Market structure"}
The characteristics of a market that affect how firms behave: the number and size of firms, how similar their products are and how easily firms can enter or leave.
:::

:::definition{term="Competitive market"}
A market with many buyers and many sellers, none of which can control the market price, selling similar products, with easy entry and exit.
:::

:::definition{term="Barriers to entry"}
Obstacles that make it difficult or impossible for new firms to enter a market, such as high start-up costs, licences or patents.
:::

:::definition{term="Price taker"}
A firm that must accept the market price, because it is too small to influence it.
:::

## Characteristics of competitive markets

- **Many buyers and sellers**, each small compared with the whole market.
- **Similar (close substitute) products**, so consumers can easily switch.
- **Easy entry and exit**: low barriers, so new firms join when profits are high and leave when there are losses.
- **Good information**: buyers know the prices charged by different sellers.
- As a result, no single firm can set a price much above its rivals'.

## The effect of many firms on price, quality, choice and profit

- **Price:** competition keeps prices low and close to the cost of production. A firm that charges more loses customers to rivals.
- **Quality:** firms try to attract customers with better quality, service and new ideas; poor-quality firms lose sales.
- **Choice:** many sellers usually means a wide choice of products, places to buy and prices.
- **Profit:** in the short run some firms can earn high profits, but high profits attract new firms, which increases supply and pushes the price down, so profits are low (normal) in the long run.

:::worked-solution[Worked example 1: how new firms reduce price and profit]
A fictional tomato market in a town. When existing growers are earning high profits at a price of US\$1.20 per kg, new growers enter, increasing the quantity supplied at every price.

| Price (US\$ per kg) | Quantity demanded (kg per day) | Quantity supplied before entry (kg per day) | Quantity supplied after entry (kg per day) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.90 | 7,000 | 3,400 | 5,800 |
| 1.00 | 6,400 | 4,000 | 6,400 |
| 1.10 | 5,800 | 4,600 | 7,000 |
| 1.20 | 5,200 | 5,200 | 7,600 |
| 1.30 | 4,600 | 5,800 | 8,200 |

1. Before entry, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (5,200 kg) at **US\$1.20**: the market price.
2. After entry, they are equal (6,400 kg) at **US\$1.00**: the price falls by 20 US cents.
3. Consumers gain: a lower price and a larger quantity bought (6,400 kg instead of 5,200 kg).
4. If a typical grower's cost per kg (including a normal return) is US\$1.00, the abnormal profit has now been competed away, so entry stops.
:::

## Advantages of competitive markets

- Low prices and value for money for consumers.
- Firms have a strong incentive to be efficient and keep costs down, otherwise they go out of business.
- Resources move to the goods consumers want: profits attract firms, losses drive them out.
- A wide choice, and firms respond quickly to changes in consumers' tastes.
- Pressure to improve quality and service.

## Disadvantages of competitive markets

- Small firms cannot gain large **economies of scale**, so their costs may be higher than a large firm's.
- Low profits leave little money for **research and development** or new investment.
- **Duplication** of facilities (many small stalls, delivery vans and shops) may waste resources.
- Firms may struggle to survive a fall in demand, so business failure and job losses are common.
- Competition may lead firms to cut corners on safety, wages or the environment to keep costs down.

## Perfect competition (ZIMSEC)

Perfect competition is a theoretical market structure with these features:

1. **A very large number of small buyers and sellers**: no one can influence the market price.
2. **Homogeneous (identical) products**: consumers see no difference between the output of different firms; there is no branding.
3. **Perfect knowledge**: all buyers and sellers know the prices and products of everyone in the market.
4. **Freedom of entry and exit**: no barriers.
5. **Perfect mobility of factors of production** between uses and places.
6. **No transport costs** and no government interference.

Because of these features each firm is a **price taker**: the market price is set by the demand and supply of the whole market, and the firm can sell any quantity it wants at that price. If it charged even slightly more, buyers with perfect knowledge would buy identical goods from someone else; it has no reason to charge less. The firm's demand curve is therefore **perfectly elastic** (horizontal at the market price), and **price = AR = MR**.

:::example{title="A price-taking farmer"}
Sipho is one of hundreds of fictional maize growers who sell to buyers at a market price of US\$0.80 per kg. Whether he sells 1,000 kg or 2,500 kg, he receives US\$0.80 per kg, so his revenue from 2,500 kg is $0.80 \times 2{,}500 = 2{,}000$, or US\$2,000. If he asked US\$0.85, buyers would go to other growers selling identical maize. Each extra kilogram adds US\$0.80 to his revenue, so his marginal revenue equals the price.
:::

In practice no market meets every condition. Markets for agricultural products such as maize, vegetables and eggs, foreign currency trading and some financial markets come close. Perfect competition is useful as a **benchmark** for judging other market structures: in the long run it gives only normal profit, the lowest possible average cost (productive efficiency) and price equal to marginal cost (allocative efficiency).

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying firms in competitive markets make no profit"}
They earn **normal** profit in the long run, which is enough to keep them in the industry. In the short run some can earn high profits.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating every market with several firms as perfectly competitive"}
Perfect competition needs identical products, perfect knowledge and free entry. A market of branded soft drinks is competitive but not perfectly competitive.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the role of entry"}
The key reason profits fall in competitive markets is that new firms enter and increase supply. Always explain this step.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**Explain** the effect of many firms on price, quality, choice and profit one by one, each with a reason.
:::

:::exam-tip
**Describe** the features of perfect competition as a list and explain why they make the firm a price taker (ZIMSEC).
:::

:::exam-tip
**Discuss** whether competition benefits consumers: lower prices, better quality and choice against small firms' higher costs, little research and possible cutting of corners, then conclude.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Competitive markets have many buyers and sellers of similar products, easy entry and exit and good information.
- Many firms keep prices low, push up quality, widen choice and keep profits normal in the long run.
- Advantages: low prices, efficiency, choice, response to consumers. Disadvantages: no large economies of scale, little research, duplication.
- Perfect competition (ZIMSEC): very many small firms, identical products, perfect knowledge, free entry and exit; each firm is a price taker with price = AR = MR.
:::
