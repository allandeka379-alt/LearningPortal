## Introduction

Chipo runs a small tailoring business in Gweru. Her customers pay by bank transfer and mobile money, she keeps her takings in a current account, and last year she borrowed from her bank to buy two industrial sewing machines. Behind her bank stands the **Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ)**, the country's central bank, which issues the national currency, supervises banks and sets monetary policy.

Banks matter to every household and firm: they keep money safe, make payments possible, and channel savings into loans that finance investment. Both syllabuses ask you to explain the role and importance of **commercial banks** and of the **central bank**; ZIMSEC also asks about **merchant banks** and **building societies**. You should already know what money is and what forms it takes.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Commercial bank"}
A profit-seeking bank that accepts deposits from households and firms, makes loans to them, and provides payment and other financial services. Also called a retail bank.
:::

:::definition{term="Central bank"}
The bank that manages a country's currency and money supply, acts as banker to the government and to the commercial banks, and carries out monetary policy. In Zimbabwe, the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe.
:::

:::definition{term="Merchant bank"}
A bank that serves firms rather than the general public: it arranges finance for businesses, advises on and underwrites new share issues, and advises on mergers and takeovers.
:::

:::definition{term="Building society"}
A financial institution that accepts savings from the public and lends mainly to people buying or building houses (mortgages).
:::

:::definition{term="Lender of last resort"}
The central bank's role of lending to commercial banks that are short of cash when no one else will, to prevent a bank from failing and depositors from panicking.
:::

## Functions of commercial banks

1. **Accepting deposits.** Banks offer current (demand) accounts for everyday payments and savings and fixed-deposit accounts that earn interest. Money is kept safe from theft and fire.
2. **Lending.** Banks lend to households (personal loans, mortgages, overdrafts) and firms (working capital, loans for machinery and buildings). They charge a higher rate of interest on loans than they pay on deposits; the difference is their main source of profit.
3. **Making payments.** Banks transfer money between accounts by bank transfer, debit and credit cards, cheques and online banking, and link to mobile-money platforms.
4. **Other services.** Foreign exchange, safe custody of valuables and documents, financial advice, and insurance through partners.

:::worked-solution[Worked example 1: how a bank earns its income]
A fictional commercial bank holds deposits of US\$2,400,000, on which it pays 4% interest a year. It lends US\$1,800,000 at 13% a year.

1. Interest paid to depositors = $2{,}400{,}000 \times 0.04 = 96{,}000$, that is US\$96,000.
2. Interest received from borrowers = $1{,}800{,}000 \times 0.13 = 234{,}000$, that is US\$234,000.
3. Net interest income = $234{,}000 - 96{,}000 = 138{,}000$, that is US\$138,000, out of which it pays wages, rent and other costs.
4. The bank has not lent all its deposits: it keeps US\$600,000 as cash and liquid reserves, so it can pay depositors who withdraw money.
:::

### Liquidity versus profitability

A bank faces a conflict. Loans earn interest (**profitability**), but cash earns nothing. Yet the bank must always be able to pay depositors who ask for their money (**liquidity**). If it lends too much, it may run out of cash and depositors may panic and rush to withdraw (a "run on the bank"). If it lends too little, it earns little profit. Banks therefore hold a mix of cash, short-term assets and loans.

## Credit creation (in outline)

When a bank makes a loan, the borrower spends it and the money is paid into another bank account. That bank can then lend most of the new deposit again. In this way, banks as a group create new deposits, and deposits are money. The total is limited by the share of deposits banks keep as reserves (the **reserve ratio**).

:::worked-solution[Worked example 2: credit creation with a 20% reserve ratio]
Tatenda deposits US\$5,000 of new cash. Every bank keeps 20% of new deposits as reserves and lends the rest.

| Bank | New deposit (US\$) | Kept as reserves, 20% (US\$) | Lent out (US\$) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bank A | 5,000 | 1,000 | 4,000 |
| Bank B | 4,000 | 800 | 3,200 |
| Bank C | 3,200 | 640 | 2,560 |
| Bank D | 2,560 | 512 | 2,048 |

1. After four banks, total deposits are $5{,}000 + 4{,}000 + 3{,}200 + 2{,}560 = 14{,}760$ dollars.
2. The process continues, each round smaller. The maximum total deposits = initial deposit ÷ reserve ratio = $5{,}000 \div 0.2 = 25{,}000$, that is US\$25,000.
3. The credit multiplier is $1 \div 0.2 = 5$.
4. New loans created at most = $25{,}000 - 5{,}000 = 20{,}000$ dollars. In reality, less is created, because people hold some cash and banks may not find enough safe borrowers.
:::

## Importance of commercial banks

- They channel **savings into investment**, which raises output and economic growth.
- They make **payments** quick and safe, supporting trade.
- Lending to small firms and farmers supports **enterprise and jobs**.
- They help people **manage income** over time (saving, borrowing for houses).

## Other financial institutions (ZIMSEC)

**Merchant banks** deal mainly with firms: they arrange large loans, accept bills of exchange, underwrite share issues (promise to buy unsold shares), and advise on mergers and investments. **Building societies** collect savings and lend for housing, which is important for home ownership. Other institutions include insurance companies, pension funds and microfinance institutions, which lend small sums to people without access to ordinary banks.

## Functions of the central bank

The Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe illustrates the usual functions:

1. **Issuing the currency.** It has the sole right to issue the national currency (notes and coins), which helps control the money supply and maintain confidence.
2. **Banker to the government.** It holds the government's accounts, handles its payments and receipts, and manages government borrowing.
3. **Banker to the commercial banks.** Banks keep accounts at the central bank, which are used to settle payments between banks.
4. **Lender of last resort.** It lends to banks in temporary difficulty, preventing panics.
5. **Monetary policy.** It sets the central bank's interest rate (the bank rate or policy rate) and uses other tools to influence the money supply and credit, with the aim of keeping inflation low and stable.
6. **Managing foreign exchange.** It holds the country's foreign currency and gold reserves and manages the exchange rate.
7. **Supervising and regulating banks.** It licenses banks, sets rules on reserves and capital, and inspects banks to protect depositors.

:::example[Example 3: why a lender of last resort matters]
Suppose a rumour spreads that a fictional bank in Masvingo has made bad loans. Depositors queue to withdraw their savings. Even a sound bank cannot repay all its deposits at once, because most of the money has been lent out. If the bank fails, depositors elsewhere may panic too. The central bank lends the bank cash against its assets, the bank pays everyone who asks, and the panic dies down. The central bank's supervision is meant to stop banks making risky loans in the first place.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying commercial banks issue notes"}
Only the **central bank** issues the national currency. Commercial banks create money in the form of **deposits** when they lend.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying banks lend all their deposits"}
Banks keep part of their deposits as reserves for withdrawals. This is what limits credit creation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing functions without explaining importance"}
"Importance" questions want the effect on the economy: savings financing investment, safe payments supporting trade, lender of last resort preventing panics.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**State:** one function per line using the standard terms (accept deposits, lend, payments; issue currency, banker to government, lender of last resort...).
:::

:::exam-tip
**Explain:** add what the function does and why it matters: "As lender of last resort the central bank lends to banks short of cash, which stops a run on one bank spreading to others."
:::

:::exam-tip
**Calculate:** for credit creation, maximum deposits = initial deposit ÷ reserve ratio (as a decimal). For a bank's income, interest = amount × rate.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Commercial banks accept deposits, lend, make payments and offer other services; they profit from the gap between loan and deposit interest.
- Banks balance liquidity against profitability.
- By lending, banks create deposits; maximum deposits = initial deposit ÷ reserve ratio.
- Merchant banks serve firms; building societies lend for housing.
- The central bank (the RBZ) issues currency, banks for the government and banks, is lender of last resort, runs monetary policy, manages reserves and the exchange rate, and supervises banks.
:::
