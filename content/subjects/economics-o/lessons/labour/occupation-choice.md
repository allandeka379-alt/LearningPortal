## Introduction

When Tendai finishes school he may train as a motor mechanic, apply to a bank, join his uncle's tobacco farm, or start selling airtime and phone accessories in Mbare. Each choice offers a different wage, but also different hours, risks, training, security and status. Economists study how people make this choice because it decides how many workers each occupation attracts, and so it shapes the supply of labour and wages.

ZIMSEC asks you to explain labour and its reward, describe types of workers and list what employees want from employment. Both ZIMSEC and Cambridge ask you to explain the **wage and non-wage factors** that affect a person's choice of occupation. Questions often give you two jobs and ask which a person should choose, or why someone accepts a lower wage.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Labour"}
Human effort, physical and mental, used in producing goods and services. Its reward is the **wage** (or salary).
:::

:::definition{term="Wage factors"}
The money rewards of a job: the basic wage or salary, overtime pay, bonuses, commission and the chance of pay rises over time.
:::

:::definition{term="Non-wage factors"}
Everything else about a job that makes it more or less attractive: working conditions, hours, holidays, job security, fringe benefits, training and promotion chances, status, location and job satisfaction.
:::

:::definition{term="Fringe benefits"}
Rewards given to employees in addition to the wage, such as free or subsidised housing, a company car, transport, medical aid, pension contributions and staff discounts.
:::

## Types of workers

ZIMSEC groups workers by the skill a job needs:

- **Unskilled workers** do simple tasks that need no formal training and can be learned in a short time on the job: general hands on farms, cleaners, loaders at a depot.
- **Semi-skilled workers** have some training or experience and can operate equipment or follow set procedures: machine operators, drivers, shop assistants, security guards.
- **Skilled workers** have long training and recognised qualifications: doctors, engineers, accountants, electricians, teachers.

As a rule, the more skill and training a job needs, the fewer people can do it, so supply is smaller and the wage tends to be higher. Skills also make workers more productive, which raises the demand for them.

## What employees want from a job

Employees' interests (what workers look for and what trade unions bargain for) include:

- **Fair wages** that rise with the cost of living and with experience.
- **Job security**: a permanent contract and protection from unfair dismissal.
- **Health and safety**: protective clothing, safe machines, reasonable hours, compensation if injured.
- **Fringe benefits**: housing, transport, medical aid, pensions, paid leave.
- **Training and promotion** so they can earn more in future.

## Choosing an occupation: wage factors

Most people compare **gross pay**, but sensible comparisons look at the whole package:

- **Basic wage or salary**, and how often it is paid.
- **Overtime** (often at a higher rate, such as time and a half), **bonuses** and **commission** on sales.
- **Future earnings**: a trainee lawyer may earn little now but a great deal later.

:::worked-solution[Worked example 1: comparing two job packages]
Nyasha has two job offers. All figures are monthly.

| Item (per month) | Job A: bank clerk, Harare (US\$) | Job B: teacher, rural school (US\$) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Basic wage | 620 | 540 |
| Free housing (value) | 0 | 150 |
| Transport allowance | 60 | 0 |
| Medical aid paid by employer | 45 | 0 |
| **Total package** | **725** | **690** |

1. Basic wage alone: Job A pays US\$80 a month more than Job B ($620 - 540$).
2. Add the value of fringe benefits: Job A totals $620 + 60 + 45 = 725$; Job B totals $540 + 150 = 690$.
3. The gap in money terms shrinks to $725 - 690 = 35$, that is US\$35 a month, or $35 \times 12 = 420$, that is US\$420 a year.
4. Non-wage factors may now decide: rents in Harare are higher, but Job A may offer quicker promotion; Job B offers long school holidays and a strong sense of purpose.
:::

:::worked-solution[Worked example 2: overtime raises weekly pay]
Farai works in a fictional factory in Bulawayo. His basic rate is US\$4 an hour for 45 hours a week, and overtime is paid at time and a half.

1. Basic pay: $45 \times 4 = 180$ dollars.
2. Overtime rate: $4 \times 1.5 = 6$ dollars an hour.
3. Six hours of overtime: $6 \times 6 = 36$ dollars.
4. Weekly pay: $180 + 36 = 216$, that is US\$216. Jobs with regular overtime can pay much more than the basic rate suggests, but at the cost of long hours.
:::

## Choosing an occupation: non-wage factors

People often accept a lower wage for a job that is better in other ways. Important non-wage factors are:

- **Working conditions**: safety, cleanliness, physical effort. Mining and construction must pay more to attract workers because they are dangerous.
- **Hours and holidays**: regular daytime hours and long holidays are attractive.
- **Job security**: civil service jobs are often chosen for security and a pension even when private firms pay more.
- **Training and promotion prospects**: a job with training raises future income.
- **Status (prestige) and job satisfaction**: many people choose nursing or teaching because the work is respected and rewarding.
- **Location**: being near family, or the cost of living and transport in a city, matters.
- **Fringe benefits**: as in Worked example 1, these can be worth a large share of pay.

:::example[Why Rudo chose the lower-paid job]
Rudo is offered US\$700 a month at a gold mine near Kwekwe or US\$560 a month as a laboratory technician at a hospital in Gweru, where her family lives. She chooses the hospital: the work is safer, it has regular hours, a pension and training towards a higher qualification, and she saves on rent by living at home. The difference of US\$140 a month is the price she pays for these non-wage advantages, and many workers make similar choices.
:::

The choice also depends on the person: their qualifications and ability, their age and family commitments, and how much risk they are willing to take.

:::keyfact{title="Link to the labour market"}
If a job has poor non-wage features (danger, isolation, unsocial hours), fewer people choose it, so supply is lower and employers must pay a higher wage to attract workers. Attractive non-wage features raise supply and allow a lower wage.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Treating a job as its wage only"}
Answers that compare only the basic wage miss half the marks. Always add fringe benefits and at least one non-wage factor.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling fringe benefits non-wage factors only"}
Fringe benefits are non-wage factors, but many have a clear money value (housing, transport, medical aid). Use that value when comparing packages.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing skill levels"}
A driver who has a licence and some training is semi-skilled, not unskilled. Skilled workers have long, formal training and qualifications.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**State / identify:** list factors briefly ("job security", "promotion prospects"). For "identify two non-wage factors", wages, overtime and bonuses do not count.
:::

:::exam-tip
**Explain:** say *why* the factor makes the job more or less attractive, for example: "A pension gives security in old age, so a worker may accept a lower wage."
:::

:::exam-tip
**Discuss** (should a person take job A or B?): compare wage factors and non-wage factors, consider the person's circumstances, and reach a conclusion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Labour is human effort used in production; its reward is a wage or salary.
- Workers are unskilled, semi-skilled or skilled, depending on the training and qualifications the job needs.
- Employees want fair pay, security, safe conditions, fringe benefits, and training and promotion.
- Choice of occupation depends on wage factors (basic pay, overtime, bonuses, future earnings) and non-wage factors (conditions, hours, security, status, location, benefits).
- Compare whole packages: a job with a lower wage may be more attractive overall.
:::
