## Introduction

Thandeka owns a small hardware shop in Bulawayo. She records every amount that goes through her bank account, keeps copies of invoices and knows what her customers owe at the year end, but she never writes up a sales ledger or a purchases ledger. To prepare her income statement her accountant needs her **sales** and **purchases** for the year, and neither figure is written down anywhere.

The figures can be found because double entry still holds even when nobody recorded it. Every account must balance, so if we know all the entries in an account except one, the missing one is simply the figure that makes the account balance. Examiners use this idea in almost every incomplete-records question: ZIMSEC in Form 4, Cambridge 0452 in topic 5. The missing-figure workings usually carry several method marks each.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC questions often say **debtors** and **creditors** and **total debtors account**; Cambridge 0452 says **trade receivables** and **trade payables**. **Irrecoverable debts** (Cambridge) are **bad debts** in ZIMSEC questions. The method is the same.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Missing figure"}
An amount that was never recorded but can be calculated because it is the only unknown entry in an account that must balance.
:::

:::definition{term="Total trade receivables account"}
A summary account for all credit customers taken together. Its missing figure is usually credit sales.
:::

:::definition{term="Total trade payables account"}
A summary account for all credit suppliers taken together. Its missing figure is usually credit purchases.
:::

:::definition{term="Cash and bank summary"}
A summary of all receipts and payments for the year, built from bank statements, paying-in slips and cheque counterfoils. Its missing figure is often drawings or cash sales.
:::

## The four tools

**1. Total trade receivables account → credit sales.** Debit the opening balance and the credit sales; credit the money received, discount allowed, sales returns and irrecoverable debts; the closing balance is the balance c/d. Rearranged:

**Credit sales = receipts + discount allowed + sales returns + irrecoverable debts + closing receivables − opening receivables**

**2. Total trade payables account → credit purchases.** Credit the opening balance and the credit purchases; debit payments, discount received and purchases returns:

**Credit purchases = payments + discount received + purchases returns + closing payables − opening payables**

Total revenue is then **cash sales + credit sales**, and total purchases is **cash purchases + credit purchases**.

**3. Cash and bank summary → the missing receipt or payment.** List the opening balance and every known receipt on the debit side, every known payment on the credit side, and the closing balance. The difference is the missing item, typically drawings. When takings are spent before banking, cash sales are found the same way: cash sales = cash banked + expenses paid from the till + drawings from the till + closing cash − opening cash.

**4. Expense accounts → the charge for the year.** Money paid is not the same as the expense. Adjust for amounts owing and prepaid at each end:

**Expense for the year = paid − opening accrual + closing accrual + opening prepayment − closing prepayment**

:::worked-solution[Example 1: credit sales and credit purchases]
Thandeka's records for 2025 show:

- Trade receivables: 1 January US\$3,640; 31 December US\$4,280. Received from credit customers US\$41,870; discount allowed US\$520; sales returns US\$690; irrecoverable debts written off US\$310.
- Trade payables: 1 January US\$2,950; 31 December US\$3,420. Paid to suppliers US\$30,460; discount received US\$380; purchases returns US\$540.
- Cash sales were US\$6,150 and cash purchases US\$1,200.

**Total trade receivables account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 3,640 | 31 Dec | Bank (receipts) | 41,870 |
| 31 Dec | Credit sales (missing figure) | 44,030 | 31 Dec | Discount allowed | 520 |
|  |  |  | 31 Dec | Sales returns | 690 |
|  |  |  | 31 Dec | Irrecoverable debts | 310 |
|  |  |  | 31 Dec | Balance c/d | 4,280 |
|  |  | **47,670** |  |  | **47,670** |
| 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 4,280 |  |  |  |

**Total trade payables account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 31 Dec | Bank (payments) | 30,460 | 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 2,950 |
| 31 Dec | Discount received | 380 | 31 Dec | Credit purchases (missing figure) | 31,850 |
| 31 Dec | Purchases returns | 540 |  |  |  |
| 31 Dec | Balance c/d | 3,420 |  |  |  |
|  |  | **34,800** |  |  | **34,800** |
|  |  |  | 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 3,420 |

1. Credit side total of the receivables account: 41,870 + 520 + 690 + 310 + 4,280 = 47,670. Credit sales = 47,670 − 3,640 = 44,030.
2. Debit side total of the payables account: 30,460 + 380 + 540 + 3,420 = 34,800. Credit purchases = 34,800 − 2,950 = 31,850.
3. Revenue = 6,150 + 44,030 = US\$50,180. Purchases = 1,200 + 31,850 = US\$33,050.

Note that the sales returns are deducted from revenue in the income statement, and the purchases returns from purchases, as usual.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: drawings and the expense for the year]
Tatenda runs a stationery shop in Mutare. His bank balance was US\$1,860 on 1 January 2025 and US\$2,410 on 31 December 2025. During the year he banked US\$38,450 from credit customers and US\$9,720 of cash sales. He paid suppliers US\$29,880, rent US\$3,300, wages US\$6,240 and electricity US\$1,170. All his drawings were taken from the bank, but he cannot say how much.

At 1 January 2025 rent of US\$250 was prepaid and electricity of US\$140 was owing. At 31 December 2025 rent of US\$300 was prepaid and electricity of US\$95 was owing.

**Bank account (summary)**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 1,860 | 31 Dec | Payments to trade payables | 29,880 |
| 31 Dec | Receipts from trade receivables | 38,450 | 31 Dec | Rent | 3,300 |
| 31 Dec | Cash sales banked | 9,720 | 31 Dec | Wages | 6,240 |
|  |  |  | 31 Dec | Electricity | 1,170 |
|  |  |  | 31 Dec | Drawings (missing figure) | 7,030 |
|  |  |  | 31 Dec | Balance c/d | 2,410 |
|  |  | **50,030** |  |  | **50,030** |
| 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 2,410 |  |  |  |

**Rent account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 Jan | Balance b/d (prepaid) | 250 | 31 Dec | Income statement | 3,250 |
| 31 Dec | Bank | 3,300 | 31 Dec | Balance c/d (prepaid) | 300 |
|  |  | **3,550** |  |  | **3,550** |
| 1 Jan | Balance b/d (prepaid) | 300 |  |  |  |

**Electricity account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 31 Dec | Bank | 1,170 | 1 Jan | Balance b/d (accrued) | 140 |
| 31 Dec | Balance c/d (accrued) | 95 | 31 Dec | Income statement | 1,125 |
|  |  | **1,265** |  |  | **1,265** |
|  |  |  | 1 Jan | Balance b/d (accrued) | 95 |

1. Total receipts including the opening balance: 1,860 + 38,450 + 9,720 = 50,030.
2. Known payments plus closing balance: 29,880 + 3,300 + 6,240 + 1,170 + 2,410 = 43,000.
3. Drawings = 50,030 − 43,000 = US\$7,030.
4. Rent expense = 3,300 + 250 − 300 = US\$3,250 (less than was paid, because more is prepaid at the end than at the start).
5. Electricity expense = 1,170 − 140 + 95 = US\$1,125 (part of the payment settled last year's bill).
:::

## Choosing the right account

Ask "which account would this figure have been posted to?" and build that account:

| Figure you need | Account to reconstruct | Missing figure on the |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Credit sales | Total trade receivables | Debit side |
| Credit purchases | Total trade payables | Credit side |
| Drawings, or cash sales | Cash or bank summary | Credit side (drawings) / debit side (cash sales) |
| Expense for the year | That expense account | Credit side (transfer to the income statement) |
| Closing receivables or payables | Total receivables / payables | Balance c/d |

The same accounts can be turned round to find something else. If credit sales are known (perhaps from a mark-up), the total trade receivables account gives the closing receivables or the amount received.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using cash received as the sales figure"}
Receipts from customers include payments for last year's sales and leave out sales not yet paid for. Credit sales must come from the total trade receivables account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting discounts on the wrong side"}
Discount allowed reduces what customers owe: it is a credit in the receivables account. Discount received reduces what we owe: a debit in the payables account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting cash sales or cash purchases"}
The control accounts give only the credit figures. Add any cash sales or cash purchases to get the totals used in the income statement.
:::

:::mistake{title="Charging the amount paid as the expense"}
The expense for the year must be adjusted for accruals and prepayments at both the start and the end of the year.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Show each reconstruction as a full account (or a clearly labelled calculation) and label the missing figure. Examiners give method marks for each correct entry even if an arithmetic slip makes the final figure wrong.
:::

:::exam-tip
Read every line of the question for items that belong in a control account: contra entries, cheques dishonoured, irrecoverable debts, refunds. Tick each one as you use it.
:::

:::exam-tip
Remember the double effect: a figure you find (such as drawings or rent paid in advance) is usually needed again in the statement of financial position.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- An account must balance, so a single unknown entry can always be calculated.
- Total trade receivables account → credit sales; total trade payables account → credit purchases.
- Total revenue = cash sales + credit sales; total purchases = cash purchases + credit purchases.
- A cash or bank summary finds missing receipts or payments, often drawings.
- Expense for the year = paid − opening accrual + closing accrual + opening prepayment − closing prepayment.
:::
