## Introduction

Nyasha owns a grocery shop in Gweru. She keeps her bank statements and knows her inventory, customers and suppliers at each year end, but she has no ledger. Her bank manager has asked for a proper income statement and statement of financial position, not just a profit figure. The accountant must rebuild the year from the evidence that exists.

This is the most demanding incomplete-records task and a favourite Paper 2 question for both boards. It combines everything from the two previous lessons: the opening statement of affairs gives the opening capital, the control accounts give sales and purchases, the bank summary gives missing payments, and the expense accounts give the true charges. This lesson adds the last tool: using **mark-up**, **margin** and **inventory turnover** to find figures that cannot be found any other way. Cambridge 0452 examines this only for **trading** businesses, never for service or manufacturing businesses.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC: **income statement**, **net profit**, **debtors** and **creditors**, **stock**. Cambridge 0452: **statement of profit or loss**, **profit for the year**, **trade receivables** and **trade payables**, **inventory**. Expenses owing and paid in advance are **accruals** and **prepayments** for both; Cambridge lists them as other payables and other receivables.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Mark-up"}
Gross profit expressed as a percentage of cost of sales.
:::

:::definition{term="Margin (gross profit margin)"}
Gross profit expressed as a percentage of revenue (selling price).
:::

:::definition{term="Rate of inventory turnover"}
Cost of sales divided by average inventory: the number of times the average inventory is sold and replaced in the year.
:::

:::definition{term="Opening capital"}
The owner's capital at the start of the year, found from the opening statement of affairs when no ledger exists.
:::

## The step-by-step method

1. **Opening statement of affairs** → opening capital.
2. **Bank and cash summary** → closing bank balance, or a missing figure such as drawings.
3. **Total trade receivables account** → credit sales; add cash sales for revenue.
4. **Total trade payables account** → credit purchases; add cash purchases.
5. **Expense accounts** → the charge for each expense after accruals and prepayments.
6. **Depreciation** on non-current assets, including any bought during the year.
7. **Income statement**, then the **statement of financial position**, with capital = opening capital + profit − drawings (+ capital introduced).

If the statement of financial position balances, every step is probably right. If it does not, the difference often points to the error, for example a drawings figure left out.

## Using mark-up, margin and inventory turnover

Sometimes a figure such as revenue or closing inventory cannot be found from the records, for example after a fire or a theft. If the trader always uses the same mark-up, the trading section can be completed backwards:

- With a mark-up of 25%, every US\$100 of cost is sold for US\$125, so **cost of sales = revenue × 100/125** and gross profit = revenue × 25/125.
- With a margin of 20%, gross profit = revenue × 20/100, and cost of sales = revenue × 80/100.
- With a known **rate of inventory turnover**, average inventory = cost of sales ÷ turnover. Closing inventory = 2 × average inventory − opening inventory.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: inventory lost in a burglary]
Thabo's electrical shop in Bulawayo was burgled on 31 December 2025. Revenue for the year was US\$48,000, all goods are sold at a mark-up of 25%, opening inventory was US\$5,200 and purchases US\$37,900. The inventory left after the burglary was counted at US\$3,950. How much inventory was stolen?

1. Gross profit = 48,000 × 25/125 = 9,600.
2. Cost of sales = 48,000 − 9,600 = 38,400.
3. Closing inventory that should be held = 5,200 + 37,900 − 38,400 = 4,700.
4. Inventory stolen = 4,700 − 3,950 = US\$750.

**Calculation of closing inventory**

|  | US\$ | US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue |  | 48,000 |
| Less: Gross profit (25/125 × 48,000) |  | (9,600) |
| Cost of sales |  | 38,400 |
|  |  |  |
| Opening inventory |  | 5,200 |
| Add: Purchases |  | 37,900 |
| Goods available for sale |  | 43,100 |
| Less: Cost of sales |  | (38,400) |
| Closing inventory that should be held |  | 4,700 |
| Less: Inventory actually counted |  | (3,950) |
| **Inventory lost** |  | 750 |

The common error is to take 25% of revenue (12,000) as gross profit. Mark-up is a percentage of **cost**, so the fraction of revenue is 25/125.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: full statements for Nyasha's Grocery]
At 1 January 2025 Nyasha had shop equipment US\$8,000, inventory US\$6,300, trade receivables US\$2,150, bank US\$1,970 and trade payables US\$3,480.

Her bank account for 2025 shows: receipts from credit customers US\$53,600; cash sales banked US\$14,800 (all cash sales are banked); paid to suppliers US\$48,900; wages US\$7,200; rent US\$3,600; general expenses US\$1,850; drawings US\$6,000; new equipment US\$2,000.

At 31 December 2025: inventory US\$7,150; trade receivables US\$2,640; trade payables US\$3,910; rent prepaid US\$300; general expenses owing US\$140. Equipment is depreciated at 10% of the cost held at the year end.

1. Opening capital: 8,000 + 10,420 − 3,480 = **14,940**.
2. Closing bank: 1,970 + 53,600 + 14,800 − (48,900 + 7,200 + 3,600 + 1,850 + 6,000 + 2,000) = **820**.
3. Credit sales: 53,600 + 2,640 − 2,150 = 54,090. Revenue = 54,090 + 14,800 = **68,890**.
4. Credit purchases: 48,900 + 3,910 − 3,480 = **49,330**.
5. Rent: 3,600 − 300 = 3,300. General expenses: 1,850 + 140 = 1,990.
6. Equipment cost 8,000 + 2,000 = 10,000; depreciation 10% = **1,000**.

**Nyasha's Grocery**

**Income statement (statement of profit or loss)**

**for the year ended 31 December 2025**

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue |  | 68,890 |
| Less: Cost of sales |  |  |

|  | 55,630 |  |
| Less: Closing inventory | (7,150) | (48,480) |
| **Gross profit** |  | 20,410 |
| Less: Expenses |  |  |
| Wages | 7,200 |  |
| Rent (3,600 − 300) | 3,300 |  |
| General expenses (1,850 + 140) | 1,990 |  |
| Depreciation: shop equipment | 1,000 | (13,490) |
| **Profit for the year (net profit)** |  | 6,920 |

**Nyasha's Grocery**

**Statement of financial position**

**at 31 December 2025**

|  | US\$ | US\$ | US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Non-current assets** | Cost | Accumulated depreciation | Net book value |
| Shop equipment | 10,000 | 1,000 | 9,000 |
| **Current assets** |  |  |  |
| Inventory |  | 7,150 |  |
| Trade receivables |  | 2,640 |  |
| Other receivables (rent prepaid) |  | 300 |  |
| Bank |  | 820 |  |
|  |  | 10,910 |  |
| **Current liabilities** |  |  |  |
| Trade payables |  | 3,910 |  |
| Other payables (expenses owing) |  | 140 |  |
|  |  | (4,050) |  |

|  |  |  |  |

Net assets and closing capital agree at US\$15,860, so the reconstruction is complete. Notice that the US\$2,000 spent on equipment is capital expenditure: it is added to the asset, not charged as an expense.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing mark-up with margin"}
A mark-up of 25% is a margin of 20%. Always ask: is the percentage of cost or of selling price?
:::

:::mistake{title="Charging the purchase of equipment as an expense"}
Payments for non-current assets appear in the bank summary but are capital expenditure. Only the depreciation goes in the income statement.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the opening statement of affairs"}
Without it there is no opening capital, and the statement of financial position cannot be completed.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving accruals and prepayments out of the statement of financial position"}
Every adjustment made to an expense in the income statement also appears as a current asset or current liability.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Number your workings (W1, W2, …) and refer to them in the statements. Examiners follow your figures through, so a slip in one working costs only that working's marks.
:::

:::exam-tip
Check that the statement of financial position balances before moving on. If it is out, look first for drawings, capital introduced, depreciation and closing bank.
:::

:::exam-tip
When a question gives a mark-up or margin, write the fraction you are using (for example 25/125) in your working; that line alone usually earns a method mark.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Start with the opening statement of affairs to find opening capital.
- Reconstruct bank, receivables, payables and expense accounts to find each missing figure.
- With a mark-up m%, gross profit = revenue × m/(100 + m); with a margin g%, gross profit = revenue × g/100.
- Average inventory = cost of sales ÷ rate of inventory turnover.
- Prepare the income statement, then the statement of financial position; they must agree.
- Cambridge examines incomplete records for trading businesses only.
:::
