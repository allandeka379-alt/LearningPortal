## Introduction

Nyasha runs a catering business in Harare. Some customers pay her in cash at functions, others swipe a debit card or pay by bank transfer, and she pays her suppliers by cheque or online. She needs to know two things at any moment: how much cash is in the cash box, and how much is in the business bank account.

The **cash book** answers both questions. It is the most-used book in any small business, and examiners use it constantly: you may be asked to write it up from a list of transactions, to balance it, or to explain what an entry or a balance means. It is also the starting point for bank reconciliation later in the course.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC lists the cash book among the **subsidiary books** (books of original entry); Cambridge calls it a **book of prime entry**. Both boards describe its **dual function**: it is a book of prime entry and also a ledger account for cash and bank.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Two-column cash book"}
A cash book with a **Cash** column and a **Bank** column on each side. Receipts go on the debit (left) side; payments go on the credit (right) side.
:::

:::definition{term="Dual function of the cash book"}
The cash book is both a book of prime entry (transactions are first recorded there) and part of the ledger (it replaces the cash account and the bank account). Only the other half of each entry has to be posted.
:::

:::definition{term="Contra entry"}
A transaction between the cash and bank columns, such as paying cash into the bank. Both halves are in the cash book, on opposite sides; each is marked **C**.
:::

:::definition{term="Bank overdraft"}
Borrowing from the bank by paying out more than is in the account. It appears as a **credit** balance in the bank column and is a current liability.
:::

## Which column?

The column depends on **how** money moves, not on who pays.

| Method | Column |
| --- | --- |
| Notes and coins | Cash |
| Cheque received and banked, or cheque paid | Bank |
| Debit card or credit card receipt from a customer | Bank |
| Paying by debit card | Bank |
| Online payment, bank transfer, standing order or direct debit | Bank |

Customers' card payments are paid into the business bank account by the card company, so they go in the bank column. A purchase made with the business's **own credit card** is different: the business owes the card company, and the bank column is used only when the monthly card bill is paid.

## Contra entries

Contra entries move money between the two columns. Nothing is posted to the ledger, because both accounts (cash and bank) are already in the cash book.

- Cash paid into the bank: **credit** the cash column ("Bank C") and **debit** the bank column ("Cash C").
- Cash withdrawn from the bank for business use: **debit** the cash column ("Bank C") and **credit** the bank column ("Cash C").

## Balancing the cash book

Each column is balanced separately, exactly like a ledger account.

1. Add each column on both sides.
2. Put the difference on the smaller side as the balance carried down, dated the last day of the period.
3. Rule off with equal totals on both sides of each column.
4. Bring each balance down on the opposite side on the first day of the next period.

The cash column can never have a credit balance: you cannot pay out more notes and coins than you have. The bank column can, and a credit balance is an overdraft.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Nyasha's cash book for March]
On 1 March 2025 Nyasha had US\$320 cash and US\$2,450 in the bank. In March:

- 3 Mar: cash sales US\$640.
- 5 Mar: paid rent US\$900 by bank transfer.
- 8 Mar: Tendai Moyo, a customer, paid US\$1,150 by bank transfer.
- 10 Mar: paid wages in cash US\$380.
- 12 Mar: paid US\$400 cash into the bank.
- 15 Mar: card sales US\$1,260.
- 19 Mar: paid Mutare Meats, a supplier, US\$1,740 by cheque.
- 24 Mar: withdrew US\$250 from the bank for office cash.
- 28 Mar: Nyasha took US\$150 cash for her own use.

1. Receipts go on the debit side, payments on the credit side, in the correct column.
2. The 12 March and 24 March entries are contras, marked C, and appear on both sides.
3. Cash column: $320 + 640 + 250 = 1,210$ debit; $380 + 400 + 150 = 930$ credit; balance $280$.
4. Bank column: $2,450 + 1,150 + 400 + 1,260 = 5,260$ debit; $900 + 1,740 + 250 = 2,890$ credit; balance $2,370$.

5. Nyasha starts April with US\$280 cash and US\$2,370 in the bank.

## Posting from the cash book

Because the cash book is itself the cash account and the bank account, you post only the **other** account, on the **opposite** side:

- A receipt (debit in the cash book) is credited to the other account: sales, a customer, capital or a loan.
- A payment (credit in the cash book) is debited to the other account: an expense, a supplier, drawings or an asset.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Posting both halves of a contra"}
A contra is complete inside the cash book. Do not post it to any ledger account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting card receipts in the cash column"}
Card and transfer receipts go straight into the bank account, so they belong in the bank column.
:::

:::mistake{title="Overdraft on the wrong side"}
An opening overdraft is a credit balance b/d. At the end of the period an overdraft is carried down on the debit side and brought down on the credit side.
:::

:::mistake{title="A credit balance in the cash column"}
If your cash column has more payments than receipts, something is wrong: recheck the columns.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Read the method of payment in every line before choosing the column. Words such as cheque, card, transfer, online and direct debit all mean the bank column.
:::

:::exam-tip
Label contras with C and balance both columns. Write "Balances c/d" and "Balances b/d" when both are on the same side, and date them correctly.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The two-column cash book has cash and bank columns; receipts are debits, payments are credits.
- It has a dual function: book of prime entry and ledger account for cash and bank.
- Contra entries move money between cash and bank; both halves are in the cash book, marked C.
- Each column is balanced separately; a credit balance in the bank column is an overdraft, a current liability.
- Only the other account is posted, on the opposite side.
:::
