## Introduction

Tendai sells electrical goods in Bulawayo. Now and then a customer brings back a kettle that does not work, or a builder returns cable that was the wrong size. Tendai also sends goods back to his own suppliers when a delivery is damaged. Each return reduces a debt: the customer owes Tendai less, or Tendai owes the supplier less.

Returns are recorded in two books of prime entry: the **sales returns journal** and the **purchases returns journal**. They work exactly like the sales and purchases journals, but every posting is the other way round. In the examination returns appear in almost every question on ledger accounts, control accounts and the income statement, so you must know both the postings and where the totals end up.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Sales returns are also called **returns inwards** and purchases returns **returns outwards**. ZIMSEC calls the returns journals **subsidiary books** (books of original entry); Cambridge calls them **books of prime entry**. The income statement is the **statement of profit or loss** in Cambridge papers.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Sales returns (returns inwards)"}
Goods sold on credit that customers send back to the business, for example because they are faulty or not what was ordered.
:::

:::definition{term="Purchases returns (returns outwards)"}
Goods bought on credit that the business sends back to its suppliers.
:::

:::definition{term="Credit note"}
A document issued by the **seller** to the buyer showing that the buyer's account has been reduced, usually because goods were returned or overcharged.
:::

:::definition{term="Debit note"}
A document a buyer sends to a supplier with goods returned, asking for the account to be reduced. Some businesses write up the purchases returns journal from copies of their debit notes; others wait for the supplier's credit note.
:::

## Which document, which book?

| Situation | Document | Book of prime entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A customer returns goods to us | Credit note we send (copy kept) | Sales returns journal |
| We return goods to a supplier | Credit note we receive (or a copy of our debit note) | Purchases returns journal |

The credit note always travels **from the seller to the buyer**. So the business sends credit notes for returns inwards and receives them for returns outwards.

If goods were sold with trade discount, the credit note is for the **net** price, the same amount that was charged on the invoice.

## Posting the returns journals

**Sales returns journal**

1. Each credit note is posted to the **credit** of the customer's account (the customer owes less).
2. The total is posted to the **debit** of the returns inwards account.

**Purchases returns journal**

1. Each credit note received is posted to the **debit** of the supplier's account (the business owes less).
2. The total is posted to the **credit** of the returns outwards account.

The returns accounts are kept separately from sales and purchases. This lets the owner see how much is being returned, which may point to poor quality goods or careless ordering.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Tendai's sales returns journal]
On 2 July 2025 Tendai sold goods on credit to Rudo Moyo for US\$2,400 (after trade discount). During July he issued these credit notes:

- 6 July: Rudo Moyo, goods listed at US\$400 returned, trade discount 25% had been given, CN 18.
- 14 July: Gwanda Clinic, US\$180, CN 19.

Farai runs a store in Harare. In August 2025 he received credit notes from Harare Wholesalers for US\$240 (8 Aug) and US\$95 (28 Aug), and from Bulawayo Foods for US\$135 (19 Aug).

## Returns in the income statement

Returns inwards are **deducted from sales** and returns outwards are **deducted from purchases** in the trading section. They are never shown as an expense or as other income. Mixing them up changes both net sales and cost of sales, so the gross profit is wrong by twice the amounts involved.

## What the returns figures tell the owner

Because returns are kept in their own accounts, the owner can compare them with sales and purchases. If Tendai's returns inwards grow from 1% to 5% of sales, customers may be unhappy with the quality of a product line or with wrong deliveries, and he should find out why before he loses them. High returns outwards may mean a supplier sends damaged goods, or that Tendai's own staff order carelessly. Every return also costs time and transport, so a falling returns figure is a sign of a well-run business.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Crediting the supplier for returns outwards"}
Goods sent back to a supplier reduce what the business owes, so the supplier's account is **debited**. The credit goes to returns outwards.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking the buyer issues the credit note"}
The seller issues the credit note. When Tendai's customer returns goods, Tendai sends the credit note.
:::

:::mistake{title="Recording returns at list price"}
If trade discount was given on the sale, the return is valued at the same net price that was invoiced.
:::

:::mistake{title="Deducting returns inwards from purchases"}
Returns inwards belong with sales; returns outwards belong with purchases.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Use the pairing to check yourself: inwards goes with sales (customers), outwards goes with purchases (suppliers). Each returns posting is the reverse of the original invoice posting.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked to "explain" a returns entry in a personal account, state what happened (goods were returned) and the effect (the customer owes less, or the business owes the supplier less).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Returns inwards (sales returns) come back from customers; returns outwards (purchases returns) go back to suppliers.
- The seller issues the credit note; it is the source document for both returns journals.
- Sales returns journal: credit each customer, debit returns inwards with the total.
- Purchases returns journal: debit each supplier, credit returns outwards with the total.
- In the trading section, returns inwards are deducted from sales and returns outwards from purchases.
:::
