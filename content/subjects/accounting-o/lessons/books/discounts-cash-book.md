## Introduction

Tatenda runs a wholesale business in Masvingo selling cooking oil, sugar and rice to tuck shops and supermarkets. He gives regular trade buyers a lower price than the public, and he also offers a small extra reduction to any customer who pays within 14 days, because money in the bank sooner is worth a lot to a growing business. His own suppliers make him the same kind of offer.

These two reductions are treated completely differently in the books. One never appears in the ledger at all; the other is recorded in an extra column of the cash book and ends up in the income statement. Examiners test the difference in multiple-choice questions every year, and the three-column cash book is a favourite structured question.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use **trade discount**, **cash discount**, **discount allowed** and **discount received**. ZIMSEC calls the cash book a subsidiary book; Cambridge calls it a book of prime entry. The income statement is the **statement of profit or loss** in Cambridge papers.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Trade discount"}
A reduction in the list price given to trade customers, for example for buying in bulk. It is shown on the invoice and is **not** entered in the ledger.
:::

:::definition{term="Cash discount"}
A reduction in the amount owed, offered to a credit customer for paying within a stated time. It is recorded in the books.
:::

:::definition{term="Discount allowed"}
Cash discount the business gives to its customers. It is an **expense**.
:::

:::definition{term="Discount received"}
Cash discount the business receives from its suppliers. It is **income**.
:::

## Trade discount and cash discount compared

| Feature | Trade discount | Cash discount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why it is given | To trade buyers, for bulk orders | For paying promptly |
| When it is known | When the invoice is made out | Only when payment is made in time |
| Recorded in the ledger? | No: the invoice shows the net amount | Yes: discount allowed or received |
| Where it ends up | Nowhere (sales or purchases are already net) | Income statement: expense or income |

Cash discount is always calculated on the amount **owed**, which is the invoice total after any trade discount.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: calculating both discounts]
Tatenda sells goods with a list price of US\$5,000 to Rudo Banda's supermarket. He allows 20% trade discount and offers 5% cash discount for payment within 14 days. Rudo pays on day 10.

1. Trade discount: $5,000 \times 20\% = 1,000$.
2. Invoice amount (recorded in the sales journal): $5,000 - 1,000 = 4,000$.
3. Cash discount on the amount owed: $4,000 \times 5\% = 200$.
4. Amount Rudo pays: $4,000 - 200 = 3,800$.
5. Entries: sales journal US\$4,000; cash book receipt US\$3,800 with US\$200 in the discount column. The US\$1,000 trade discount is never recorded.

If Rudo had paid on day 20, she would have paid the full US\$4,000 and no discount would be recorded.
:::

## The three-column cash book

The three-column cash book adds a **Discount** column on each side, next to the cash and bank columns.

- **Debit side**: discount **allowed**, written on the same line as the receipt from the customer.
- **Credit side**: discount **received**, written on the same line as the payment to the supplier.

The discount columns are **memorandum columns**: they are not part of the cash or bank balance. They are simply totalled at the end of the period and are **not** balanced.

### Posting the discounts

1. For each customer: credit the customer's account with the money received **and** with the discount allowed. Together they clear the debt.
2. For each supplier: debit the supplier's account with the payment **and** with the discount received.
3. The **total** of the discount allowed column is debited to the discount allowed account (expense).
4. The **total** of the discount received column is credited to the discount received account (income).

The double entry is complete: the individual credits to customers equal the single debit to discount allowed, and the debits to suppliers equal the single credit to discount received.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Tatenda's three-column cash book]
On 1 July 2025 Tatenda had US\$420 cash and US\$3,150 in the bank. In July:

- 4 Jul: Rudo Banda paid by cheque to settle her account of US\$1,200, less 5% cash discount.
- 7 Jul: paid Gweru Millers US\$2,000 owed, less 2.5% cash discount, by bank transfer.

## Discounts in the income statement

Discount allowed is shown with the **expenses**. Discount received is added to gross profit as **other income**. Neither is deducted from sales or purchases: that treatment belongs to returns, not discounts.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Recording trade discount in the discount column"}
Only cash discount goes in the discount columns. Trade discount has already been deducted on the invoice.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calculating cash discount on the list price"}
Cash discount is a percentage of the amount owed, which is the invoice total after trade discount.
:::

:::mistake{title="Balancing the discount columns"}
The discount columns are totalled, not balanced. The debit total is discount allowed and the credit total is discount received; they are unrelated.
:::

:::mistake{title="Posting only the cash received to the customer"}
The customer must be credited with the money **and** the discount, or the account will show a small balance still owing.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"In full settlement of" tells you there is a discount: the discount is the difference between the amount owed and the amount paid.
:::

:::exam-tip
Remember the sides: discount allowed sits with receipts (debit side), discount received sits with payments (credit side). Write the totals clearly, because examiners award marks for them.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Trade discount reduces the list price on the invoice and is never recorded in the ledger.
- Cash discount rewards prompt payment and is calculated on the amount owed after trade discount.
- Discount allowed (expense) is in the debit discount column; discount received (income) is in the credit discount column.
- Discount columns are memorandum columns: totalled, not balanced, and their totals are posted to the discount accounts.
- Personal accounts are credited or debited with both the payment and the discount.
:::
