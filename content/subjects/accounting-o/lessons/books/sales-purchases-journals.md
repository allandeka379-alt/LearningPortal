## Introduction

Rudo runs a hardware shop in Gweru. Builders and small contractors collect cement, roofing sheets and paint during the month and pay later. Every one of those credit sales produces an invoice. If Rudo posted each invoice straight to the sales account, that account would fill up with dozens of small entries every month. Instead she lists the invoices in a **sales journal**, adds up the list once a month, and posts a single total to the sales account.

The purchases journal does the same job for goods Rudo buys on credit from her suppliers. These two books save time, keep the ledger tidy and give an instant figure for credit sales or credit purchases for the month.

Examiners regularly give a list of invoices and ask you to enter them in the correct journal, total it and post it. Marks are lost by putting cash sales in the sales journal, by recording the list price instead of the net price, or by posting to the wrong side.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC calls these books **subsidiary books** or books of original entry; Cambridge calls them **books of prime entry**. The sales journal is also called the **sales day book** and the purchases journal the **purchases day book**. Customers' accounts are kept in the **trade receivables ledger** (ZIMSEC) or **sales ledger** (Cambridge); suppliers' accounts in the **trade payables ledger** or **purchases ledger**. ZIMSEC expects a folio column; Cambridge does not.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Sales journal"}
The book of prime entry in which credit sales of goods are listed from copies of the sales invoices sent to customers.
:::

:::definition{term="Purchases journal"}
The book of prime entry in which credit purchases of goods for resale are listed from the invoices received from suppliers.
:::

:::definition{term="Trade discount"}
A reduction in the list (catalogue) price given to trade customers, usually for buying in bulk or because they are in the same trade. It is deducted on the invoice and is never entered in the ledger.
:::

:::definition{term="Net invoice value"}
The amount the customer must pay: list price less trade discount. This is the figure entered in the journal.
:::

## What goes into each journal

Only **credit** transactions in **goods the business trades in** go into these two journals.

| Transaction | Book of prime entry |
| --- | --- |
| Goods sold on credit | Sales journal |
| Goods for resale bought on credit | Purchases journal |
| Goods sold for cash or by card | Cash book |
| Goods for resale bought and paid for immediately | Cash book |
| A delivery van or shelving bought on credit | General journal |

A shop fitting or a vehicle is not bought for resale, so it is not a "purchase" in the accounting sense even when it is bought on credit. It is a non-current asset and is recorded in the general journal.

### Layout

Each journal has a date column, the name of the customer or supplier, the invoice number and the amount. ZIMSEC candidates add a folio column showing the ledger page the entry was posted to. At the end of the period the amount column is totalled.

### Trade discount

Trade discount is taken off before the invoice total is calculated. The journal shows only the net amount, because that is what the customer owes. For example, goods listed at US\$2,400 with 25% trade discount are entered as US\$1,800 ($2,400 \times 0.75$).

## Posting the journals

Posting means transferring the entries to the ledger so that double entry is complete.

**Sales journal**

1. Each invoice is posted to the **debit** of the customer's account (the customer now owes more).
2. The total for the period is posted to the **credit** of the sales account (income increases).

**Purchases journal**

1. Each invoice is posted to the **credit** of the supplier's account (the business owes more).
2. The total for the period is posted to the **debit** of the purchases account (purchases are an expense).

The individual debits to customers add up to exactly the one credit to sales, so total debits still equal total credits.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Rudo's sales journal]
In May 2025 Rudo sent these invoices:

- 3 May: Tatenda Builders, goods listed at US\$2,400, trade discount 25%, invoice INV 041.
- 10 May: Farai Ncube, goods US\$860, no trade discount, INV 042.
- 18 May: Mutare Construction, goods listed at US\$3,200, trade discount 20%, INV 043.
- 27 May: Tatenda Builders, goods listed at US\$1,140, trade discount 25%, INV 044.

1. Calculate each net amount: $2,400 \times 0.75 = 1,800$; $860$; $3,200 \times 0.80 = 2,560$; $1,140 \times 0.75 = 855$.
2. Enter the invoices in date order and total the journal: $1,800 + 860 + 2,560 + 855 = 6,075$.

**Sales journal (May 2025)**

| Date | Customer | Invoice no. | US\$ |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 3 May | Tatenda Builders | INV 041 | 1,800 |
| 10 May | Farai Ncube | INV 042 | 860 |
| 18 May | Mutare Construction | INV 043 | 2,560 |
| 27 May | Tatenda Builders | INV 044 | 855 |
| 31 May | **Transferred to sales account** |  | **6,075** |

3. Post each invoice to the debit of the customer. Tatenda Builders' account shows two debits.

**Tatenda Builders account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 3 May | Sales | 1,800 |  |  |  |
| 27 May | Sales | 855 |  |  |  |

4. Post the total to the credit of the sales account.

**Sales account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
|  |  |  | 31 May | Total credit sales | 6,075 |

5. Check: debits to customers $1,800 + 860 + 2,560 + 855 = 6,075$ equal the credit to sales.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Chipo's purchases journal]
Chipo runs a general store in Chinhoyi. In June 2025 she received these invoices for goods for resale:

- 2 Jun: Harare Wholesalers, list price US\$1,500 less 10% trade discount, invoice H 2210.
- 9 Jun: Bulawayo Foods, US\$720, invoice BF 381.
- 16 Jun: Harare Wholesalers, list price US\$2,000 less 10% trade discount, invoice H 2264.
- 24 Jun: Mashonaland Millers, US\$940, invoice MM 77.

On 20 June she also bought a display fridge on credit for US\$1,200. The fridge is equipment, so it is left out.

1. Net amounts: $1,500 \times 0.90 = 1,350$ and $2,000 \times 0.90 = 1,800$.
2. Total: $1,350 + 720 + 1,800 + 940 = 4,810$.

**Purchases journal (June 2025)**

| Date | Supplier | Invoice no. | US\$ |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 2 Jun | Harare Wholesalers | H 2210 | 1,350 |

| 16 Jun | Harare Wholesalers | H 2264 | 1,800 |
| 24 Jun | Mashonaland Millers | MM 77 | 940 |

3. Credit each supplier with its invoices, then debit purchases with the total.

**Harare Wholesalers account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
|  |  |  | 2 Jun | Purchases | 1,350 |

## Interpreting the journals

A journal total tells the owner how much was sold or bought on credit in the period. An entry in a customer's account dated with an invoice tells you that goods were sold to that customer on credit on that day, and the details column names the other account (sales). In the exam, "explain the entry" questions expect both parts: what happened, and its effect (the customer owes more, or the business owes the supplier more).

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Putting cash sales in the sales journal"}
The sales journal is for credit sales only. Goods sold for cash, by card or by bank transfer go straight into the cash book.
:::

:::mistake{title="Recording the list price"}
Trade discount is deducted on the invoice and never appears in the ledger. Enter the net amount; there is no "discount" column in these journals.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting non-current assets in the purchases journal"}
Purchases means goods bought for resale. Equipment, vehicles and furniture bought on credit go in the general journal.
:::

:::mistake{title="Posting the total to the customers"}
Customers and suppliers are posted with their own invoices; only the sales and purchases accounts receive the total.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Before you write anything, sort the list: credit or cash? Goods for resale or an asset? Only credit goods go in these journals. Many questions include one cash sale or one asset purchase as a trap.
:::

:::exam-tip
Show the trade discount working (list price × percentage) beside the journal. If the net figure is wrong, method marks can still be awarded.
:::

:::exam-tip
Remember the pattern: the sales journal produces debits to customers and one credit to sales; the purchases journal produces credits to suppliers and one debit to purchases.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The sales journal lists credit sales of goods; the purchases journal lists credit purchases of goods for resale.
- Entries are made from invoices and recorded at the net amount after trade discount.
- Cash transactions go to the cash book and credit purchases of non-current assets to the general journal.
- Sales journal: debit each customer, credit sales with the total. Purchases journal: credit each supplier, debit purchases with the total.
- Totalling the journal saves many separate postings and shows credit sales or purchases for the period.
:::
