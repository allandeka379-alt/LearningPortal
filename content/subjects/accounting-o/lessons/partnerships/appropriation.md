## Introduction

Tatenda and Nyasha run T & N Transport, a fleet of kombis on the Masvingo to Harare route. At the end of 2025 their income statement shows a profit for the year of US\$31,620. A sole trader would simply add the whole profit to capital. Two partners must first **divide** it, following the terms of their partnership agreement: interest on the capital each has invested, a salary for Nyasha who manages the drivers, a charge on the money each has drawn out, and finally a split of what is left.

This division is shown in the **appropriation account**, which follows straight after the income statement. It appears in almost every partnership question on both ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Cambridge 0452 Paper 2, usually together with the partners' current accounts. It is also one of the easiest places to collect marks, provided you put each item in the right place and on the right side.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC usually calls the starting figure **net profit** and sometimes writes the appropriation section as part of the "income statement and appropriation account". Cambridge uses **profit for the year** and the **appropriation account**. The items and their order are the same for both boards.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Appropriation account"}
The account (or statement) that shows how the profit or loss for the year of a partnership is shared between the partners.
:::

:::definition{term="Interest on capital"}
An amount credited to each partner, calculated as an agreed percentage of the partner's capital. It is an appropriation of profit.
:::

:::definition{term="Interest on drawings"}
A charge made on the amounts each partner has drawn, usually calculated for the time from the date of drawing to the year end. It is added in the appropriation account and charged to the partner.
:::

:::definition{term="Partner's salary"}
An agreed amount given to a partner, usually for working in the business. It is an appropriation of profit, not an expense.
:::

:::definition{term="Residual profit"}
The profit left after adding interest on drawings and deducting interest on capital and salaries. It is shared in the profit-sharing ratio.
:::

## Step 1: complete the income statement

The appropriation account starts from the profit for the year, so every **expense** must already have been deducted. The one partnership item that belongs here is **interest on a partner's loan**. A loan is money lent to the business in addition to capital; its interest is a cost of borrowing, like interest on a bank loan. If a question gives a "draft profit" before loan interest, deduct the interest first.

## Step 2: the order of the appropriation account

1. Start with the profit for the year.
2. **Add** interest on drawings (the partners pay it to the business, so more profit is available to share).
3. **Deduct** interest on capital.
4. **Deduct** partners' salaries.
5. The result is the **residual profit** (or loss).
6. Share the residual profit in the profit-sharing ratio. The shares must add up to the residual profit.

Every figure must be shown **per partner** and in total. A useful final check: interest on capital + salaries + shares − interest on drawings = the profit for the year.

### Calculating interest

- **Interest on capital** = rate × capital × time. If the capital was the same all year, time is one year. If capital was introduced part-way through, calculate each part for the months it was in the business.
- **Interest on drawings** = rate × amount drawn × months from the date of drawing to the year end ÷ 12. Some questions simply state the interest on drawings; use the figures given.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: T & N Transport]
Profit for the year ended 31 December 2025: US\$31,620. Capitals: Tatenda US\$40,000, Nyasha US\$25,000. The agreement gives interest on capital at 6% a year, a salary of US\$4,800 a year to Nyasha, and interest on drawings at 12% a year; the remaining profit is shared Tatenda 3 : Nyasha 2. Tatenda drew US\$6,000 and Nyasha US\$4,000, both on 1 July 2025.

1. Interest on drawings (six months to 31 December): Tatenda 12% × 6,000 × 6/12 = 360; Nyasha 12% × 4,000 × 6/12 = 240. Total 600.
2. Interest on capital: Tatenda 6% × 40,000 = 2,400; Nyasha 6% × 25,000 = 1,500. Total 3,900.
3. Residual profit: 31,620 + 600 − 3,900 − 4,800 = 23,520.
4. Shares: Tatenda 23,520 × 3/5 = 14,112; Nyasha 23,520 × 2/5 = 9,408.
5. Check: 3,900 + 4,800 + 23,520 − 600 = 31,620.

## When the agreement takes more than the profit

Interest on capital and salaries are given **in full** even when the profit is too small to cover them. The shortfall becomes a **residual loss**, which is shared in the profit-sharing ratio in exactly the same way as a profit. A share of a loss reduces what each partner receives.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Chipo and Sipho Hardware, Bulawayo]
The draft profit for 2025 was US\$14,250, before interest on Sipho's loan of US\$10,000 at 8% a year. Capitals: Chipo US\$30,000, Sipho US\$20,000. Interest on capital is 10% a year. Salaries: Chipo US\$6,000, Sipho US\$4,000. Profits and losses are shared equally.

1. Loan interest is an expense: 8% × 10,000 = 800. Profit for the year = 14,250 − 800 = 13,450.
2. Interest on capital: Chipo 3,000; Sipho 2,000. Salaries 10,000.
3. Residual: 13,450 − 5,000 − 10,000 = −1,550, a residual **loss**.
4. Share equally: each partner bears 775.

| --- | ---: | ---: |

Chipo's total is 3,000 + 6,000 − 775 = 8,225 and Sipho's is 2,000 + 4,000 − 775 = 5,225; together 13,450, the profit for the year. Sipho also receives the 800 loan interest, but as a creditor of the business, not as a share of profit.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Deducting interest on drawings"}
Interest on drawings is charged **to** the partners, so it is **added** in the appropriation account and debited to their current accounts.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting loan interest in the appropriation account"}
Interest on a partner's loan is an expense in the income statement. Deduct it before you reach the profit for the year.
:::

:::mistake{title="Sharing the whole profit in the ratio"}
The ratio applies only to the residual profit, after interest and salaries.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving out salaries when profit is low"}
Salaries and interest on capital are given in full; any shortfall is a residual loss shared in the ratio.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring the time of drawings or capital changes"}
Interest is for the period the money was out of, or in, the business. Count the months carefully.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Show every item partner by partner, with a total for each group. Examiners award marks for each correct line, even if a later figure is wrong.
:::

:::exam-tip
Finish with the check: the amounts credited to the partners, less the interest on drawings, must equal the profit for the year.
:::

:::exam-tip
Read whether the salary is stated "per year" or "per month", and whether the rate of interest is "per annum".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The appropriation account shares a partnership's profit for the year between the partners.
- Interest on partners' loans is an expense, deducted in the income statement first.
- Add interest on drawings; deduct interest on capital and salaries; share the residual profit or loss in the ratio.
- Interest is rate × amount × time; drawings interest runs from the date of drawing to the year end.
- Salaries and interest on capital are given in full, so a residual loss is possible.
- Check: interest on capital + salaries + shares − interest on drawings = profit for the year.
:::
