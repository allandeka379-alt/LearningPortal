## Introduction

Once the appropriation account of T & N Transport has shared out the year's profit, every figure in it must be posted to the partners' own accounts. Tatenda and Nyasha also need to see how much each of them has taken out during the year, and whether either has drawn more than his or her share. In a sole trader's books all of this goes through one capital account. A partnership normally keeps **two** accounts for each partner: a **capital account** for the long-term investment, and a **current account** for the year-to-year share of profit and drawings.

Current accounts are tested in nearly every partnership question in ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Cambridge 0452 Paper 2. Examiners expect the **columnar** layout, with one money column for each partner on each side, and they often include a partner whose current account ends with a **debit** balance. This lesson shows how to prepare the accounts and present them in the statement of financial position.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use **capital account**, **current account**, **fixed capital** and **fluctuating capital**. ZIMSEC papers may say **net profit** where Cambridge says **profit for the year**, and some ZIMSEC questions call the statement of financial position extract the "financed by" section. The layouts are the same.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Capital account (fixed capital)"}
A partner's account showing the long-term capital invested. Under the fixed capital method it changes only when capital is introduced or withdrawn by agreement.
:::

:::definition{term="Current account"}
A partner's account that records the year's appropriations (interest on capital, salary, share of profit or loss) and the partner's drawings and interest on drawings.
:::

:::definition{term="Fluctuating capital"}
A method that keeps only one account per partner, in which every appropriation and all drawings are recorded, so the capital changes every year.
:::

:::definition{term="Debit balance on a current account"}
The position when a partner has drawn more (including interest on drawings and any share of loss) than the amounts credited to him or her. The partner owes this amount to the business.
:::

## Fixed capital and current accounts

Partners usually agree to keep their capital **fixed**. This has three advantages:

- the capital agreed in the partnership agreement stays visible, so interest on capital is easy to calculate;
- the current account shows clearly whether a partner has drawn more than he or she earned;
- a partner who keeps drawing heavily cannot hide the fact that the capital is being run down.

Entries in each account:

| Current account: debit side | Current account: credit side |
| --- | --- |
| Opening debit balance | Opening credit balance |
| Drawings (cash, goods, private expenses) | Interest on capital |
| Interest on drawings | Partner's salary |
| Share of residual loss | Share of residual profit |
| Closing credit balance c/d | Closing debit balance c/d |

The **capital account** is credited with capital introduced and debited with capital withdrawn. Nothing from the appropriation account goes there under the fixed capital method.

With **fluctuating capitals** there is no current account: all the items in the table above are entered in the capital account itself. A question will tell you which method to use; if it gives both capital and current account balances, use fixed capitals.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: current accounts of T & N Transport]
On 1 January 2025 Tatenda's current account had a **credit** balance of US\$1,850 and Nyasha's a **debit** balance of US\$420. Their fixed capitals were US\$40,000 and US\$25,000. From the appropriation account for 2025: interest on capital Tatenda 2,400, Nyasha 1,500; salary Nyasha 4,800; interest on drawings Tatenda 360, Nyasha 240; shares of profit Tatenda 14,112, Nyasha 9,408. Drawings were Tatenda 6,000 and Nyasha 4,000.

1. Tatenda: credits 1,850 + 2,400 + 14,112 = 18,362; debits 6,000 + 360 = 6,360; closing credit balance 12,002.
2. Nyasha: credits 1,500 + 4,800 + 9,408 = 15,708; debits 420 + 4,000 + 240 = 4,660; closing credit balance 11,048.

## Partners in the statement of financial position

The "capital" section of a partnership's statement of financial position shows both accounts for every partner:

1. the capital accounts, with their total;
2. the current accounts, with their total; a **debit** balance is shown in brackets and **deducted**;
3. the grand total, which equals the net assets in the top half of the statement.

Some textbooks show the current accounts in full (opening balance, add appropriations, less drawings) inside the statement. Either layout is accepted if the closing figures are right.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: a debit current account at Thandeka and Rudo, Harare]
Thandeka and Rudo run a hair salon and beauty supply shop. Fixed capitals: Thandeka US\$30,000, Rudo US\$20,000. Current account balances on 1 January 2025: Thandeka US\$1,500 credit, Rudo US\$310 credit. For 2025: interest on capital Thandeka 1,500, Rudo 1,000; salary Rudo 2,400; shares of profit Thandeka 4,200, Rudo 2,800; drawings Thandeka 4,860, Rudo 7,330. There is no interest on drawings.

1. Thandeka: 1,500 + 1,500 + 4,200 − 4,860 = 2,340 credit.
2. Rudo: 310 + 1,000 + 2,400 + 2,800 = 6,510 credited, but she drew 7,330, so her balance is 820 **debit**.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Posting appropriations to the capital account"}
Under the fixed capital method, interest on capital, salaries, shares of profit and drawings all go to the **current** account. The capital account changes only for capital introduced or withdrawn.
:::

:::mistake{title="Crediting interest on drawings"}
Interest on drawings is a charge on the partner: it goes on the **debit** side of the current account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adding a debit current account balance"}
A debit balance is owed by the partner. Show it in brackets and deduct it in the statement of financial position.
:::

:::mistake{title="Placing the balances c/d on the wrong side"}
Balance each partner's column separately. One partner may need a balance c/d on the debit side and the other on the credit side.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Use one columnar account with a column per partner. It saves time, and examiners mark each line for each partner.
:::

:::exam-tip
Before posting, write down each partner's credits and debits as a quick sum. If a partner's debits are larger, expect a debit balance c/d on the credit side.
:::

:::exam-tip
Bring the balances down to 1 January of the next year. The b/d line often carries a mark.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Partners usually keep a fixed capital account and a current account each.
- Current account credits: opening credit balance, interest on capital, salary, share of profit.
- Current account debits: opening debit balance, drawings, interest on drawings, share of loss.
- Prepare current accounts in columnar form and balance each partner's column separately.
- In the statement of financial position, show capital accounts and current accounts; deduct a debit current account balance.
- Fluctuating capital puts all of these entries in one capital account per partner.
:::
