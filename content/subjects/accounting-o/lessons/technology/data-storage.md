## Introduction

One rainy season the roof of Rudo's bakery office in Bulawayo leaked over a weekend. On Monday the box files holding two years of invoices, wage sheets and bank statements were a soggy mess. A month later a tax officer asked to see the records supporting her 2024 return. Rudo had nothing to show.

Accounting records are only useful if they still exist, can be found, and have not been seen or changed by the wrong people. Businesses must keep records for years: for tax inspections, for disputes with customers and suppliers, and to compare results over time. This lesson is part of Cambridge 0452 topic 7 (accounting concepts and modern practice). You may be asked to explain how digital applications are used for records, to compare storage methods, and to explain why safe and sustainable storage matters and what can go wrong without it.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Data storage"}
The way a business keeps its accounting records (paper or digital) so that they can be found and used later.
:::

:::definition{term="Back-up"}
An extra copy of data, kept in a different place, so that the records can be restored if the original is lost or damaged.
:::

:::definition{term="Cloud storage"}
Keeping data on servers owned by a service provider and reached over the internet, usually for a monthly or yearly fee.
:::

:::definition{term="Data storage device"}
Equipment that holds data locally, such as a computer's hard drive, an external hard drive or a USB flash drive.
:::

:::definition{term="Sustainable storage"}
Storing records in a way that limits harm to the environment (less paper, energy and electronic waste) while keeping them safe for as long as they are needed.
:::

## Digital applications for accounting records

Businesses can use digital applications for **all or part** of their records:

- **accounting software** for books of prime entry, ledgers and statements;
- **spreadsheets** for journals, budgets and ratio calculations;
- **digital source documents**: invoices sent by email, receipts photographed with a phone, bank statements downloaded from online banking;
- **mobile money and online banking records**, which provide a permanent history of payments and receipts;
- **point-of-sale systems** that record every cash sale and update inventory.

A small business may keep a paper cash book but store photographs of its receipts; a larger one may be fully digital. Either way, the records have to be stored.

## Methods of storage compared

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manual (paper files, box files, cabinets) | No power or skills needed; easy to inspect; cannot be hacked | Takes space; damaged by fire, water and insects; easily lost or stolen; hard to search; uses paper |
| Storage device (computer drive, external hard drive, USB drive) | Cheap; large capacity; quick to search; no internet needed | Can fail, be lost or stolen; viruses; a single copy in the office is lost with the office |
| Cloud storage | Copies kept away from the premises; reached from anywhere; provider makes its own back-ups; easy to share with an accountant | Needs internet; ongoing fees; depends on the provider; risk of hacking if passwords are weak |
| Other digital services (email archives, online banking and mobile money histories) | Records kept automatically; often free; useful evidence | Not designed as the main record; may be deleted after a time; scattered across many places |

Most businesses use a **combination**: for example, working files on a computer, a weekly back-up on an external drive kept at home, and a cloud copy.

:::worked-solution{title="Example 1: choosing storage for Rudo's bakery"}
After the flood Rudo lists what she needs and checks each method against it.

| Need | Paper in the office | External drive kept at home | Cloud service |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Survives a fire or flood at the bakery | No | Yes | Yes |
| Works during a power cut or without internet | Yes | Only with a charged laptop | No |
| Can be searched quickly for one invoice | No | Yes | Yes |
| Can be shared with her accountant in Harare | Only by posting copies | Only by copying files | Yes |
| Protected from people who should not see it | Locked cabinet | Password on the files | Password and two-step login |

1. No single method meets every need, so Rudo decides to scan her documents and keep the digital records in **two** places: an external drive kept at home and a cloud service.
2. She keeps the original paper documents that the tax authority may ask to see in a lockable, waterproof box, off the floor.
3. The decision is based on **risk**: the flood showed that one copy in one place is not enough.
:::

## Why safe storage matters, and the risks of getting it wrong

Records must be stored **safely** because businesses need them:

- as **evidence** for tax authorities (records are normally kept for several years) and in disputes;
- to prepare **financial statements** and compare performance over time;
- to know **who owes the business money** and whom it owes;
- to protect **personal data** about customers and employees.

If records are not stored safely, the business risks:

- **loss** of data through fire, flood, theft, device failure or viruses, so debts cannot be collected and statements cannot be prepared;
- **unauthorised access**, so confidential information (wages, customer details, bank details) is seen or misused;
- **fraud**, where records are changed to hide theft;
- **penalties** from the tax authority when records cannot be produced;
- **damage to reputation** if customers' data are leaked.

Good practice includes regular back-ups kept in a different place, strong passwords, giving staff access only to what they need, antivirus software, and checking that back-ups can actually be restored.

## Why sustainable storage matters

Sustainable storage reduces harm to the environment. Printing and filing every document uses paper, ink and space; storing digitally reduces this. But digital storage is not free of impact: devices use electricity, data centres use energy and water, and old devices become electronic waste. Sustainable choices include printing only what must be kept on paper, keeping records only for as long as required, disposing of old devices responsibly (and wiping the data first), and choosing efficient services.

:::worked-solution{title="Example 2: comparing three years of storage costs"}
Rudo compares the cost of three options over three years (36 months).

| Option | Workings | Cost over 3 years US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Two external drives (one at the bakery, one at home) | 2 × 75 | 150 |
| Cloud service at US\$6 a month | 6 × 36 | 216 |
| Paper: fireproof cabinet US\$180, plus paper and ink US\$9 a month | 180 + (9 × 36) | 504 |

1. The drives are cheapest, US\$66 less than the cloud (216 − 150), but both are in Rudo's hands: if she forgets to update the home copy, it is out of date.
2. The cloud costs more than the drives but copies automatically and is reached from anywhere.
3. Paper costs US\$288 more than the cloud (504 − 216), uses the most materials, and is still at risk of theft.
4. Cost is only one factor: safety, convenience and sustainability also count, which is why many businesses combine a drive and the cloud.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying cloud storage has no risks"}
Cloud data can be hacked if passwords are weak, cannot be reached without internet, and depend on the provider continuing to operate.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing a back-up with a copy in the same place"}
A second copy on the same computer, or in the same office, is lost in the same fire or theft. A back-up must be kept separately.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating 'sustainable' as 'paperless' only"}
Digital storage also uses energy and creates electronic waste. Mention both sides.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "compare" questions, state an advantage **and** a disadvantage of each method, using the same features (cost, safety, access, power) so that the comparison is clear.
:::

:::exam-tip
For "explain the risks", link each risk to a consequence for the business: "If the records are lost in a fire, the business cannot prove the amounts customers owe, so it may not collect them."
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Digital applications (software, spreadsheets, digital documents, online banking) can hold all or part of the records.
- Storage may be manual, on devices or in the cloud; each has advantages and disadvantages.
- Records must be stored safely as evidence, for statements and to protect confidential data.
- Risks include loss, unauthorised access, fraud, penalties and damage to reputation.
- Back-ups kept in a separate place, passwords and limited access reduce the risks.
- Sustainable storage limits paper, energy and electronic waste.
:::
