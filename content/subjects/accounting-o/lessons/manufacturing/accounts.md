## Introduction

Tendai Mapfumo makes fitted kitchen units in a workshop in Harare's industrial area. At the end of each year his accountant prepares three statements, not two. First comes the **manufacturing account**, which works out what the units completed during the year cost to make. That figure then takes the place of "purchases" in the **income statement**, and the **statement of financial position** shows not one inventory but three: raw materials, work in progress and finished goods.

Both ZIMSEC (Form 4) and Cambridge 0452 ask candidates to prepare a manufacturing account, the income statement and the statement of financial position of a manufacturer from a trial balance with year-end notes. The questions look long, but they test the same adjustments you already know. The new skills are deciding **which statement each cost belongs in** and **where each of the three inventories goes**.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC questions may say **trading and profit and loss account** for the income statement, **net profit**, **stock** for inventory and **cost of production** for the production cost of goods completed. Cambridge 0452 uses **statement of profit or loss**, **profit for the year**, **inventory**, **other payables** (accruals) and **other receivables** (prepayments). The layouts are the same.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Manufacturing account"}
A statement prepared before the income statement that calculates the production cost of goods completed in the year: raw materials consumed, prime cost, factory overheads and the work in progress adjustment.
:::

:::definition{term="Finished goods"}
Products that are complete and ready for sale. Their opening and closing inventories are used in the cost of sales section of the income statement.
:::

:::definition{term="Apportionment between factory and office"}
Splitting a shared cost, such as rent or insurance of a building used partly as a factory and partly as offices, in the ratio given in the question.
:::

## How the three statements link

| Item | Manufacturing account | Income statement | Statement of financial position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raw materials inventory | Opening added, closing deducted | — | Closing: current asset |
| Work in progress | Opening added, closing deducted | — | Closing: current asset |
| Finished goods inventory | — | Opening added, closing deducted in cost of sales | Closing: current asset |
| Direct costs and factory overheads | Yes | — | Accruals and prepayments as liabilities and assets |
| Office, selling and finance costs | — | Expenses | Accruals and prepayments as liabilities and assets |
| Production cost of goods completed | Final figure | Replaces purchases in cost of sales | — |

Cost of sales of a manufacturer = opening inventory of finished goods + production cost of goods completed (+ any purchases of finished goods) − closing inventory of finished goods.

## A method that works every time

1. Read every note and mark each trial balance item **M** (manufacturing account), **I** (income statement) or **S** (statement of financial position). Mark shared items **M/I** and write the split beside them.
2. Adjust the expenses first: add accruals, deduct prepayments, then split shared costs. Do the adjustment **before** the split, unless the note says the accrual belongs to one part only.
3. Calculate depreciation, and charge it to the part of the business that uses the asset: factory machinery and factory buildings to overheads; office equipment and delivery vans to the income statement.
4. Prepare the manufacturing account, then the income statement, then the statement of financial position.
5. Check: every adjustment appears twice, the three closing inventories are all in current assets, and net assets equal closing capital.

## Bought-in finished goods and shared costs

Some manufacturers also buy finished items to sell alongside their own products. These purchases of finished goods are **not** raw materials: they skip the factory and are added in the income statement's cost of sales, after the production cost of goods completed.

When a cost is shared, use the fraction given and make sure the two parts add back to the full adjusted figure. A common question gives electricity for the whole building with "five-sixths relates to the factory". The factory part is an overhead; the office part is an expense in the income statement.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Sipho's Soap Works]
Sipho makes soap in Bulawayo. For the year ended 31 December 2025: raw materials at 1 January US\$2,150, purchases of raw materials US\$18,640, raw materials at 31 December US\$2,480; direct wages US\$12,300; electricity US\$7,200 (five-sixths factory); depreciation of factory equipment US\$2,700; factory maintenance US\$1,360; work in progress at 1 January US\$1,940 and at 31 December US\$2,210. He also bought soap dishes to resell for US\$3,200. Finished goods were US\$5,600 at 1 January and US\$6,350 at 31 December. Revenue was US\$79,500; office wages US\$9,800; selling expenses US\$2,450.

1. Electricity: factory 7,200 × 5/6 = 6,000; office 1,200.
2. Production cost of goods completed: 30,610 + 10,060 + 1,940 − 2,210 = 40,400.
3. Cost of sales: 5,600 + 40,400 + 3,200 − 6,350 = 42,850; gross profit 79,500 − 42,850 = 36,650.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Putting finished goods in the manufacturing account"}
Opening and closing inventories of finished goods belong in the cost of sales section of the income statement. Only raw materials and work in progress are adjusted in the manufacturing account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Splitting before adjusting"}
Adjust a shared expense for accruals or prepayments first, then split it. Splitting the trial balance figure and adjusting only one part gives the wrong charge to both statements.
:::

:::mistake{title="Charging all depreciation to the factory"}
Depreciation follows the asset: factory machinery is an overhead, but office equipment and delivery vans are income statement expenses.
:::

:::mistake{title="Showing one inventory figure"}
The statement of financial position must show raw materials, work in progress and finished goods. Missing one understates current assets and the statement will not balance.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write the M / I / S letters next to the trial balance before you start. It takes two minutes and stops items from being used twice or forgotten.
:::

:::exam-tip
Show the split of every shared cost as a working (for example "Rent 18,000 × 3/4 = 13,500"). Method marks are available even if the figure carried forward is wrong.
:::

:::exam-tip
Carry your own production cost into the income statement. Examiners give "own figure" marks, so a wrong manufacturing account does not lose you the income statement marks.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A manufacturer prepares a manufacturing account, then the income statement, then the statement of financial position.
- The manufacturing account ends with the production cost of goods completed, which replaces purchases in cost of sales.
- Cost of sales = opening finished goods + production cost (+ purchases of finished goods) − closing finished goods.
- Shared costs are adjusted first, then split between factory (overheads) and office (expenses).
- Depreciation is charged where the asset is used.
- Three closing inventories (raw materials, work in progress, finished goods) appear in current assets.
:::
