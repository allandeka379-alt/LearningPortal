## Introduction

Nyasha Moyo makes school desks and chairs in a small factory in Gweru. A trader such as a hardware shop buys finished goods and sells them, so its cost of sales is simply what it paid for the goods. Nyasha's business is different: she buys timber, glue and screws, pays carpenters to cut and join them, runs machines that use electricity and wear out, and rents a workshop. Only when all of these costs are added together does she know what her finished desks cost to make.

Knowing that figure matters. Nyasha needs it to set a selling price that covers her costs, to value the desks still in the store at the year end, and to see whether any cost is getting out of hand. In the examination, both ZIMSEC (Form 4, Manufacturing Accounts) and Cambridge 0452 ask you to **classify costs** and to **calculate prime cost and the production cost of goods completed**, adjusting for work in progress. This lesson builds those figures step by step; the next lesson puts them into a full manufacturing account and the financial statements.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use **prime cost**, **factory overheads** and **work in progress**. The final figure of the manufacturing account may be called the **production cost of goods completed**, the **cost of production** or the **factory cost of goods completed**: they mean the same thing. Factory overheads are also called **indirect manufacturing costs** or **factory indirect expenses**.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Direct cost"}
A cost that can be traced to each unit made: direct materials, direct labour and direct expenses.
:::

:::definition{term="Indirect cost (factory overhead)"}
A cost of running the factory that cannot be traced to a particular unit, such as factory rent, a supervisor's salary or depreciation of factory machinery.
:::

:::definition{term="Prime cost"}
The total of the direct costs: direct materials consumed + direct labour + direct expenses.
:::

:::definition{term="Work in progress"}
Goods that have been started but not finished at a given date. They have used some materials, labour and overheads but cannot yet be sold.
:::

:::definition{term="Production cost of goods completed"}
Total factory cost (prime cost + factory overheads) adjusted for opening and closing work in progress. It is the cost of the finished goods transferred to the warehouse during the year.
:::

## Classifying costs

Ask two questions about every cost.

1. **Is it a factory cost at all?** Office salaries, the manager's car, advertising and delivery costs belong to administration or selling. They never enter the manufacturing account; they are expenses in the income statement.
2. **If it is a factory cost, can it be traced to each unit?** If yes, it is direct. If no, it is an overhead.

- **Direct materials** are the materials that become part of the product: timber in a desk, fabric in a uniform, flour in bread. Carriage inwards on raw materials is part of their cost.
- **Direct labour** is the wages of the workers who make the product (machinists, carpenters, bakers). Wages of people who support production without making it, such as a supervisor, storekeeper or cleaner, are **indirect wages** and go under overheads.
- **Direct expenses** are other costs traced to each unit, usually royalties or licence fees paid per item made, or the hire of a special machine for one product.
- **Factory overheads** include factory rent and rates, factory power and lighting, factory insurance, repairs to factory machinery, depreciation of factory buildings and machinery, and indirect wages.

When a shared cost (for example rent for a building that holds both the factory and the office) is given with a split such as "three-quarters to the factory", put only the factory share into overheads and the rest into the income statement expenses.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Nyasha's Furniture, classifying costs and finding prime cost]
Nyasha's records for the year ended 31 December 2025 show: raw materials at 1 January US\$4,200; purchases of raw materials US\$38,600; carriage inwards on raw materials US\$900; raw materials at 31 December US\$5,100; carpenters' wages US\$24,500; royalties to the desk designer (paid per chair made) US\$1,200; factory supervisor's salary US\$9,000; factory rent US\$6,000; factory electricity US\$2,750; depreciation of factory machinery US\$3,400; factory cleaning US\$800. She also paid office salaries of US\$7,800 and delivery van costs of US\$2,300.

**Step 1: classify.**

| Cost | Classification |
| --- | --- |
| Timber and glue used | Direct materials |
| Carpenters' wages | Direct labour |
| Royalty per chair to the designer | Direct expense |
| Factory supervisor's salary | Factory overhead (indirect) |
| Factory rent and electricity | Factory overhead (indirect) |
| Depreciation of factory machinery | Factory overhead (indirect) |
| Office salaries | Not a manufacturing cost (administration) |
| Delivery van running costs | Not a manufacturing cost (selling and distribution) |

**Step 2: calculate.**

**Calculation of prime cost and factory cost**

|  | US\$ | US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Opening inventory of raw materials | 4,200 |  |
| Add: Purchases of raw materials | 38,600 |  |
| Add: Carriage inwards on raw materials | 900 |  |
|  | 43,700 |  |
| Less: Closing inventory of raw materials | (5,100) |  |
| Cost of raw materials consumed |  | 38,600 |
| Direct labour |  | 24,500 |
| Direct expenses: royalties |  | 1,200 |
| **Prime cost** |  | 64,300 |
| Add: Factory overheads |  |  |
| Factory supervisor's salary | 9,000 |  |
| Factory rent | 6,000 |  |
| Factory electricity | 2,750 |  |
| Depreciation of factory machinery | 3,400 |  |
| Factory cleaning | 800 | 21,950 |
| **Total factory cost** |  | 86,250 |

1. Raw materials consumed: 4,200 + 38,600 + 900 − 5,100 = 38,600. (It is a coincidence that this equals the purchases figure: the inventory rose by 900 and the carriage added 900.)
2. Prime cost: 38,600 + 24,500 + 1,200 = 64,300.
3. Overheads: 9,000 + 6,000 + 2,750 + 3,400 + 800 = 21,950. Total factory cost: 64,300 + 21,950 = 86,250.
4. The office salaries (US\$7,800) and van costs (US\$2,300) are left out: they go to the income statement.
:::

## Raw materials consumed

Materials are not used up the moment they are bought. Some are still in the store at the year end, and some were already in the store at the start. The cost of raw materials **consumed** (used) is therefore:

opening inventory of raw materials + purchases + carriage inwards − returns of raw materials − closing inventory of raw materials.

This is the same idea as cost of sales in a trading business, applied to materials instead of goods for resale.

## Work in progress

On 31 December some desks will be half-built: the timber has been cut but not varnished. Their cost is already included in this year's materials, labour and overheads, yet they are not finished goods. So:

- **closing work in progress is deducted** from total factory cost (it is carried forward as an asset), and
- **opening work in progress is added** (last year's half-finished goods were completed this year).

Production cost of goods completed = prime cost + factory overheads + opening work in progress − closing work in progress.

Work in progress is adjusted **after** factory overheads, because half-finished goods have absorbed overheads as well as direct costs.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Chipo's Uniform Factory, adjusting for work in progress]
Chipo makes school uniforms in Chinhoyi. For the year ended 31 December 2025 her direct materials consumed were US\$26,350, direct labour US\$19,800 and a design licence fee paid per uniform US\$1,700. Factory overheads were: indirect wages (supervisor and cleaner) US\$7,400, factory rent US\$4,800, factory power US\$2,520 and depreciation of sewing machines US\$1,600. Work in progress was US\$3,640 at 1 January 2025 and US\$4,410 at 31 December 2025. She completed 1,000 uniforms in the year.

**Production cost of goods completed**

|  | US\$ | US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Direct materials consumed |  | 26,350 |
| Direct labour |  | 19,800 |
| Direct expenses: design licence fee |  | 1,700 |
| **Prime cost** |  | 47,850 |
| Add: Factory overheads |  |  |
| Indirect wages (supervisor and cleaner) | 7,400 |  |
| Factory rent | 4,800 |  |
| Factory power | 2,520 |  |
| Depreciation of sewing machines | 1,600 | 16,320 |
| Total factory cost |  | 64,170 |
| Add: Opening work in progress |  | 3,640 |
|  |  | 67,810 |
| Less: Closing work in progress |  | (4,410) |
| **Production cost of goods completed** |  | 63,400 |

1. Prime cost: 26,350 + 19,800 + 1,700 = 47,850.
2. Overheads: 7,400 + 4,800 + 2,520 + 1,600 = 16,320; total factory cost 47,850 + 16,320 = 64,170.
3. Work in progress: 64,170 + 3,640 − 4,410 = 63,400.
4. Cost per uniform completed: 63,400 ÷ 1,000 = US\$63.40. Chipo must sell above this, and also cover her office and selling expenses, to make a profit.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Putting office and selling costs in the factory"}
Office rent, office salaries, advertising and delivery costs are not manufacturing costs. Including them overstates prime cost or overheads and understates the expenses in the income statement.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating all wages as direct labour"}
Only the wages of the workers who make the product are direct. Supervisors, storekeepers, cleaners and maintenance staff are paid indirect wages, which are factory overheads.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using purchases instead of materials consumed"}
Prime cost uses the raw materials **consumed**, after adjusting for opening and closing inventory of raw materials and carriage inwards, not the purchases figure.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adjusting work in progress in the wrong place or direction"}
Work in progress comes after factory overheads. Opening work in progress is added and closing work in progress is deducted; reversing them changes the answer by twice the difference.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Label every subtotal: "Cost of raw materials consumed", "Prime cost", "Total factory cost", "Production cost of goods completed". Examiners award marks for correctly labelled subtotals even when a figure above is wrong.
:::

:::exam-tip
Read the notes for splits such as "rent is to be shared: factory three-quarters, office one-quarter". Show the calculation beside the figure.
:::

:::exam-tip
In a classification question, give the precise category (direct labour, factory overhead, administration expense), not just "direct" or "indirect".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Direct costs (materials, labour, expenses) can be traced to each unit; factory overheads cannot.
- Raw materials consumed = opening raw materials + purchases + carriage inwards − returns − closing raw materials.
- Prime cost = direct materials consumed + direct labour + direct expenses.
- Total factory cost = prime cost + factory overheads.
- Production cost of goods completed = total factory cost + opening work in progress − closing work in progress.
- Office, selling and distribution costs are not manufacturing costs; they go to the income statement.
:::
