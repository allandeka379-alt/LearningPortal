## Introduction

Thandeka runs a building-supplies business in Masvingo and buys cement, timber and roofing sheets on credit from about forty suppliers. Each supplier has an account in her **purchases ledger**. Just as the sales ledger control account checks the customers' accounts, a **purchases ledger control account** checks the suppliers' accounts. It is built from the totals of the purchases journal, the returns outwards journal, the cash book and the general journal, and its balance should equal the total amount Thandeka owes her suppliers.

The purchases ledger control account is the mirror image of the sales ledger control account. If you understand one, you understand the other: just remember that suppliers' accounts normally have **credit** balances. Examiners on both boards frequently set the two control accounts together, linked by a contra.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC calls the suppliers' ledger the **trade payables ledger** (or creditors ledger) and the account the **trade payables control account** or creditors control account. Cambridge uses **purchases ledger** and **purchases ledger control account**. Both use **discount received**, **returns outwards** (purchases returns) and **contra**.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Purchases ledger control account"}
A summary account in the general ledger, prepared from totals of the books of prime entry, whose balance should equal the total of the suppliers' balances in the purchases (trade payables) ledger.
:::

:::definition{term="Debit balance on a supplier's account"}
An amount owed **to** the business by a supplier, for example after the business overpaid or returned goods it had already paid for.
:::

## What goes where

The account is a summary supplier's account. Ask: "does this increase or decrease what we owe our suppliers?"

- **Credit side** (increases what we owe): opening credit balances, credit purchases, refunds **received** from suppliers (these cancel debit balances), and closing **debit** balances carried down.
- **Debit side** (decreases what we owe): opening debit balances, payments to suppliers, discount received, returns outwards, contras with the sales ledger, and the closing **credit** balance carried down.

Sources of information:

| Item | Source (book of prime entry) | Side of the control account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Credit purchases | Purchases journal (total) | Credit |
| Returns outwards | Returns outwards journal (total) | Debit |
| Payments to suppliers | Cash book (credit side) | Debit |
| Discount received | Cash book (discount column) | Debit |
| Refunds received from suppliers | Cash book (debit side) | Credit |
| Contras (set-offs) with the sales ledger | General journal | Debit |

Items that never appear: **cash purchases** (no supplier account), **trade discount**, and purchases of non-current assets on credit **unless** the question says the supplier's account is in the purchases ledger (normally they are kept separately as other payables).

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Thandeka's purchases ledger control account]
Thandeka's records for December 2025: amount owed to suppliers on 1 December US\$5,380; credit purchases US\$22,640; payments to suppliers US\$20,155; discount received US\$497; returns outwards US\$865.

1. Credit side: opening balance 5,380 and credit purchases 22,640, total 28,020.
2. Debit side: payments 20,155, discount received 497, returns outwards 865, total 21,517.
3. Closing credit balance: $28,020 - 21,517 = 6,503$.

**Purchases ledger control account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 31 Dec | Bank | 20,155 | 1 Dec | Balance b/d | 5,380 |
| 31 Dec | Discount received | 497 | 31 Dec | Purchases | 22,640 |
| 31 Dec | Returns outwards | 865 |  |  |  |
| 31 Dec | Balance c/d | 6,503 |  |  |  |
|  |  | **28,020** |  |  | **28,020** |
|  |  |  | 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 6,503 |

Thandeka owes her suppliers US\$6,503 at 31 December 2025. This is the trade payables figure in her statement of financial position.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Tendai's account with a contra and debit balances]
Tendai's purchases ledger figures for December 2025 were:

| Item | US\$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Balances on 1 December: credit | 7,940 |
| Balances on 1 December: debit | 120 |
| Credit purchases | 31,285 |
| Cash purchases | 2,600 |
| Payments to suppliers | 28,760 |
| Discount received | 612 |
| Returns outwards | 1,048 |
| Contra with the sales ledger | 530 |
| Refund received from a supplier with a debit balance | 120 |
| Debit balances on 31 December | 85 |

1. Ignore the cash purchases.
2. Credit side: opening credit balances 7,940, purchases 31,285, the refund received 120, closing debit balances c/d 85: total 39,430.
3. Debit side: opening debit balances 120, payments 28,760, discount received 612, returns outwards 1,048, contra 530: total 31,070.
4. Closing credit balance: $39,430 - 31,070 = 8,360$.

**Purchases ledger control account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 Dec | Balance b/d | 120 | 1 Dec | Balance b/d | 7,940 |
| 31 Dec | Bank | 28,760 | 31 Dec | Purchases | 31,285 |
| 31 Dec | Discount received | 612 | 31 Dec | Bank (refund from supplier) | 120 |
| 31 Dec | Returns outwards | 1,048 | 31 Dec | Balance c/d | 85 |
| 31 Dec | Sales ledger control (contra) | 530 |  |  |  |
| 31 Dec | Balance c/d | 8,360 |  |  |  |
|  |  | **39,430** |  |  | **39,430** |
| 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 85 | 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 8,360 |

The contra appears on the **debit** side here and on the credit side of the sales ledger control account: the amount a customer owes Tendai is set against what Tendai owes the same person as a supplier.
:::

## Comparing the two control accounts

| Item | Sales ledger control | Purchases ledger control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal balance | Debit | Credit |
| Credit transactions | Sales (debit) | Purchases (credit) |
| Money | Receipts (credit) | Payments (debit) |
| Cash discount | Discount allowed (credit) | Discount received (debit) |
| Returns | Returns inwards (credit) | Returns outwards (debit) |
| Contra | Credit | Debit |
| Refunds | Paid to customers (debit) | Received from suppliers (credit) |

## The impact of digital recording

Many businesses now record purchases and payments in accounting software:

- Each supplier's account and the control account are updated **automatically** from the same entry, so the control account always equals the total of the ledger and arithmetical errors in posting are rare.
- Up-to-date totals of trade payables are available at any time, which helps cash planning and paying suppliers on time.
- Because the software keeps them in agreement, the control account no longer **detects** posting errors in the same way; errors of input (wrong supplier, wrong amount, a missed invoice) still happen and must be found by checking suppliers' statements.
- Access controls, passwords and audit trails replace part of the fraud protection that came from dividing the work between two clerks.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the sales ledger sides"}
In the purchases ledger control account, purchases and opening balances are **credits**; payments, discounts, returns and contras are **debits**. Reversing them is the most common error.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting a refund received on the debit side"}
A refund received from a supplier cancels a debit balance (the supplier owed us). It is a credit in the control account, with the debit in the cash book.
:::

:::mistake{title="Including cash purchases"}
Only credit transactions with suppliers in the purchases ledger are included.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Draw the T account first and label the sides "decreases what we owe" (debit) and "increases what we owe" (credit). Then place every item.
:::

:::exam-tip
If the question gives the closing debit balances, carry them down on the credit side first; the closing credit balance is then the balancing figure. Finish by checking that both sides total the same.
:::

:::exam-tip
Cambridge often asks for one advantage and one limitation of computerised control accounts. Name the automatic updating as the advantage and input errors or reliance on the system as the limitation.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The purchases ledger control account summarises the suppliers' accounts; its normal balance is a credit equal to total trade payables.
- Credit side: opening credit balances, credit purchases, refunds received, closing debit balances c/d.
- Debit side: opening debit balances, payments, discount received, returns outwards, contras, closing credit balance c/d.
- Cash purchases and trade discount are excluded.
- Digital systems update the ledger and the control account together, giving instant totals, but input errors must still be checked.
:::
