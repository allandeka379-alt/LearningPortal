## Introduction

Chipo keeps the books of a grocery wholesaler in Harare. When she finds a mistake in the ledger she does not rub it out or cross it through. Accounting records must show clearly what was recorded and how it was put right, so that an auditor, the owner or the tax authority can follow every change. Instead, Chipo writes a **correcting entry in the general journal**, with a short explanation called a **narrative**, and then posts it to the ledger like any other journal entry.

Correcting errors is one of the most common examination tasks. ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Cambridge Paper 2 both ask candidates to write journal entries to correct a list of errors, often followed by a suspense account or a statement of corrected profit. This lesson deals with the errors that do **not** affect the trial balance, so no suspense account is needed. The next lesson adds the suspense account.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards call the book used for corrections the **general journal** (or simply the journal). ZIMSEC questions may refer to **debtors** and **creditors**; Cambridge uses **trade receivables** and **trade payables**. Cambridge does not require folio columns; ZIMSEC journals sometimes include one. Narratives are expected by both boards.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Correcting entry"}
A journal entry that removes the effect of an error from the ledger and records the correct effect, so that every account shows the balance it would have had if no error had been made.
:::

:::definition{term="Narrative"}
A brief explanation written below a journal entry stating why the entry is made, for example "Receipt from R. Zhou wrongly credited to R. Zulu".
:::

## The three-step method

For every error, write down three things before you write the journal entry:

1. **What was done** (the wrong entries actually made).
2. **What should have been done** (the correct double entry).
3. **The correction**: the entries needed to turn (1) into (2).

Example: rent of US\$300 paid by bank was debited to the wages account.

| Step | Debit | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Done | Wages 300 | Bank 300 |
| Should have been | Rent 300 | Bank 300 |
| Correction | Rent 300 | Wages 300 |

The bank entry was already correct, so it does not appear in the correction. Only the accounts that are wrong are adjusted.

## Correcting each type of error

| Error | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Omission | Make the full original double entry now. |
| Commission | Debit (or credit) the correct account and reverse the wrong one. |
| Principle | Move the amount from the wrong class of account to the correct one. |
| Original entry | Adjust both accounts by the **difference** between the correct and the recorded amount. |
| Complete reversal | Enter **twice** the amount on the correct sides: once to cancel the wrong entry, once to make the right one. |
| Compensating | Adjust each of the accounts that were wrong by the amount of the error. |

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Omission, commission and principle]
Chipo found these errors at 31 December 2025, after the trial balance had agreed.

1. A credit sale of US\$940 to N. Dube had not been recorded. Nothing was done; the correct entry is debit N. Dube, credit sales. The correction is the full entry.
2. US\$315 received from R. Zhou by bank had been credited to R. Zulu's account. Done: credit R. Zulu. Should have been: credit R. Zhou. Correction: debit R. Zulu (to cancel the wrong credit) and credit R. Zhou.
3. A computer for office use costing US\$1,180 had been debited to office expenses. Done: debit office expenses. Should have been: debit office equipment. Correction: debit office equipment, credit office expenses.

**Journal (corrections)**

| Date | Details | Debit US\$ | Credit US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 31 Dec | N. Dube | 940 |  |
|  | Sales |  | 940 |
|  | *Credit sale to N. Dube omitted from the books* |  |  |
| 31 Dec | R. Zulu | 315 |  |
|  | R. Zhou |  | 315 |
|  | *Receipt from R. Zhou wrongly credited to R. Zulu* |  |  |
| 31 Dec | Office equipment | 1,180 |  |
|  | Office expenses |  | 1,180 |
|  | *Computer wrongly debited to office expenses* |  |  |

Each entry has equal debits and credits, and each has a narrative.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Original entry, complete reversal and compensating errors]
Three more errors were found in the same books.

1. A purchase invoice from Lowveld Traders for US\$2,370 was entered in the purchases journal as US\$2,730. Both purchases (debit) and Lowveld Traders (credit) are overstated by $2,730 - 2,370 = 360$. Correction: debit Lowveld Traders 360, credit purchases 360.
2. Cash sales of US\$560 were debited to the sales account and credited to the cash account. To cancel the wrong entries needs debit cash 560, credit sales 560; to make the correct entries needs another debit cash 560, credit sales 560. Combined: debit cash 1,120, credit sales 1,120.
3. The sales account and the wages account were both overcast by US\$150. Sales (a credit balance) is 150 too high, so debit sales 150; wages (a debit balance) is 150 too high, so credit wages 150.

**Journal (corrections)**

| Date | Details | Debit US\$ | Credit US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 31 Dec | Lowveld Traders | 360 |  |
|  | Purchases |  | 360 |
|  | *Invoice of US\$2,370 entered as US\$2,730 in the purchases journal* |  |  |
| 31 Dec | Cash | 1,120 |  |
|  | Sales |  | 1,120 |
|  | *Cash sales of US\$560 debited to sales and credited to cash; reversal corrected* |  |  |
| 31 Dec | Sales | 150 |  |
|  | Wages |  | 150 |
|  | *Sales and wages both overcast by US\$150* |  |  |
:::

## Errors involving drawings and returns

Some commonly examined errors need careful thought about which accounts are involved:

- **Goods taken by the owner** recorded nowhere: debit drawings, credit purchases (at cost).
- **Goods taken by the owner** debited to wages: debit drawings, credit wages.
- **Goods returned by a customer** entered in the returns outwards journal: the customer was debited and returns outwards credited. Correct: debit returns inwards, credit the customer. Correction: debit returns inwards, debit returns outwards (to cancel), credit the customer **twice** the amount.
- **Private electricity** paid from the business bank account and debited to electricity: debit drawings, credit electricity.

## Posting the corrections

After writing the journal, each line is posted to the ledger account it names. The wrong entry stays in the account; the correction sits beside it, and the balance becomes correct. In the example above, the office expenses account shows the original debit of 1,180 and a credit of 1,180 dated 31 December, so its balance no longer includes the computer.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Correcting the account that was already right"}
In "rent debited to wages", the bank entry was correct. Including bank in the correction would undo a correct entry and create a new error.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the full amount for an error of original entry"}
Only the **difference** between the correct and the recorded figure is adjusted. In Example 2 the correction is 360, not 2,370 or 2,730.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the single amount for a complete reversal"}
A reversal needs double the amount: one amount cancels the wrong entry, the second makes the right one.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving out the narrative"}
Examiners usually award a mark for narratives. Write a short, specific reason for each entry.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write the debit line first and the credit line underneath, slightly indented if you are writing by hand, with the narrative on the next line. Head the answer "Journal" and use Debit and Credit money columns.
:::

:::exam-tip
Before you write the entry, jot down the three lines Done / Should have been / Correction in pencil. It takes seconds and prevents most mistakes.
:::

:::exam-tip
Check that every correcting entry has equal debits and credits. If a question says the trial balance agreed, none of the corrections needs a suspense account.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Errors are corrected by journal entries with narratives, never by crossing out.
- Method: what was done, what should have been done, the correction.
- Omission: make the full entry. Commission and principle: move the amount to the right account. Original entry: adjust by the difference. Complete reversal: twice the amount. Compensating: adjust each wrong account.
- Only the accounts that are wrong appear in the correction.
:::
