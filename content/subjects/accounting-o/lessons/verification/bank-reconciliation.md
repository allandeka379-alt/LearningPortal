## Introduction

In the previous lesson Tatenda updated his cash book for bank charges, standing orders and other items he learned about from his bank statement. His updated cash book now shows US\$2,777 in the bank, but the statement shows US\$3,315. Is something wrong? Not necessarily. Two cheques he wrote at the end of December have not yet been paid by the bank, and the takings he deposited on the last afternoon reached his account only in January.

A **bank reconciliation statement** explains this kind of difference item by item, proving that the two records agree once timing is taken into account. It is prepared regularly (monthly is common) by every well-run business, from a tuck shop to a large company. ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Cambridge Paper 2 both set reconciliation questions, usually straight after an updated cash book.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use **bank reconciliation statement**, **unpresented cheques** and **bank errors**. Cambridge calls deposits not yet on the statement **uncredited deposits** (or lodgements); ZIMSEC often says **outstanding deposits** or "deposits not yet credited". Cambridge also asks how **digital transactions** affect the reconciliation.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Bank reconciliation statement"}
A statement prepared at a date which explains the difference between the balance in the updated cash book and the balance on the bank statement.
:::

:::definition{term="Unpresented cheque"}
A cheque that has been sent to a supplier and recorded in the cash book, but has not yet been presented to and paid by the bank, so it is not yet on the bank statement.
:::

:::definition{term="Uncredited deposit"}
Money paid into the bank and recorded in the cash book which has not yet been credited to the account by the bank (also called an outstanding deposit or lodgement).
:::

:::definition{term="Bank error"}
A mistake made by the bank, such as charging another customer's cheque to the business's account. It is shown in the reconciliation and the bank is asked to correct it.
:::

## Why prepare a bank reconciliation statement?

- It **proves the cash book balance is correct** and the figure in the statement of financial position can be trusted.
- It **detects errors** made by the business or by the bank.
- It **helps detect fraud**, such as unauthorised payments or stolen receipts, because every difference must be explained.
- It shows **unpresented cheques** that have been outstanding for a long time, which may need to be followed up.
- It encourages up-to-date records, so the business knows how much money it really has.

## Causes of the difference

After the cash book has been updated, three items normally remain:

| Item | In the cash book? | On the bank statement? | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unpresented cheques | Yes (credit) | Not yet | Timing: the supplier has not banked the cheque yet |
| Uncredited deposits | Yes (debit) | Not yet | Timing: the deposit reached the bank after the statement date |
| Bank errors | No | Yes | The bank made a mistake |

## Preparing the statement from the cash book balance

1. Start with the **updated** cash book balance. Show an overdraft in brackets.
2. **Add** unpresented cheques: the bank has not yet paid them out, so its balance is higher.
3. **Deduct** uncredited deposits: the bank has not yet received them, so its balance is lower.
4. Adjust for bank errors in the direction of the bank's figure: if the bank has wrongly taken money out of the account, its balance is lower, so **deduct**; if it has wrongly credited money to the account, **add**.
5. The result must equal the bank statement balance.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Tatenda's reconciliation]
Tatenda's updated cash book at 31 December 2025 shows a debit balance of US\$2,777. Comparing it with the bank statement, which shows US\$3,315 in funds:

- cheque 0418 for US\$412 to Sakubva Motors and cheque 0421 for US\$1,056 to Mutare Tyres have not been presented;
- takings of US\$930 deposited on 31 December do not appear on the statement.

1. Start with 2,777.
2. Add the unpresented cheques: $412 + 1,056 = 1,468$.
3. Deduct the uncredited deposit 930.
4. $2,777 + 1,468 - 930 = 3,315$, which agrees with the statement.

**Tatenda: Bank reconciliation statement at 31 December 2025**

|  | US\$ | US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Balance as per updated cash book |  | 2,777 |
| **Add:** |  |  |
| Cheque 0418 to Sakubva Motors | 412 |  |
| Cheque 0421 to Mutare Tyres | 1,056 |  |
|  |  | 1,468 |
| **Less:** Uncredited deposit |  | (930) |
| **Balance as per bank statement** |  | **3,315** |
:::

## Overdrafts

An overdraft is a **negative** amount. Treat it with brackets and the same rules: adding to an overdraft makes it smaller; deducting from it makes it larger. Many candidates find it easier to think in signed numbers: an overdraft of 359 is −359.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Chipo's overdraft and a bank error]
Chipo's updated cash book at 31 December 2025 shows an overdraft of US\$359. The bank statement shows an overdraft of US\$1,299. She finds:

- a cheque for US\$285 to Gweru Textiles has not been presented;
- a deposit of US\$1,150 made on 31 December has not been credited;
- the bank has charged a cheque of US\$75 belonging to another customer to Chipo's account.

1. Start with $-359$.
2. Add the unpresented cheque: $-359 + 285 = -74$.
3. Deduct the uncredited deposit and the bank error (the bank's balance is lower because of both): $-74 - 1,150 - 75 = -1,299$.

**Chipo: Bank reconciliation statement at 31 December 2025**

|  | US\$ | US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Balance as per updated cash book (overdraft) |  | (359) |
| **Add:** Unpresented cheque to Gweru Textiles |  | 285 |
| **Less:** |  |  |
| Uncredited deposit | 1,150 |  |
| Bank error: cheque of another customer charged to this account | 75 |  |
|  |  | (1,225) |
| **Balance as per bank statement (overdraft)** |  | **(1,299) overdrawn** |

The same reconciliation can begin with the bank statement balance. Every adjustment is then reversed:

**Chipo: Bank reconciliation statement at 31 December 2025 (starting from the bank statement)**

|  | US\$ | US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Balance as per bank statement (overdraft) |  | (1,299) |
| **Add:** |  |  |
| Uncredited deposit | 1,150 |  |
| Bank error: cheque of another customer charged to this account | 75 |  |
|  |  | 1,225 |
| **Less:** Unpresented cheque to Gweru Textiles |  | (285) |
| **Balance as per updated cash book (overdraft)** |  | **(359) overdrawn** |

Chipo should ask the bank to reverse the US\$75 charge. She does **not** enter it in her cash book.
:::

## Starting from the bank statement balance

Examiners may tell you which balance to start from. If you start from the bank statement:

- **deduct** unpresented cheques (they will still reduce the bank balance);
- **add** uncredited deposits (they will still increase it);
- reverse the effect of bank errors.

## Digital transactions and reconciliation

Online banking, card machines and mobile money transfers change the reconciliation in several ways:

- Fewer cheques means fewer unpresented cheques; electronic payments usually clear the same day.
- Statements can be viewed daily, so the cash book can be updated and reconciled more often, even weekly.
- More items reach the bank first (transfers in, direct debits, card fees), so more updating of the cash book is needed before reconciling.
- Some accounting software imports bank data and matches items automatically, but someone must still check unmatched items, wrong references and possible fraud.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Reconciling before updating the cash book"}
Bank charges, standing orders and dishonoured cheques belong in the updated cash book, not in the reconciliation. Start the reconciliation from the **updated** balance.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adding and deducting the wrong way"}
From the cash book: add unpresented cheques, deduct uncredited deposits. From the bank statement: the opposite. Check by making sure your final figure equals the other balance.
:::

:::mistake{title="Losing the overdraft sign"}
Write an overdraft in brackets throughout and label the final line "overdrawn" or "(overdraft)". A missing bracket costs the final mark.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Head the statement with the business name and "Bank reconciliation statement at" the date. List unpresented cheques individually with a subtotal; examiners award marks for each item.
:::

:::exam-tip
The bank reconciliation statement is **not** part of the double-entry system. Nothing in it is posted to the ledger.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A bank reconciliation statement explains the difference between the updated cash book balance and the bank statement balance.
- Remaining differences: unpresented cheques, uncredited deposits and bank errors.
- From the cash book: add unpresented cheques, deduct uncredited deposits, adjust for bank errors. From the bank statement: reverse each step.
- Show overdrafts in brackets; the final figure must equal the other balance.
- Reconciliation proves the cash book, detects errors and fraud, and highlights old cheques. Digital banking makes it faster and more frequent but still needs checking.
:::
