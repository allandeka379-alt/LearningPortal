## Introduction

Nyasha runs a tuck shop next to a secondary school in Chinhoyi. Every sale, purchase and payment during the year has been posted to her ledger with a debit entry and a matching credit entry. At the end of the year, before she prepares her financial statements, she wants to know whether all that posting was done arithmetically correctly. The quickest test is to list every balance in the ledger in two columns, debits on one side and credits on the other, and add them up. That list is the **trial balance**.

Because every transaction was recorded with equal debits and credits, the two totals should agree. If they do not, something has gone wrong and Nyasha must find the error before going further. If they do agree, she has a tidy summary of every account, which is exactly what she needs to prepare her income statement and statement of financial position.

In the examination you will be asked to prepare a trial balance from a list of balances, to decide on which side each balance belongs, to state the purposes and limitations of a trial balance, and to redraft a trial balance that someone else has prepared badly.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use the term **trial balance**. ZIMSEC questions often call trade receivables **debtors** and trade payables **creditors**, and may say "income statement" where Cambridge says "statement of profit or loss". Cambridge calls cash discounts given to customers **discount allowed** and received from suppliers **discount received**; ZIMSEC uses the same names.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Trial balance"}
A list of all the balances in the ledger (and the cash book) at a particular date, with debit balances in one column and credit balances in the other, prepared to check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry.
:::

:::definition{term="Debit balance"}
A balance where the debit side of an account is larger than the credit side. Assets, expenses, drawings and losses normally have debit balances.
:::

:::definition{term="Credit balance"}
A balance where the credit side of an account is larger than the debit side. Liabilities, capital, income and gains normally have credit balances.
:::

## Purposes of a trial balance

A trial balance is prepared for four main reasons:

1. **To check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry.** If total debits equal total credits, every transaction has probably been entered with an equal debit and credit.
2. **To help locate errors.** If the totals differ, the difference tells the bookkeeper that at least one error exists and gives a clue to its size.
3. **To provide a summary of the ledger balances** in one place, so the owner does not have to open every account.
4. **To serve as the starting point for the financial statements.** The income statement and the statement of financial position are prepared from the balances in the trial balance plus the adjustments given in notes.

## Limitations of a trial balance

A trial balance that agrees does **not** prove that the books are free of errors. Some errors leave the totals equal because they affect the debit and credit columns by the same amount (or not at all). These are errors of omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal and compensating errors, which are studied in the next lesson. A trial balance is also not a financial statement: it is an internal working document, not part of the double-entry system, and it does not show profit.

## Deciding the side of each balance

A useful memory aid is **DEAD CLIC**:

| DEAD: debit balances | CLIC: credit balances |
| --- | --- |
| **D**ebit balances are: | **C**redit balances are: |
| **E**xpenses (wages, rent, carriage, purchases, discount allowed, returns inwards) | **L**iabilities (trade payables, loans, bank overdraft) |
| **A**ssets (non-current assets, inventory, trade receivables, bank, cash) | **I**ncome (sales, discount received, rent received, returns outwards) |
| **D**rawings | **C**apital |

Some balances need care:

- **Returns inwards** (sales returns) is a debit, because it reduces sales. **Returns outwards** (purchases returns) is a credit, because it reduces purchases.
- **Discount allowed** is an expense (debit); **discount received** is income (credit).
- **Inventory at the start of the year** (opening inventory) is a debit. **Closing inventory** is normally given as a note below the trial balance, because it is counted after the trial balance has been extracted.
- A **bank overdraft** is a credit balance (the business owes the bank). Money in the bank is a debit.
- If the **capital** figure is missing, it is the figure that makes the two columns agree.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Extracting a trial balance from a list of balances]
Nyasha Moyo gave the following balances at 31 December 2025: drawings 2,400; equipment 5,600; motor vehicle 8,500; loan from Chinhoyi Building Society 5,000; inventory at 1 January 2025 1,850; purchases 23,400; sales 38,900; returns inwards 420; returns outwards 310; carriage inwards 260; wages 6,300; rent 3,600; electricity 940; discount allowed 150; discount received 230; trade receivables 1,730; trade payables 2,480; bank (in funds) 3,210; cash 380. Capital is not given.

1. Classify each balance with DEAD CLIC: assets, expenses and drawings to the debit column; the loan, sales, returns outwards, discount received and trade payables to the credit column.
2. Add the debit column: $2,400 + 5,600 + 8,500 + 1,850 + 23,400 + 420 + 260 + 6,300 + 3,600 + 940 + 150 + 1,730 + 3,210 + 380 = 58,740$.
3. Add the credit balances you know: $5,000 + 38,900 + 310 + 230 + 2,480 = 46,920$.
4. Capital is the missing credit: $58,740 - 46,920 = 11,820$.

## Amending a trial balance that contains errors

Examiners often give a trial balance drafted by an inexperienced clerk, with some balances in the wrong column, and ask you to redraft it. Work down the list and test every balance with DEAD CLIC. Moving a balance from one column to the other changes **both** totals: the wrong column loses the amount and the right column gains it.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Redrafting Tatenda's trial balance]
Tatenda Chuma's clerk prepared this trial balance at 31 December 2025. The totals do not agree.

**Draft trial balance (with errors)**

| Account | Debit US\$ | Credit US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Capital |  | 12,490 |
| Drawings |  | 3,100 |
| Fixtures and fittings | 7,200 |  |
| Purchases | 18,650 |  |
| Sales |  | 29,400 |
| Returns inwards |  | 540 |
| Discount received | 360 |  |
| General expenses | 4,820 |  |
| Inventory at 1 January 2025 | 2,240 |  |
| Trade receivables | 2,960 |  |
| Trade payables | 1,870 |  |
| Bank | 4,610 |  |
| **Total** | **42,710** | **45,530** |

1. Check each line. Drawings is a debit (owner's withdrawals reduce capital). Returns inwards is a debit (it reduces sales). Discount received is income, a credit. Trade payables is a liability, a credit. All the other balances are on the correct side.
2. Adjust the debit total: $42,710 + 3,100 + 540 - 360 - 1,870 = 44,120$.
3. Adjust the credit total: $45,530 - 3,100 - 540 + 360 + 1,870 = 44,120$.
4. The redrafted trial balance agrees:

**Tatenda Chuma: Trial balance at 31 December 2025**

| Account | Debit US\$ | Credit US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Capital |  | 12,490 |
| Drawings | 3,100 |  |
| Fixtures and fittings | 7,200 |  |
| Purchases | 18,650 |  |
| Sales |  | 29,400 |
| Returns inwards | 540 |  |
| Discount received |  | 360 |
| General expenses | 4,820 |  |
| Inventory at 1 January 2025 | 2,240 |  |
| Trade receivables | 2,960 |  |
| Trade payables |  | 1,870 |
| Bank | 4,610 |  |
| **Total** | **44,120** | **44,120** |
:::

Notice that each misplaced balance created a difference of twice its amount between the original columns. The original difference was $45,530 - 42,710 = 2,820$, which equals $2 \times (3,100 + 540) - 2 \times (360 + 1,870)$. Halving a difference is a good first test when looking for a balance entered on the wrong side.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Putting closing inventory in the trial balance"}
The inventory figure inside a trial balance is the **opening** inventory. Closing inventory is counted at the year end and appears as a note; it is used in the income statement and statement of financial position, not added to the trial balance.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating returns as the same side as sales or purchases"}
Returns inwards reduce sales, so they are a **debit**, the opposite of sales. Returns outwards reduce purchases, so they are a **credit**, the opposite of purchases.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking an agreed trial balance means no errors"}
Six kinds of error leave the totals equal. An agreed trial balance shows arithmetical accuracy only, not that every entry is in the right account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Making a bank overdraft a debit"}
An overdraft is money owed to the bank, a liability, so it is a credit balance.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Head the trial balance with the business name and "Trial balance at 31 December 2025" (a trial balance is at a date, not for a period). Use two money columns headed Debit and Credit, and rule off the totals.
:::

:::exam-tip
When capital is not given, calculate it as the balancing figure and show the working. You earn marks for the method even if one other figure is misplaced.
:::

:::exam-tip
For "state two limitations" questions, name two of the six error types and say why each does not affect the totals, for example "an error of omission: neither the debit nor the credit was recorded".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A trial balance lists every ledger balance at one date in debit and credit columns; the totals should agree.
- Purposes: check arithmetical accuracy, help find errors, summarise the ledger, and provide the basis for the financial statements.
- Limitation: six types of error do not stop the totals agreeing, so agreement does not prove the books are correct.
- DEAD CLIC: drawings, expenses and assets are debits; capital, liabilities and income are credits. Returns inwards and discount allowed are debits; returns outwards and discount received are credits.
- Opening inventory is in the trial balance; closing inventory is a note. A missing capital is the balancing figure.
- To amend a trial balance, move every misplaced balance to its correct column and re-add both totals.
:::
