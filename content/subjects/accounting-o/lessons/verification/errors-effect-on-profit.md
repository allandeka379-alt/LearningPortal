## Introduction

Nyasha runs a stationery and uniform shop in Bulawayo. Her accountant has already prepared draft financial statements showing a profit of US\$14,260 when several errors come to light. Rather than prepare all the statements again, the accountant writes a short **statement of corrected profit**: start with the draft profit, add the corrections that increase it, deduct the ones that reduce it, and arrive at the corrected figure.

Examiners love this task because it tests whether you really understand where each account ends up. A correction that changes an income or expense account changes profit; a correction between two asset or liability accounts does not. ZIMSEC and Cambridge Paper 2 questions on suspense accounts usually finish with "prepare a statement showing the corrected profit for the year", and Cambridge also asks for the effect on items in the statement of financial position.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC calls the result the **net profit** (statement of corrected net profit) and the statement an **income statement**; Cambridge uses **profit for the year** and **statement of profit or loss**. Both boards use **gross profit**. **Trade receivables** and **trade payables** may appear as debtors and creditors in ZIMSEC questions.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Statement of corrected profit"}
A statement that starts with the profit as originally calculated, adds corrections that increase profit, deducts those that reduce it, and shows the corrected profit for the year.
:::

:::definition{term="Income statement item"}
An account that is closed off to the income statement at the year end: sales, purchases, returns, carriage, expenses and other income. Correcting one of these changes profit.
:::

:::definition{term="Statement of financial position item"}
An asset, liability, capital or drawings account. A correction that moves an amount only between such accounts does not change profit.
:::

## Will the correction change profit?

Look at the two accounts in the correcting journal entry:

| Accounts in the correction | Effect on profit |
| --- | --- |
| Income statement account and statement of financial position account (including suspense) | Profit changes |
| Two income statement accounts in different sections (trading and expenses) | Gross profit changes; profit for the year may not |
| Two income statement accounts in the same section | No change |
| Two statement of financial position accounts | No change |

Then decide the direction:

- **Income increased** (sales credited, discount received credited) or **expense reduced** (expense credited): profit **increases**.
- **Income reduced** (sales debited) or **expense increased** (expense debited): profit **decreases**.

Suspense is treated as a statement of financial position account. So "debit sales, credit suspense" reduces profit; "debit suspense, credit rent" increases it.

### Gross profit or profit for the year?

Gross profit depends only on the trading items: sales, returns inwards, purchases, returns outwards, carriage inwards and inventory. A correction that moves an amount between a trading item and an expense or other income changes gross profit but not necessarily the profit for the year. For example, moving 120 from carriage outwards to carriage inwards reduces gross profit by 120, but profit for the year is unchanged because total expenses are the same.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Nyasha's corrected profit]
Nyasha's draft profit for the year ended 31 December 2025 was US\$14,260. These errors were found:

1. The sales journal was undercast by US\$400.
2. Carriage inwards of US\$120 had been debited to carriage outwards.
3. A shelving unit costing US\$850 had been debited to purchases.
4. Wages of US\$300 paid had not been posted to the wages account.
5. A receipt of US\$500 from a customer had been credited to the wrong customer.
6. Rent received of US\$240 had been credited to the sales account.

Effects:

| Error | Effect on gross profit US\$ | Effect on profit for the year US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 Sales journal undercast | +400 | +400 |
| 2 Carriage inwards debited to carriage outwards | (120) | nil |
| 3 Shelving debited to purchases | +850 | +850 |
| 4 Wages not posted | nil | (300) |
| 5 Receipt credited to the wrong customer | nil | nil |
| 6 Rent received credited to sales | (240) | nil |
| **Total** | **+890** | **+950** |

1. Error 1: sales increase, so profit rises by 400.
2. Errors 2 and 6 move amounts between the trading section and the other items, so gross profit changes but profit for the year does not.
3. Error 3: purchases fall by 850 (the shelving becomes a non-current asset), so profit rises by 850.
4. Error 4: wages increase, so profit falls by 300.
5. Error 5 involves two customers' accounts only.

**Nyasha Mlilo: Statement of corrected profit for the year ended 31 December 2025**

|  | US\$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Profit for the year as originally calculated | 14,260 |
| **Add:** |  |
| Sales journal undercast | 400 |
| Shelving wrongly debited to purchases | 850 |
| **Less:** |  |
| Wages not posted | (300) |
| **Corrected profit for the year** | **15,210** |

Check: $14,260 + 400 + 850 - 300 = 15,210$.
:::

## Effects on the statement of financial position

The other half of each correction lands in the statement of financial position. Cambridge questions often give a table to complete: item, draft figure, corrected figure. Ask, for each item, which errors touch it.

- **Trade receivables** change when a customer's account is corrected (but not for an error of commission between two customers: the total stays the same).
- **Trade payables** change when a supplier's account is corrected or an invoice is omitted.
- **Inventory** changes when the closing inventory was counted or valued wrongly. Closing inventory is in both statements, so an error in it changes profit **and** current assets by the same amount.
- **Capital** at the end of the year changes by the change in profit, less any change in drawings.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Rudo's profit and statement of financial position items]
Rudo's draft profit for the year ended 31 December 2025 was US\$9,870. Draft figures: inventory 3,145, trade receivables 4,315, trade payables 2,760, drawings 5,400. These errors were found:

1. US\$650 received from a customer by bank had not been posted to the customer's account (a suspense error).
2. A purchase invoice for US\$1,240 had been omitted completely.
3. Closing inventory had been undervalued by US\$380.
4. Goods costing US\$200 taken by Rudo had been debited to wages instead of drawings.

Profit: error 1 involves the customer and suspense only, so no effect. Error 2 increases purchases: profit falls by 1,240. Error 3 increases closing inventory, which reduces cost of sales: profit rises by 380. Error 4 removes 200 from wages: profit rises by 200.

**Rudo Sithole: Statement of corrected profit for the year ended 31 December 2025**

|  | US\$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Profit for the year as originally calculated | 9,870 |
| **Add:** |  |
| Closing inventory undervalued | 380 |
| Drawings wrongly debited to wages | 200 |
| **Less:** |  |
| Purchase invoice omitted | (1,240) |
| **Corrected profit for the year** | **9,210** |

Statement of financial position items:

| Item | Draft US\$ | Adjustment US\$ | Corrected US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Inventory | 3,145 | +380 | 3,525 |
| Trade receivables | 4,315 | (650) | 3,665 |
| Trade payables | 2,760 | +1,240 | 4,000 |
| Drawings | 5,400 | +200 | 5,600 |

Capital at the end of the year changes by the change in profit less the extra drawings: $(9,210 - 9,870) - 200 = -860$.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Changing profit for an error between two customers"}
An error of commission between two personal accounts has no effect on profit or on the total of trade receivables.
:::

:::mistake{title="Getting the direction wrong for purchases"}
Purchases is an expense. If purchases are **overstated** (an asset was debited to purchases), correcting them **increases** profit.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting that closing inventory affects two statements"}
Closing inventory reduces cost of sales and is a current asset, so an inventory error changes profit and the statement of financial position by the same amount.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Use the headings **Add** and **Less** with one line per error and a short description ("Sales journal undercast 400"). Errors with no effect on profit are best left out of the statement, but you may mention them as "no effect" in a note if the question asks for every error.
:::

:::exam-tip
Draw a quick table with columns Error / Gross profit / Profit for the year / Statement of financial position before writing anything. It shows the examiner your reasoning and prevents double counting.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A correction changes profit only if one side is an income statement account and the other is not.
- Income up or expenses down: profit increases. Income down or expenses up: profit decreases.
- Moving amounts between trading items and expenses changes gross profit but not profit for the year.
- The statement of corrected profit starts with the draft profit, then Add and Less, then corrected profit.
- Check the matching statement of financial position items: receivables, payables, inventory, drawings and capital.
:::
