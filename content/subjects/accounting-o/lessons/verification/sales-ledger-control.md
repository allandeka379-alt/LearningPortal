## Introduction

Nyasha runs a school-uniform business in Bulawayo that sells on credit to more than two hundred parents and several schools. Each customer has a personal account in the **sales ledger**. Checking two hundred accounts one by one to find an error would take days. Instead, Nyasha's bookkeeper keeps a single summary account, the **sales ledger control account**, built from the **totals** of the books of prime entry. Its balance should equal the total of all the customers' balances. If it does, the sales ledger is very likely to be arithmetically correct; if not, the bookkeeper knows the error is somewhere in the sales ledger and nowhere else.

Control accounts appear regularly on both ZIMSEC and Cambridge Paper 2, usually as "prepare the sales ledger control account for the month" from a list of totals, often with a trap such as cash sales or a provision for doubtful debts that does not belong in it.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC calls the customers' ledger the **trade receivables ledger** (or debtors ledger) and the account the **trade receivables control account** or debtors control account. Cambridge uses **sales ledger** and **sales ledger control account**, and **irrecoverable debts** where ZIMSEC says **bad debts**. Both boards call a set-off a **contra**.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Control account"}
A summary account kept in the general ledger which records, in total, the transactions entered individually in a subsidiary ledger. Its balance should equal the total of the balances in that ledger.
:::

:::definition{term="Sales ledger control account"}
The control account for the sales (trade receivables) ledger. It is debited with totals that increase what customers owe and credited with totals that reduce it.
:::

:::definition{term="Contra (set-off)"}
A transfer between a business's sales ledger account and purchases ledger account for the same person, when that person is both a customer and a supplier. The smaller balance is set off against the larger.
:::

## Purposes of control accounts

1. **To check the arithmetical accuracy** of a ledger: the control balance should equal the total of the individual balances.
2. **To locate errors quickly**: a difference shows which ledger contains the error.
3. **To give a quick total** of trade receivables (or payables) for the trial balance and the financial statements without listing every account.
4. **To help prevent fraud**: the control account is often kept by a different person from the clerk who keeps the individual accounts, so a dishonest change in one ledger would be noticed (internal check).

## Types of control account

There are two main types: the **sales ledger control account** (trade receivables) and the **purchases ledger control account** (trade payables), studied in the next lesson.

## Sources of information

Every figure in a control account is a **total** taken from a book of prime entry:

| Item | Source (book of prime entry) | Side of the control account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Credit sales | Sales journal (total) | Debit |
| Returns inwards | Returns inwards journal (total) | Credit |
| Receipts from credit customers | Cash book (bank or cash column) | Credit |
| Discount allowed | Cash book (discount column) | Credit |
| Dishonoured cheques | Cash book (credit side) | Debit |
| Refunds paid to customers | Cash book (credit side) | Debit |
| Irrecoverable debts written off | General journal | Credit |
| Interest charged on overdue accounts | General journal | Debit |
| Contras (set-offs) with the purchases ledger | General journal | Credit |

Items that do **not** belong in the sales ledger control account include **cash sales** (no customer account), the **allowance (provision) for doubtful debts** (it is a separate account; no customer is affected), and **trade discount** (never recorded).

## Layout

The control account looks like a large customer's account. On the **debit** side are the opening debit balance and everything that increases the amount owed. On the **credit** side are everything that reduces it and the closing debit balance carried down.

Occasionally a customer has a **credit balance**, for example after overpaying or returning goods already paid for. Credit balances are shown separately: opening credit balance b/d on the credit side, closing credit balance c/d on the debit side. A refund paid to such a customer is debited.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Nyasha's sales ledger control account]
Nyasha's records for December 2025 showed: balance of trade receivables on 1 December US\$6,240; credit sales US\$18,750; receipts from customers US\$16,385; discount allowed US\$412; returns inwards US\$638; irrecoverable debts written off US\$290; a customer's cheque of US\$345 was dishonoured.

1. Debit side: opening balance 6,240, credit sales 18,750, dishonoured cheque 345.
2. Credit side: receipts 16,385, discount allowed 412, returns inwards 638, irrecoverable debts 290.
3. Closing balance: $6,240 + 18,750 + 345 - 16,385 - 412 - 638 - 290 = 7,610$.

**Sales ledger control account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 Dec | Balance b/d | 6,240 | 31 Dec | Bank | 16,385 |
| 31 Dec | Sales | 18,750 | 31 Dec | Discount allowed | 412 |
| 31 Dec | Bank (dishonoured cheque) | 345 | 31 Dec | Returns inwards | 638 |
|  |  |  | 31 Dec | Irrecoverable debts | 290 |
|  |  |  | 31 Dec | Balance c/d | 7,610 |
|  |  | **25,335** |  |  | **25,335** |
| 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 7,610 |  |  |  |

The total of the customers' balances in the sales ledger should also be US\$7,610.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Farai's account with debit and credit balances]
Farai sells timber in Mutare. His figures for December 2025 were:

| Item | US\$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Balances on 1 December: debit | 8,915 |
| Balances on 1 December: credit | 186 |
| Credit sales | 24,360 |
| Cash sales | 5,000 |
| Receipts from credit customers | 21,470 |
| Discount allowed | 538 |
| Returns inwards | 714 |
| Irrecoverable debts written off | 455 |
| Dishonoured cheques | 620 |
| Interest charged on overdue accounts | 48 |
| Contra with the purchases ledger | 370 |
| Refund paid to a customer with a credit balance | 95 |
| Credit balances on 31 December | 212 |

1. Leave out the cash sales: they never pass through a customer's account.
2. Debit: opening debit balances, credit sales, dishonoured cheques, interest charged, refund, and the closing **credit** balances c/d (212).
3. Credit: opening credit balances, receipts, discount allowed, returns inwards, irrecoverable debts, the contra.
4. The closing debit balance is the figure that makes both sides equal: debit side $8,915 + 24,360 + 620 + 48 + 95 + 212 = 34,250$; credit items $186 + 21,470 + 538 + 714 + 455 + 370 = 23,733$; closing debit balance $34,250 - 23,733 = 10,517$.

**Sales ledger control account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 Dec | Balance b/d | 8,915 | 1 Dec | Balance b/d | 186 |
| 31 Dec | Sales | 24,360 | 31 Dec | Bank | 21,470 |
| 31 Dec | Bank (dishonoured cheques) | 620 | 31 Dec | Discount allowed | 538 |
| 31 Dec | Interest on overdue accounts | 48 | 31 Dec | Returns inwards | 714 |
| 31 Dec | Bank (refund to customer) | 95 | 31 Dec | Irrecoverable debts | 455 |
| 31 Dec | Balance c/d | 212 | 31 Dec | Purchases ledger control (contra) | 370 |
|  |  |  | 31 Dec | Balance c/d | 10,517 |
|  |  | **34,250** |  |  | **34,250** |
| 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 10,517 | 1 Jan | Balance b/d | 212 |

In the statement of financial position, trade receivables are US\$10,517 (a current asset); the US\$212 of credit balances is money Farai owes to customers, so it is a current liability (usually included with trade payables).
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Including cash sales"}
Only transactions with credit customers go in the control account. Cash sales are recorded in the cash book and sales account only.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting the contra on the debit side"}
A contra reduces what the customer owes us (we set it off against what we owe them), so it is a **credit** in the sales ledger control account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Including the allowance for doubtful debts"}
Creating or changing an allowance does not touch any customer's account, so it never appears in the control account. Irrecoverable debts actually written off do.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the credit balances"}
Opening credit balances go on the credit side; closing credit balances c/d go on the debit side. Do not net them against the debit balances unless told to.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Before you start, cross out any item that does not belong (cash sales, allowance for doubtful debts, trade discount). Examiners include at least one.
:::

:::exam-tip
Remember the pattern: the control account is a **summary customer account**. Ask "does this increase or decrease what customers owe?" for every item.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A control account summarises a ledger using totals from the books of prime entry; its balance should equal the total of the individual balances.
- Purposes: arithmetical check, locating errors, quick totals for the statements, and fraud prevention through division of duties.
- Debit side: opening debit balances, credit sales, dishonoured cheques, interest charged, refunds, closing credit balances c/d.
- Credit side: opening credit balances, receipts, discount allowed, returns inwards, irrecoverable debts, contras, closing debit balance c/d.
- Cash sales, trade discount and the allowance for doubtful debts are never included.
:::
