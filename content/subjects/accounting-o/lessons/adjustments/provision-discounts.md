## Introduction

Rudo runs a wholesale grocery business in Mutare. To encourage prompt payment she offers her credit customers a 2% cash discount if they settle within fourteen days. At 31 December many customers still owe her money, and most of them will pay early in January and take the discount. The discounts they take in January relate to sales Rudo made in December.

The matching concept says that cost belongs to this year. So, as well as a provision for doubtful debts, Rudo keeps a **provision for discounts allowable** on her year-end trade receivables. The ZIMSEC syllabus lists it with the provision for doubtful debts in Form 3, and Paper 2 questions on provisions often include both. The working is very similar to the provision for doubtful debts; the one extra rule is the order in which the two provisions are calculated.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
This topic is in the **ZIMSEC** syllabus only; Cambridge 0452 does not examine it. ZIMSEC calls the related items **bad debts** and the **provision for doubtful debts** (Cambridge: irrecoverable debts and the allowance for irrecoverable debts).
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Discount allowed"}
A cash discount given to a credit customer for paying within an agreed time. It is an expense of the business.
:::

:::definition{term="Provision for discounts allowable"}
An estimate of the cash discounts that customers who owe money at the year end are expected to take when they pay. It is deducted from trade receivables in the statement of financial position.
:::

## Why make the provision?

- **Matching:** the discounts customers will take in January belong to the sales made in this year, so the expected cost is charged this year.
- **Prudence:** trade receivables are reduced to the amount the business actually expects to receive in cash.

## The order of calculation

Discounts are given only to customers who **pay**. Customers who are expected not to pay (bad and doubtful debts) will not take a discount. So the calculation is always in this order:

1. Trade receivables at the year end.
2. **Less** further bad debts written off.
3. **Less** the provision for doubtful debts (calculated on the amount after step 2).
4. Apply the discount percentage to what remains: this is the provision for discounts allowable **needed**.

**Provision for discounts allowable = discount % × (trade receivables − bad debts − provision for doubtful debts)**

## The double entry

The provision for discounts allowable has its own account with a credit balance, treated exactly like the provision for doubtful debts:

| Event | Debit | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Create or increase the provision | Income statement | Provision for discounts allowable |
| Reduce the provision | Provision for discounts allowable | Income statement |

Only the **change** in the provision affects profit. An increase is an expense; a decrease is added to gross profit as income (or deducted from expenses).

:::worked-solution[Example 1: creating both provisions]
At 31 December 2024 Rudo's trade receivables were US\$40,000. She decided to create a provision for doubtful debts of 5% and a provision for discounts allowable of 2%. There were no bad debts to write off.

1. Provision for doubtful debts: 5% × 40,000 = 2,000.
2. Receivables expected to pay: 40,000 − 2,000 = 38,000.
3. Provision for discounts allowable: 2% × 38,000 = 760.
4. Both provisions are new, so the income statement for 2024 shows expenses of 2,000 and 760.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: adjusting both provisions the next year]
At 31 December 2025 Rudo's trade receivables were US\$46,500, and a bad debt of US\$1,500 was to be written off. The provisions are kept at the same rates: 5% for doubtful debts and 2% for discounts allowable.

1. Trade receivables after the bad debt: 46,500 − 1,500 = 45,000.
2. Provision for doubtful debts: 5% × 45,000 = 2,250; increase 2,250 − 2,000 = 250.
3. Provision for discounts allowable: 2% × (45,000 − 2,250) = 2% × 42,750 = 855; increase 855 − 760 = 95.
4. Income statement for 2025 (expenses): bad debts 1,500; increase in provision for doubtful debts 250; increase in provision for discounts allowable 95.

## Discounts actually allowed during the year

The provision does not replace the discount allowed account. Discounts actually given during the year are still recorded from the cash book (debit discount allowed, credit the customer) and shown as an expense. The provision only deals with the discounts **expected** on the balances outstanding at the year end.

## Effect on profit and the statement of financial position

- Creating or increasing the provision reduces profit and reduces net trade receivables.
- Reducing the provision (for example, because trade receivables have fallen) increases profit.
- In the statement of financial position both provisions are deducted from trade receivables, doubtful debts first.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calculating the discount on the full trade receivables"}
Deduct bad debts and the provision for doubtful debts first. Customers who will not pay will not take a discount.
:::

:::mistake{title="Charging the whole provision every year"}
After the first year only the increase or decrease is entered in the income statement.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the provision with discount allowed"}
Discount allowed is the actual discount given during the year; the provision is an estimate for next year's discounts on this year's balances.
:::

:::mistake{title="Showing the provision as a liability"}
It is deducted from trade receivables under current assets.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Set out a short working: trade receivables, less bad debts, less provision for doubtful debts, then the discount percentage. Show both provisions and both changes clearly labelled as increases or decreases.
:::

:::exam-tip
Read the rate carefully: "a provision for discounts of 2.5%" applies to the receivables left after the doubtful debts provision, not to the original figure.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The provision for discounts allowable estimates cash discounts customers will take on year-end trade receivables (ZIMSEC only).
- It applies matching and prudence.
- Calculate it after bad debts and the provision for doubtful debts: discount % × (trade receivables − bad debts − provision for doubtful debts).
- Only the change in the provision goes to the income statement: increase = expense, decrease = income.
- In the statement of financial position, deduct both provisions from trade receivables.
:::
