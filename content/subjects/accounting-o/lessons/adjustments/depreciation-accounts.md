## Introduction

Chipo owns a bakery in Chinhoyi. She knows how to calculate depreciation on her ovens and mixers, but her bookkeeper asks a practical question: where do the figures go? Depreciation has to be recorded by double entry like any other expense, and the records must still show what each asset originally cost.

In the exam you will be asked to write the journal entry for depreciation, to prepare the non-current asset account and the provision for depreciation account for one or more years, and to show the asset in the statement of financial position. These accounts are also the starting point for disposals, which come in the next lesson.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use the **provision for depreciation account**; Cambridge also calls its balance **accumulated depreciation**. The depreciation expense goes to the **income statement** (ZIMSEC) or **statement of profit or loss** (Cambridge). Net book value is also called **carrying amount**.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Non-current asset account"}
The ledger account in which an asset (or a class of assets, such as motor vehicles) is recorded at cost. Depreciation is never credited to this account.
:::

:::definition{term="Provision for depreciation account"}
A ledger account with a credit balance that builds up the total depreciation charged on a class of assets so far. It is deducted from cost in the statement of financial position.
:::

:::definition{term="Depreciation expense"}
The depreciation for the current year, charged to the income statement as an expense.
:::

## Three accounts, three jobs

| Account | Balance | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset at cost (for example, Equipment) | Debit | What the assets cost; unchanged by depreciation |
| Provision for depreciation: equipment | Credit | Total depreciation charged so far (accumulated) |
| Income statement | — | This year's depreciation only, as an expense |

Keeping the asset at cost means users can see both what was paid and how much has been used up. The provision account is a **contra-asset account**: its credit balance reduces the debit balance of the asset in the statement of financial position.

## The double entry

At the end of each year:

- **Debit** the income statement (depreciation expense) with this year's charge.
- **Credit** the provision for depreciation account with the same amount.

Some businesses first debit a **depreciation account** and then transfer its balance to the income statement at the year end. The result is the same: one year's charge in the income statement and the running total in the provision account.

The provision account is balanced at the year end. Its balance is carried down and brought down at the start of the next year, so it grows every year until the asset is sold or scrapped.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Chipo's bakery equipment over three years]
Chipo bought bakery equipment on 1 January 2023 for US\$21,000, paying by bank transfer. She depreciates equipment at 10% per annum on cost (straight line). Her year end is 31 December.

## Buying more assets during the year

When a new asset is bought, debit the asset account with its cost and credit the bank (or the supplier). At the year end the depreciation charge depends on the method and the stated policy:

- **Straight line on cost:** rate × cost of each asset, time-apportioned if the policy says so.
- **Reducing balance:** rate × (total cost − provision brought forward). With a full year's charge in the year of purchase, the new asset's cost is included in full.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: motor vehicles with an addition]
Rudo's transport business had motor vehicles at cost of US\$36,000 on 1 January 2025 and a provision for depreciation of US\$13,500. On 1 July 2025 she bought another vehicle for US\$12,000 by bank transfer. Vehicles are depreciated at 20% per annum on the reducing balance, with a full year's charge in the year of purchase. She also owns premises costing US\$60,000, which are not depreciated.

1. Cost at the year end: 36,000 + 12,000 = 48,000.
2. Net book value before this year's charge: 48,000 − 13,500 = 34,500.
3. Depreciation for 2025: 20% × 34,500 = 6,900.
4. Provision at the year end: 13,500 + 6,900 = 20,400.

## Where the figures appear

- **Income statement:** this year's depreciation, listed with the other expenses (one line per class of asset is usual).
- **Statement of financial position:** each class of non-current asset at cost, less the closing balance of its provision account, giving the net book value. Total the net book values to find total non-current assets.
- **Trial balance:** the asset at cost (debit) and the provision brought forward (credit). The provision in a trial balance is usually the **opening** figure, so this year's charge must still be added.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Crediting depreciation to the asset account"}
The asset stays at cost. Depreciation is credited to the provision for depreciation account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting the accumulated total in the income statement"}
Only this year's charge is an expense. The accumulated total appears in the statement of financial position.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the trial balance provision as the closing figure"}
The trial balance shows the provision brought forward; add this year's charge before deducting it from cost.
:::

:::mistake{title="Showing the provision as a liability"}
The provision is deducted from the asset's cost; it is not money owed to anyone.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In ledger answers, date every entry, use "Income statement" (or "Statement of profit or loss") as the details for the year's charge, and bring the balance down. Marks are lost for missing b/d balances.
:::

:::exam-tip
Use the three-column layout (cost, accumulated depreciation, net book value) in the statement of financial position. It earns the presentation marks and makes your arithmetic easy to check.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Keep the non-current asset account at cost; never credit it with depreciation.
- Journal: debit income statement (depreciation), credit provision for depreciation.
- The provision account has a credit balance that grows each year; balance it and bring it down.
- The income statement shows one year's charge; the statement of financial position shows cost less accumulated depreciation.
- For reducing balance, apply the rate to cost less the provision brought forward; follow the stated policy for additions.
- A trial balance usually gives the opening provision: add this year's charge.
:::
