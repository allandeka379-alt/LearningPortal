## Introduction

Nyasha owns a wholesale business in Bulawayo with a warehouse that is bigger than she needs. She lets part of it to a transport company for US\$600 a month and also earns commission for selling a seed company's products to her customers. At the end of the year the tenant had not yet paid December's rent, while the seed company had paid some commission in advance for January.

Most businesses at this level earn some income besides sales: rent receivable, commission receivable, interest receivable. The matching concept applies to income in exactly the same way as to expenses. The income statement shows the income **earned** in the year, not the cash received. Exam questions give the amount received and ask you to adjust it, to prepare the income account with opening and closing balances, or to place the balances correctly in the statement of financial position.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use **accrued income** (income due but not received) and **income received in advance** (also called prepaid income). In the statement of financial position, accrued income is part of **other receivables** and income received in advance is part of **other payables**. The income statement is the **statement of profit or loss** in Cambridge papers.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Accrued income"}
Income earned in the current period that has not been received by the year end. It is a current asset (other receivables).
:::

:::definition{term="Income received in advance (prepaid income)"}
Income received in the current period that belongs to a later period. It is a current liability (other payables), because the business owes the service.
:::

:::definition{term="Other income"}
Income from sources other than sales, such as rent, commission and interest receivable. It is added to gross profit in the income statement.
:::

## The rule for income

**Income for the year = amount received + closing accrued income − closing income received in advance**

With balances from the previous year:

**Income for the year = received − opening accrued income + opening received in advance + closing accrued income − closing received in advance**

Compare this with expenses: the signs are the same, but the **sides of the balances are the opposite**, because income accounts have credit entries for money received.

| Item | Effect on this year's income | Statement of financial position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accrued income (due, not received) | Increase | Current asset: other receivables |
| Income received in advance | Decrease | Current liability: other payables |

## In the ledger

An income account is credited with money received (debit bank). At the year end:

- **Accrued income** is carried down on the **credit** side and brought down on the **debit** side (an asset).
- **Income received in advance** is carried down on the **debit** side and brought down on the **credit** side (a liability).
- The income earned is transferred to the income statement on the **debit** side of the income account: the balancing figure.

As a journal entry, accrued income is debit other receivables, credit the income account; income received in advance is debit the income account, credit other payables.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: rent receivable accrued]
Nyasha lets part of her warehouse for US\$600 a month. During 2025 she received US\$6,600; December's rent was still owing at 31 December 2025. There were no balances at 1 January 2025.

1. Rent earned in 2025: 600 × 12 = 7,200.
2. Accrued income: 7,200 − 6,600 = 600.

**Rent receivable account**

The tenant owes Nyasha 600, so it is an asset; the full year's rent of 7,200 is income.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: commission with opening and closing balances]
At 1 January 2025 the seed company owed Nyasha commission of US\$320 for 2024. During 2025 she received US\$4,150, which included the 320 and US\$280 paid in advance for January 2026.

1. Commission earned in 2025: 4,150 − 320 − 280 = 3,550.
2. The opening accrued income is a debit balance brought down; the amount received in advance is carried down on the debit side and brought down on the credit side.

## Why income received in advance is a liability

When the seed company pays commission for January 2026 in December 2025, Nyasha has not yet done the work. If she stopped trading, she would have to repay it. Until the work is done the money is owed back in services, which is why it is a current liability. Treating it as this year's income would break the matching and prudence concepts by overstating profit.

## Effect on profit and working capital

- Accrued income **increases** profit and increases current assets.
- Income received in advance **reduces** profit (compared with the cash received) and increases current liabilities.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Treating income received in advance as an asset"}
The business owes a service, so it is a liability (other payables), not other receivables.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the expense sides for income accounts"}
In an income account, accrued income is carried down on the credit side; income received in advance on the debit side.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adding other income to sales"}
Rent and commission receivable are added to gross profit, not to revenue, because they do not come from selling goods.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring the opening balance"}
An opening accrued income was earned last year; when it is received this year it is deducted.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Before adjusting, ask "Has the business earned it this year?" Earned but not received: add. Received but not earned: deduct.
:::

:::exam-tip
In a full set of statements, list accrued income with prepaid expenses under other receivables, and income received in advance with accrued expenses under other payables, unless the question asks for separate lines.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Income is matched to the period in which it is earned, not when it is received.
- Income = received − opening accrued + opening in advance + closing accrued − closing in advance.
- Accrued income is a current asset (other receivables); income received in advance is a current liability (other payables).
- In the income account, the income statement transfer is a debit and is the balancing figure.
- Other income is added to gross profit in the income statement.
:::
