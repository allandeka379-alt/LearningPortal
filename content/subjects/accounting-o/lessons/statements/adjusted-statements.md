## Introduction

Rudo Chikore runs a hardware shop in Mutare. Her bookkeeper has balanced the ledger and drawn up a trial balance at 31 December 2025. Before the financial statements can be prepared, Rudo's accountant gives her a list of notes: the closing inventory count, the depreciation to charge, wages still owed, insurance paid in advance, a customer who will never pay, and goods Rudo took home. Each note changes at least two figures.

This is the most common Paper 2 question in both syllabi: **a trial balance followed by a list of adjustments, and the instruction to prepare the income statement and the statement of financial position**. It brings together everything from the adjustments lessons. The secret is method: deal with every note **twice**, once in each statement, and tick it off as you go.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC papers say **bad debts** and **provision for doubtful debts**, **net profit**, and sometimes debtors and creditors. Cambridge 0452 says **irrecoverable debts**, **allowance for irrecoverable debts**, **profit for the year**, **other payables** (accruals) and **other receivables** (prepayments). The treatment is identical.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Year-end adjustment"}
A change made to the trial balance figures at the end of the year so that the statements follow the matching (accruals) and prudence concepts.
:::

:::definition{term="Accrued expense (other payable)"}
An expense of the year that has not yet been paid: added to the expense and shown as a current liability.
:::

:::definition{term="Prepaid expense (other receivable)"}
An amount paid this year for a later period: deducted from the expense and shown as a current asset.
:::

:::definition{term="Allowance for irrecoverable debts (provision for doubtful debts)"}
An estimate of the trade receivables that may not be collected. Only the **change** in the allowance goes to the income statement; the full new allowance is deducted from trade receivables.
:::

## The two-effect rule

Every adjustment has two effects, one in each statement:

| Adjustment | Income statement | Statement of financial position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closing inventory | Deduct in cost of sales | Current asset |
| Depreciation for the year | Expense | Add to accumulated depreciation |
| Accrued expense | Add to the expense | Current liability |
| Prepaid expense | Deduct from the expense | Current asset |
| Income due (accrued income) | Add to the income | Current asset |
| Income received in advance | Deduct from the income | Current liability |
| Irrecoverable debt written off | Expense | Deduct from trade receivables |
| Increase in allowance | Expense (only the increase) | Deduct the new allowance from receivables |
| Decrease in allowance | Other income (only the decrease) | Deduct the new allowance from receivables |
| Goods taken by the owner | Deduct from purchases | Add to drawings |

Write the adjusted figures next to the trial balance before you start, or show the working beside each line of the statements.

## Service businesses with income adjustments

A service business has no trading section, but it can have income adjustments. Rent received from a tenant may include an amount for next year (income in advance, a liability), or commission may be due but not yet received (income due, an asset). Loan interest owing at the year end is an accrued expense like any other.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Nyasha's Courier Service]
Nyasha's trial balance at 31 December 2025 showed: fees received US\$58,400; rent received US\$3,600; wages US\$21,300; fuel US\$9,850; insurance US\$2,400; loan interest US\$900; general expenses US\$1,630; motorcycles at cost US\$45,000 (provision for depreciation US\$18,000); office equipment at cost US\$8,000 (provision for depreciation US\$3,200); trade receivables US\$6,200; bank US\$2,310; cash US\$340; trade payables US\$1,260; loan repayable 2029 US\$10,000; drawings US\$8,200; capital US\$11,670.

Notes: motorcycles are depreciated at 20% on the reducing balance and office equipment at 20% on cost; loan interest of US\$300 is owing; insurance of US\$600 is prepaid; rent received includes US\$300 for January 2026.

1. Depreciation: motorcycles 20% × (45,000 − 18,000) = 5,400; office equipment 20% × 8,000 = 1,600.
2. Loan interest 900 + 300 = 1,200; insurance 2,400 − 600 = 1,800; rent received 3,600 − 300 = 3,300.
3. Profit: 58,400 + 3,300 − (21,300 + 9,850 + 1,800 + 1,200 + 1,630 + 5,400 + 1,600) = 61,700 − 42,780 = 18,920.

## The effect of one change on other figures

Examiners often ask "what would happen if …". Trace the double entry. If Rudo's closing inventory had been US\$500 higher, cost of sales would fall by US\$500, so gross profit, profit for the year, current assets, working capital and closing capital would all rise by US\$500. If an accrual of US\$200 had been missed, profit would be overstated by US\$200 and current liabilities understated by US\$200, so working capital would be overstated too.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Charging the whole new allowance as an expense"}
Only the change (new allowance − old allowance) goes to the income statement. The full new allowance is deducted from receivables.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calculating the allowance before the write-off"}
Write off the irrecoverable debt first, then take the percentage of the remaining trade receivables.
:::

:::mistake{title="Reducing balance on cost"}
Reducing-balance depreciation is a percentage of cost less the provision brought forward, not of cost.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using an adjustment only once"}
An accrual added to wages but missing from current liabilities makes the statement of financial position fail to balance. Tick each note twice.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Read every note before you start writing, and mark beside each trial balance item the note that changes it. Most lost marks come from an adjustment that was forgotten.
:::

:::exam-tip
Show workings for adjusted figures (for example "Wages 12,600 + 450") beside the line or in a separate working. Method marks are awarded even if the arithmetic slips.
:::

:::exam-tip
If the statement of financial position does not balance, finish the question anyway. Each correct line earns its mark; a balancing figure earns nothing.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Each adjustment has two effects: one in the income statement and one in the statement of financial position.
- Accruals increase expenses and are current liabilities; prepayments reduce expenses and are current assets.
- Depreciation is an expense and is added to accumulated depreciation; reducing balance uses cost less the provision.
- Write off irrecoverable debts first, then charge only the change in the allowance; deduct the full allowance from receivables.
- Goods taken by the owner reduce purchases and increase drawings.
:::
