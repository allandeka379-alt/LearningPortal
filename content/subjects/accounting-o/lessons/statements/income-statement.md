## Introduction

Tendai owns a hardware shop in Gweru. He buys cement, roofing sheets and tools from wholesalers and sells them to builders and households. At the end of each year he wants to know one thing above all: did the business make a profit, and how big was it? The statement that answers this question is the **income statement**.

The income statement summarises the income and expenses of one accounting period, usually a year. It is prepared from the balances in the trial balance once the ledger has been balanced. It is the most examined layout in O-Level Accounting: almost every Paper 2 has a question that asks you to prepare a full income statement or part of one, and many Paper 1 questions ask you to find gross profit, cost of sales or profit for the year from a few figures.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC calls this statement the **income statement** (older books: trading and profit and loss account) and the final figure **net profit**. Cambridge 0452 calls it the **statement of profit or loss** and the final figure **profit for the year**. The layout and the arithmetic are the same. Inventory was called stock in older books.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Income statement (statement of profit or loss)"}
A financial statement that shows the revenue, cost of sales, gross profit, other income, expenses and profit (or loss) of a business for an accounting period.
:::

:::definition{term="Cost of sales"}
The cost of the goods actually sold in the period: opening inventory plus net purchases (and carriage inwards) less closing inventory.
:::

:::definition{term="Gross profit"}
Revenue (net of sales returns) less cost of sales: the profit made on buying and selling goods before running expenses are deducted.
:::

:::definition{term="Profit for the year (net profit)"}
Gross profit plus other income less all expenses of the period. A negative result is a loss for the year (net loss).
:::

## The two parts of the statement

The statement has two sections, one under the other (the **vertical** layout).

**1. The trading section** finds the gross profit:

- **Revenue** is sales of goods, less **sales returns** (returns inwards). The result is sometimes called net revenue or net turnover.
- **Cost of sales** starts with the **opening inventory**, adds **purchases** and **carriage inwards** (the cost of bringing goods in is part of their cost), deducts **purchases returns** (returns outwards), and finally deducts the **closing inventory** still unsold at the year end.

**2. The profit and loss section** finds the profit for the year:

- **Other income** that does not come from selling goods is added: discount received, rent received, commission received, interest received.
- **Expenses** are deducted: wages, rent, electricity, insurance, carriage outwards, discount allowed, depreciation, irrecoverable debts and so on.

The two key formulas are:

**Gross profit = (revenue − sales returns) − cost of sales**

**Profit for the year = gross profit + other income − expenses**

:::worked-solution[Example 1: a trading business]
Tendai's trial balance at 31 December 2025 includes: revenue US\$86,400; sales returns US\$1,400; inventory at 1 January 2025 US\$7,250; purchases US\$52,300; carriage inwards US\$860; purchases returns US\$1,110; discount received US\$420; rent received US\$1,800; wages and salaries US\$9,600; rent and rates US\$4,800; electricity US\$1,350; carriage outwards US\$740; discount allowed US\$380; general expenses US\$1,165. Inventory at 31 December 2025 was valued at US\$8,900.

1. Net revenue: 86,400 − 1,400 = 85,000.
2. Goods available for sale: 7,250 + 52,300 + 860 − 1,110 = 59,300.
3. Cost of sales: 59,300 − 8,900 = 50,400.
4. Gross profit: 85,000 − 50,400 = 34,600.
5. Add other income: 34,600 + 420 + 1,800 = 36,820.
6. Total expenses: 9,600 + 4,800 + 1,350 + 740 + 380 + 1,165 = 18,035.
7. Profit for the year: 36,820 − 18,035 = 18,785.

## Where does each item go?

| Item | Section | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales returns (returns inwards) | Trading | Deducted from revenue |
| Purchases returns (returns outwards) | Trading | Deducted from purchases |
| Carriage inwards | Trading | Added to purchases |
| Opening inventory | Trading | First line of cost of sales |
| Closing inventory | Trading | Deducted at the end of cost of sales |
| Carriage outwards | Profit and loss | Expense |
| Discount allowed | Profit and loss | Expense |
| Discount received | Profit and loss | Other income |

Closing inventory is the only figure here that is normally **not** in the trial balance. It is counted and valued after the books are balanced, so it is given as a note below the trial balance. Inventory **in** the trial balance is always the **opening** inventory.

## Service businesses

A hair salon, a transport operator or a driving school does not buy goods to resell. It has no trading section, so there is no cost of sales and no gross profit. The statement starts with the fees (or revenue) earned, adds other income and deducts expenses. Materials used in giving the service, such as hair products, are an expense.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: a service business]
Chipo runs a hair studio in Masvingo. For the year ended 31 December 2025 her fees from clients were US\$31,500 and she received commission of US\$640 for selling a supplier's products. Her expenses were: wages US\$10,800; rent US\$4,200; hair products used US\$3,150; electricity and water US\$1,480; telephone and internet US\$560; depreciation of salon equipment US\$900.

1. Total income: 31,500 + 640 = 32,140.
2. Total expenses: 10,800 + 4,200 + 3,150 + 1,480 + 560 + 900 = 21,090.
3. Profit for the year: 32,140 − 21,090 = 11,050.

## Why the income statement matters

The owner uses the profit figure to decide how much can safely be taken out as drawings and whether the business is worth continuing. The tax authority (ZIMRA) uses it to assess tax, and a bank looks at it before lending. The profit for the year is also carried to the statement of financial position, where it is added to the owner's capital: the two statements are linked.

If expenses are greater than gross profit plus other income, the result is a **loss for the year (net loss)**, shown in brackets and deducted from capital.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Putting carriage outwards in cost of sales"}
Only carriage **inwards** is part of the cost of goods. Carriage outwards is a selling expense in the profit and loss section.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the inventory in the trial balance as closing inventory"}
The inventory figure in the trial balance is the **opening** inventory. Closing inventory is given in the notes.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating discount received as an expense"}
Discount received is a gain (other income). Discount allowed is the expense.
:::

:::mistake{title="Including drawings or the purchase of non-current assets"}
Drawings are not an expense of the business, and buying a van is capital expenditure. Neither appears in the income statement; only the depreciation of the van does.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Always write the full three-line heading: business name, statement name, and "for the year ended …". Examiners award a mark for it, and "at 31 December" is wrong for an income statement because it covers a period.
:::

:::exam-tip
Label your subtotals: "Cost of sales", "Gross profit", "Profit for the year". A correct figure without its label may lose the mark.
:::

:::exam-tip
Show deductions in brackets and keep to two money columns. If a figure is wrong but carried forward correctly, examiners still give the follow-through ("own figure") marks when the layout is right.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The income statement shows revenue, cost of sales, gross profit, other income, expenses and profit for a period.
- Cost of sales = opening inventory + purchases + carriage inwards − purchases returns − closing inventory.
- Gross profit = net revenue − cost of sales; profit for the year = gross profit + other income − expenses.
- Carriage outwards and discount allowed are expenses; discount received is other income.
- A service business has no trading section and no gross profit.
- ZIMSEC: income statement and net profit; Cambridge: statement of profit or loss and profit for the year.
:::
