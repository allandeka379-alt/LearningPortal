## Introduction

Tatenda owns a general dealer in Masvingo with two sections under one roof: groceries at the front and hardware at the back. His income statement shows a profit for the whole shop, but it cannot tell him which section earns that profit. Is hardware, with its slow-moving cement and tools, really worth the space it takes? To answer, Tatenda needs **departmental accounts**: an income statement split into one column per department.

Departmental accounts are part of the ZIMSEC syllabus (Form 3). Examination questions give the revenue, inventories and purchases of each department, a list of shared expenses and the basis for sharing them, and ask you to prepare a columnar departmental income statement and comment on the results. Cambridge 0452 does not examine this topic.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Department"}
A section of a business that sells a distinct group of goods or services and whose revenue and costs can be recorded separately.
:::

:::definition{term="Departmental accounts"}
Trading and profit and loss accounts (income statements) prepared in columns, one column per department and a total column, to show the gross and net profit of each department.
:::

:::definition{term="Apportionment"}
Sharing an expense that benefits several departments between them on a fair basis, such as floor area or the number of staff.
:::

:::definition{term="Direct (departmental) expense"}
An expense that belongs to one department only, such as the wages of the hardware sales assistant; it is charged in full to that department.
:::

## Purpose of departmental accounts

Departmental accounts help the owner to:

- compare the gross profit and net profit of each department;
- calculate the gross profit margin of each department and spot falling margins or theft of goods;
- decide where to spend on advertising or where to give more floor space;
- decide whether a department should be expanded, changed or closed;
- reward departmental managers on the results of their own department.

## Setting out the columns

Each item that can be identified with a department (revenue, purchases, inventories, direct wages) is recorded in that department's column. The total column is the sum of the department columns and equals the figures in the ordinary income statement. Shared expenses are apportioned using the basis given in the question. The usual bases are:

| Expense | Common basis |
| --- | --- |
| Rent, rates, insurance of the building, electricity, cleaning | Floor area |
| Wages and salaries (shared staff) | Number of staff or time spent |
| Advertising, carriage outwards, discount allowed | Revenue (sales) |
| Depreciation of equipment | Value of equipment used in each department |
| Expenses with no fair basis | Equally |

To apportion, add the parts of the ratio, divide the expense by the total, and multiply by each department's part. Always check that the department shares add back to the total.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Tatenda's General Dealer]
For the year ended 31 December 2025:

| Item | Groceries US\$ | Hardware US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | 84,000 | 56,000 |
| Inventory at 1 January 2025 | 6,200 | 9,400 |
| Purchases | 63,500 | 38,900 |
| Inventory at 31 December 2025 | 7,100 | 10,300 |

Shared expenses: wages US\$18,000 (staff: 5 in groceries, 3 in hardware); rent US\$9,600 and electricity US\$2,400 (floor area: groceries 300 m², hardware 500 m²); advertising US\$2,800 (in proportion to revenue); general expenses US\$1,500 (equally).

1. Wages: 18,000 ÷ 8 = 2,250 per staff member; groceries 5 × 2,250 = 11,250, hardware 3 × 2,250 = 6,750.
2. Rent: 9,600 × 300/800 = 3,600 and 9,600 × 500/800 = 6,000. Electricity: 900 and 1,500 on the same basis.
3. Advertising: revenue ratio 84,000 : 56,000 = 3 : 2, so 1,680 and 1,120.
4. General expenses: 750 each.

| Expense | Total US\$ | Basis | Groceries US\$ | Hardware US\$ |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Wages | 18,000 | Staff 5 : 3 | 11,250 | 6,750 |
| Rent | 9,600 | Floor area 300 : 500 | 3,600 | 6,000 |
| Electricity | 2,400 | Floor area 300 : 500 | 900 | 1,500 |
| Advertising | 2,800 | Revenue 84,000 : 56,000 | 1,680 | 1,120 |
| General expenses | 1,500 | Equally | 750 | 750 |

**Tatenda's General Dealer**

**Departmental income statement**

**for the year ended 31 December 2025**

|  | Groceries US\$ | Hardware US\$ | Total US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | 84,000 | 56,000 | 140,000 |
| Less: Cost of sales |  |  |  |
| Opening inventory | 6,200 | 9,400 | 15,600 |
| Add: Purchases | 63,500 | 38,900 | 102,400 |
|  | 69,700 | 48,300 | 118,000 |
| Less: Closing inventory | (7,100) | (10,300) | (17,400) |
| Cost of sales | (62,600) | (38,000) | (100,600) |
| **Gross profit** | 21,400 | 18,000 | 39,400 |
| Less: Expenses |  |  |  |
| Wages (staff 5 : 3) | 11,250 | 6,750 | 18,000 |
| Rent (floor area 300 : 500) | 3,600 | 6,000 | 9,600 |
| Electricity (floor area) | 900 | 1,500 | 2,400 |
| Advertising (revenue) | 1,680 | 1,120 | 2,800 |

| Total expenses | (18,180) | (16,120) | (34,300) |

Groceries sells more, but hardware earns a higher gross profit margin: 18,000 ÷ 56,000 × 100 = 32.1% against 21,400 ÷ 84,000 × 100 = 25.5% for groceries. Hardware's net profit is lower mainly because it is charged more rent for its larger floor area.
:::

## The combined statements

The total column is the business's ordinary income statement: total gross profit US\$39,400, total expenses US\$34,300 and net profit US\$5,100. The statement of financial position is not split by department; it is prepared for the business as a whole in the usual way, with the closing inventories of all departments added together (US\$17,400 for Tatenda).

## Should a loss-making department be closed?

A department can show a net loss after apportionment and still help the business. Many shared expenses, such as rent and insurance of the building, would **still be paid** if the department closed: they would simply be charged to the remaining departments. Before advising closure, work out what would really be saved.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: Thandeka's shoe department]
Thandeka's Store in Gweru has clothing and shoe departments. Her departmental statement for 2025 shows:

**Thandeka's Store**

**Departmental income statement**

**for the year ended 31 December 2025**

|  | Clothing US\$ | Shoes US\$ | Total US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | 72,000 | 24,000 | 96,000 |
| Less: Cost of sales |  |  |  |
| Opening inventory | 8,000 | 4,500 | 12,500 |
| Add: Purchases | 50,600 | 17,400 | 68,000 |
|  | 58,600 | 21,900 | 80,500 |
| Less: Closing inventory | (9,200) | (5,100) | (14,300) |
| Cost of sales | (49,400) | (16,800) | (66,200) |
| **Gross profit** | 22,600 | 7,200 | 29,800 |
| Less: Expenses |  |  |  |
| Wages (staff 4 : 2) | 8,400 | 4,200 | 12,600 |
| Rent (floor area 2 : 1) | 4,800 | 2,400 | 7,200 |
| Insurance (floor area) | 1,200 | 600 | 1,800 |
| Advertising (revenue) | 1,440 | 480 | 1,920 |
| Total expenses | (15,840) | (7,680) | (23,520) |
| **Net profit / (loss)** | 6,760 | (480) | 6,280 |

Thandeka thinks she should close the shoe department. If she does, the rent and insurance of the whole building will still be paid; only the two shoe assistants' wages and the shoe advertising will be saved.

1. Gross profit lost: 7,200.
2. Costs saved: wages 4,200 + advertising 480 = 4,680.
3. Net effect: the business loses 7,200 − 4,680 = 2,520 of profit.
4. Profit for the business would fall from 6,280 to 6,280 − 2,520 = 3,760.

Advice: keep the shoe department. Its gross profit pays its own wages and advertising and contributes 2,520 towards the shared rent and insurance. She could instead try to raise its margin or use part of its space better.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Apportioning direct expenses"}
An expense that belongs to one department (for example the wages of staff who work only in hardware) goes in full to that department. Only shared expenses are apportioned.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the wrong basis"}
Use the basis given in the question. If none is given, choose a sensible one and state it: rent by floor area, not by revenue.
:::

:::mistake{title="A total column that does not add across"}
Every total must equal the sum of the department columns. Check each line across as well as down.
:::

:::mistake{title="Closing a department because of its net loss"}
Shared fixed costs continue after closure. Compare the gross profit lost with the costs actually saved.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Show your apportionment workings in a small table before the statement. Examiners give method marks for the ratio even if a later figure is wrong.
:::

:::exam-tip
Head each column with the department name and US\$, and include a total column unless the question says otherwise.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked to comment, use figures: compare gross profit margins and say what would be saved by closing a department, not only that it made a loss.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Departmental accounts show the gross and net profit of each department in columns, with a total column.
- Direct items go to their own department; shared expenses are apportioned on a fair basis.
- Floor area for building costs, staff numbers for wages, revenue for selling costs, equally when there is no better basis.
- The total column is the business's income statement; the statement of financial position is prepared for the whole business.
- Before closing a loss-making department, compare the gross profit lost with the costs actually saved.
:::
