## Introduction

Masvingo Golf Club has about 150 members. Most pay their subscription for the year in January, but some pay late, settling last year's subscription months afterwards, and a few keen members pay next year's subscription early. The club also runs a refreshment bar at the clubhouse, which buys drinks and snacks on credit and sells them to members and visitors. At the year end the treasurer has a cash summary, but the members want to know two things: how much subscription income **belongs to this year**, and whether the bar is actually making a profit.

These two calculations come before the income and expenditure account. Both boards examine them: ZIMSEC Form 4 asks for subscriptions accounts with adjustments, missing figures and the trading accounts of profit-making projects, and Cambridge 0452 asks for accounts for revenue-generating activities and subscriptions. They are also favourite places for "find the missing figure" questions.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC often speaks of the **trading account** of a **profit-making project** (bar, tuck shop, poultry project); Cambridge speaks of **revenue-generating activities** and may call the account a **bar trading account** or **statement**. Subscriptions **in arrears** may be called **subscriptions owing** or **accrued subscriptions**; subscriptions **in advance** may be called **prepaid subscriptions**.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Subscriptions in arrears"}
Subscriptions for a period that members have not yet paid. They are owed to the club: a current asset.
:::

:::definition{term="Subscriptions in advance"}
Subscriptions received for a future period. The club owes those members future membership: a current liability.
:::

:::definition{term="Subscriptions account"}
A ledger account used to find the subscription income that belongs to the year, after allowing for arrears and payments in advance at the start and end of the year.
:::

:::definition{term="Revenue-generating activity"}
An activity a club runs to raise money, such as a refreshment bar, tuck shop or poultry project, for which a separate trading account is prepared.
:::

## The subscriptions account

The cash received for subscriptions during the year (from the receipts and payments account) is rarely the income of the year. It may include last year's arrears and next year's advances, and it leaves out this year's subscriptions still unpaid. The subscriptions account sorts this out, in the same way as an account for rent received.

| Debit side | Credit side |
| --- | --- |
| Balance b/d: arrears at the start | Balance b/d: advances at the start |
| Income and expenditure: **income for the year** (balancing figure) | Bank: cash received in the year |
| Balance c/d: advances at the end | Balance c/d: arrears at the end |

A quicker check is the formula:

**income for the year = cash received − opening arrears + opening advances + closing arrears − closing advances**

Some clubs follow the prudence concept and do **not** count arrears as income until they are paid. Use the club's policy if the question states one; otherwise include them.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: subscriptions of Masvingo Golf Club]
At 1 January 2025 members owed US\$420 of subscriptions for 2024, and US\$180 had been received in advance for 2025. During 2025 the club received US\$9,360 in subscriptions. At 31 December 2025 subscriptions in arrears for 2025 were US\$510, and US\$240 had been received in advance for 2026.

1. Income for 2025 = 9,360 − 420 + 180 + 510 − 240 = 9,390.
2. The US\$420 of arrears and US\$240 of advances are inside the cash received but do not belong to 2025.

**Masvingo Golf Club**

**Subscriptions account**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 Jan 2025 | Balance b/d (in arrears) | 420 | 1 Jan 2025 | Balance b/d (in advance) | 180 |
| 31 Dec 2025 | Income and expenditure | 9,390 | 31 Dec 2025 | Bank | 9,360 |
| 31 Dec 2025 | Balance c/d (in advance) | 240 | 31 Dec 2025 | Balance c/d (in arrears) | 510 |
|  |  | **10,050** |  |  | **10,050** |
| 1 Jan 2026 | Balance b/d (in arrears) | 510 | 1 Jan 2026 | Balance b/d (in advance) | 240 |

The 9,390 goes to the income and expenditure account. In the statement of financial position the arrears of 510 are a current asset and the advances of 240 a current liability.
:::

## Revenue-generating activities

When a club runs a bar, tuck shop or project that buys and sells goods, members want to see its profit separately. A **trading account** is prepared for it exactly like a sole trader's, from sales down to gross profit, less any expenses that belong only to the activity (such as the bar steward's wages). The profit (or loss) is then a single line of income (or expenditure) in the income and expenditure account.

### Deducing missing figures

The treasurer usually knows only the **cash paid** to suppliers. Purchases for the year are found with a payables (total trade payables) account, or the formula:

**purchases = cash paid to suppliers − opening payables + closing payables**

In the same way, **credit sales** = cash received − opening receivables + closing receivables, and **opening or closing inventory** can be found from the cost of sales.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: the refreshment bar of Masvingo Golf Club]
For 2025: bar sales (all cash) US\$19,650; cash paid to bar suppliers US\$12,420; bar payables US\$860 at 1 January and US\$1,140 at 31 December; bar inventory US\$1,320 at 1 January and US\$1,580 at 31 December; bar steward's wages US\$2,600.

1. Purchases: 12,420 − 860 + 1,140 = 12,700.

**Bar payables account (total trade payables)**

| Date | Details | US\$ | Date | Details | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 31 Dec 2025 | Bank | 12,420 | 1 Jan 2025 | Balance b/d | 860 |
| 31 Dec 2025 | Balance c/d | 1,140 | 31 Dec 2025 | Bar purchases (balancing figure) | 12,700 |
|  |  | **13,560** |  |  | **13,560** |
|  |  |  | 1 Jan 2026 | Balance b/d | 1,140 |

2. Cost of sales: 1,320 + 12,700 − 1,580 = 12,440. Gross profit: 19,650 − 12,440 = 7,210.
3. Bar profit after the steward's wages: 7,210 − 2,600 = 4,610.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the cash received as the year's subscriptions"}
Cash received includes other years' subscriptions. Adjust for arrears and advances at both ends of the year.
:::

:::mistake{title="Swapping arrears and advances"}
Opening arrears are a debit balance b/d (owed to the club); opening advances are a credit balance b/d (owed by the club). Closing balances c/d go on the opposite sides.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using cash paid to suppliers as purchases"}
Adjust for payables at the start and end of the year, or the cost of sales and bar profit will be wrong.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing bar sales and bar purchases in the income and expenditure account"}
Prepare a separate trading account and carry only the profit or loss on the activity to the income and expenditure account.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Label every balance in the subscriptions account "(in arrears)" or "(in advance)". It shows the examiner you know why each balance is on its side.
:::

:::exam-tip
After the subscriptions account, write down where each closing balance goes: arrears to current assets, advances to current liabilities.
:::

:::exam-tip
Check a missing figure by putting it back: opening payables + purchases − payments must equal closing payables.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The subscriptions account finds the subscription income for the year; it is the balancing figure transferred to income and expenditure.
- Arrears are owed to the club (asset, debit balance); advances are owed by the club (liability, credit balance).
- Income = cash received − opening arrears + opening advances + closing arrears − closing advances.
- A club activity such as a bar has its own trading account; only its profit or loss goes to income and expenditure.
- Purchases = payments to suppliers − opening payables + closing payables.
:::
