## Introduction

Chinhoyi Sports Club has a clubhouse, a bar, a groundsman and about two hundred members. Its treasurer can produce a receipts and payments account showing the money in the bank, but the committee needs to know more before it raises subscriptions or buys new equipment. Did the club's income for 2025 cover its running costs? How much is the club worth? Those questions are answered by the **income and expenditure account** and the **statement of financial position**.

An income and expenditure account is the club's version of an income statement: it applies the accruals concept, so it includes all income and expenditure **belonging to the year**, whether paid or not, and it charges depreciation instead of the cost of new equipment. In both ZIMSEC and Cambridge Paper 2 this is the most common club question, built from a receipts and payments account plus a list of adjustments, exactly like a sole trader question built from a trial balance.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards say **income and expenditure account**, **surplus**, **deficit** and **accumulated fund**. ZIMSEC calls clubs **non-trading concerns**; Cambridge calls them **clubs and societies**. In the statement of financial position Cambridge may label accrued expenses **other payables** and subscriptions in arrears **other receivables**.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Income and expenditure account"}
A statement of a club's income and expenditure for the year, prepared on the accruals basis, ending in a surplus or deficit.
:::

:::definition{term="Accumulated fund"}
The club's capital: assets less liabilities. Closing accumulated fund = opening accumulated fund + surplus (or − deficit).
:::

:::definition{term="Revenue expenditure"}
Running costs of the year, such as wages, electricity and repairs, which go in the income and expenditure account.
:::

:::definition{term="Capital expenditure"}
Spending on assets that will last for more than a year, such as equipment. It is recorded as a non-current asset; only its depreciation goes in the income and expenditure account.
:::

## From receipts and payments to income and expenditure

Work through the receipts and payments account line by line:

1. **Opening accumulated fund**: list the assets and liabilities at the start of the year (statement of affairs).
2. **Subscriptions**: use the subscriptions account to find the income for the year.
3. **Activities** (bar, tuck shop, competitions, dinners): prepare a trading account or net the takings against the direct costs; carry only the profit or loss.
4. **Expenses**: adjust for amounts owing and prepaid at both ends of the year, just as for a sole trader: paid − opening accrual + closing accrual (prepayments the other way round).
5. **Capital items**: remove purchases of equipment; add them to non-current assets and charge **depreciation** instead.
6. **Income and expenditure account**: total income less total expenditure gives the surplus or deficit.
7. **Statement of financial position**: non-current assets, current assets and liabilities, and the accumulated fund.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: income and expenditure account of Chinhoyi Sports Club]
At 1 January 2025 the club's assets were: clubhouse at cost US\$60,000, depreciation to date US\$12,000; equipment at cost US\$9,000, depreciation to date US\$3,600; bar inventory US\$1,450; subscriptions in arrears US\$300; bank US\$2,880. ## The club's statement of financial position

The layout is the same as a sole trader's, with two differences: the capital section is the **accumulated fund**, and there are no drawings. Subscriptions in arrears are a current asset; subscriptions in advance are a current liability. The bank balance is the closing balance of the receipts and payments account.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: statement of financial position of Chinhoyi Sports Club]
Using Example 1: the bank balance at 31 December 2025 is 2,880 + 33,210 receipts − 24,510 payments = 11,580.

1. Clubhouse: cost 60,000, depreciation 12,000 + 1,200 = 13,200. Equipment: cost 9,000 + 2,600 = 11,600, depreciation 3,600 + 2,320 = 5,920.
2. Current assets 13,720; current liabilities 1,760; working capital 11,960.
3. Net assets: 52,480 + 11,960 = 64,440. Accumulated fund: 56,620 + 7,820 = 64,440.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Including capital purchases as expenditure"}
New equipment or buildings are non-current assets. Charge only depreciation in the income and expenditure account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Copying cash figures without adjustments"}
Subscriptions, expenses and bar purchases must be adjusted for amounts owing, in arrears and in advance at both the start and the end of the year.
:::

:::mistake{title="Showing bar sales and purchases in the income and expenditure account"}
Prepare the bar trading account separately and carry only the profit or loss.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the opening accumulated fund"}
The closing fund is the opening fund plus the surplus. Find the opening fund from the assets and liabilities at the start of the year, including subscriptions in arrears and advance.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Set out workings for every adjusted figure (subscriptions, purchases, each expense) before the statement. Examiners give method marks for clear workings even when an earlier figure is wrong.
:::

:::exam-tip
Use the words "surplus" and "deficit", never profit or loss, for the club as a whole. "Profit" is correct only for a single activity such as the bar.
:::

:::exam-tip
If the question gives "donations for the building fund" or another specific purpose, read the instruction carefully: such donations may be credited to a separate fund rather than to income.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- An income and expenditure account is a club's income statement, prepared on the accruals basis.
- Start with the opening accumulated fund (assets − liabilities at the start of the year).
- Adjust subscriptions and expenses for arrears, advances, accruals and prepayments; include only the profit or loss on activities.
- Capital items become non-current assets; charge depreciation instead.
- Surplus = income − expenditure; closing accumulated fund = opening fund + surplus (− deficit).
- The statement of financial position lists the club's assets and liabilities and ends with the accumulated fund.
:::
