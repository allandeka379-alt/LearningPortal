## Introduction

Masvingo Hardware started as a partnership between two brothers. As it opened branches in Chiredzi and Zvishavane, it needed far more capital than the brothers could provide, and they worried that a single bad debt could cost them their homes. Their solution was to turn the business into a **limited company**. Now the business can raise capital by selling **shares** to many investors, and each investor risks only the amount he or she paid for the shares.

A company's capital is recorded differently from a sole trader's or a partnership's. There is no single capital account and no drawings. Instead there is **share capital**, **reserves** and possibly **loan capital** in the form of debentures. Both ZIMSEC (Form 4) and Cambridge 0452 expect you to use these terms accurately, to calculate dividends and debenture interest, and to present the equity section of a company's statement of financial position.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC usually says **retained profits** and **shareholders' funds**; Cambridge says **retained earnings** and **equity**. They mean the same. ZIMSEC papers may say **net profit** where Cambridge says **profit for the year**. In Zimbabwe a private company's name ends with "(Private) Limited", often shortened to "(Pvt) Ltd".
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Limited company"}
A business that is a separate legal entity from its owners (the shareholders), whose liability for its debts is limited to the amount unpaid on their shares.
:::

:::definition{term="Limited liability"}
Shareholders can lose only the money they have invested in shares; their private possessions cannot be taken to pay the company's debts.
:::

:::definition{term="Ordinary shares"}
Shares that carry voting rights and receive a variable dividend, paid only after the preference dividend. Ordinary shareholders bear the greatest risk and receive the greatest reward. Together with reserves they make up most of the equity.
:::

:::definition{term="Preference shares"}
Shares that receive a fixed rate of dividend, paid before any ordinary dividend, and are repaid before ordinary shares if the company is wound up. They usually carry no voting rights.
:::

:::definition{term="Debentures"}
Long-term loans made to a company, usually at a fixed rate of interest and repayable on a stated date. Debenture holders are creditors, not owners, and their interest is an expense.
:::

:::definition{term="Dividend"}
The share of a company's profit paid to shareholders, usually stated as an amount per share or as a percentage of the nominal value.
:::

:::definition{term="Equity (shareholders' funds)"}
Issued share capital plus reserves (such as the general reserve and retained earnings): the amount the company owes to its shareholders.
:::

## Advantages and disadvantages of a limited company

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| Limited liability for shareholders | Costly and slow to set up and register |
| Large amounts of capital can be raised by issuing shares | Accounts must be prepared under company law and (for larger companies) audited |
| Continuity: the company continues if a shareholder dies or sells shares | Some financial information must be published |
| Easier to borrow, for example by issuing debentures | Owners (shareholders) may lose control to directors |
| Shares can be transferred to new owners | Profits are shared among many shareholders and the company pays tax on profits |

## Kinds of share capital

The money raised from shares is described at several levels:

- **Authorised share capital**: the maximum the company is allowed to issue, as stated in its founding documents. It is shown as a note, not added into the totals.
- **Issued share capital**: the shares actually issued to shareholders, at their nominal (face) value.
- **Called-up capital**: the part of the issued capital that the company has asked shareholders to pay so far.
- **Paid-up capital**: the amount actually received. Amounts called but not yet received are **calls in arrear**.

Only issued (paid-up) share capital appears in the equity total.

| Example | US\$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Authorised: 1,000,000 ordinary shares of US\$0.50 | 500,000 |
| Issued: 600,000 ordinary shares of US\$0.50 | 300,000 |
| Called up: US\$0.40 per share | 240,000 |
| Paid up (calls received) | 236,000 |
| Calls in arrear | 4,000 |

## Dividends

Dividends are paid out of profits, **after** the preference dividend has been met:

1. **Preference dividend** = number of preference shares × nominal value × fixed rate.
2. **Ordinary dividend** = number of ordinary shares issued × dividend per share (or nominal value × percentage).

Dividends paid during the year are recorded in the statement of changes in equity as a deduction from retained earnings, **not** as an expense in the income statement.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: dividends of Masvingo Hardware Limited]
Masvingo Hardware Limited has issued 300,000 ordinary shares of US\$1 each and 50,000 6% preference shares of US\$1 each, all fully paid. In 2025 the directors paid the preference dividend in full and an ordinary dividend of US\$0.08 per share.

1. Preference dividend: 50,000 × 1 × 6% = 3,000.
2. Ordinary dividend: 300,000 × 0.08 = 24,000.
3. Total dividends paid: 27,000, deducted from retained earnings.

| Dividend | Calculation | US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Preference dividend | 50,000 × US\$1 × 6% | 3,000 |
| Ordinary dividend | 300,000 shares × US\$0.08 | 24,000 |
| **Total dividends** |  | 27,000 |

An ordinary dividend of US\$0.08 on a US\$1 share can also be described as an 8% dividend.
:::

## Reserves and loan capital

Profits that are not paid out as dividends are kept in the company as **retained earnings**. The directors may transfer part of them to a **general reserve**, to show that the money is being kept for the long term (for example, for expansion). Reserves belong to the ordinary shareholders and are part of equity; they are **not** cash.

**Debentures** are loan capital. Debenture interest = nominal value × rate, and it must be paid whether or not the company makes a profit. It is a **finance cost** in the income statement. The debentures themselves are a **non-current liability**, unless they are repayable within twelve months.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: the equity section of Masvingo Hardware Limited]
At 31 December 2025 the company has the shares described in Example 1, a general reserve of US\$40,000, retained earnings of US\$62,500 and 8% debentures of US\$100,000 repayable in 2032.

1. Equity = 300,000 + 50,000 + 40,000 + 62,500 = 452,500.
2. Debentures are a non-current liability of 100,000. Annual debenture interest = 8% × 100,000 = 8,000, charged as an expense.

**Masvingo Hardware Limited**

**Statement of financial position (extract)**

**at 31 December 2025**

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Adding authorised capital into equity"}
Authorised capital is a limit, shown only as a note. Use the issued (paid-up) capital in the totals.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating debentures as share capital"}
Debenture holders lend money to the company. Debentures are liabilities and their interest is an expense; shares are equity and dividends are appropriations.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calculating dividends on authorised shares"}
Dividends are paid only on shares that have been issued.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking reserves are cash"}
A general reserve is part of equity. The matching resources may be held as buildings, inventory or any other asset.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write the nominal value in every share line, for example "200,000 ordinary shares of US\$0.50 each". Then multiply carefully: 200,000 shares of US\$0.50 is US\$100,000, not US\$200,000.
:::

:::exam-tip
A "6% preference share" pays 6% of its nominal value each year, regardless of the profit, as long as the directors declare dividends.
:::

:::exam-tip
In comparison questions, set out the features in pairs: ordinary versus preference shares (variable or fixed dividend, voting rights, order of repayment), and shares versus debentures (owners or lenders, dividend or interest, equity or liability).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A limited company is a separate legal entity; its shareholders have limited liability.
- Ordinary shares carry votes and a variable dividend; preference shares receive a fixed dividend first.
- Authorised capital is the maximum; issued, called-up and paid-up capital describe what has been issued, requested and received.
- Debentures are long-term loans: a non-current liability whose interest is an expense.
- Dividends are appropriations deducted from retained earnings; a general reserve is retained profit set aside.
- Equity (shareholders' funds) = issued share capital + reserves.
:::
