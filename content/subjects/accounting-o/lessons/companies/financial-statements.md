## Introduction

Mutare Timber Products Limited buys logs from plantations in the Eastern Highlands and sells sawn timber to builders and furniture makers. Its trading activities are the same as those of a sole trader or a partnership, so its income statement is prepared in the same way, with the same adjustments for depreciation, accruals, prepayments and irrecoverable debts. What changes is the **ownership** part of the accounts. A company has no capital account and no drawings. Instead it reports how its **equity** changed during the year: new shares issued, profit earned, dividends paid and amounts moved into reserves.

Both boards examine three statements for a company: the **income statement** (statement of profit or loss), the **statement of changes in equity**, and the **statement of financial position**, often as extracts. ZIMSEC Form 4 focuses on the statement of changes in equity and statement of financial position extracts; Cambridge 0452 may ask for all three.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
ZIMSEC: **income statement**, **net profit**, **retained profits**, **shareholders' funds**. Cambridge: **statement of profit or loss**, **profit for the year**, **retained earnings**, **equity**. Both boards use **statement of changes in equity** and **general reserve**, and Cambridge calls debenture interest a **finance cost**.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Statement of changes in equity"}
A statement that shows, for each part of equity (share capital, general reserve, retained earnings), the opening balance, the changes during the year and the closing balance.
:::

:::definition{term="Profit from operations"}
The profit after all operating expenses but before finance costs such as debenture interest.
:::

:::definition{term="Finance costs"}
Interest on borrowings, such as debenture interest and loan interest. They are deducted from the profit from operations.
:::

:::definition{term="Transfer to general reserve"}
Moving part of the retained earnings into the general reserve. It changes the make-up of equity but not its total.
:::

## The income statement of a company

The income statement follows the sole trader layout down to gross profit and the operating expenses. Two points are special to companies:

1. **Directors' fees and salaries** are expenses (directors are employees of the company).
2. **Debenture interest** is a finance cost, deducted after the profit from operations. Charge the **full year's** interest even if some of it is still owing; the unpaid part is a current liability.

Dividends are **not** expenses. They are distributions of profit to the owners, so they appear in the statement of changes in equity.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: income statement and statement of changes in equity]
Mutare Timber Products Limited, year ended 31 December 2025. Revenue US\$384,000; cost of sales US\$236,500; wages and salaries US\$52,300; general expenses US\$18,450; depreciation US\$14,200; 8% debentures US\$60,000 in issue all year.

On 1 January 2025 equity was: ordinary shares of US\$0.50 each US\$200,000 (400,000 shares); general reserve US\$25,000; retained earnings US\$38,900. During 2025:

- on 1 July 100,000 more ordinary shares of US\$0.50 were issued at par for US\$50,000;
- the final dividend for 2024 of US\$0.04 per share was paid in March, on the 400,000 shares then in issue (16,000);
- an interim dividend of US\$0.03 per share was paid in October, on all 500,000 shares (15,000);
- the directors transferred US\$15,000 to the general reserve.

1. Gross profit: 384,000 − 236,500 = 147,500.
2. Profit from operations: 147,500 − 84,950 = 62,550.
3. Debenture interest: 8% × 60,000 = 4,800. Profit for the year: 57,750.

## The statement of changes in equity: the rules

- One column for each part of equity and a total column.
- **Share issues** increase share capital (at nominal value when issued at par).
- **Profit for the year** increases retained earnings; a **loss** reduces them.
- **Dividends paid** during the year reduce retained earnings. A dividend paid in 2025 may relate to 2024 profits (the "final dividend"); it is still deducted in 2025, the year it is paid. Dividends that are only **proposed** at the year end are not deducted; at most they are mentioned in a note.
- **Transfers to reserves** reduce retained earnings and increase the general reserve.

The closing balances of every column go straight into the equity section of the statement of financial position.

## The statement of financial position of a company

The assets section is the same as for any business. The equity and liabilities section shows:

1. **Equity**: each class of issued share capital, the general reserve and retained earnings, with a total;
2. **Non-current liabilities**: debentures and long-term loans;
3. **Current liabilities**: trade payables, other payables (accruals, debenture interest owing), bank overdraft.

Total assets must equal total equity and liabilities.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: statement of financial position of Mutare Timber Products Limited]
At 31 December 2025 the company's balances (after preparing the statements in Example 1) include: premises at cost US\$210,000; machinery at cost US\$120,000, accumulated depreciation US\$38,600; motor vehicles at cost US\$45,000, accumulated depreciation US\$18,000; inventory US\$34,720; trade receivables US\$28,910; bank US\$42,400; trade payables US\$21,380. 3. Liabilities: debentures 60,000 (non-current); trade payables 21,380 and debenture interest owing 2,400 (current), 23,780.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Deducting dividends in the income statement"}
Dividends are paid to owners out of profit. They go in the statement of changes in equity, never among the expenses.
:::

:::mistake{title="Charging only the debenture interest paid"}
Charge the full year's interest as a finance cost. Any unpaid interest is an accrual (other payables).
:::

:::mistake{title="Counting a transfer to reserve as a reduction of equity"}
A transfer to the general reserve only moves profit from one reserve to another. Total equity does not change.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the closing number of shares for every dividend"}
Each dividend is paid on the shares in issue on the date it is paid. Shares issued later in the year do not receive an earlier dividend.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Prepare the statement of changes in equity before the statement of financial position. Its closing balances are your equity figures.
:::

:::exam-tip
In a trial balance question, look for "debenture interest" paid; compare it with the full-year charge (rate × debentures) to find any amount owing.
:::

:::exam-tip
Label the profit line with the board's term: "Net profit" for ZIMSEC, "Profit for the year" for Cambridge.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A company's income statement follows the sole trader layout, with directors' fees as expenses and debenture interest as a finance cost.
- Dividends paid are deducted from retained earnings in the statement of changes in equity, not in the income statement.
- The statement of changes in equity shows opening balances, share issues, profit, dividends paid, transfers and closing balances for each part of equity.
- A transfer to the general reserve does not change total equity.
- The statement of financial position shows equity, non-current liabilities (debentures) and current liabilities (including interest owing).
:::
