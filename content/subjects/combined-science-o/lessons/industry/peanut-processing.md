## Introduction

Groundnuts (*nzungu* in Shona) are one of the most important crops grown by smallholder farmers in Zimbabwe. A farmer who sells a bag of raw groundnuts at harvest gets a low price. A farmer or school club that turns the same nuts into jars of peanut butter (*dovi*) sells a product that is worth more, keeps for months and creates work. Turning a raw product into a more valuable one like this is called **value addition**.

About half of the mass of a groundnut kernel is oil, and about a quarter is protein. In this lesson you will learn the stages of making peanut butter, the equipment used at each stage, how oil is pressed out of peanut butter and what the oil is used for. You will also see how the processing uses the separation methods and the physical and chemical changes you study in chemistry.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Value addition"}
Processing a raw product, such as groundnuts, into a product of higher value, such as peanut butter or peanut oil.
:::

:::definition{term="Kernel"}
The edible seed inside the groundnut shell (pod).
:::

:::definition{term="Aflatoxins"}
Poisons made by moulds that grow on damp or damaged groundnuts. They damage the liver, and roasting does not destroy them completely.
:::

:::definition{term="Rancid"}
Describes oil or fat that has reacted with oxygen in the air and developed a bad smell and taste.
:::

## Making peanut butter

The four main processes are **shelling**, **roasting**, **grinding** and **packaging**, with cleaning and sorting along the way.

### 1. Shelling and sorting

The pods are cracked open and the kernels removed, either by hand or with a **groundnut sheller**, a hand-turned or motor-driven machine that cracks the pods and lets the kernels fall through a sieve. Pieces of shell are then separated from the kernels by **winnowing**: the mixture is tossed in a **winnowing basket** (*rusero*) in a breeze, and the light shells are blown away while the heavier kernels fall back into the basket.

The kernels are then **sorted** by hand. Stones, soil and shrivelled, broken, discoloured or mouldy kernels are removed. This matters for safety: mouldy kernels may contain **aflatoxins**, and roasting does not remove them.

### 2. Roasting

The kernels are heated in a **roaster** (a rotating drum over a fire or gas burner) or stirred continuously in a large pan over a fire until they are evenly light golden-brown. Roasting:

- develops the flavour, smell and brown colour of peanut butter;
- drives off moisture, which makes the kernels easier to grind and helps the product keep;
- loosens the thin red-brown skins;
- kills many of the microorganisms on the nuts.

The roasted nuts are spread out to cool quickly so that they do not go on cooking. Then they are rubbed between the hands or in a sack to loosen the skins, and the skins are winnowed away.

### 3. Grinding

The roasted kernels are ground into a paste with a **peanut butter making machine** (a motor-driven mill), or traditionally on a **grinding stone** (*guyo* or *imbokodo*) or in a **mortar and pestle**. Grinding breaks open the cells of the kernels, releasing their oil, which mixes with the crushed solids to form a smooth paste. A little salt is often added for flavour. The paste becomes warm because of friction in the mill.

### 4. Packaging

The peanut butter is filled into **clean, dry jars or tubs**, sealed **airtight** and **labelled** with the name of the product, its ingredients, its mass, the producer and a date. Airtight packaging keeps out:

- **air**, whose oxygen slowly makes the oil rancid;
- **moisture**, which allows moulds to grow;
- **dust and insects**.

The jars are stored in a cool, dark place.

:::note{title="Hygiene throughout"}
Workers wash their hands, wear clean aprons and cover their hair, and all equipment is cleaned before use. A batch of peanut butter is only as safe as the dirtiest stage of its production.
:::

### Equipment

| Stage | Equipment |
| --- | --- |
| Shelling | Hands, groundnut sheller |
| Separating shells and skins | Winnowing basket (*rusero*) |
| Roasting | Roaster, or a pan over a fire |
| Grinding | Peanut butter making machine, grinding stone (*guyo*/*imbokodo*), mortar and pestle |
| Packaging | Clean jars or tubs with airtight lids, labels |

:::worked-solution[A school club's batch of peanut butter]
A school club shells $40\ \text{kg}$ of groundnuts in their pods and obtains $28\ \text{kg}$ of kernels. Sorting removes $1.0\ \text{kg}$ of bad kernels, and roasting drives off $0.6\ \text{kg}$ of water. All the roasted kernels are ground into peanut butter.

1. Shelling percentage: $\dfrac{28}{40} \times 100\% = 70\%$ of the mass of the pods is kernel.
2. Mass after sorting: $28 - 1.0 = 27\ \text{kg}$.
3. Mass after roasting: $27 - 0.6 = 26.4\ \text{kg}$ of peanut butter.
4. Number of $400\ \text{g}$ jars: $\dfrac{26\,400\ \text{g}}{400\ \text{g}} = 66$ jars.
:::

## Peanut oil from peanut butter

If a jar of peanut butter without stabiliser stands for some weeks, a layer of oil collects on top. The oil is **less dense** than the solid particles, so it rises. Pressing gets the oil out much faster:

1. The peanut butter is **warmed slightly**, which makes the oil runnier so that it flows out more easily.
2. It is packed into a strong **cloth bag** and put into a **press** (a screw press or a lever press).
3. As the press squeezes the bag, **oil is forced out through the cloth** and runs into a container. The solid **groundnut cake** stays in the bag.
4. The oil is left to **stand** so that fine solid particles sink to the bottom (sedimentation), and the clear oil is then **decanted** or **filtered**.

The cloth works like a filter paper: the liquid oil passes through, while the solid particles are held back.

### Uses of the oil and the cake

| Product | Uses |
| --- | --- |
| Peanut oil | Cooking and frying; making margarine; making soap (oils react with an alkali such as sodium hydroxide, see the lesson on soaps); skin creams and hair oils; fuel for oil lamps |
| Groundnut cake | Rich in protein: used in animal feed for poultry, pigs and cattle, and as food |

:::worked-solution[Oil yield]
A small processor presses $5.0\ \text{kg}$ of peanut butter and collects $1.2\ \text{kg}$ of oil.

1. Percentage of oil obtained: $\dfrac{1.2}{5.0} \times 100\% = 24\%$.
2. Mass of cake left (if nothing else is lost): $5.0 - 1.2 = 3.8\ \text{kg}$.

Pressing does not remove all the oil: a groundnut kernel is about half oil, but much of it stays in the cake.
:::

## Science in the process

| Step | Science |
| --- | --- |
| Winnowing, sorting, filtering, decanting | **Separation** methods: by lightness in a breeze, by hand, by passing through cloth and by pouring off the upper liquid |
| Shelling, grinding, pressing | **Physical changes**: no new substances are formed |
| Roasting | A **chemical change**: new substances give the brown colour and roasted flavour, and the change cannot be reversed |
| Oil going rancid | A **chemical change**: the oil reacts with oxygen in the air |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Roasting makes mouldy nuts safe"}
Aflatoxins made by moulds are not destroyed completely by roasting. Mouldy or damaged kernels must be removed by sorting before roasting.
:::

:::mistake{title="Grinding is a chemical change"}
Grinding breaks the kernels into small particles and releases oil, but it forms no new substances, so it is a physical change. Roasting is the chemical change.
:::

:::mistake{title="Packaging is only for appearance"}
Airtight packaging keeps out the oxygen, moisture, dust and insects that spoil peanut butter. The label also tells the buyer what is in the jar and when it was made.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Value addition turns raw groundnuts into products of higher value: peanut butter and peanut oil.
- Peanut butter: shell (sheller), winnow away shells (winnowing basket), sort out bad and mouldy kernels, roast (roaster), remove skins, grind (peanut butter machine, grinding stone or mortar and pestle), then package in clean, airtight, labelled jars.
- Roasting develops flavour and colour, drives off moisture, loosens skins and kills microorganisms; it is a chemical change.
- Oil is pressed out of slightly warmed peanut butter in a cloth bag; the oil is left to settle and decanted or filtered; the solid cake is used as animal feed.
- Peanut oil is used for cooking, margarine, soap, cosmetics and lamp fuel.
:::
