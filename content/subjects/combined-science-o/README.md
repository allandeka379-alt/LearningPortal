# Combined Science (O-Level) — `combined-science-o`

Study content for **ZIMSEC O-Level Combined Science (Forms 1–4 syllabus, 2015-2022)**. Combined Science covers
Biology, Chemistry and Physics at a lighter depth than the three separate sciences, so this family is built mostly
from content that already exists: its syllabus file (`content/syllabi/zimsec/o-level/combined-science/2015-2022.json`)
borrows the concepts of `biology-o`, `chemistry-o`, `physics-o` and `mathematics-o` through `conceptFamilies`, and each
Combined Science topic `covers` the borrowed concepts that teach it. Content was written **for Combined Science itself**
only where no other family teaches the topic: the seven `cs.*` concepts in `content/concepts/combined-science-o.json`.
Formats, quality bar and workflow: `docs/CONTENT_PIPELINE.md`.

> **Review status.** The seven Combined Science lessons and their questions were written by AI directly in the main
> session (2026-10-08) and checked by the automated validator, `content:check` and a marking harness, **but not by an
> independent AI review or by a human**. The borrowed content carries the review status of its own family (see each
> family's README; physics-o has had no independent AI review yet). Nothing of this family has been imported yet: the
> import, DEV preview publish and smoke test are queued for the owner's PC (see "DEV preview"). Production publishing
> requires a human reviewer (§41).

**Own content.** The seven `cs.*` concepts are hygiene and disease transmission, Ebola, peanut butter and cooking-oil
processing, fluid systems (siphons, hydraulics and pumps), gears, solar cookers and solar water heaters, and
telecommunication. Their 146 questions: 83 multiple choice (5 "select all that apply"), 13 true/false, 14 numerical, 7
short answer, 3 fill-in-the-blank, 7 matching, 17 structured and 2 extended response; difficulty 1–4 split 17 / 72 /
42 / 15; 18 exam-style and 24 daily-question eligible. 87 of their 267 marks (33 %) are self-marked written answers
while AI marking is off. Lessons run to about 1,350–2,000 words, with worked examples, common mistakes, exam tips and a
summary, set in Zimbabwean contexts (Chisumbanje irrigation, Christmas Pass, Blair pumps, Binga and Zaka solar
cookers, rural clinics and a call from Murehwa to Bulawayo).

**How borrowing works.** A borrowed item is shown to Combined Science students when one of its concepts is covered by
a Combined Science topic and its `applies` allows ZIMSEC (items restricted to Cambridge are left out). The importer
tags borrowed items to Combined Science topics only when **their own family is imported again** after the Combined
Science syllabus exists (see "DEV preview").

Validation: borrowed items and the blueprints that draw on them can only be checked with all five families loaded:
`npm run content:validate -- combined-science-o biology-o chemistry-o physics-o mathematics-o --strict --no-cache`
gives **0 errors and 0 warnings** (1,246 files, 11,900 items), including proof that every topic test and mock can be
filled. (`content:validate -- combined-science-o` on its own reports the blueprints as unfillable, because it cannot
see the borrowed questions.) `npm run content:check -- combined-science-o` reports no findings, and
`npx tsx --conditions=react-server .scratch/combined-science/alt-check.ts` marks 11 alternative correct answers with
the real engine (11/11 full marks).

## Assessments

All tests are blueprints: each attempt draws fresh questions from the published bank (own and borrowed questions
alike). Files: `assessments/topic-tests/zimsec-topic-tests.json` and `assessments/mocks/zimsec-mocks.json`, generated
by `.scratch/combined-science/build-assessments.mjs`, which proves every rule against the bank inventory
(`.scratch/combined-science/inventory.ts`).

**Topic tests (19)** — one per topic (7.1.1–7.1.7, 7.2.1–7.2.6, 7.3.1–7.3.6), each 20 marks in 25 minutes:

- Section A: 11–13 one-mark questions (difficulty 1–3) spread over the topic's subtopics.
- Section B: one medium question (2–4 marks, difficulty 2–4) and one exam-style structured question (5 or 6 marks,
  difficulty 3–5) drawn from the whole topic.
- Descriptions and instructions tell students that most questions come from the O-Level Biology, Chemistry and
  Physics banks and that an item labelled "(Supplement)" or written for another syllabus may go beyond Combined
  Science.

**Mock exams (2)** — labelled "Mock exam in the style of …", each stating that it is an original practice paper, not
an official paper:

| Mock | Time | Marks | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 (multiple choice) | 1 h | 40 | 40 one-mark multiple-choice questions (difficulty 1–4) over the topics of the syllabus's specification grid: Biology 13 (cells 2, nutrition 3, respiratory 2, transport 2, reproduction 2, health 2), Chemistry 13 (matter 4, acids 2, industrial processes 3, redox 2, organic 2), Physics 14 (measurement 2, force 4, energy 4, magnetism 2, electricity 2). |
| Paper 2 (theory) | 2 h | 100 | Section A: six compulsory exam-style questions (nutrition 7, health 7, industrial processes 7, organic chemistry 7, energy 6, force 6 marks; 40 marks). Sections B (Biology), C (Chemistry) and D (Physics): five 5-mark exam-style questions each, answer any four (20 marks per section; the best four count). |

The real Paper 2 offers three free-response questions per science section of which candidates answer two (10 marks
each); the bank has very few questions of 10 marks, so the mock keeps the time, the section marks and the choice with
5-mark questions, and says so in its instructions. Paper 3 (practical, 1 h 30 min) has no online mock.

## Known gaps

1. **Borrowed depth.** Most items were written for the separate O-Level sciences and Cambridge IGCSE, so some go
   beyond Combined Science (mole calculations, isotopes, bond energies, detailed genetics) and hundreds of chemistry
   and biology items carry Cambridge "(Supplement)" labels that mean nothing to a ZIMSEC Combined Science student.
   Topic tests and mocks warn about this. A Combined Science-specific pass would restrict the deepest items with
   `applies` (excluding `zimsec/o-level/combined-science`) or write simpler items for the thinnest topics.
2. **Thin Physics topics.** Moments (13 questions), hydro and thermal power generation (9), static electricity (12),
   Ohm's law (14), electrical power (14), electrical safety (13), engines (11) and density (16) have few questions,
   and Physics has only 73 exam-style questions (Magnetism 7, Electricity 8, Measurement 6). Paper 2 Section D and
   Section A's force rule draw from 2–4 candidates each, so mock attempts repeat questions; the physics-o deepening
   pass (more exam-style questions per concept) would fix this.
3. **Low difficulty in Physics.** Only 58 of 711 Physics-section questions are difficulty 4–5 (none in heat transfer
   and magnets).
4. **No diagrams.** Apparatus, circuits, gears, pumps, solar collectors and cell networks are described in words.
5. **Self-marked written answers** while AI marking is switched off (about a third of the own content's marks).
6. **No Paper 3 (practical) mock**, and no continuous-assessment tasks (35 % of the ZIMSEC grade).
7. **Syllabus unverified.** The syllabus was read from a provincial copy (expired https certificate) through a text
   rendering; check every heading, objective and the assessment section against a clean copy. The subject code is
   not printed (commonly cited as 4003). Under the Heritage-Based Curriculum (2024-2030) November 2026 and later
   candidates may sit a new syllabus.
8. **Answer cues in borrowed banks.** In many borrowed single-answer MCQs the key is clearly the longest option
   (biology-o 342 of 997, chemistry-o 233 of 975); the own Combined Science items and physics-o were fixed on
   2026-10-08.

## Needs a specialist teacher's check

- **Ebola:** the 21-day maximum incubation period and the contact-tracing and safe-burial practice as taught.
- **Peanut processing:** aflatoxin facts (not reliably destroyed by roasting), groundnut cake as animal feed, and the
  small-scale process steps.
- **Solar heating:** typical box-cooker temperatures (about 100–150 °C), curved reflectors reaching 200 °C or more,
  collectors facing north at roughly 20° in Zimbabwe.
- **Telecommunication:** the simplified account of base stations, switching centres and hand-over, and email via mail
  servers; the statement that fibre links connect Zimbabwe's towns to undersea cables via Mozambique and South Africa.
- **Fluid systems and gears:** the Blair pump described as a lift pump, and the 10 m limit for lift pumps and siphons.
- **Syllabus mapping:** which borrowed concepts each Combined Science subtopic `covers` (e.g. data presentation drawn
  from mathematics-o statistics; laboratory safety from biology-o and chemistry-o).

## Maintenance

- Own questions and flashcards are generated: edit `.scratch/combined-science/slices/*.mjs` and run
  `node .scratch/combined-science/build-content.mjs` (it balances MCQ answer positions before writing). Lessons are
  edited directly in `lessons/`.
- After the bank changes, refresh the inventory (`npx tsx --conditions=react-server
  .scratch/combined-science/inventory.ts .scratch/combined-science/inventory.json`), then
  `node .scratch/combined-science/build-assessments.mjs --write` and the coverage table
  (`.scratch/combined-science/coverage.ts`).
- The syllabus file is generated by `node .scratch/combined-science/build-syllabus.mjs`; a published syllabus version
  cannot change structure, so later changes need a new version.
