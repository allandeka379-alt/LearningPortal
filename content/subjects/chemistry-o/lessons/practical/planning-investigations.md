## Introduction

Both boards test whether you can plan an experiment, not just follow one. Cambridge Papers 5 and 6 usually end with a planning question worth several marks ("Plan an experiment to find out..."), and ZIMSEC Paper 3 and school-based projects ask you to design investigations, such as finding which water source near your school is hardest or which fuel heats water best. A good plan is clear enough that another student could carry it out and get the same results.

This lesson gives you a checklist for planning and shows how to identify variables, choose a fair test and work safely.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Independent variable"}
The variable you deliberately change, such as the temperature or the concentration of acid.
:::

:::definition{term="Dependent variable"}
The variable you measure to see the effect, such as the time taken or the volume of gas produced.
:::

:::definition{term="Control variables"}
All the other variables that could affect the result and must be kept the same, so that the test is fair.
:::

:::definition{term="Fair test"}
An investigation in which only the independent variable is changed and all the control variables are kept constant.
:::

:::definition{term="Hazard and precaution"}
A hazard is something that could cause harm (for example a corrosive acid); a precaution is what you do to reduce the risk (for example wearing eye protection).
:::

## A checklist for planning

1. **Aim and prediction**: what are you finding out, and what do you expect?
2. **Variables**: name the independent, dependent and main control variables.
3. **Apparatus**: name each item, with sizes (a 100 cm³ gas syringe, a 50 cm³ measuring cylinder).
4. **Method**: numbered steps with quantities (volumes, concentrations, masses).
5. **Range**: at least five different values of the independent variable, spread across a sensible range.
6. **Repeats**: repeat each measurement and calculate a mean; identify anomalous results.
7. **Safety**: specific hazards and precautions.
8. **Results**: say what you will record and plot, and how you will reach a conclusion.

## Worked example: a plan for a rate investigation

:::worked-solution[Plan: how does temperature affect the rate of reaction between magnesium ribbon and dilute hydrochloric acid?]
1. Independent variable: temperature of the acid (20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 °C). Dependent variable: time for a 2 cm length of magnesium ribbon to disappear.
2. Control variables: volume (25 cm³) and concentration (1.0 mol/dm³) of acid, length of magnesium ribbon from the same roll, same container.
3. Measure 25 cm³ of acid with a measuring cylinder into a boiling tube and warm it in a water bath to the first temperature, checking it with a thermometer.
4. Add the magnesium, start a stop-watch, and stop it when the magnesium has disappeared.
5. Repeat for each of the other temperatures, carrying out each temperature three times and calculating the mean time.
6. Safety: wear eye protection because the acid is an irritant; take care with the hot water bath.
7. Plot mean time (or rate, 1/time) against temperature and describe the trend.
:::

## Illustrative example: comparing antacid tablets

:::example{title="Which antacid tablet neutralises the most acid?"}
Independent variable: brand of tablet. Dependent variable: volume of 0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid needed to neutralise one crushed tablet (found by titration with methyl orange). Control variables: one whole tablet each time, the same acid, the same volume of water used to disperse the tablet, the same indicator. Repeat each brand until concordant titres are obtained. The tablet needing the largest volume of acid neutralises the most acid. To compare value for money, divide by the price of each tablet.
:::

## Hazards and precautions

| Hazard | Examples | Typical precautions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| corrosive | concentrated acids and alkalis | eye protection, gloves, small volumes, add acid to water |
| flammable | ethanol, paraffin, hydrogen | no naked flames nearby, small amounts, use a water bath for heating |
| toxic | chlorine, sulfur dioxide, lead compounds | fume cupboard or good ventilation, avoid skin contact |
| oxidising | concentrated hydrogen peroxide, nitrates, potassium manganate(VII) | keep away from flammable materials |
| harmful to the environment | copper and other heavy-metal compounds | collect waste in a labelled container; do not pour down the sink |

Safety statements must be specific: "wear eye protection because hydrochloric acid is an irritant" earns credit; "be careful" does not.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Vague control variables"}
"Use the same amount" does not say what. Write "the same volume (25 cm³) and concentration (1.0 mol/dm³) of acid".
:::

:::mistake{title="Changing two things at once"}
If both the temperature and the size of the marble chips change, you cannot tell which one caused the change in rate.
:::

:::mistake{title="Only one or two values"}
Two temperatures cannot show a trend. Use at least five values across a sensible range.
:::

:::mistake{title="No repeats"}
A single measurement may be anomalous. Repeat, and use the mean of results that agree.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In a planning answer, name every piece of measuring apparatus and give quantities. Examiners award marks for "measure 25 cm³ of acid with a measuring cylinder", not for "add acid".
:::

:::exam-tip
Say how the dependent variable will be measured and when: "record the volume in the gas syringe every 30 seconds for 5 minutes".
:::

:::exam-tip
Finish your plan by saying how the results will answer the question: what you will plot and what pattern would support your prediction.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Change one independent variable, measure one dependent variable, and keep the control variables the same.
- A good plan names apparatus and quantities and gives numbered steps.
- Use at least five values of the independent variable, repeat measurements and calculate means.
- Identify specific hazards (corrosive, flammable, toxic, oxidising, harmful to the environment) and matching precautions.
- Explain how the results will be processed and how they test the prediction.
:::
