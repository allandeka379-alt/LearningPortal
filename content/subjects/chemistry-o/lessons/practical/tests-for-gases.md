## Introduction

Most of the gases you make in the laboratory are colourless, and several have no smell. When a reaction fizzes, you cannot tell by looking whether the bubbles are hydrogen, oxygen or carbon dioxide. Each gas therefore has its own **test**: a quick procedure with a result that no other common gas gives.

Gas tests appear in almost every practical paper and in many theory questions, often as one-mark recall: "Name the gas and give a test for it." They are also needed to complete other tests, such as the tests for carbonate, ammonium and nitrate ions. You should already know the reactions of acids with metals and carbonates.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Effervescence"}
Bubbling or fizzing caused by a gas being produced in a liquid.
:::

:::definition{term="Limewater"}
A dilute solution of calcium hydroxide in water, used to test for carbon dioxide.
:::

:::definition{term="Glowing splint"}
A wooden splint that has been lit and then blown out so that only its tip glows red.
:::

## The six gas tests

| Gas | Test | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ammonia, $\ce{NH3}$ | hold damp red litmus paper in the gas | turns blue |
| carbon dioxide, $\ce{CO2}$ | bubble the gas through limewater | limewater turns milky |
| chlorine, $\ce{Cl2}$ | hold damp litmus paper in the gas | litmus is bleached (turns white) |
| hydrogen, $\ce{H2}$ | hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube | squeaky "pop" |
| oxygen, $\ce{O2}$ | put a glowing splint into the gas | splint relights |
| sulfur dioxide, $\ce{SO2}$ | bubble the gas into acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) | purple to colourless |

:::note{title="Another sulfur dioxide test (ZIMSEC)"}
Some ZIMSEC courses test sulfur dioxide with acidified aqueous potassium **dichromate(VI)**, which changes from **orange to green**. Both tests rely on sulfur dioxide acting as a reducing agent.
:::

## Why the tests work

**Ammonia** is the only common alkaline gas. It dissolves in the water on damp litmus paper to form hydroxide ions, so red litmus turns blue. Dry litmus paper would not change, because the gas must dissolve first.

**Carbon dioxide** is an acidic oxide. It reacts with calcium hydroxide in limewater to form tiny particles of insoluble calcium carbonate, which make the liquid look milky:

$$
\ce{Ca(OH)2(aq) + CO2(g) -> CaCO3(s) + H2O(l)}
$$

**Chlorine** dissolves in the water on the paper, forming acids, one of which is a strong bleach. Damp blue litmus turns red and is then bleached white.

**Hydrogen** mixes with air at the mouth of the tube, and the flame ignites the mixture in a tiny explosion: $\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}$.

**Oxygen** supports combustion. The glowing wood burns faster in pure oxygen and bursts back into flame.

**Sulfur dioxide** is a reducing agent: it reduces purple manganate(VII) ions to almost colourless manganese(II) ions.

## Where each gas comes from

| Gas | Typical laboratory source |
| --- | --- |
| hydrogen | a metal such as zinc or magnesium with a dilute acid |
| oxygen | hydrogen peroxide with a manganese(IV) oxide catalyst |
| carbon dioxide | a carbonate (such as marble chips) with a dilute acid |
| ammonia | an ammonium salt warmed with an alkali; a nitrate warmed with aluminium and sodium hydroxide |
| chlorine | electrolysis of concentrated sodium chloride solution or molten chlorides (at the positive electrode) |
| sulfur dioxide | burning sulfur in air; a sulfite with a dilute acid |

Chlorine and sulfur dioxide are toxic, and ammonia is harmful, so they are made in small amounts in a fume cupboard.

## Worked examples

:::example{title="Example 1: testing the gas from a warm mixture"}
Rudo warms solid ammonium chloride with aqueous sodium hydroxide.

1. Alkali + ammonium salt gives ammonia.
2. Test: hold **damp** red litmus paper at the mouth of the test tube (not touching the liquid, which is itself alkaline).
3. Result: the paper turns blue.
:::

:::example{title="Example 2: two test tubes of gas"}
Two test tubes each contain a colourless gas: one hydrogen, one oxygen. How can you tell them apart with a single splint?

1. Light the splint and blow it out so that it glows.
2. In oxygen, the glowing splint relights.
3. Light it again and hold it at the mouth of the other tube: hydrogen burns with a squeaky pop.
:::

:::example{title="Example 3: identifying a gas from several clues"}
A colourless gas has a choking smell, turns damp blue litmus red without bleaching it, and turns acidified potassium manganate(VII) colourless.

1. Turning blue litmus red shows an acidic gas; it is not bleached, so it is not chlorine.
2. Decolourising acidified potassium manganate(VII) is the test for sulfur dioxide.
3. The gas is sulfur dioxide.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the splints"}
Oxygen **relights a glowing** splint. Hydrogen **pops with a lighted** splint. Learn them as a pair.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing the limewater result vaguely"}
Say that the **limewater** turns milky (cloudy). "The gas turns white" or "it bubbles" does not describe the result of the test.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using dry litmus paper"}
Ammonia and chlorine must dissolve in water to act on litmus, so the paper must be **damp**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying chlorine turns litmus red"}
The key result for chlorine is that litmus is **bleached**. Turning red alone could be any acidic gas.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
A gas test answer needs the **test** and the **result**: "glowing splint relights" scores; "use a splint" does not.
:::

:::exam-tip
Name the colour change in full and in the right order: "purple to colourless" for potassium manganate(VII); "red to blue" for litmus with ammonia.
:::

:::exam-tip
Learn which reaction produces each gas, because questions often ask you to name the gas from the reaction before you give the test.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Ammonia: damp red litmus turns blue. Carbon dioxide: limewater turns milky.
- Chlorine: bleaches damp litmus. Hydrogen: pops with a lighted splint.
- Oxygen: relights a glowing splint.
- Sulfur dioxide: acidified potassium manganate(VII) turns from purple to colourless (ZIMSEC also: acidified potassium dichromate(VI), orange to green).
- Toxic gases such as chlorine and sulfur dioxide are handled in a fume cupboard.
:::
