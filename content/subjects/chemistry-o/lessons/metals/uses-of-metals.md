## Introduction

Look around a Zimbabwean kitchen and you will find aluminium pots, copper wires inside the walls, a steel knife and perhaps a mobile phone with a lithium battery. Each metal was chosen for a reason. Engineers ask: does it need to conduct electricity? Must it be light? Will it be wet or in contact with food? How much does it cost?

Zimbabwe mines many of the world's important metals, including gold, platinum-group metals, chromium, nickel and lithium. Knowing what they are used for explains why they are valuable. In this lesson you will learn to link the use of a metal to its properties. Cambridge IGCSE students must know four uses of aluminium and copper; ZIMSEC students also need the uses of the metals mined in Zimbabwe. You should already know the general properties of metals.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Density"}
The mass of a unit volume of a substance, usually in g/cm³. A low-density metal is light for its size.
:::

:::definition{term="Corrosion"}
The slow reaction of a metal with substances in its surroundings, such as oxygen and water, which wears it away. Rusting is the corrosion of iron.
:::

:::definition{term="Electrical conductivity"}
How easily a material allows an electric current to pass through it.
:::

## Four uses to learn (Cambridge Core)

| Metal | Use | Properties that make it suitable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| aluminium | aircraft bodies | low density (light), and strong when alloyed |
| aluminium | overhead power cables | low density and good electrical conductivity |
| aluminium | food containers (cans, foil, trays) | resists corrosion |
| copper | electrical wiring | very good electrical conductivity; ductile (can be drawn into wires) |

Aluminium resists corrosion because its surface is covered by a thin, tough layer of aluminium oxide that stops air and water reaching the metal underneath. Copper conducts electricity better than any common metal except silver, and silver is far too expensive for wiring.

:::example{title="Why not copper for overhead cables?"}
Copper conducts electricity better than aluminium, but copper is more than three times as dense. Long overhead cables made of copper would be very heavy and need stronger, more closely spaced pylons. Aluminium cables, usually wound around a steel core for strength, carry the current while keeping the weight low.
:::

## Choosing a metal

For any use, the important properties are chosen from:

- **density** (is weight important?);
- **strength and hardness**;
- **electrical or thermal conductivity**;
- **resistance to corrosion** (will it be exposed to air, water or food?);
- **melting point**;
- **appearance**;
- **cost and availability**.

Aluminium cooking pots are a good everyday example: aluminium conducts heat well, is light to lift and does not corrode in the kitchen.

## Metals mined in Zimbabwe and their uses (ZIMSEC)

| Metal | Main uses | Properties that make it suitable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| iron (as steel) | building frames, reinforcing bars in concrete, roofing sheets, machinery, vehicles | strong, hard and cheap to produce |
| platinum-group metals (platinum, palladium, rhodium) | catalytic converters in car exhausts; jewellery; laboratory equipment | excellent catalysts, very unreactive, high melting points, do not corrode |
| gold | jewellery; electrical connectors in phones and computers; dentistry; reserves held by banks | very unreactive (does not tarnish), lustrous, very malleable, good conductor |
| chromium | stainless steel; chromium plating | makes steel resist corrosion; shiny, hard-wearing surface |
| nickel | stainless steel; rechargeable batteries; plating and coins | resists corrosion; strengthens steel |
| lithium | rechargeable lithium-ion batteries for phones, laptops and electric vehicles | the least dense metal; very reactive, so it gives a high voltage in a cell |
| copper | wiring, water pipes, alloys such as brass and bronze | conductor, ductile, does not react with water |

The name of Zimbabwe's currency, ZiG, stands for "Zimbabwe Gold": gold is valuable partly because it never corrodes, so it keeps its value for centuries.

## Worked examples

:::worked-solution[Example 1: explaining a use]
Explain why platinum is used in catalytic converters.

1. A catalytic converter must speed up the reactions that turn harmful exhaust gases (carbon monoxide, oxides of nitrogen and unburnt fuel) into less harmful ones.
2. Platinum is a very good **catalyst** for these reactions.
3. It is very **unreactive** and has a high melting point, so it is not used up or damaged by the hot exhaust gases.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: using data to choose a metal]
A wire must carry electricity inside a house. Use the data to choose a metal.

| Metal | Relative electrical conductivity | Resistance to corrosion | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| silver | 106 | good | very high |
| copper | 100 | good | moderate |
| iron | 17 | poor (rusts) | low |

1. Iron conducts poorly and rusts, so it is unsuitable.
2. Silver is the best conductor, but the cost is far too high for long lengths of wire.
3. Copper is an excellent conductor, does not corrode easily, is ductile and costs much less than silver: choose copper.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Giving a property that does not match the use"}
"Aluminium is used for aircraft because it conducts electricity" scores nothing. Match the property to what the use needs: aircraft need **low density**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying aluminium does not react"}
Aluminium is a reactive metal. It resists corrosion only because of its protective oxide layer.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using 'strong' and 'hard' for everything"}
Name the property that really matters for the use, such as conductivity for wires or corrosion resistance for food cans.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Explain why X is used for Y" needs a property **and** a link to the use: "copper is ductile, so it can be drawn into thin wires".
:::

:::exam-tip
In data questions, compare the metals on every property in the table and give a reason for rejecting each alternative.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Aluminium: aircraft (low density), overhead cables (low density and good conductor), food containers (resists corrosion).
- Copper: electrical wiring (very good conductor and ductile).
- Choose a metal by matching its properties (density, strength, conductivity, corrosion resistance, cost) to the use.
- ZIMSEC: steel for construction, PGMs in catalytic converters, gold in jewellery and electronics, chromium and nickel in stainless steel, lithium in rechargeable batteries.
:::
