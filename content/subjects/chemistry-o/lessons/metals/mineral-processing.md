## Introduction

A truck of copper ore straight from the mine is worth very little: most of it is waste rock. A tonne of pure copper cathodes is worth a great deal, and copper wire, pipes or electrical goods are worth more still. Every processing step **adds value**. For a mining country like Zimbabwe, an important question is how many of these steps happen at home, creating jobs and earnings, and how many happen abroad.

In this lesson you will follow ores through the main processing stages, look at how gold is recovered and why mercury is so dangerous, and learn what **value addition** and **beneficiation** mean. This is ZIMSEC Form 4 content, at outline level. You should already know about ores, the reactivity series and electrolysis.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Mineral processing"}
The steps that turn mined ore into a concentrated mineral and then into a pure metal.
:::

:::definition{term="Froth flotation"}
A method of concentrating finely ground ore (especially sulfide ores) in which the valuable mineral particles stick to air bubbles and float in a froth, while the waste rock sinks.
:::

:::definition{term="Smelting"}
Heating a concentrate strongly, usually with other substances, until it melts, so that the metal (or a metal-rich liquid) separates from the waste as slag.
:::

:::definition{term="Tailings"}
The finely ground waste rock and water left after the valuable mineral has been removed.
:::

:::definition{term="Value addition"}
Processing a raw material into a product that is worth more, for example ore into metal, or metal into jewellery.
:::

:::definition{term="Beneficiation"}
Processing minerals in the country where they are mined, by concentrating, smelting and refining them, instead of exporting the raw ore.
:::

## From ore to metal: the main stages

1. **Crushing and grinding (milling).** The ore is broken into small pieces and then ground to a fine powder. This frees the grains of valuable mineral from the gangue and gives a large surface area for the next steps.
2. **Concentration.** The valuable mineral is separated from most of the waste. Sulfide ores of copper, nickel and the platinum-group metals are concentrated by **froth flotation**: the powdered ore is mixed with water and small amounts of chemicals, air is bubbled through, and the mineral particles cling to the bubbles and are skimmed off with the froth. Magnetite can be separated with magnets, and gold, which is very dense, partly by gravity methods.
3. **Roasting and smelting.** Sulfide concentrates are heated in air (roasting), which turns sulfides into oxides and releases sulfur dioxide, for example $\ce{2ZnS + 3O2 -> 2ZnO + 2SO2}$. Smelting melts the concentrate in a furnace: the metal, or a molten mixture of metal sulfides called **matte**, sinks below a layer of slag. Platinum-group metal concentrates are smelted to a matte rich in nickel, copper and the PGMs.
4. **Refining.** The impure metal is purified, often by **electrolysis**.

![Flow chart from copper ore to wire: crushing and grinding, froth flotation to concentrate the mineral, roasting and smelting to impure copper (sulfur dioxide captured for sulfuric acid), electrolytic refining to copper over 99.9% pure, then drawing into wire adds more value.](media:diagrams/mineral-processing-ore-to-copper-flow.png "Figure: the stages in processing a copper ore, each step adding value")

The sulfur dioxide from roasting and smelting must not be released, because it forms acid rain. Modern plants capture it and use it to make sulfuric acid.

## Electrolytic refining of copper and nickel

- **Anode** (positive): a block of impure copper.
- **Cathode** (negative): a thin sheet of pure copper.
- **Electrolyte**: copper(II) sulfate solution, acidified with sulfuric acid.

![Electrolytic refining of copper: the impure copper anode (+) dissolves as Cu2+ ions, pure copper is deposited on the cathode (-), and unreactive silver, gold and platinum fall to the bottom as anode sludge; the electrolyte is acidified copper(II) sulfate solution.](media:diagrams/copper-refining-electrolytic-cell.png "Figure: the cell for refining copper, with the electrode equations")

At the anode, copper atoms lose electrons and go into solution:

$$
\ce{Cu -> Cu^2+ + 2e-}
$$

At the cathode, copper ions gain electrons and pure copper is deposited:

$$
\ce{Cu^2+ + 2e- -> Cu}
$$

The anode gets thinner and the cathode grows. Unreactive impurities such as silver, gold and platinum do not dissolve; they fall to the bottom as **anode sludge**, which is collected and processed because it is valuable. Nickel is refined in a similar way, using nickel anodes and a nickel salt solution.

## Recovering gold

Large gold mines crush and mill the ore, then add a dilute solution of **sodium cyanide** with air. The cyanide dissolves the tiny gold particles. The gold is then recovered from the solution, for example by adsorbing it onto activated carbon or by adding **zinc** powder: zinc is more reactive than gold and displaces it. The gold is finally melted into bars. Cyanide is extremely poisonous, so it must be stored and used carefully, kept out of rivers, and destroyed before the tailings are stored in lined dams.

### Mercury in small-scale mining

Many small-scale and artisanal miners mix crushed ore with **mercury**, which dissolves gold to form an **amalgam**. The amalgam is then heated to drive off the mercury as vapour, leaving gold. This is very dangerous:

- mercury vapour is poisonous to breathe and damages the nervous system and kidneys;
- spilt mercury pollutes soil and rivers;
- in water it is changed into compounds that build up in fish, and so in the people who eat them.

Using a **retort** (a closed container that condenses and recovers the mercury), heating amalgam well away from homes, and changing to mercury-free gravity methods all reduce the harm.

## Value addition and beneficiation in Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe smelts chromite into ferrochrome, and policy encourages processing more of its lithium and platinum-group metals within the country rather than exporting raw ore.

<!-- Reviewer: plant-specific and policy statements kept general on purpose (ferrochrome smelting; encouragement of local processing of lithium and PGMs). Confirm they are still accurate before publishing. -->

**Benefits** of beneficiation:

- more jobs and new skills in processing and refining;
- higher export earnings, because processed metals are worth more than ore;
- new local industries that use the products, such as stainless-steel or battery-materials manufacture;
- less money spent transporting waste rock overseas;
- more tax revenue for public services.

**Challenges**: smelters and refineries need large amounts of reliable electricity, water and investment, skilled workers, and careful control of pollution such as sulfur dioxide, dust and tailings.

## Worked example

:::example{title="Following copper from mine to wire"}
1. Chalcopyrite ore is crushed and ground to a fine powder.
2. Froth flotation produces a concentrate containing much more copper than the ore.
3. The concentrate is roasted and smelted to give impure copper; the sulfur dioxide is used to make sulfuric acid.
4. Electrolytic refining gives copper that is more than 99.9% pure, as needed for electrical wiring.
5. Drawing the copper into wire, and making cables, adds still more value.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the electrodes in refining"}
The **impure** copper is the **anode**; the **pure** copper is the **cathode**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking anode sludge is waste"}
Anode sludge contains silver, gold and platinum and is a valuable by-product.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing value addition with beneficiation"}
Value addition is any processing that increases value; beneficiation means doing that processing **in the country** where the mineral is mined.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "describe how an ore is processed", give the stages in order: crushing and grinding, concentration (froth flotation), roasting or smelting, refining.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked about the dangers of mercury, give a health effect **and** an environmental effect, and suggest one safer practice.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Ore is crushed and ground, concentrated (froth flotation for sulfides), roasted or smelted, and then refined.
- Electrolytic refining: impure anode, pure cathode, copper(II) sulfate electrolyte; valuable impurities collect as anode sludge.
- Gold is dissolved by cyanide and recovered, for example by displacement with zinc; cyanide and mercury are both highly toxic.
- Value addition increases the worth of a mineral; beneficiation keeps the processing in Zimbabwe, bringing jobs, skills and higher earnings.
:::
