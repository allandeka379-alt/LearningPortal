## Introduction

Ammonium nitrate (AN) is the top-dressing fertiliser that gives Zimbabwe's maize crop its nitrogen boost in the middle of the rainy season. It is made by neutralising ammonia with **nitric acid**, and the nitric acid is itself made from ammonia. The method was patented by the German chemist Wilhelm Ostwald in 1902, and it is called the **Ostwald process**. Ammonium nitrate is also the main ingredient of many explosives used to blast rock in mines and quarries.

In this lesson you will follow the nitrogen atom from ammonia to nitric acid, learn the conditions used and why, and weigh up the benefits and the environmental costs.

You should already know the Haber process, how to balance equations, and the meaning of oxidation in terms of oxygen and oxidation numbers.

:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Ostwald process"}
The industrial manufacture of nitric acid by the catalytic oxidation of ammonia.
:::

:::definition{term="Catalytic oxidation"}
Oxidation that is speeded up, and guided to the wanted product, by a catalyst. In the Ostwald process ammonia is oxidised to nitrogen monoxide on a platinum–rhodium catalyst.
:::

:::definition{term="Nitric acid"}
$\ce{HNO3}$, a strong acid and a powerful oxidising agent. Concentrated nitric acid is very corrosive.
:::

## The raw materials

- **Ammonia**, made by the Haber process.
- **Air**, which supplies the oxygen.
- **Water**, which absorbs nitrogen dioxide in the last stage.

## The three stages

**Stage 1: oxidising ammonia.** A mixture of ammonia and air is passed through a gauze (a fine mesh) made of a **platinum–rhodium alloy** at about **900 °C**:

$$
\ce{4NH3(g) + 5O2(g) -> 4NO(g) + 6H2O(g)}
$$

The product is colourless **nitrogen monoxide**, $\ce{NO}$. The reaction is strongly exothermic, so once it has started the heat it releases keeps the gauze glowing red-hot without further heating.

**Stage 2: forming nitrogen dioxide.** The hot gases are cooled and mixed with more air. Nitrogen monoxide reacts with oxygen to form brown **nitrogen dioxide**:

$$
\ce{2NO(g) + O2(g) -> 2NO2(g)}
$$

**Stage 3: absorption.** Nitrogen dioxide and more air are passed up a tall tower down which water trickles:

$$
\ce{4NO2(g) + O2(g) + 2H2O(l) -> 4HNO3(aq)}
$$

The product is a solution of about 60% nitric acid.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: checking the stage 1 equation]
$\ce{4NH3 + 5O2 -> 4NO + 6H2O}$

1. Nitrogen: $4$ on the left, $4$ on the right.
2. Hydrogen: $4 \times 3 = 12$ on the left; $6 \times 2 = 12$ on the right.
3. Oxygen: $5 \times 2 = 10$ on the left; $4 + 6 = 10$ on the right. The equation is balanced.
:::

## Why these conditions?

- **The platinum–rhodium catalyst** speeds up the reaction and, just as importantly, makes the ammonia form **nitrogen monoxide**. Without it, ammonia burning in oxygen gives mainly nitrogen gas, which is useless here: $\ce{4NH3 + 3O2 -> 2N2 + 6H2O}$. The catalyst is made as a gauze to give a **large surface area**; rhodium makes it stronger and reduces the loss of platinum. The metals are very expensive, so any platinum lost is recovered.
- **About 900 °C** makes the reaction extremely fast: the gases touch the gauze for only a tiny fraction of a second. The exothermic reaction itself keeps the temperature up.
- **A pressure of a few atmospheres** is used. A higher pressure helps stages 2 and 3, in which the number of gas molecules decreases, and allows smaller equipment.
- **Cooling** the gases helps stage 2, which is exothermic, so it is favoured at lower temperatures.

## Following the nitrogen atom

Every step is an oxidation of nitrogen:

| Substance | $\ce{NH3}$ | $\ce{NO}$ | $\ce{NO2}$ | $\ce{HNO3}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oxidation number of N | $-3$ | $+2$ | $+4$ | $+5$ |

Adding the three stages together gives the overall change: $\ce{NH3 + 2O2 -> HNO3 + H2O}$. One molecule of ammonia gives at most one molecule of nitric acid.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: mass of nitric acid from ammonia]
How much nitric acid can be made from 3.4 tonnes of ammonia? ($A_r$: H = 1, N = 14, O = 16)

1. $M_r(\ce{NH3}) = 17$ and $M_r(\ce{HNO3}) = 1 + 14 + 48 = 63$.
2. 1 mol of $\ce{NH3}$ gives 1 mol of $\ce{HNO3}$, so 17 t gives 63 t.
3. $3.4 \times \dfrac{63}{17} = 12.6$ tonnes of nitric acid.
:::

## Uses of nitric acid

- making **ammonium nitrate** fertiliser: $\ce{NH3 + HNO3 -> NH4NO3}$ (the largest use);
- making **explosives**, including ammonium nitrate explosives for mining, TNT and nitroglycerine;
- making dyes, nylon and other chemicals, and etching and cleaning metals.

## Environmental impacts and safety

- **Oxides of nitrogen** ($\ce{NO}$ and $\ce{NO2}$) escaping from the absorption towers cause **acid rain**, photochemical smog and breathing problems. Modern plants treat the waste gas, for example by reacting it with ammonia over a catalyst to turn it back into nitrogen.
- Small amounts of **nitrous oxide**, $\ce{N2O}$, form on the gauze. It is a powerful greenhouse gas, and special catalysts are used to destroy it.
- Overuse of nitrate fertilisers causes **eutrophication** of rivers and lakes and can raise nitrate levels in drinking water.
- Nitric acid is corrosive, nitrogen dioxide is toxic and ammonium nitrate can explode if it is heated strongly or mixed with fuels, so storage and handling must be strictly controlled.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the catalysts"}
Ostwald: platinum–rhodium. Haber: iron. Contact: vanadium(V) oxide.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling nitrogen monoxide brown"}
$\ce{NO}$ is colourless. It turns brown when it reacts with oxygen in the air to form $\ce{NO2}$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Starting from nitrogen gas"}
Nitric acid is made from **ammonia**, not directly from nitrogen. The nitrogen is first fixed as ammonia in the Haber process.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Learn the three equations in order and check each one balances. Examiners often give one equation and ask for the oxidation numbers of nitrogen.
:::

:::exam-tip
"Explain the conditions" needs reasons: the catalyst speeds the reaction and gives NO rather than N₂; the high temperature gives a very fast rate; the exothermic reaction keeps the gauze hot.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Raw materials: ammonia (from the Haber process), air and water.
- Stage 1: $\ce{4NH3 + 5O2 -> 4NO + 6H2O}$ on a platinum–rhodium gauze at about 900 °C.
- Stage 2: $\ce{2NO + O2 -> 2NO2}$ after cooling; Stage 3: $\ce{4NO2 + O2 + 2H2O -> 4HNO3}$.
- Nitrogen is oxidised from −3 in ammonia to +5 in nitric acid.
- Nitric acid is used for ammonium nitrate fertiliser, explosives and dyes.
- Escaping oxides of nitrogen cause acid rain and smog; nitrous oxide is a greenhouse gas; fertiliser run-off causes eutrophication.
:::
