## Introduction

A maize crop in Mashonaland needs a lot of nitrogen to grow well, and the ammonium nitrate (AN) that farmers spread as top dressing supplies it. That fertiliser, like almost every nitrogen fertiliser in the world, starts as **ammonia** made by the **Haber process**. Fritz Haber showed in 1909 that nitrogen from the air could be combined with hydrogen, and by 1913 the process was running on an industrial scale.

Nitrogen gas is very unreactive because of the strong triple bond in the $\ce{N2}$ molecule. The Haber process is a clever compromise that persuades it to react fast enough, in a reaction that is reversible.

You should already know about reversible reactions, how conditions affect the position of equilibrium, and how temperature, pressure and catalysts affect rates.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Haber process"}
The industrial manufacture of ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen, using an iron catalyst at about 450 °C and 200 atm.
:::

:::definition{term="Ammonia"}
$\ce{NH3}$: a colourless, alkaline gas with a sharp smell. It turns damp red litmus paper blue and liquefies at $-33\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

:::formula{name="The Haber process reaction"}
$$
\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)} \qquad \Delta H = -92\ \text{kJ/mol}
$$
:::

## The raw materials

| Raw material | Source |
| --- | --- |
| nitrogen | **air**, which is about 78% nitrogen; it is separated by the liquefaction and fractional distillation of air |
| hydrogen | **methane** (natural gas), which reacts with steam: $\ce{CH4 + H2O -> CO + 3H2}$; or, where electricity is cheap, the **electrolysis of water** |

The gases are purified first, because impurities such as sulfur compounds would **poison** the iron catalyst (stop it working).

## Inside the plant

1. Nitrogen and hydrogen are mixed in the ratio **1 : 3** by volume, matching the equation.
2. The mixture is **compressed to about 200 atm** (20 000 kPa).
3. It is passed over an **iron catalyst** at about **450 °C** in the converter.
4. Only about **15%** of the gases are converted into ammonia in one pass.
5. The mixture is **cooled**. Ammonia liquefies at $-33\ ^\circ\text{C}$, while nitrogen ($-196\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and hydrogen ($-253\ ^\circ\text{C}$) stay as gases, so liquid ammonia is run off.
6. The unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen are **recycled** to the converter, so almost all of them are eventually turned into ammonia.

## Why these conditions? (Supplement)

:::worked-solution[Example 1: explaining the temperature and pressure]
1. **Temperature.** $\Delta H$ is negative, so the forward reaction is exothermic. A **low** temperature would give a higher percentage of ammonia at equilibrium, but the reaction would be far too slow. About 450 °C is a **compromise**: a reasonable yield produced at a fast rate, and a temperature at which the iron catalyst works well.
2. **Pressure.** There are 4 gas molecules on the left and 2 on the right, so a **high** pressure shifts the position to the right and increases the yield; it also increases the rate. But very high pressures need thick steel pipes and vessels and powerful compressors, which are expensive to build and run and more dangerous. About 200 atm is a **compromise** between yield, cost and safety.
3. **Catalyst.** Iron increases the rate so that equilibrium is approached quickly. It does not change the yield, but it means a lower temperature can be used than would otherwise be needed.
4. **Removing ammonia** and **recycling** the unreacted gases keep the overall conversion high without needing a higher yield in each pass.
:::

## Reacting amounts

The equation shows that 1 volume of nitrogen reacts with 3 volumes of hydrogen to give 2 volumes of ammonia, and that 28 g of nitrogen gives 34 g of ammonia.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: volumes and masses]
1. **Volumes.** $50\ \text{dm}^3$ of nitrogen needs $3 \times 50 = 150\ \text{dm}^3$ of hydrogen and could form at most $2 \times 50 = 100\ \text{dm}^3$ of ammonia.
2. **Masses.** $M_r(\ce{N2}) = 28$ and $M_r(\ce{NH3}) = 17$, so 28 tonnes of nitrogen could form $2 \times 17 = 34$ tonnes of ammonia. 56 tonnes of nitrogen could therefore form $56 \times \dfrac{34}{28} = 68$ tonnes.
3. If only 15% is converted in one pass, that pass gives $0.15 \times 68 = 10.2$ tonnes of ammonia.
:::

## Uses of ammonia

- making **fertilisers**: ammonium nitrate, ammonium sulfate and urea;
- making **nitric acid** (the Ostwald process), which is used for fertilisers and **explosives**;
- household **cleaning** solutions;
- a **refrigerant** in large cold stores, and in making nylon and dyes.

## Safety and the environment (ZIMSEC)

| Hazard | Precautions |
| --- | --- |
| ammonia is toxic and corrosive to the eyes and lungs | gas detectors and alarms, masks and protective clothing, emergency showers, good ventilation |
| hydrogen is highly flammable and explosive with air | no flames or sparks, sealed equipment, leak checks |
| very high pressure | strong steel vessels, pressure-relief valves, regular inspection |

Environmental impacts include the large amounts of **energy** used (often from fossil fuels, releasing carbon dioxide), the carbon dioxide released when hydrogen is made from methane, damage to rivers if ammonia leaks (it is very toxic to fish), and **eutrophication** when nitrogen fertilisers wash off fields into lakes and rivers. Against this, nitrogen fertilisers greatly increase the food that farms can produce.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying a high temperature gives a high yield"}
The forward reaction is exothermic, so a high temperature **lowers** the yield. 450 °C is chosen for the **rate**, as a compromise.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the catalyst increases the yield"}
Iron only makes equilibrium arrive faster. The yield is decided by temperature and pressure.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the ammonia is removed as a gas"}
Ammonia is removed by **cooling** the mixture until the ammonia **liquefies**; nitrogen and hydrogen stay as gases and are recycled.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Learn the three conditions as a set: **450 °C, 200 atm (20 000 kPa), iron catalyst**. Do not mix them up with the Contact process (450 °C, 2 atm, vanadium(V) oxide).
:::

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- $\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}$; the forward reaction is exothermic ($\Delta H = -92\ \text{kJ/mol}$).
- Nitrogen comes from the air; hydrogen from methane (or from the electrolysis of water where electricity is cheap).
- Typical conditions: about 450 °C, 200 atm (20 000 kPa) and an iron catalyst.
- 450 °C and 200 atm are compromises between yield, rate, cost and safety; the catalyst increases the rate only.
- Ammonia is removed by liquefaction and unreacted gases are recycled.
- Ammonia is used for fertilisers, nitric acid, explosives and cleaning products; its production must be managed for safety and the environment.
:::
