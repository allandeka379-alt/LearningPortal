## Introduction

Sulfuric acid is one of the most important chemicals in industry. It goes into fertilisers, car batteries, detergents and paints, and it is used to extract metals from their ores. It is made by the **Contact process**, named after the moment when sulfur dioxide and oxygen meet on the surface of a solid catalyst.

The process also helps the environment. When smelters roast sulfide ores of copper, zinc or nickel, they give off sulfur dioxide. Instead of letting this gas escape and cause acid rain, it can be collected and turned into sulfuric acid.

You should already know about reversible reactions and how temperature, pressure and catalysts affect the rate and the position of equilibrium.

:::note{title="Syllabus coverage"}
For **Cambridge Supplement** you need only the conversion of sulfur dioxide into sulfur trioxide: the equation, the sources of the reactants, the conditions and the reasons for them. **ZIMSEC Form 3** also covers the absorption stage, the uses of sulfuric acid and the environmental impacts.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Contact process"}
The industrial manufacture of sulfuric acid, in which sulfur dioxide and oxygen react on the surface of a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst to form sulfur trioxide.
:::

:::definition{term="Roasting"}
Heating a sulfide ore strongly in air, which turns it into an oxide and releases sulfur dioxide.
:::

:::definition{term="Oleum (ZIMSEC)"}
Fuming sulfuric acid, $\ce{H2S2O7}$, formed when sulfur trioxide dissolves in concentrated sulfuric acid.
:::

## Stage 1: making sulfur dioxide

Sulfur dioxide is made in either of two ways:

- **burning sulfur** in air: $\ce{S(s) + O2(g) -> SO2(g)}$. Much of this sulfur is removed from natural gas and crude oil when they are purified.
- **roasting sulfide ores**, for example $\ce{2ZnS + 3O2 -> 2ZnO + 2SO2}$ for zinc sulfide or $\ce{4FeS2 + 11O2 -> 2Fe2O3 + 8SO2}$ for iron pyrites.

The oxygen comes from the **air**. The gases are cleaned and dried, because dust and some impurities would **poison** the catalyst.

## Stage 2: the converter

$$
\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) <=> 2SO3(g)} \qquad \Delta H = -196\ \text{kJ/mol}
$$

The typical conditions are:

| Condition | Typical value |
| --- | --- |
| temperature | about 450 °C |
| pressure | about 2 atm (200 kPa), just above atmospheric pressure |
| catalyst | vanadium(V) oxide, $\ce{V2O5}$ |

The gases pass through several layers ("beds") of catalyst, and are cooled between them. By the end, more than 99% of the sulfur dioxide has been converted.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: explaining the conditions (Supplement)]
1. **Temperature.** The forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature would give a higher yield of sulfur trioxide, but the reaction would be too slow, and the catalyst works well only at about 400 °C or above. About 450 °C is a compromise between yield and rate.
2. **Pressure.** There are 3 gas molecules on the left and 2 on the right, so a high pressure **would** increase the yield. But the yield is already very high at about 2 atm, so the extra cost of high-pressure equipment and compressors is not worth it. A pressure slightly above atmospheric is enough to push the gases through the plant.
3. **Catalyst.** Vanadium(V) oxide increases the rate; it does not change the yield.
4. **Excess air.** Using more oxygen than the equation needs shifts the position of equilibrium to the right, so more of the sulfur dioxide is converted.
:::

## Stage 3: absorption (ZIMSEC)

Sulfur trioxide is **not** dissolved directly in water. That reaction is so exothermic that it produces a fine **mist** of acid droplets, which is hard to collect and would escape. Instead:

1. Sulfur trioxide is absorbed in **concentrated (98%) sulfuric acid**, forming oleum: $\ce{H2SO4 + SO3 -> H2S2O7}$.
2. The oleum is then mixed carefully with the right amount of water: $\ce{H2S2O7 + H2O -> 2H2SO4}$.

## Reacting amounts

Every sulfur atom ends up in one molecule of sulfuric acid: $\ce{S -> SO2 -> SO3 -> H2SO4}$, so 32 g of sulfur can make 98 g of sulfuric acid.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: from sulfur to sulfuric acid]
1. 32 tonnes of sulfur can give at most 98 tonnes of sulfuric acid.
2. So 8.0 tonnes of sulfur can give $8.0 \times \dfrac{98}{32} = 24.5$ tonnes of sulfuric acid.
3. **Volumes:** 400 dm³ of sulfur dioxide needs 200 dm³ of oxygen. Air is about 21% oxygen, so about $200 \div 0.21 = 952$ dm³ of air is needed, and in practice more is used (excess air).
:::

## Uses of sulfuric acid (ZIMSEC)

- making **fertilisers**: ammonium sulfate and superphosphate;
- the electrolyte in **lead-acid car batteries**;
- making detergents, paints, pigments and many other chemicals;
- cleaning ("pickling") steel before it is galvanised or painted, and extracting metals from ores;
- as a drying agent (the concentrated acid).

## Environmental impacts (ZIMSEC)

- **Sulfur dioxide or sulfur trioxide escaping** causes acid rain and breathing problems. Plants convert as much as possible, and the waste gas can be "scrubbed" with an alkali such as calcium hydroxide before it is released.
- **Acid spills** damage soil, water and living things, so storage tanks need careful design and inspection.
- The reactions are exothermic, and the heat is used to raise steam and generate electricity, which saves fuel.
- Turning smelter sulfur dioxide into sulfuric acid **reduces** air pollution near mines and smelters.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the Haber process conditions"}
The Contact process uses about **2 atm** and **vanadium(V) oxide**; the Haber process uses about 200 atm and iron. Both use about 450 °C.
:::

:::mistake{title="Dissolving sulfur trioxide in water"}
This forms an acid mist. Sulfur trioxide is absorbed in concentrated sulfuric acid to form oleum, which is then diluted.
:::

:::mistake{title="Making sulfuric acid from sulfur dioxide and water"}
Sulfur dioxide and water give sulfurous acid, $\ce{H2SO3}$, not sulfuric acid. The sulfur must first be oxidised to sulfur trioxide.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When explaining why a low pressure is used, say that the yield is **already high** at low pressure, so high pressure would add cost for little gain.
:::

:::exam-tip

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Sulfur dioxide comes from burning sulfur or roasting sulfide ores; oxygen comes from the air.
- $\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) <=> 2SO3(g)}$ is exothermic and is carried out at about 450 °C, 2 atm (200 kPa) with a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst.
- 450 °C is a compromise between yield and rate; a low pressure is enough because the yield is already high; excess air increases the conversion.
- ZIMSEC: sulfur trioxide is absorbed in concentrated sulfuric acid to form oleum, which is diluted with water.
- ZIMSEC: sulfuric acid is used for fertilisers, batteries, detergents, paints and metal treatment; escaping sulfur oxides cause acid rain.
:::
