## Introduction

A fertiliser company does not simply ask "Which conditions give the most product?" It asks "Which conditions let us make the product **safely**, at the **lowest cost per tonne**, so that farmers can afford it and we still make a profit?" The answer is nearly always a **compromise**.

In this lesson you will use what you know about rates and equilibrium to explain the choices made in industry, read yield data, and do simple cost calculations of the kind a plant manager uses.

You should already know how temperature, pressure and catalysts affect the rate of a reaction and the position of equilibrium, and the conditions used in the Haber and Contact processes.

:::note{title="Syllabus coverage"}
Explaining the conditions in terms of rate, equilibrium, safety and cost is **Cambridge Supplement** (6.3). **ZIMSEC Form 4** also asks you to link conditions to production costs and to compare the cost of making a product with its value.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Yield"}
The amount of product obtained compared with the maximum possible. For a reversible reaction, the percentage of product in the equilibrium mixture is often used.
:::

:::definition{term="Compromise conditions"}
Conditions chosen to balance competing needs (a good yield, a fast rate, low cost and safety) rather than to make any one of them as large as possible.
:::

:::definition{term="Cost of production"}
The total cost of making one tonne of product: raw materials, energy, equipment, catalyst, wages and safety measures, divided by the number of tonnes made.
:::

## Four things to balance

| Factor | What helps | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rate** | high temperature, high pressure, a catalyst | high temperature may lower the yield; high pressure is costly |
| **Yield** | conditions that shift the equilibrium towards the products | they may make the reaction slow or expensive |
| **Cost** | moderate conditions, cheap raw materials, recycling, energy recovery | the cheapest option may be too slow |
| **Safety** | moderate pressures and temperatures | very high pressure risks leaks and explosions |

## Reading yield data

The table shows the approximate percentage of ammonia in the equilibrium mixture for the Haber process, $\ce{N2 + 3H2 <=> 2NH3}$, $\Delta H = -92\ \text{kJ/mol}$.

| Temperature / °C | at 100 atm | at 300 atm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 300 | 52% | 71% |
| 400 | 25% | 47% |
| 500 | 11% | 26% |

- Reading **down** a column: the yield falls as the temperature rises, because the forward reaction is exothermic.
- Reading **across** a row: the yield rises with pressure, because 4 gas molecules form 2.
- The highest yield in the table (71% at 300 °C and 300 atm) is **not** what industry uses. At 300 °C the reaction is far too slow, even with a catalyst, and 300 atm needs very expensive equipment. About 450 °C and 200 atm gives a smaller percentage, but quickly and at a sensible cost.

## A fast, low yield can beat a slow, high yield

:::worked-solution[Example 1: product per hour]
Imagine one reactor that can be run in two ways. Each time it is filled it holds the same mass of reactants.

| | Conversion each time | Time each time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Condition A (low temperature) | 40% | 60 minutes |
| Condition B (higher temperature) | 25% | 12 minutes |

1. In one hour, A can be filled once: $1 \times 40\% = 40\%$ of one fill is converted.
2. In one hour, B can be filled $60 \div 12 = 5$ times: $5 \times 25\% = 125\%$ of one fill is converted.
3. B makes more than **three times** as much product per hour, even though its yield each time is lower. The unreacted material is not wasted: it is recycled.
:::

## Keeping costs down

- **Catalysts** speed up the reaction so a lower, cheaper temperature can be used; a cheap catalyst such as iron is ideal.
- **Recycling** unreacted reactants means that almost none of the raw materials is wasted: in a Haber plant almost all of the nitrogen and hydrogen is eventually converted, even though only about 15% reacts in each pass.
- **Energy recovery:** exothermic reactions release heat that can preheat incoming gases or raise steam to generate electricity.
- **Cheap raw materials:** air is free; methane is cheap where natural gas is available; where electricity is cheap (for example from hydroelectric power), hydrogen can be made by electrolysing water instead.
- **Continuous running:** a plant that runs day and night spreads its fixed costs over more tonnes of product.
- **Location:** building near raw materials, water, a power supply and good road or rail links reduces transport costs.

## Cost of production compared with value (ZIMSEC)

:::worked-solution[Example 2: choosing between two pressures]
A company estimates its daily costs and output for two possible operating pressures.

| | Daily cost / US\$ | Output / tonnes per day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Option P (150 atm) | 60 000 | 400 |
| Option Q (300 atm) | 90 000 | 500 |

1. Cost per tonne for P $= 60\,000 \div 400 =$ US\$150.
2. Cost per tonne for Q $= 90\,000 \div 500 =$ US\$180.
3. Q makes more product, but each tonne costs US\$30 more. If the product sells for US\$200 a tonne, P makes $(200 - 150) \times 400 =$ US\$20 000 profit a day and Q makes $(200 - 180) \times 500 =$ US\$10 000. P is the better choice, and it is also safer.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Choosing the conditions for the highest yield"}
Industry chooses conditions for the most **profitable and safe** production, which usually means a compromise between yield and rate.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying a catalyst increases the yield"}
A catalyst increases the **rate**, so equilibrium is reached sooner, and lets a lower temperature be used. The percentage at equilibrium is unchanged.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring the cost of pressure"}
High pressure increases yield and rate, but needs thick-walled vessels, powerful compressors and lots of energy, and it is more dangerous.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In "justify the conditions" questions, mention **rate, yield (equilibrium), cost and safety** for each condition you discuss.
:::

:::exam-tip
When reading a yield table or graph, describe the trend **and** link it to the equation: "yield increases with pressure because there are fewer gas molecules on the right".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Industrial conditions are compromises between rate, yield, cost and safety.
- For an exothermic reaction, low temperature gives a higher yield but a slower rate.
- High pressure favours the side with fewer gas molecules but costs more and is more dangerous.
- A catalyst lets equilibrium be reached quickly at a lower temperature without changing the yield.
- Recycling, energy recovery, cheap raw materials and continuous running keep the cost per tonne down.
- ZIMSEC: compare cost per tonne with the selling price to judge which conditions are most profitable.
:::
