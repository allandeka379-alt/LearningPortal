## Introduction

Ethanol, $\ce{C2H5OH}$, is the best known of the alcohols. Nurses wipe skin with it before an injection, it is in the hand gels used at school entrances, it carries the scent in perfumes, it is blended into petrol at Zimbabwean filling stations, and it is the alcohol in beer, wine and spirits. Few chemicals touch daily life in so many ways, for good and for harm.

In this lesson you will learn how ethanol burns and why it is used as a solvent and a fuel (Cambridge Core and ZIMSEC). ZIMSEC Form 4 also covers its physical properties, its oxidation to ethanoic acid, its dehydration to ethene, a chemical test for it and the social effects of drinking alcohol. You should already know how ethanol is made and what a homologous series is.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Ethanol"}
The alcohol $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$ (also written $\ce{C2H5OH}$): the second member of the alcohol homologous series, with the $\ce{-OH}$ functional group.
:::

:::definition{term="Oxidation (of ethanol)"}
A reaction in which ethanol gains oxygen (and loses hydrogen) to form ethanoic acid, $\ce{CH3COOH}$.
:::

:::definition{term="Dehydration (ZIMSEC)"}
The removal of water from a molecule; ethanol is dehydrated to ethene.
:::

## Physical properties (ZIMSEC)

- a colourless liquid with a characteristic smell;
- boiling point **78 °C**, much higher than that of propane (−42 °C), whose molecules are about the same mass: the $\ce{-OH}$ group makes the forces between ethanol molecules much stronger;
- **mixes completely with water** in all proportions, again because of the $\ce{-OH}$ group, and dissolves many substances that hardly dissolve in water, such as iodine and the scented oils used in perfumes;
- freezes only at −114 °C, so it stays liquid in very cold conditions.

## Combustion (Cambridge Core; ZIMSEC)

Ethanol burns with a clean, pale blue flame:

$$
\ce{C2H5OH + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O}
$$

It releases a lot of heat and little soot, which is why it is used as a **fuel**: in spirit burners, in camping stoves and blended with petrol.

:::worked-solution[Example 1 (Supplement): mass of carbon dioxide from burning ethanol]
Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide formed when 9.2 g of ethanol burns completely. ($A_r$: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16)

1. $M_r(\ce{C2H5OH}) = 24 + 6 + 16 = 46$, so $n = 9.2 \div 46 = 0.20$ mol.
2. 1 mol of ethanol gives 2 mol of $\ce{CO2}$: $n(\ce{CO2}) = 0.40$ mol.
3. Mass $= 0.40 \times 44 = 17.6$ g.
:::

## Oxidation to ethanoic acid (ZIMSEC; Cambridge Supplement in the carboxylic acids topic)

Ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid when it is warmed with an oxidising agent:

$$
\ce{C2H5OH + 2[O] -> CH3COOH + H2O}
$$

Here $\ce{[O]}$ stands for oxygen supplied by the oxidising agent.

- **Acidified potassium dichromate(VI)** turns from **orange to green**. This colour change is a **test for ethanol** (and other similar alcohols); early breathalysers used it.
- **Acidified potassium manganate(VII)** turns from **purple to colourless**.
- **Bacteria** in air slowly oxidise the ethanol in wine or beer left open, which is how **vinegar** is made and why opened wine goes sour.

## Dehydration to ethene (ZIMSEC)

When ethanol vapour is passed over heated **aluminium oxide** (or ethanol is heated with excess concentrated sulfuric acid), a molecule of water is removed:

$$
\ce{C2H5OH -> C2H4 + H2O}
$$

:::worked-solution[Example 2: making ethene from ethanol in the laboratory]
1. Soak ceramic wool in ethanol at the closed end of a boiling tube, with aluminium oxide powder in the middle.
2. Heat the aluminium oxide strongly; warm the wool gently so that ethanol vapour passes over the hot catalyst.
3. Collect the gas over water and throw away the first tube (air).
4. Shake the gas with bromine water: orange → colourless, so the gas is an alkene, **ethene**.
5. Remove the delivery tube from the water before you stop heating, to prevent suck-back.
:::

## Uses of ethanol

| Use | Property it depends on |
| --- | --- |
| fuel, alone or blended with petrol (Cambridge Core) | burns readily, releasing a lot of heat with little soot |
| solvent for perfumes, inks, varnishes and medicines (Cambridge Core) | dissolves many substances and evaporates quickly |
| antiseptic wipes and hand sanitisers (ZIMSEC) | kills many bacteria and viruses on the skin |
| alcoholic drinks (ZIMSEC) | made by fermentation; a drug that slows the nervous system |
| liquid in some thermometers (ZIMSEC) | stays liquid down to −114 °C |

Fuel blends are named by their ethanol content: E10 is petrol containing 10% ethanol. Ethanol made from sugar cane is renewable, and blending reduces the amount of petrol that must be imported.

## Social effects of alcohol (ZIMSEC)

Drinking alcohol in moderation is legal for adults, but misuse causes serious harm:

- **health:** addiction, liver damage, damage to the brain and heart, harm to an unborn baby;
- **safety:** drunk driving causes road accidents; drunkenness leads to injuries and violence;
- **family and money:** domestic violence, money spent on alcohol instead of food and school fees, lost jobs and absenteeism;
- **risky behaviour:** alcohol lowers judgement, increasing the risk of unprotected sex and HIV infection.

:::note{title="Methanol poisoning"}
Methylated spirits and some illegally brewed spirits contain **methanol**, which is poisonous: even small amounts can cause blindness or death. Methylated spirits must never be drunk.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling ethanol an alkali because it has –OH"}
The –OH group in ethanol is covalently bonded; ethanol does not release hydroxide ions, and its solution is neutral.
:::

:::mistake{title="Getting the dichromate colours the wrong way round"}
Acidified potassium dichromate(VI) starts **orange** and turns **green** when it oxidises ethanol.
:::

:::mistake{title="Unbalanced combustion equations"}
Ethanol already contains one oxygen atom. $\ce{C2H5OH + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O}$ has 7 oxygen atoms on each side; check by counting.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Ethanol burns cleanly: $\ce{C2H5OH + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O}$; it is used as a fuel and a solvent.
- ZIMSEC: colourless liquid, boils at 78 °C, mixes completely with water.
- ZIMSEC: oxidised to ethanoic acid by acidified $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$ (orange → green, the test for ethanol) or by bacteria (vinegar).
- ZIMSEC: dehydrated to ethene over hot aluminium oxide: $\ce{C2H5OH -> C2H4 + H2O}$.
- ZIMSEC: uses in fuel blends, perfumes, sanitisers and drinks; misuse harms health, safety, families and finances.
:::
