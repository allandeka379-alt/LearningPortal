## Introduction

Vinegar gives chips and pickled vegetables their sharp taste. Its sourness comes from **ethanoic acid**, $\ce{CH3COOH}$, the best-known carboxylic acid. Mix a carboxylic acid with an alcohol and you can make an **ester**: esters give bananas, pineapples and many sweets their smell and are used in perfumes and flavourings.

In this lesson you will learn how ethanoic acid reacts as an acid (Cambridge Core and ZIMSEC), how it is made from ethanol, and how it reacts with alcohols to form esters (Cambridge Supplement and ZIMSEC). You should already know the general reactions of acids with metals, bases and carbonates, and the alcohol homologous series.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Carboxylic acid"}
An organic acid containing the carboxyl group, $\ce{-COOH}$; general formula $\ce{C_nH_{2n+1}COOH}$.
:::

:::definition{term="Weak acid (Supplement)"}
An acid that is only partly ionised (dissociated) in water, so it gives fewer hydrogen ions than a strong acid of the same concentration.
:::

:::definition{term="Ethanoate"}
A salt of ethanoic acid, containing the ethanoate ion $\ce{CH3COO-}$, for example sodium ethanoate, $\ce{CH3COONa}$.
:::

:::definition{term="Esterification (Supplement; ZIMSEC)"}
The reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol, using an acid catalyst, to form an ester and water.
:::

## Reactions of ethanoic acid

Ethanoic acid shows all the typical reactions of an acid:

| Reacts with | Products | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a reactive metal | salt + hydrogen | $\ce{2CH3COOH + Mg -> (CH3COO)2Mg + H2}$ |
| a base (metal oxide or hydroxide) | salt + water | $\ce{CH3COOH + NaOH -> CH3COONa + H2O}$ |
| a carbonate | salt + water + carbon dioxide | $\ce{2CH3COOH + Na2CO3 -> 2CH3COONa + H2O + CO2}$ |

The salts are named **ethanoates**: magnesium ethanoate, $\ce{(CH3COO)2Mg}$; sodium ethanoate, $\ce{CH3COONa}$. The ethanoate ion has a charge of 1−, so a 2+ metal needs two ethanoate ions, just as it needs two chloride ions.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: vinegar and limescale]
Vinegar removes limescale (calcium carbonate) from a kettle. Write the equation and name the salt formed.

1. Acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide.
2. Calcium forms $\ce{Ca^2+}$, so the salt needs two ethanoate ions: $\ce{(CH3COO)2Ca}$, **calcium ethanoate**.
3. $\ce{2CH3COOH + CaCO3 -> (CH3COO)2Ca + H2O + CO2}$.
4. Check: C 5 = 5 (4 + 1), H 8 = 8, O 7 = 7, Ca 1 = 1. The calcium ethanoate is soluble, so the scale dissolves and fizzes.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2 (Supplement): carbon dioxide from ethanoic acid]
Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide (at r.t.p.) given off when 6.0 g of ethanoic acid reacts with excess sodium carbonate. ($A_r$: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16; 1 mol of gas = 24 dm³ at r.t.p.)

1. $M_r(\ce{CH3COOH}) = 24 + 4 + 32 = 60$, so $n = 6.0 \div 60 = 0.10$ mol.
2. $\ce{2CH3COOH + Na2CO3 -> 2CH3COONa + H2O + CO2}$: 2 mol of acid give 1 mol of $\ce{CO2}$, so $n(\ce{CO2}) = 0.050$ mol.
3. Volume $= 0.050 \times 24 = 1.2\ \text{dm}^3$.
:::

### A weak acid (Supplement)

Ethanoic acid is a **weak acid**: in water only a small fraction of its molecules release hydrogen ions:

$$
\ce{CH3COOH(aq) <=> CH3COO^-(aq) + H+(aq)}
$$

So a solution of ethanoic acid has a higher pH than hydrochloric acid of the same concentration (at 0.1 mol/dm³, about pH 3 compared with pH 1), and it reacts **more slowly** with metals and carbonates, although it gives the same products.

## Making ethanoic acid (Supplement; ZIMSEC)

Ethanoic acid is made by **oxidising ethanol**:

- in the laboratory, by warming ethanol with acidified **potassium manganate(VII)** (purple → colourless) or acidified **potassium dichromate(VI)** (orange → green);
- by **bacteria**: when wine, beer or a dilute ethanol solution is left open to the air, bacteria oxidise the ethanol to ethanoic acid. This is how vinegar is made.

$$
\ce{C2H5OH + 2[O] -> CH3COOH + H2O}
$$

## Esters (Supplement; ZIMSEC)

A carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol when they are warmed with a few drops of **concentrated sulfuric acid**, which acts as a **catalyst**. Water is formed as well as the ester, and the reaction is reversible:

$$
\ce{CH3COOH + C2H5OH <=> CH3COOC2H5 + H2O}
$$

ethanoic acid + ethanol ⇌ **ethyl ethanoate** + water

Name an ester from its two parts: the **alcohol** part first (-yl), then the **acid** part (-oate). In the formula, the acid part is written first.

:::worked-solution[Example 3: an ester from methanol]
Name the ester formed from ethanoic acid and methanol, and write its structural formula.

1. Alcohol part: methanol → **methyl**.
2. Acid part: ethanoic acid → **ethanoate**.
3. Name: **methyl ethanoate**.
4. Formula: the $\ce{CH3COO\bond{-}}$ group from the acid joined to the $\ce{-CH3}$ group from the alcohol: $\ce{CH3COOCH3}$. The OH of the acid and the H of the alcohol's –OH leave as water.
:::

In the laboratory, about 1 cm³ each of the acid and the alcohol are warmed with a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid in a water bath (both are flammable, so no naked flame near them). The mixture is poured into sodium carbonate solution, which neutralises the leftover acids, so the sweet, fruity smell of the ester can be noticed. Smell it by **wafting**, never by sniffing directly.

## Uses (ZIMSEC)

| Substance | Uses |
| --- | --- |
| ethanoic acid (as vinegar) | flavouring food; **preserving** food by pickling (the low pH stops most bacteria and moulds growing); making esters |
| esters | **perfumes** and cosmetics (pleasant smells); food **flavourings** (fruity tastes in sweets and drinks); solvents, for example in glues and some nail-varnish removers |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Writing the salt formula with the wrong number of ethanoate ions"}
Ethanoate is $\ce{CH3COO-}$ (1−). Magnesium ethanoate is $\ce{(CH3COO)2Mg}$, not $\ce{CH3COOMg}$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking a weak acid is a dilute acid (Supplement)"}
"Weak" means only partly ionised; "dilute" means little acid in a lot of water. Concentrated ethanoic acid is still a weak acid.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the water in esterification"}
Esterification gives two products: the ester **and water**. The catalyst (concentrated sulfuric acid) is written above the arrow, not as a reactant.
:::

:::mistake{title="Naming esters back to front"}
The ester from ethanol and methanoic acid is ethyl methanoate, $\ce{HCOOC2H5}$: alcohol part first in the name, acid part first in the formula.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Ethanoic acid, $\ce{CH3COOH}$, forms ethanoates with metals (+ $\ce{H2}$), bases (+ $\ce{H2O}$) and carbonates (+ $\ce{H2O}$ + $\ce{CO2}$); Supplement: it is a weak acid, only partly ionised.
- Supplement; ZIMSEC: ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid by acidified $\ce{KMnO4}$ or $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$, or by bacteria (vinegar).
- Supplement; ZIMSEC: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water, catalysed by concentrated sulfuric acid.
- Ester names: alcohol part (-yl) + acid part (-oate), e.g. ethyl ethanoate $\ce{CH3COOC2H5}$.
- ZIMSEC: vinegar flavours and preserves food; esters are used in perfumes, flavourings and solvents.
:::
