## Introduction

In markets across Zimbabwe you can buy bundles of dried **zumbani** leaves for tea, and in many rural homes a pot of **aloe** grows by the door. Farmers soak neem leaves or crushed chillies to make sprays that keep insects off their vegetables. This knowledge of useful plants, built up and passed down over generations, is part of our **indigenous knowledge**.

Chemistry explains why some of these plants have an effect: they contain **chemicals** that act on the body or on pests. Many modern medicines were first discovered in plants. But a plant remedy is not automatically safe or effective: the amount of active chemical varies, some plants are poisonous, and many traditional uses have never been properly tested. In this lesson (ZIMSEC Form 4) you will describe how herbs are used in health and agriculture and weigh up their advantages and disadvantages.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Herb"}
A plant whose leaves, roots, bark, flowers or seeds are used for their medicinal, flavouring or pest-repelling properties.
:::

:::definition{term="Active ingredient"}
The chemical (or chemicals) in a plant or medicine that produces its effect.
:::

:::definition{term="Botanical pesticide"}
A pesticide made from plants, such as a spray made from neem leaves or chillies.
:::

:::definition{term="Indigenous knowledge"}
Knowledge developed by a community over many generations through experience and passed down, for example about which plants are useful.
:::

## Herbs in health

The table shows some plants that are **traditionally used** in Zimbabwe. "Traditionally used for" describes how people use them; it does not mean the use has been proven to work.

| Plant | Part used | Traditional use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| zumbani (Lippia javanica; umsuzwane in Ndebele), also called fever tea | leaves, as a tea | traditionally used for coughs, colds and fevers; the crushed leaves are also used to keep insects away |
| aloe (gavakava in Shona, inhlaba in Ndebele) | sap from the leaves | traditionally applied to minor burns, cuts and skin irritation; added to the drinking water of village chickens |
| moringa (Moringa oleifera) | leaves, fresh or dried | eaten as a nutritious vegetable rich in vitamins and minerals |
| lemon grass | leaves, as a tea | herbal tea and flavouring; related grasses give citronella oil, used in insect repellents |

The effects come from chemicals in the plants. Zumbani and lemon grass contain **essential oils**, mixtures of volatile, strong-smelling compounds. Hot water dissolves some of the plant's chemicals when a tea is made; essential oils can be separated by **steam distillation**.

:::keyfact
Several important medicines were developed from plant chemicals: **aspirin** from a substance in willow bark, **quinine** (for malaria) from cinchona bark and **artemisinin** (for malaria) from sweet wormwood. Scientists isolated the active ingredient, tested it and worked out a safe, effective dose.
:::

## Herbs in agriculture

| Plant | How it is used | Active chemicals and effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| neem | leaves or crushed seeds soaked in water to make a spray | **azadirachtin** repels insects and stops them feeding and growing |
| tephrosia (Tephrosia vogelii) | leaves crushed and soaked to make a pesticide; also grown to improve soil | **rotenoids** poison insects; they are very poisonous to fish; as a legume the plant adds nitrogen to the soil |
| chilli (hot pepper) | ground fruits soaked in soapy water as a spray | **capsaicin** irritates and repels insects |
| garlic | crushed cloves soaked in water as a spray | strong-smelling sulfur compounds repel pests |

:::worked-solution[Example 1: why neem sprays must be repeated]
A farmer sprays neem extract on her tomatoes. A week later, after heavy rain, aphids return. Explain why, and what she should do.

1. The active chemicals in neem are natural compounds that are **broken down** by sunlight and microorganisms within days.
2. Rain also **washes** the spray off the leaves.
3. So the protection does not last: she should spray again after rain and repeat every few days while pests are present.
4. The same quick breakdown is an advantage: little residue is left on the food or in the soil.
:::

:::example{title="Example 2: making a chilli and garlic spray safely"}
Crush a handful of hot chillies and some garlic cloves, soak them overnight in water, strain the liquid and add a little soap so that it spreads over the leaves. Wear gloves, keep your hands away from your eyes, test the spray on a few plants first, and label the container so that nobody drinks it. Spray in the cool of the evening to protect bees.
:::

## Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| cheap or free and **available locally**, even far from shops and clinics | the **dose is uncertain**: the amount of active chemical varies with the plant part, season, place and preparation |
| part of **indigenous knowledge** and culture | **side effects and poisoning**: some plants are toxic, and some are harmful to children, pregnant women or animals |
| botanical pesticides **break down quickly**, leaving little residue on food or in soil | most traditional uses have **not been scientifically tested** for safety or effectiveness |
| fewer synthetic chemicals released into the environment | relying on herbs may **delay proper treatment** of serious diseases such as malaria or tuberculosis |
| plants are a source of new medicines when their chemicals are studied | herbs can **interact** with prescribed medicines; wrong plants may be picked; over-harvesting makes wild plants rare |
| | botanical pesticides must be reapplied often and can also harm useful insects and fish |

:::note{title="Using herbs safely"}
Herbal remedies should never replace a visit to a clinic for a serious illness. Tell the health worker about any herbs you are taking, and do not give unknown remedies to babies and young children.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="\"Natural means safe\""}
Many of the most poisonous substances known come from plants. Whether a remedy is safe depends on the chemicals in it and the dose, not on whether it is natural.
:::

:::mistake{title="Claiming a herb cures a disease"}
In exams, write that a plant is "traditionally used for" a condition. Do not claim that a herb cures a disease unless it has been proven by scientific testing.
:::

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Herbs are plants used for their medicinal, flavouring or pest-repelling properties; their effects come from chemicals they contain (active ingredients).
- Health: zumbani tea is traditionally used for colds; aloe sap for minor burns; moringa leaves as a nutritious food.
- Agriculture: neem (azadirachtin), tephrosia (rotenoids), chilli (capsaicin) and garlic sprays repel or kill pests.
- Advantages: cheap, local, part of indigenous knowledge, botanical pesticides break down quickly.
- Disadvantages: uncertain dose, side effects and toxicity, lack of testing, delayed medical treatment, frequent reapplication of sprays.
:::
