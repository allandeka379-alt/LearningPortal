## Introduction

Zimbabwe enjoys strong sunshine for most of the year, and the Sun's **ultraviolet (UV) radiation** can burn skin and damage eyes, especially around midday. Much more UV would reach us without a thin, invisible shield high in the atmosphere: the **ozone layer**.

In the 1970s and 1980s scientists discovered that the ozone layer was being damaged by chemicals that people had thought were completely safe. This lesson explains how, and how the world worked together to repair the damage. You should already know about covalent molecules and the idea of a catalyst.

:::note{title="Syllabus coverage"}
The depletion of the ozone layer is in the **ZIMSEC** O-Level syllabus as an effect of waste and pollution on the environment.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Ozone"}
A form of oxygen made of molecules containing three oxygen atoms, $\ce{O3}$.
:::

:::definition{term="Ozone layer"}
A region of the stratosphere, mostly between about 15 and 35 km above the ground, where ozone is most concentrated.
:::

:::definition{term="CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons)"}
Compounds of carbon, chlorine and fluorine, such as $\ce{CCl2F2}$, once widely used in refrigerators, air conditioners, aerosol sprays and plastic foams.
:::

## How the ozone layer protects life

High in the stratosphere, UV radiation splits some oxygen molecules into atoms, and these atoms join with other oxygen molecules to form ozone:

$$
\ce{O2 ->[UV] 2O} \qquad \ce{O + O2 -> O3}
$$

Ozone itself **absorbs** most of the Sun's harmful UV radiation, so only a small amount reaches the ground. Normally ozone is made and broken down at about the same rate, so the layer stays roughly the same.

## How CFCs destroy ozone

CFCs are very **unreactive**. That made them seem safe, but it also means they last for decades in the air, long enough to drift up into the stratosphere. There, strong UV radiation breaks their carbon–chlorine bonds and releases **chlorine atoms**, which attack ozone:

$$
\ce{Cl + O3 -> ClO + O2}
$$

$$
\ce{ClO + O -> Cl + O2}
$$

:::example{title="Example 1: one chlorine atom, thousands of ozone molecules"}
Add the two equations together: the $\ce{ClO}$ cancels and the chlorine atom appears on both sides, so the overall change is $\ce{O3 + O -> 2O2}$. The chlorine atom is **not used up**: it comes out of the second reaction ready to attack another ozone molecule. It behaves like a catalyst, so a single chlorine atom can destroy many thousands of ozone molecules before it is finally removed.
:::

Other **ozone-depleting substances** include **halons** (once used in some fire extinguishers) and **methyl bromide** (once widely used to fumigate soil, including tobacco seedbeds), because they release bromine atoms that act in the same way.

The damage is most severe over Antarctica, where an "ozone hole" forms each spring. It was first reported in 1985.

## Effects of a thinner ozone layer

More UV radiation reaches the Earth's surface, which leads to:

- more **sunburn** and **skin cancer**;
- **cataracts** and other eye damage;
- a weaker immune system;
- damage to **crops** and to **plankton** in the oceans, the start of many food chains.

People with fair skin, and especially people with **albinism**, whose skin has little of the pigment melanin that normally absorbs UV, need particular protection: hats, long sleeves, sunglasses and sunscreen.

## Protecting the ozone layer

In **1987**, countries agreed the **Montreal Protocol**, an international treaty to phase out CFCs and other ozone-depleting substances. Today:

- refrigerators and air conditioners use other coolants, such as hydrocarbons (for example isobutane) or hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs), which contain no chlorine;
- aerosol sprays use other propellants;
- old refrigerators should be taken for proper disposal, so that any CFCs still inside are recovered rather than released;
- methyl bromide has been phased out for most uses.

HFCs do not harm the ozone layer, but they are powerful greenhouse gases, so their use is now also being reduced. The ozone layer is slowly recovering and is expected to heal over the coming decades.

:::note{title="Good up high, bad nearby"}
Ozone in the stratosphere protects us. Ozone at **ground level**, formed in photochemical smog from vehicle pollution, is a harmful pollutant that irritates the lungs and eyes.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Linking the ozone hole to global warming"}
Ozone depletion lets in more **ultraviolet** radiation. Global warming is caused by greenhouse gases trapping **thermal** energy. They are separate problems.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying carbon dioxide destroys ozone"}
The main ozone-destroying chemicals are CFCs and other compounds containing chlorine or bromine.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Explain ozone depletion as a chain: CFCs are **unreactive** → reach the **stratosphere** → **UV** releases **chlorine atoms** → chlorine atoms **break down ozone** and are **regenerated**.
:::

:::exam-tip
For effects, give health effects (skin cancer, cataracts) **and** environmental effects (crops, plankton).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Ozone ($\ce{O3}$) in the stratosphere, about 15–35 km up, absorbs most of the Sun's harmful UV radiation.
- CFCs are unreactive, so they reach the stratosphere, where UV releases chlorine atoms that destroy ozone.
- Each chlorine atom is regenerated, so it can destroy many thousands of ozone molecules.
- More UV causes skin cancer, cataracts and damage to crops and plankton.
- The Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs; the ozone layer is slowly recovering.
:::
