## Introduction

When the rains fail, Zimbabwe feels it everywhere: maize withers in the fields, cattle grow thin, and when the water level in Lake Kariba falls, less electricity can be generated at the hydroelectric power station. Scientists expect a warming climate to make droughts in southern Africa more frequent and more severe, and to make extreme storms and heatwaves more common.

The main cause of this warming is the extra **carbon dioxide** and **methane** that human activities release into the atmosphere. In this lesson you will learn how these gases warm the Earth, how photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide, and what can be done to slow climate change.

You should already know the sources of carbon dioxide and methane, and how fuels burn.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Greenhouse gas"}
A gas in the atmosphere that absorbs thermal (infrared) energy emitted by the Earth's surface, for example carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour.
:::

:::definition{term="Global warming"}
The rise in the average temperature of the Earth's surface caused by increasing amounts of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere.
:::

:::definition{term="Climate change"}
Long-term changes in temperature, rainfall and weather patterns, such as more droughts, floods and heatwaves, caused by global warming.
:::

:::definition{term="Renewable energy"}
Energy from sources that will not run out, such as sunlight, wind and flowing water.
:::

## How greenhouse gases warm the Earth (Supplement)

1. Energy from the Sun, mostly visible light, passes through the atmosphere. Some is **reflected** back to space by clouds and ice; the rest is absorbed and warms the Earth's surface.
2. The warm surface **emits** thermal energy (infrared radiation) back towards space.
3. Greenhouse gases **absorb** some of this thermal energy and then **emit** it in all directions, including back towards the Earth's surface.
4. So **less thermal energy is lost to space**, and the surface and lower atmosphere stay warmer.

Some greenhouse effect is natural and essential: without it, the Earth's average temperature would be well below freezing. The problem is the **extra** greenhouse gas from human activities, which increases global warming.

## Where the extra gases come from

| Gas | Human sources |
| --- | --- |
| carbon dioxide | burning fossil fuels (coal in power stations, petrol and diesel in vehicles); cutting down and burning forests; making cement and lime from limestone |
| methane | cattle and other farm animals (digestion); rubbish dumps and rotting vegetation; leaks from gas and coal mining |

Methane is present in far smaller amounts than carbon dioxide, but each molecule traps much more thermal energy.

## Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide

Green plants take in carbon dioxide and water and, using energy from light absorbed by **chlorophyll**, make glucose and oxygen:

$$
\text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \rightarrow \text{glucose} + \text{oxygen}
$$

$$
\ce{6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2} \quad \text{(Supplement)}
$$

Respiration, combustion and decay put carbon dioxide into the air; photosynthesis takes it out. Cutting down forests (for farmland, or for firewood to cure tobacco) both removes trees that would absorb carbon dioxide and, if the wood is burnt, releases more of it.

## Calculations

:::worked-solution[Example 1: carbon dioxide from fuels]
($A_r$: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16)

1. **Charcoal (carbon):** $\ce{C + O2 -> CO2}$. 12 kg of carbon gives 44 kg of carbon dioxide, so 1.0 kg gives $1.0 \times \dfrac{44}{12} = 3.67$ kg.
2. **Methane:** $\ce{CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O}$. 16 kg of methane gives 44 kg of carbon dioxide, so 1.0 kg gives $1.0 \times \dfrac{44}{16} = 2.75$ kg.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: carbon dioxide used in photosynthesis]
How much carbon dioxide does a plant absorb to make 180 g of glucose? ($M_r$: $\ce{C6H12O6}$ = 180, $\ce{CO2}$ = 44)

1. 180 g of glucose is 1 mol.
2. From the equation, 1 mol of glucose needs 6 mol of $\ce{CO2}$.
3. Mass of $\ce{CO2} = 6 \times 44 = 264$ g.
:::

## Effects of climate change

- higher average temperatures and more heatwaves;
- changing rainfall: more frequent droughts in some regions and heavier storms and floods in others;
- melting ice and warming oceans, which raise sea levels and flood coastal land;
- crop failures and water shortages, which threaten food supplies and hydroelectric power;
- loss of habitats, and some pests and diseases spreading to new areas.

## Reducing climate change

- **Plant trees** and protect forests, so that more carbon dioxide is absorbed by photosynthesis.
- **Reduce livestock farming**, which releases methane.
- **Use less fossil fuel:** save energy at home and in industry, and use public transport.
- **Use more hydrogen and renewable energy:** solar panels (Zimbabwe has plentiful sunshine), wind and hydroelectric power generate electricity without burning fuels.
- **Capture methane** from rubbish dumps and from animal dung in biogas digesters and use it as a fuel, instead of letting it escape.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing global warming with the hole in the ozone layer"}
Global warming is caused by greenhouse gases trapping thermal energy. The ozone hole lets more ultraviolet radiation reach the ground. They are different problems with different main causes.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying greenhouse gases trap the Sun's rays"}
Greenhouse gases let most sunlight through. They absorb the **thermal (infrared) energy emitted by the Earth's surface**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide"}
Carbon dioxide is the main greenhouse gas from burning fuels. Carbon monoxide is a toxic gas from incomplete combustion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Carbon dioxide (from burning fuels and deforestation) and methane (from livestock and decay) are greenhouse gases.
- Supplement: greenhouse gases absorb thermal energy from the Earth's surface and emit it back, reducing heat loss to space.
- More greenhouse gases cause increased global warming, which leads to climate change: droughts, floods, heatwaves and rising seas.
- Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (Supplement: $\ce{6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2}$).
- Strategies: plant trees, reduce livestock farming, burn less fossil fuel, use hydrogen and renewable energy such as solar and wind.
:::
