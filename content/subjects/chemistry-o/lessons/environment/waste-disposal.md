## Introduction

A town of a few hundred thousand people produces many tonnes of rubbish every day, and millions of litres of sewage. All of it has to go somewhere. Left in open heaps it breeds flies and rats, catches fire, and washes into rivers; untreated sewage spreads diseases such as cholera and typhoid. The aim of waste management is to deal with waste in ways that protect health and the environment and, where possible, to turn it back into useful materials or energy.

In this lesson (ZIMSEC Form 4) you will learn the main disposal methods (incineration, landfill and the activated sludge treatment of sewage), how composting and biogas make use of waste, and how reducing, reusing and recycling cut the amount of waste. You will also learn to weigh up the advantages and disadvantages of each method, which exam questions often ask you to do.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Incineration"}
Burning waste at a high temperature in a specially designed furnace (incinerator).
:::

:::definition{term="Landfill"}
Burying waste in a large pit, where it is compacted and covered with soil.
:::

:::definition{term="Leachate"}
The polluted liquid formed when rainwater seeps through waste in a landfill or dump.
:::

:::definition{term="Activated sludge process"}
A method of treating sewage in which air is bubbled through it so that aerobic bacteria (the activated sludge) break down the organic matter.
:::

:::definition{term="Recycling"}
Processing used materials so that they can be made into new products. **Reuse** means using an item again without processing it.
:::

## Incineration

Waste is burned at a high temperature (above about 850 °C). The ash and metal that remain are a small fraction of the original volume.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| greatly reduces the volume of waste, saving landfill space | releases carbon dioxide and, unless gases are cleaned, carbon monoxide, soot and toxic gases such as hydrogen chloride (from PVC) |
| destroys harmful microorganisms, so it is used for **medical waste** | the ash may contain poisonous metals and must be buried safely |
| the heat can be used to raise steam and generate electricity | expensive to build and run; destroys materials that could have been recycled |

## Landfill

In a properly engineered (sanitary) landfill, the pit is lined with clay or plastic sheeting; waste is spread in layers, compacted and covered with soil every day; pipes collect the leachate for treatment and the gas for burning.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| cheap and simple; can take large amounts of mixed waste | uses large areas of land |
| methane from rotting waste can be collected and used as fuel | **leachate** can pollute groundwater and rivers if the lining fails |
| when full, the land can be grassed over and reused | **methane** builds up: it is explosive and a greenhouse gas; smells, flies and rats if badly managed |
| | non-biodegradable waste stays there for centuries |

An open **dump**, where waste is simply piled up and left uncovered, has all of these problems and more: fires, smoke, scavenging animals and the spread of disease.

## The activated sludge treatment of sewage

1. **Screening:** sewage passes through screens that remove rags, plastics and grit.
2. **Primary settling:** in large tanks, solids sink to form sludge.
3. **Aeration:** the liquid flows into tanks where **air is bubbled through** it. Aerobic bacteria and other microorganisms, the **activated sludge**, use the oxygen to break down dissolved organic matter into carbon dioxide, water and more microorganisms.
4. **Secondary settling:** the microorganisms settle out. Some of this sludge is **returned** to the aeration tank so that a large population of bacteria is always ready to treat new sewage.
5. The clear effluent may be **chlorinated** to kill remaining microbes, then released into a river.
6. The surplus sludge is digested without air, giving **biogas**, and the digested solid can be dried and used as a fertiliser.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| fast and efficient; produces clean water that can safely return to rivers | air pumps use a lot of electricity |
| works in a fairly small area | expensive to build and needs skilled operators |
| gives biogas and fertiliser from the sludge | if it is overloaded or the power fails, untreated sewage may be released |

## Composting and biogas

**Composting:** food and garden waste is piled in a heap or pit with some soil, kept moist and turned regularly so that air reaches it. Aerobic bacteria and fungi break it down into **compost**, which improves the soil. It is cheap and keeps biodegradable waste out of landfill, but it is slow and only suitable for biodegradable waste.

**Biogas digesters** break down dung and food waste without air, giving fuel gas and a fertiliser slurry (see the lesson on fuels from biomass).

## Reduce, reuse, recycle

- **Reduce:** buy goods with less packaging, carry a cloth shopping bag, repair rather than replace.
- **Reuse:** refill bottles, reuse containers and bags.
- **Recycle:**
  - **paper:** pulped with water, cleaned of ink and made into new paper and cardboard, saving trees;
  - **glass:** sorted by colour, crushed and melted (with sand) into new bottles, which saves raw materials and energy;
  - **metals:** aluminium cans are melted and recast, using far less energy than extracting aluminium from bauxite; steel cans are removed from mixed rubbish with magnets;
  - **plastics:** sorted by type, shredded, melted and remoulded; PET bottles can be made into fibres or broken down into monomers and re-polymerised.

Recycling conserves raw materials, saves energy, reduces landfill and pollution, and creates jobs for collectors and processors. But collecting, sorting and transporting materials costs money and energy, mixed or dirty materials are hard to recycle, and paper fibres get shorter each time they are recycled.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: choosing a method for each waste]
Suggest the best way to deal with (a) used syringes from a clinic, (b) vegetable peelings, (c) aluminium cans and (d) household sewage.

1. (a) **High-temperature incineration**: it destroys harmful microorganisms and the syringes cannot be reused safely.
2. (b) **Composting** (or a biogas digester): the peelings are biodegradable and become a fertiliser.
3. (c) **Recycling**: aluminium is easily melted and reused, saving energy and bauxite.
4. (d) **Sewage treatment** by the activated sludge process.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: how much is left after incineration?]
An incinerator burns 80 tonnes of waste a day and leaves 18 tonnes of ash. By what percentage is the mass reduced?

1. Mass removed $= 80 - 18 = 62$ tonnes.
2. Percentage reduction $= \dfrac{62}{80} \times 100 = 77.5\%$.
3. The volume is reduced even more than the mass, because the ash is compact.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying recycling has no disadvantages"}
Recycling needs collection, sorting, transport and energy for processing. In "evaluate" questions, give both sides.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing reuse and recycling"}
Refilling a bottle is **reuse**; melting it down to make a new bottle is **recycling**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking the bacteria in activated sludge need no air"}
The activated sludge process is **aerobic**: air is bubbled through to supply oxygen. It is the sludge digester afterwards that works without air.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Describe the advantages and disadvantages" of a method: give at least two of each, linked to health, environment and cost. A table or paired points is clearest.
:::

:::exam-tip
"Evaluate" or "suggest the best method" questions are marked against reasons: match the method to the type of waste (biodegradable, hazardous, recyclable) and justify your choice.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Incineration reduces volume and destroys microbes (medical waste) but pollutes the air and is costly.
- Landfill is cheap but uses land, produces leachate and methane, and keeps non-biodegradable waste for centuries.
- Activated sludge: screening, settling, aeration with aerobic bacteria, settling (some sludge returned), clean effluent; sludge gives biogas and fertiliser.
- Composting and biogas turn biodegradable waste into fertiliser and fuel.
- Reduce, reuse and recycle (paper, glass, metals, plastics) to save raw materials, energy and landfill space.
:::
