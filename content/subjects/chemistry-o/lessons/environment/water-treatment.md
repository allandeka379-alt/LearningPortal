## Introduction

Water taken straight from a dam or river may look clean, but it can carry mud, algae, dissolved chemicals and the microbes that cause cholera and typhoid. Before it reaches our taps, a town's water is treated at a water treatment works; for example, much of Harare's water is drawn from Lake Chivero and treated at the Morton Jaffray works. When treatment fails or water is collected from untreated sources, waterborne diseases can spread quickly, so understanding treatment is a matter of public health.

<!-- Reviewer: Morton Jaffray Water Treatment Works (Harare, supplied from Lake Chivero) is named as a neutral context only, with no claims about its current performance. -->

In this lesson you will follow water through each stage of treatment and learn what each stage removes. Cambridge IGCSE requires sedimentation, filtration, carbon and chlorination; ZIMSEC Form 4 adds flocculation with aluminium sulfate and the roles of aluminium sulfate and chlorine. You should already know about filtration and the substances found in natural water.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Potable water"}
Water that is safe to drink.
:::

:::definition{term="Flocculation (ZIMSEC)"}
Making tiny suspended particles clump together into larger clumps (flocs) so that they settle more quickly. Aluminium sulfate is used for this.
:::

:::definition{term="Sedimentation"}
Allowing water to stand in large tanks so that solid particles settle to the bottom.
:::

:::definition{term="Filtration"}
Passing water through beds of sand and gravel to trap the small solid particles that did not settle.
:::

:::definition{term="Chlorination"}
Adding chlorine to water to kill bacteria and other microbes.
:::

## The stages of treatment

| Stage | What happens | What it removes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| screening | water passes through metal screens | large objects: sticks, leaves, plastic, fish |
| flocculation (ZIMSEC) | aluminium sulfate is mixed into the water | makes fine clay particles clump into flocs |
| sedimentation | water stands in large settling tanks | flocs and heavy particles settle as sludge |
| filtration | water trickles through beds of sand and gravel | the remaining small solid particles |
| carbon (Cambridge) | water passes through activated carbon (charcoal) | substances that cause unpleasant tastes and odours |
| chlorination | chlorine is added | kills bacteria and other microbes |

![Flow chart of water treatment: screening removes litter, aluminium sulfate makes fine particles clump (flocculation), flocs settle in tanks (sedimentation), sand and gravel filter the rest, activated carbon removes tastes and odours, and chlorine kills microbes, giving safe drinking water.](media:diagrams/water-treatment-works-stages.png "Figure: the stages of treating river water and what each one removes")

Cambridge sums up the process as: **sedimentation and filtration** to remove solids, **carbon** to remove tastes and odours, and **chlorination** to kill microbes.

### The role of aluminium sulfate (ZIMSEC)

The tiny clay particles that make river water cloudy are so small and light that they would take a very long time to settle. When aluminium sulfate is added, these particles **coagulate**: they stick together, along with a jelly-like precipitate of aluminium hydroxide that forms in the water, into larger, heavier flocs. The flocs settle quickly in the sedimentation tanks, so much less solid reaches the sand filters.

### The role of chlorine

Chlorine **kills bacteria and other harmful microbes** that pass through the filters, such as those that cause cholera and typhoid. A small amount of chlorine is left in the water so that it stays safe as it travels through the pipes to homes. Too much chlorine gives the water a strong taste, so the amount added is carefully controlled.

:::keyfact{title="What treatment does not remove"}
Sedimentation and filtration remove **solids**, and chlorine kills **microbes**, but none of these steps removes dissolved salts. Treated tap water is safe to drink but is not pure water.
:::

## Problems in water treatment (ZIMSEC)

- When the source water is heavily polluted, for example with algae, sewage or fertiliser run-off, more aluminium sulfate and chlorine are needed, which increases costs.
- The treatment chemicals and the electricity for pumping must be paid for; shortages of either interrupt supplies.
- Old or leaking pipes can let dirty water back into treated water.
- Algae can block sand filters, so they must be cleaned more often.

## Treating water at home

When safe tap water is not available, water from a river, dam or unprotected well should be treated before drinking:

1. let it stand so that mud settles, then pour it off or filter it through a clean cloth;
2. **boil** it (bring it to a rolling boil) to kill microbes, or add **chlorine** tablets or drops following the instructions on the packet;
3. store it in a clean, covered container and use a clean cup to draw it out.

Filtering through cloth removes mud but **not** microbes, so boiling or chlorinating is still essential.

## Worked examples

:::example{title="Example 1: what each stage is for"}
Water from a muddy river is treated at a town works.

1. Screens remove floating litter and plant material.
2. Aluminium sulfate makes the fine mud particles clump together.
3. In the sedimentation tanks the clumps settle out.
4. Sand filters trap the fine particles that are left.
5. Chlorine kills the remaining bacteria, so the water is safe to drink.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: dose of chlorine]
A treatment works adds chlorine at a dose of $2.0\ \text{mg}$ per dm³ to $5\,000\,000\ \text{dm}^3$ of water a day. Calculate the mass of chlorine used each day, in kg.

1. Mass $= 2.0 \times 5\,000\,000 = 10\,000\,000\ \text{mg}$.
2. $1\ \text{kg} = 1\,000\,000\ \text{mg}$, so mass $= 10\ \text{kg}$ per day.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying filtration kills bacteria"}
Sand filters remove solid particles. Microbes are killed by **chlorine**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying chlorine removes mud"}
Chlorine kills microbes; solids are removed by sedimentation and filtration.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling treated water 'pure'"}
Treated water is **potable** (safe to drink), but it still contains dissolved substances.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Water is treated by screening, (ZIMSEC) flocculation with aluminium sulfate, sedimentation, filtration through sand, (Cambridge) activated carbon, and chlorination.
- Aluminium sulfate makes fine particles clump together so they settle quickly (ZIMSEC).
- Chlorine kills bacteria and other microbes; a little remains to keep water safe in the pipes.
- Sedimentation and filtration remove solids; carbon removes tastes and odours; dissolved salts are not removed.
- At home, settle and filter water, then boil it or add chlorine tablets, and store it covered.
:::
