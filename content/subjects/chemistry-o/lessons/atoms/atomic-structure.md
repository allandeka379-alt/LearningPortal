## Introduction

Everything around you, from the granite kopjes near Masvingo to the air you breathe, is made of atoms. Atoms are unimaginably small: even a tiny grain of sand contains billions of billions of them.

Atoms are themselves made of even smaller particles. Knowing how many of each an atom has explains which element it is, how heavy it is and, later in the course, how it bonds and reacts. In this lesson you will learn the three particles in an atom, how they are arranged, and how to count them in atoms and ions.

You should already know that an element is made of only one type of atom.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Proton number (atomic number), Z"}
The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. Every atom of the same element has the same proton number.
:::

:::definition{term="Mass number (nucleon number), A"}
The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.
:::

:::definition{term="Ion"}
A particle with an electric charge, formed when an atom (or group of atoms) loses or gains electrons.
:::

## The structure of an atom

An atom has a tiny, dense **nucleus** at its centre, containing **protons** and **neutrons**. Around the nucleus, **electrons** are arranged in **shells** (energy levels). Most of an atom is empty space: if an atom were the size of the National Sports Stadium in Harare, its nucleus would be about the size of a pea on the centre spot. Yet almost all of the mass of the atom is in that tiny nucleus.

| Particle | Where it is | Relative charge | Relative mass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Proton | Nucleus | $+1$ | $1$ |
| Neutron | Nucleus | $0$ | $1$ |
| Electron | Shells around the nucleus | $-1$ | $\frac{1}{1840}$ (almost nothing) |

An atom has **no overall charge**, because it has the same number of electrons as protons: the negative charges exactly cancel the positive charges.

:::keyfact{title="Counting the particles in an atom"}
- Protons $= Z$ (the proton number)
- Electrons $= Z$ (in a neutral atom)
- Neutrons $= A - Z$ (mass number minus proton number)
:::

## Nuclide notation

A nuclide symbol shows the mass number at the top left and the proton number at the bottom left of the element's symbol:

![Atom of potassium-39: a nucleus of 19 protons and 20 neutrons (mass number 39) surrounded by 19 electrons in shells of 2, 8, 8 and 1; the numbers of protons and electrons are equal so the atom is neutral.](media:diagrams/atom-structure-potassium-39.png "Figure: the particles in an atom of potassium-39")

$$
\ce{^{A}_{Z}X}
$$

The mass number is always the larger number (except for ordinary hydrogen, $\ce{^{1}_{1}H}$, where both are 1).

:::worked-solution[Example 1: the particles in $\ce{^{39}_{19}K}$]
1. The bottom number is the proton number: $Z = 19$, so potassium has 19 protons.
2. It is a neutral atom, so it has 19 electrons.
3. The top number is the mass number: $A = 39$.
4. Neutrons $= A - Z = 39 - 19 = 20$.
:::

## Ions

When an atom **loses** electrons it becomes a **positive ion**; when it **gains** electrons it becomes a **negative ion**. Only the electrons change: the nucleus, with its protons and neutrons, is untouched.

$$
\text{charge on the ion} = \text{number of protons} - \text{number of electrons}
$$

:::worked-solution[Example 2: the particles in $\ce{^{24}_{12}Mg^{2+}}$ and $\ce{^{16}_{8}O^{2-}}$]
1. $\ce{^{24}_{12}Mg^{2+}}$ has 12 protons and $24 - 12 = 12$ neutrons.
2. The charge is $2+$, so the atom has lost 2 electrons: $12 - 2 = 10$ electrons.
3. $\ce{^{16}_{8}O^{2-}}$ has 8 protons and $16 - 8 = 8$ neutrons.
4. The charge is $2-$, so the atom has gained 2 electrons: $8 + 2 = 10$ electrons.
5. Check with the formula: Mg, $12 - 10 = +2$; O, $8 - 10 = -2$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: identifying a particle from its numbers]
A particle has 13 protons, 14 neutrons and 10 electrons. Identify it and write its nuclide symbol.
1. The proton number is 13, so the element is aluminium (Al).
2. Mass number $= 13 + 14 = 27$.
3. Charge $= 13 - 10 = +3$, so it is an ion that has lost three electrons.
4. Nuclide symbol: $\ce{^{27}_{13}Al^{3+}}$.
:::

:::note{title="Drawing the structure of an atom"}
In a diagram, draw a small central circle for the nucleus, labelled with the numbers of protons and neutrons, and circles around it for the electron shells, with the electrons marked as dots or crosses. A beryllium-9 atom, for example, has 4 protons and 5 neutrons in its nucleus, 2 electrons in the first shell and 2 in the second. You will learn how electrons fill the shells in the lesson on electronic configuration.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Taking the mass number as the number of neutrons"}
The mass number counts protons **and** neutrons. Always subtract the proton number to find the neutrons.
:::

:::mistake{title="Changing the protons when an ion forms"}
Ions form by losing or gaining **electrons** only. A sodium ion still has 11 protons; if the number of protons changed, it would be a different element.
:::

:::mistake{title="Getting the electron count of ions the wrong way round"}
A positive charge means electrons were **lost** (fewer electrons than protons); a negative charge means electrons were **gained**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting electrons in the nucleus"}
Only protons and neutrons are in the nucleus. Electrons are in shells around it.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"State the relative charge and relative mass" questions want exact values: proton $+1$ and $1$; neutron $0$ and $1$; electron $-1$ and $\frac{1}{1840}$ (or "negligible").
:::

:::exam-tip
Show your subtraction when you work out neutrons or electrons, for example "neutrons $= 56 - 26 = 30$". A wrong final answer can still earn a method mark.
:::

:::exam-tip
The proton number identifies the element. If two particles have different proton numbers, they are different elements, whatever their other numbers.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- An atom has a tiny nucleus of protons and neutrons, with electrons in shells around it; almost all the mass is in the nucleus.
- Relative charges: proton $+1$, neutron $0$, electron $-1$. Relative masses: proton 1, neutron 1, electron $\frac{1}{1840}$.
- Proton number $Z$ = number of protons; mass number $A$ = protons + neutrons; neutrons $= A - Z$.
- In a neutral atom, electrons = protons. In an ion, charge = protons − electrons.
- Positive ions have lost electrons; negative ions have gained electrons; the nucleus does not change.
:::
