## Introduction

Sodium is a soft metal that fizzes violently in water. Neon, the gas in some glowing advertising signs, does not react with anything. A sodium atom has just one more proton and one more electron than a neon atom, yet the two elements behave completely differently. The reason lies in how their electrons are arranged.

The arrangement of electrons in the shells of an atom is called its **electronic configuration**. It explains why elements react as they do, and it is the key to the Periodic Table. In this lesson you will work out the configurations of atoms and ions of the first 20 elements and use them to find an element's group and period.

You should already know that electrons are found in shells around the nucleus and how to find the number of electrons from the proton number.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Electron shell"}
An energy level around the nucleus that can hold up to a fixed number of electrons. Shells closest to the nucleus fill first.
:::

:::definition{term="Electronic configuration"}
The arrangement of the electrons in the shells of an atom or ion, written as the number in each shell from the innermost outwards, for example 2,8,3.
:::

:::definition{term="Outer-shell electrons"}
The electrons in the outermost occupied shell (also called valence electrons). They decide how an element reacts.
:::

## Filling the shells

For the first 20 elements, fill the shells from the nucleus outwards:

:::keyfact{title="Shell capacities for elements 1 to 20"}
- First shell: up to **2** electrons.
- Second shell: up to **8** electrons.
- Third shell: **8** electrons, after which the fourth shell starts filling (potassium and calcium).
:::

| Z | Element | Configuration | Z | Element | Configuration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | H | 1 | 11 | Na | 2,8,1 |
| 2 | He | 2 | 12 | Mg | 2,8,2 |
| 3 | Li | 2,1 | 13 | Al | 2,8,3 |
| 4 | Be | 2,2 | 14 | Si | 2,8,4 |
| 5 | B | 2,3 | 15 | P | 2,8,5 |
| 6 | C | 2,4 | 16 | S | 2,8,6 |
| 7 | N | 2,5 | 17 | Cl | 2,8,7 |
| 8 | O | 2,6 | 18 | Ar | 2,8,8 |
| 9 | F | 2,7 | 19 | K | 2,8,8,1 |
| 10 | Ne | 2,8 | 20 | Ca | 2,8,8,2 |

A **shell diagram** shows the same information: the nucleus in the middle, a circle for each shell and the electrons drawn as dots or crosses on the circles. For aluminium, 2,8,3, you would draw 2 electrons on the first circle, 8 on the second and 3 on the third.

## Configuration and the Periodic Table

:::keyfact{title="Reading the Periodic Table from a configuration"}
- The **number of occupied shells** equals the **period** number.
- For Groups I to VII, the **number of outer-shell electrons** equals the **group** number.
- The noble gases (Group VIII, also called Group 0) have **full outer shells**: helium has 2 outer electrons; neon and argon have 8.
:::

Elements in the same group have the same number of outer-shell electrons, which is why they have similar chemical properties.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: chlorine, Z = 17]
1. A chlorine atom has 17 electrons.
2. First shell: 2 (15 left). Second shell: 8 (7 left). Third shell: 7.
3. Configuration: 2,8,7. Check: $2 + 8 + 7 = 17$.
4. Three occupied shells, so chlorine is in **Period 3**.
5. Seven outer-shell electrons, so chlorine is in **Group VII**.
:::

## Configurations of ions

To write the configuration of an ion, start from the atom and remove electrons (positive ions) or add them (negative ions). The ions of the first 20 elements usually end up with the same configuration as a noble gas.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: the magnesium ion, $\ce{Mg^2+}$]
1. A magnesium atom (Z = 12) is 2,8,2.
2. $\ce{Mg^2+}$ has lost 2 electrons, so it has $12 - 2 = 10$ electrons.
3. The two outer electrons are lost, leaving 2,8: the same configuration as neon.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: which element is 2,8,8,2?]
1. Total electrons $= 2 + 8 + 8 + 2 = 20$, so the proton number is 20: the element is calcium.
2. It has four occupied shells: **Period 4**.
3. It has two outer-shell electrons: **Group II**.
:::

| Ion | Electrons | Configuration | Same as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\ce{Na+}$, $\ce{Al^3+}$, $\ce{O^2-}$, $\ce{F-}$ | 10 | 2,8 | Neon |
| $\ce{K+}$, $\ce{Ca^2+}$, $\ce{S^2-}$, $\ce{Cl-}$ | 18 | 2,8,8 | Argon |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Overfilling the first shell"}
The first shell holds only 2 electrons. Lithium is 2,1, not 3.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting 9 electrons in the third shell of potassium"}
For the first 20 elements the fourth shell starts after 8 electrons in the third shell: potassium is 2,8,8,1, and calcium is 2,8,8,2.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting to change the electrons for an ion"}
$\ce{Cl-}$ has one more electron than a chlorine atom: 2,8,8, not 2,8,7. Always add up your numbers and compare with $Z$ minus the charge.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the number of shells for the group"}
The number of shells gives the **period**; the number of **outer** electrons gives the group.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write configurations with commas, innermost shell first: 2,8,5. Then check that the numbers add up to the number of electrons.
:::

:::exam-tip
In a dot-and-cross or shell diagram, draw every shell unless the question says "outer shell only", and keep the electrons in pairs where you can, so they are easy to count.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Electrons fill shells from the nucleus outwards: 2 in the first shell, then 8, then 8 (for elements 1 to 20).
- Configurations are written as numbers from the innermost shell, for example Al 2,8,3 and K 2,8,8,1.
- Number of occupied shells = period; outer-shell electrons = group number (Groups I to VII).
- Noble gases have full outer shells (helium 2, the others 8).
- For ions, remove electrons for positive ions and add them for negative ions; the result is usually a noble-gas configuration.
:::
