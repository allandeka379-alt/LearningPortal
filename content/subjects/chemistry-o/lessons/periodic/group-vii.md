## Introduction

The tap water in Harare and Bulawayo is treated with **chlorine** to kill harmful microbes. A clinic nurse may dab **iodine** solution on a cut as an antiseptic, and toothpaste contains fluorides, which are compounds of **fluorine**. These elements belong to **Group VII**, the **halogens**. The name means "salt-former": halogens react with metals to make salts such as sodium chloride.

In this lesson you will learn what the halogens look like, how their properties change down the group, how **displacement reactions** show which halogen is most reactive, and how to predict the properties of halogens you have not studied.

You should know that Group VII atoms have seven outer-shell electrons and that non-metal atoms can share electrons to form covalent bonds.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Halogens"}
The elements of Group VII: fluorine, chlorine, bromine, iodine and astatine. They are non-metals whose atoms have seven outer-shell electrons.
:::

:::definition{term="Diatomic molecule"}
A molecule made of two atoms. Every halogen exists as diatomic molecules: $\ce{F2}$, $\ce{Cl2}$, $\ce{Br2}$, $\ce{I2}$, held together by a single covalent bond.
:::

:::definition{term="Halide ion"}
The ion with a $1-$ charge formed when a halogen atom gains one electron: fluoride $\ce{F-}$, chloride $\ce{Cl-}$, bromide $\ce{Br-}$, iodide $\ce{I-}$.
:::

:::definition{term="Volatility"}
How easily a substance turns into a vapour. A volatile substance has a low boiling point.
:::

:::definition{term="Displacement reaction"}
A reaction in which a more reactive element takes the place of a less reactive element in a compound.
:::

## The halogens at room temperature

| Halogen | Formula | State at r.t.p. | Appearance | Melting point / °C | Boiling point / °C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fluorine | $\ce{F2}$ | gas | pale yellow | $-220$ | $-188$ |
| Chlorine | $\ce{Cl2}$ | gas | pale yellow-green | $-101$ | $-34$ |
| Bromine | $\ce{Br2}$ | liquid | red-brown; gives off a red-brown vapour | $-7$ | $59$ |
| Iodine | $\ce{I2}$ | solid | shiny grey-black; gives a purple vapour when warmed | $114$ | $184$ |

In school you will usually meet them as solutions in water: **chlorine water** is very pale green, **bromine water** is orange (yellow-orange when dilute) and **iodine solution** is brown.

:::keyfact{title="Trends down Group VII (from fluorine to iodine)"}
- The colour gets **darker**.
- The state changes from **gas** to **liquid** to **solid**: melting and boiling points **increase**.
- **Volatility decreases** (iodine is the least volatile).
- **Density increases**.
- **Reactivity decreases**.
:::

The melting and boiling points rise because the molecules get bigger down the group, so the weak forces between the molecules get stronger and more energy is needed to separate them.

## Electrons and reactivity

A chlorine atom is 2,8,7. When a halogen reacts with a metal, each atom **gains one electron** to complete its outer shell, forming a halide ion: $\ce{Cl-}$ is 2,8,8. With non-metals, halogens share electrons instead.

Down the group, the outer shell is further from the nucleus. The attraction between the nucleus and an incoming electron is weaker, so the atom gains an electron less easily. That is why **reactivity decreases down Group VII**, the opposite of the trend in Groups I and II, where atoms lose electrons.

## Displacement reactions

The order of reactivity can be shown in the laboratory. A **more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halogen from a solution of its halide**.

The halide solutions (potassium chloride, potassium bromide and potassium iodide) are all **colourless**. A halogen solution is added to each:

| Halogen added | Potassium chloride | Potassium bromide | Potassium iodide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chlorine water | (no test) | turns **orange**: bromine formed | turns **brown**: iodine formed |
| Bromine water | no reaction | (no test) | turns **brown**: iodine formed |
| Iodine solution | no reaction | no reaction | (no test) |

Chlorine displaces both bromine and iodine, bromine displaces only iodine and iodine displaces neither, so the order is chlorine > bromine > iodine.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: chlorine water and sodium iodide solution]
Chlorine water is added to colourless sodium iodide solution. Describe and explain what happens.
1. Compare reactivity: chlorine is above iodine in Group VII, so chlorine is more reactive and displaces iodine.
2. Observation: the colourless solution turns **brown** as iodine forms.
3. Word equation: chlorine + sodium iodide $\rightarrow$ sodium chloride + iodine.
4. Symbol equation: $\ce{Cl2(aq) + 2NaI(aq) -> 2NaCl(aq) + I2(aq)}$.
5. **Supplement:** the sodium ions do not change, so they are spectator ions. The ionic equation is $\ce{Cl2(aq) + 2I^-(aq) -> 2Cl^-(aq) + I2(aq)}$.
:::

:::note{title="Displacement as redox (Supplement)"}
In $\ce{Cl2 + 2I- -> 2Cl- + I2}$, each chlorine atom **gains** an electron (it is reduced) and each iodide ion **loses** an electron (it is oxidised). Chlorine is the oxidising agent. A more reactive halogen is a stronger oxidising agent.
:::

## Predicting the properties of other halogens

Because all the halogens have seven outer electrons, they react in similar ways, and the trends let you predict the properties of fluorine (at the top) and astatine (at the bottom). Astatine is so radioactive that only tiny amounts have ever been made, so most of its properties are predictions.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: predicting the properties of astatine]
Use the trends to predict the state, colour and reactivity of astatine, At, and whether chlorine would react with sodium astatide solution.
1. Melting and boiling points increase down the group, and iodine (above astatine) is already a solid, so astatine is a **solid** at room temperature.
2. Colours get darker down the group, so astatine is expected to be a **black** solid.
3. Reactivity decreases down the group, so astatine is the **least reactive** halogen. Like the other halogens it would be expected to form $\ce{At2}$ molecules and $\ce{At-}$ ions, so sodium astatide would be NaAt.
4. Chlorine is more reactive than astatine, so it **would** displace astatine: $\ce{Cl2 + 2NaAt -> 2NaCl + At2}$.
:::

## Safety

Chlorine and bromine are **toxic** and **corrosive**: chlorine attacks the lungs and liquid bromine burns the skin. Experiments that release chlorine or bromine vapour are done in a **fume cupboard**. Wear eye protection and gloves when handling bromine, and use dilute aqueous solutions (chlorine water, bromine water) for test-tube reactions. Iodine is harmful and stains the skin.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up halogens and halides"}
Chlorine is the element ($\ce{Cl2}$, a pale yellow-green gas). Chloride is the ion ($\ce{Cl-}$) found in salts such as sodium chloride. "Chlorine displaces bromide" is wrong; "chlorine displaces bromine from bromide ions" is right.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing single atoms"}
The halogens are diatomic: write $\ce{Cl2}$, $\ce{Br2}$ and $\ce{I2}$, then balance with $2\ce{Br-}$ or 2KBr.
:::

:::mistake{title="Reading the halogen's own colour as a reaction"}
When bromine water is added to potassium chloride solution, the mixture is orange, but only because bromine water is orange. Nothing has reacted.
:::

:::mistake{title="Copying the Group I trend"}
Reactivity **increases** down Groups I and II but **decreases** down Group VII.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
State colours precisely: chlorine is a **pale yellow-green gas**, bromine a **red-brown liquid**, iodine a **grey-black solid**. In displacement reactions, bromine forms an **orange** solution and iodine a **brown** one.
:::

:::exam-tip
For "explain" in a displacement question, give both parts: the observation (the colourless solution turns brown) **and** the reason (chlorine is more reactive than iodine, so it displaces iodine from the iodide).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The halogens are diatomic non-metals with seven outer-shell electrons; they gain one electron to form $1-$ halide ions.
- At r.t.p. chlorine is a pale yellow-green gas, bromine a red-brown liquid and iodine a grey-black solid.
- Down the group: colour darkens, melting and boiling points and density increase, volatility decreases and reactivity decreases.
- A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from its halide: $\ce{Cl2 + 2Br- -> 2Cl- + Br2}$.
- Trends let you predict fluorine and astatine; chlorine and bromine are toxic and are used in a fume cupboard.
:::
