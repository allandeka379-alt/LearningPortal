## Introduction

Think about the fuels used in one Zimbabwean day. In a village, breakfast is cooked over firewood. In town, a family switches on an LPG stove during a power cut, while a neighbour fans a charcoal brazier. Kombis and lorries run on petrol and diesel, tractors and borehole pumps on diesel, and tobacco barns are heated with wood or coal. Most of our electricity is generated by burning coal at Hwange or by water flowing through the turbines at Kariba.

A **fuel** stores chemical energy that is released, usually as heat, when it burns. In this lesson (ZIMSEC Form 3) you will classify fuels by their physical state and by whether they are fossil or renewable, list the properties of a good fuel and weigh up the effects of different fuels on health and the environment. How to measure the energy a fuel releases is covered in the lesson on fuel efficiency.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Fuel"}
A substance that releases useful energy, usually as heat, when it burns (reacts with oxygen).
:::

:::definition{term="Combustion"}
A reaction in which a substance reacts with oxygen and releases heat (and often light). It is exothermic.
:::

:::definition{term="Fossil fuel"}
A fuel formed over millions of years from the buried remains of plants and animals: coal, crude oil (petroleum) and natural gas, and the fuels made from them. Fossil fuels are **non-renewable**: they cannot be replaced once used.
:::

:::definition{term="Renewable fuel"}
A fuel that can be replaced within a human lifetime, such as wood from replanted trees, biogas, ethanol and biodiesel made from crops.
:::

## Classifying fuels by physical state

| Solid fuels | Liquid fuels | Gaseous fuels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| firewood | petrol | LPG (propane and butane) |
| charcoal | diesel | natural gas (mainly methane) |
| coal | paraffin (kerosene) | biogas (mainly methane) |
| coke | ethanol, biodiesel | coal gas, hydrogen |

:::note{title="Why LPG is a gaseous fuel"}
LPG stands for **liquefied petroleum gas**. It is a liquid inside the cylinder only because it is kept under pressure. When the valve is opened it comes out and burns as a gas, so it is classified as a gaseous fuel.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 1: classifying fuels]
Classify charcoal, petrol, natural gas, coke, paraffin and biodiesel by state and as fossil or renewable.

1. Solids: **charcoal** (from wood, renewable if the trees are replanted) and **coke** (from coal, fossil).
2. Liquids: **petrol** and **paraffin** (from crude oil, fossil) and **biodiesel** (from plant oils, renewable).
3. Gas: **natural gas** (fossil).
4. So the renewable fuels are charcoal and biodiesel; the rest are fossil fuels or made from them.
:::

## Properties of a good fuel

A good fuel should:

1. have a **high heating value**: release a lot of energy per gram;
2. be **cheap** and **readily available**;
3. be **easy and safe to store and transport**;
4. **ignite easily**, but not so easily that it catches fire by accident;
5. burn at a **steady, controllable rate**;
6. produce **little smoke, ash or harmful gases**;
7. contain **little moisture** (wet fuel wastes energy evaporating water).

No fuel is best at everything. LPG burns cleanly and has a high heating value, but it is expensive, imported and explosive if it leaks. Firewood is free in many rural areas, but it is smoky, bulky and slow to collect.

:::example{title="Example 2: choosing a fuel for a rural clinic"}
A clinic far from the electricity grid needs to boil water to sterilise equipment several times a day. Firewood is available but smoky, and someone must collect it. Paraffin releases more heat per kilogram, but it must be bought and carried from town and is a fire risk. A biogas digester fed with cow dung from nearby farms gives a clean, renewable gas that is easy to control. If enough dung is available, biogas meets most of the properties of a good fuel, with LPG as a back-up.
:::

## Fuels, health and the environment

| Fuel | Main problems |
| --- | --- |
| firewood | smoke indoors damages lungs and eyes; cutting trees causes **deforestation** and soil erosion |
| charcoal | carbon monoxide from braziers in closed rooms; making it uses large amounts of wood |
| coal | smoke, dust and sulfur dioxide (acid rain); carbon dioxide (global warming); mining damages land |
| paraffin | poisoning if children drink it from unlabelled bottles; fires and burns; smoky flame |
| LPG | explosive if it leaks; cylinders are costly; carbon dioxide when burnt |
| petrol and diesel | carbon monoxide, oxides of nitrogen and soot from engines; carbon dioxide |
| biogas | low-pressure gas; a digester needs a steady supply of waste and must be kept airtight |

Burning any carbon-containing fuel releases **carbon dioxide**, a greenhouse gas. Burning fossil fuels adds carbon that was locked underground for millions of years; renewable fuels release carbon that the plants took in recently, which is why they are better for the climate when the plants are regrown.

:::keyfact
Safety at home: store paraffin only in clearly labelled containers out of reach of children; never use a brazier or heater in a closed room; check LPG fittings for leaks with soapy water (bubbles show a leak), never with a flame.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling electricity a fuel"}
Electricity is a form of energy, not a fuel. It is generated from fuels (coal at Hwange) or from other sources such as falling water (Kariba) and sunlight.
:::

:::mistake{title="Classifying LPG as a liquid fuel"}
LPG is a liquid only under pressure in its cylinder; it burns as a gas, so it is a gaseous fuel.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying coal is renewable because it came from plants"}
Coal took millions of years to form. It cannot be replaced on a human timescale, so it is a non-renewable fossil fuel.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Classify" questions often want a table: put each fuel under solid, liquid or gas. If asked, add whether it is fossil or renewable.
:::

:::exam-tip
"State the properties of a good fuel" is a list question: give separate points (high heating value, cheap, available, safe to store, burns steadily, little pollution). Do not just write "it burns well".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A fuel releases useful energy (heat) when it burns.
- Solid: wood, charcoal, coal, coke. Liquid: petrol, diesel, paraffin, ethanol, biodiesel. Gas: LPG, natural gas, biogas.
- Fossil fuels (coal, oil products, natural gas) are non-renewable; wood, biogas, ethanol and biodiesel are renewable.
- A good fuel has a high heating value, is cheap, available, safe to store, burns steadily and produces little pollution.
- Fuels can harm health (smoke, CO, paraffin poisoning) and the environment (deforestation, acid rain, carbon dioxide).
:::
