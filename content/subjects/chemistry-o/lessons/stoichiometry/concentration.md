## Introduction

A tobacco farmer mixing a spray in a knapsack tank, a nurse preparing a drip and a water engineer dosing chlorine at a treatment works in Harare all need the same thing: a solution of the right **concentration**. Too weak and it does not work; too strong and it can burn leaves, harm a patient or make water undrinkable.

**Cambridge Core** students need to know the two units of concentration, g/dm³ and mol/dm³. The calculations in this lesson are Cambridge **Supplement** content and ZIMSEC Form 3 content. You should already be able to use amount $=$ mass $\div$ molar mass.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Solution, solute and solvent"}
A solution is a mixture formed when a solute (the substance that dissolves) dissolves in a solvent (the liquid it dissolves in). In aqueous solutions the solvent is water.
:::

:::definition{term="Concentration"}
The amount of solute dissolved in a stated volume of **solution**, usually 1 dm³. It is measured in grams per cubic decimetre (g/dm³) or moles per cubic decimetre (mol/dm³).
:::

:::definition{term="Standard solution"}
A solution whose concentration is known accurately, made by dissolving a weighed mass of solute and making the solution up to a known volume in a volumetric flask.
:::

:::formula{name="Concentration"}
$$
\text{concentration (g/dm}^3) = \frac{\text{mass of solute (g)}}{\text{volume of solution (dm}^3)}
$$

$$
\text{concentration (mol/dm}^3) = \frac{\text{amount of solute (mol)}}{\text{volume of solution (dm}^3)}
$$

So amount (mol) $=$ concentration (mol/dm³) $\times$ volume (dm³).
:::

:::keyfact{title="Always work in dm³"}
Divide a volume in cm³ by 1000 to get dm³: $250\ \text{cm}^3 = 0.250\ \text{dm}^3$ and $25.0\ \text{cm}^3 = 0.0250\ \text{dm}^3$.
:::

## Calculating concentrations and amounts

Data for this lesson: H = 1, O = 16, Na = 23, S = 32, Cu = 64.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: concentration in both units]
4.0 g of sodium hydroxide is dissolved in water and made up to $250\ \text{cm}^3$ of solution. Calculate the concentration in g/dm³ and in mol/dm³.

1. Volume $= 250 \div 1000 = 0.250\ \text{dm}^3$.
2. Concentration $= 4.0 \div 0.250 = 16$ g/dm³.
3. $M(\ce{NaOH}) = 40$ g/mol, so $n = 4.0 \div 40 = 0.10$ mol.
4. Concentration $= 0.10 \div 0.250 = 0.40$ mol/dm³.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: amount in a measured volume]
How many moles of hydrochloric acid are in $25.0\ \text{cm}^3$ of 0.200 mol/dm³ acid?

1. Volume $= 25.0 \div 1000 = 0.0250\ \text{dm}^3$.
2. Amount $= 0.200 \times 0.0250 = 0.00500$ mol.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: mass needed to make a solution]
What mass of anhydrous copper(II) sulfate is needed to make $500\ \text{cm}^3$ of a 0.100 mol/dm³ solution?

1. Amount needed $= 0.100 \times 0.500 = 0.0500$ mol.
2. $M(\ce{CuSO4}) = 64 + 32 + 64 = 160$ g/mol.
3. Mass $= 0.0500 \times 160 = 8.00$ g.
:::

## Converting between g/dm³ and mol/dm³

Multiply by the molar mass to go from mol/dm³ to g/dm³; divide by it to go back.

:::example{title="Sulfuric acid"}
0.050 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid ($M = 98$ g/mol) contains $0.050 \times 98 = 4.9$ g of $\ce{H2SO4}$ in every dm³, so its concentration is 4.9 g/dm³.
:::

## Dilution

Adding water to a solution does not change the **amount** of solute; it only spreads it through a larger volume.

:::worked-solution[Example 4: diluting a solution]
$10.0\ \text{cm}^3$ of 2.00 mol/dm³ acid is diluted with water to $100\ \text{cm}^3$. What is the new concentration?

1. Amount of acid $= 2.00 \times 0.0100 = 0.0200$ mol.
2. New volume $= 0.100\ \text{dm}^3$.
3. New concentration $= 0.0200 \div 0.100 = 0.200$ mol/dm³. (The volume increased ten times, so the concentration fell ten times.)
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using cm³ in the formula"}
$0.10\ \text{mol} \div 250 = 0.0004$ mol/dm³ is a thousand times too small. Convert $250\ \text{cm}^3$ to $0.250\ \text{dm}^3$ first.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up mass and amount"}
g/dm³ uses grams; mol/dm³ uses moles. Check which unit the question asks for before you finish.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the volume of water instead of the volume of solution"}
Concentration is per dm³ of **solution**. "Made up to 250 cm³" means the final solution has a volume of 250 cm³, not that 250 cm³ of water was added.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting that some formulae give more than one ion"}
0.10 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid, $\ce{H2SO4}$, contains 0.20 mol/dm³ of hydrogen ions, because each formula unit releases two $\ce{H+}$ ions.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write the volume in dm³ as a separate first line of working, for example "$25.0\ \text{cm}^3 = 0.0250\ \text{dm}^3$". It is the step most often missed and often earns its own mark.
:::

:::exam-tip
Quote concentrations to 3 significant figures and always include the unit, g/dm³ or mol/dm³.
:::

:::exam-tip
In a "describe how to make a standard solution" question, mention weighing the solid, dissolving it in a small volume of distilled water, transferring the solution and the washings to a volumetric flask, making up to the mark and mixing.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Concentration is the amount of solute per dm³ of solution, in g/dm³ or mol/dm³ (Cambridge Core: know both units).
- Convert cm³ to dm³ by dividing by 1000.
- Concentration (mol/dm³) = amount (mol) ÷ volume (dm³), so amount = concentration × volume.
- Convert mol/dm³ to g/dm³ by multiplying by the molar mass.
- Dilution keeps the amount of solute the same but increases the volume, so the concentration falls.
:::
