## Introduction

On a Sunday in Mutare, women in white uniforms of the church women's fellowship lead singing and collect the offering, while a woman minister preaches in the Methodist church on the other side of town. At the same time, in a mosque nearby, Fatima attends a women's class where a woman teacher explains the meaning of a verse in the Qur'an, and her brother Ibrahim goes to the men's prayer. In both religions women and men have roles in worship, family and community, yet believers explain these roles in different ways.

This lesson describes what Christianity and Islam teach about men and women, what practices follow from those teachings, and how believers within each religion interpret them differently. It continues the topic from the previous lesson, which treated Indigenous Religion and Judaism. You should know the terms gender, role, equality and equity, and the main beliefs and scriptures of Christianity (the Bible) and Islam (the Qur'an and Hadith). A Religious Studies answer reports teachings as teachings, names the scripture, and says which group of believers holds which view.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Ordination"}
The ceremony by which a Christian is set apart as a priest, pastor or minister to lead worship and administer the sacraments.
:::

:::definition{term="Headship"}
The idea, held by some Christians, that the husband is the head of the family in the way Christ is head of the church (Ephesians 5), understood by them as loving responsibility. Other Christians read the same passage as teaching mutual submission and partnership.
:::

:::definition{term="Mahr"}
The gift that a Muslim husband gives to his wife at marriage, which belongs to her.
:::

:::definition{term="Hijab"}
In common use, the modest dress, including a head covering, worn by many Muslim women. Muslims have different views about what modest dress requires and whether it should be required by law.
:::

:::keyfact
Learn: Christianity **Genesis 1:27**, **Galatians 3:28**, **Ephesians 5** (interpreted in different ways); Catholic and Orthodox churches ordain only men, many Protestant churches ordain women. Islam: equality before God (**Qur'an 4:1**: humanity was created from a single soul), women's rights to **mahr, property and inheritance**; **Khadijah** (first wife of the Prophet) and **Aisha** (scholar and teller of Hadith).
:::

## Gender in Christianity

### Biblical teachings

| Passage | Teaching (paraphrased) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 1:27 | Men and women are both made in the image of God | The basis for belief in equal worth |
| Galatians 3:28 | In Christ there is no difference between Jew and Greek, slave and free, male and female: all are one | Often used to teach equal standing in the church |
| Ephesians 5:21-33 | Husbands and wives are to be devoted to one another; verse 21 speaks of submitting to one another; the passage compares the husband to Christ as head of the church | Some Christians take this to teach male headship; others stress mutual love and submission |
| 1 Timothy 2:11-12 | Women are to learn quietly and not teach men | Interpreted by some as a rule for all time and by others as advice for a particular church |

### Women in the ministry of Jesus

The Gospels record women who followed Jesus and supported his work (Luke 8:1-3). Jesus speaks to a Samaritan woman at a well (John 4), visits the sisters Mary and Martha (Luke 10:38-42), and, according to the Gospels, women are the first to find the empty tomb on Easter morning, with Mary Magdalene named as the first to see the risen Jesus (John 20). Christians draw on these accounts to show that Jesus valued women as disciples and witnesses.

### Practice today

- **Ordination.** The Catholic and Orthodox churches ordain only men as priests, teaching that this follows the example of the apostles. Many Protestant churches, including Methodist and Lutheran churches, ordain women as ministers, and Anglican provinces differ. African Independent Churches vary, and many give women important roles as prophets and healers.
- **Women's groups.** Church women's fellowships such as **Ruwadzano** (in Shona-speaking congregations) and **Manyano** (in Ndebele-speaking congregations) are found in several denominations. Women pray together, visit the sick, raise funds, help orphans and support families.
- **Family.** Christians stress marriage as a lifelong partnership and the care of children; they differ over whether the husband should be head of the family or whether the partners share authority equally.

:::example{title="Two Christian views of Ephesians 5"}
In a youth group in Gweru, two young people read Ephesians 5 together. Tendai says it teaches that husbands should lead the family, as Christ loves and serves the church. Nyasha points to verse 21, which speaks of submitting to one another, and says the passage describes a partnership of love in which neither person dominates. Their pastor says that Christians have read the passage in both ways, and that all agree on the call to love and respect. The example shows why an exam answer should say "some Christians believe ...; others believe ...".
:::

## Gender in Islam

### Equality before God

Muslims believe that men and women are equal in worth before God. Qur'an 4:1 reminds humanity that all people were created from a single soul, and Qur'an 33:35 (paraphrased) promises the same reward to believing men and women who pray, give, fast and do good. Both are required to perform the Five Pillars. Islam also teaches that women and men have different responsibilities in some areas, and Muslims interpret those differences in different ways.

### Rights of women

- **Marriage.** A woman must consent to marriage. The husband gives her the **mahr**, which is hers alone.
- **Property and earnings.** A woman has the right to own property and to keep her earnings and her possessions.
- **Inheritance.** The Qur'an (4:7 and 4:11-12) gives daughters, wives and mothers fixed shares of an inheritance, which was a major change from earlier practice. In many cases a daughter's share is half that of a son, a rule explained by Muslim scholars by the husband's duty to support his family; this is discussed and debated by Muslims today.
- **Education.** Muslims teach that seeking knowledge is a duty for every Muslim.
- **Leadership.** Khadijah, the first wife of the Prophet Muhammad, was a successful businesswoman and, in Muslim tradition, the first person to accept Islam. Aisha, his later wife, taught many students and passed on a large number of Hadith. Women scholars and teachers have been important through Islamic history.

### Dress and modesty

Both men and women are taught to dress and behave modestly. Many Muslim women wear a head covering (hijab) or other modest dress as part of their faith, while other Muslim women interpret modesty differently. Muslims also differ on whether dress should be a matter of choice or of law. A fair answer reports these differences.

:::example{title="Fatima's inheritance class"}
In a madrasah in Harare, Fatima's teacher, a woman scholar, explains that before Islam many societies gave women no share of an inheritance. She tells the class that the Qur'an gave women fixed shares, so that daughters, wives and mothers have rights to property. A student asks about the rule that a son often receives double the share of a daughter. The teacher explains that scholars link this to a son's duty to support the family, and that Muslims continue to discuss how the rule applies today. The example shows both a right and the area of debate.
:::

## Comparison and differences within each religion

| Feature | Christianity | Islam |
|---|---|---|
| Basis of equality | Genesis 1:27; Galatians 3:28 | Equality before God; Qur'an 4:1 and 33:35 |
| Leadership of worship | Men only (Catholic, Orthodox); men and women in many Protestant churches | In congregational prayer led by a man in most mosques; women can teach and lead women's groups |
| Women in the founding story | Mary Magdalene and other disciples | Khadijah and Aisha |
| Family | Different views on headship and partnership | Mahr, consent, property and inheritance rights; roles interpreted differently |
| Women's groups | Ruwadzano, Manyano | Women's classes, madrasah and associations |
| Differences within | Catholic, Protestant, AIC | Between scholars and countries |

Within both religions, believers disagree. Some argue that traditional teachings support differing roles; others argue that the deepest teaching is equality and that old interpretations were shaped by their time. A balanced answer reports both.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Writing 'Christians believe women cannot be priests'"}
Catholic and Orthodox churches teach this; many Protestant churches ordain women. Name the group.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing Muslim dress as the same everywhere"}
Muslims hold different views and customs. Say "many Muslim women wear ..." and avoid judging.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating the religions as 'good' or 'bad' for women"}
Report what each teaches and how believers interpret it, with the evidence. Do not rank religions.
:::

:::mistake{title="Quoting scripture from memory"}
Paraphrase and cite (Galatians 3:28; Qur'an 4:1). Do not guess a reference you are not sure of.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Christians cite Genesis 1:27 and Galatians 3:28 for equal worth; Ephesians 5 is read in different ways.
- Jesus is recorded as valuing women as followers and witnesses; Catholic and Orthodox churches ordain only men, many Protestant churches ordain women.
- Ruwadzano and Manyano are women's groups in several churches in Zimbabwe.
- Muslims teach equality before God; women have rights to mahr, property and inheritance, and Khadijah and Aisha are models.
- Believers in both religions interpret gender roles in different ways, and answers must show this.
:::
