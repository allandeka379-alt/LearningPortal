## Introduction

When Aisha's baby cousin was born in Bulawayo, the family did not wait long to welcome the child into the Muslim community. The father whispered words in the baby's ear, and a week later the family held a gathering where the baby was named. Months later, Aisha attended her uncle's funeral, where the whole community prayed together and the burial took place the same day.

These events are **rites of passage**: ceremonies that mark important stages of life. In this lesson you learn how Muslims mark birth, circumcision, marriage and death, and what each rite means. You should already know the Shahadah, the Qur'an and the practice of prayer, because they appear in each rite.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Rite of passage"}
A ceremony that marks a person's move from one stage of life to another, such as birth, marriage or death.
:::

:::definition{term="Aqiqah"}
The naming ceremony held, ideally, on the seventh day after a birth, with an animal sacrificed and the meat shared with others.
:::

:::definition{term="Nikah"}
The Muslim marriage contract, made with the consent of both partners, in the presence of witnesses.
:::

:::definition{term="Mahr"}
A gift of money or property that the groom gives to the bride, which belongs to her.
:::

:::keyfact
**Birth:** adhan whispered, tahnik, aqiqah on the seventh day. **Marriage:** consent, mahr, witnesses, walimah. **Death:** washing (ghusl), shrouding (kafan), funeral prayer (janazah), burial facing Makkah.
:::

## Birth

When a baby is born, Muslims believe the first words that the child hears should be about God. The father or another family member whispers the **adhan** into the baby's right ear and the shorter call (iqamah) into the left ear. In **tahnik** an elder softens a piece of date and gently rubs it on the baby's palate, as a sign of sweetness and blessing; a family that has no dates may use something similar.

On the seventh day the family holds the **aqiqah**. The baby is given a good name, which is chosen to have a good meaning, often the name of a prophet or a Companion. The baby's head is shaved in many families, and the family gives charity equal to the weight of the hair in silver or its value. A sheep or goat is sacrificed, and the meat is cooked and shared with relatives, neighbours and the poor. The aqiqah teaches thankfulness for the gift of a child and generosity to others.

## Circumcision

Muslim boys are circumcised (**khitan**). The age varies between families and countries, from the first week of life to before puberty. Muslims regard it as part of the tradition going back to Ibrahim and a matter of cleanliness. Circumcision applies to boys. Harmful cutting of girls is not required by the Qur'an, is dangerous to health and is opposed by the World Health Organization and health workers.

## Marriage

Islam encourages marriage and regards it as a contract and a partnership based on mercy and love. The conditions of the **nikah** are:

| Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Consent | Both partners must freely agree; no one may be forced to marry |
| Mahr | A gift from the groom to the bride, which is hers to keep |
| Witnesses | At least two witnesses attend the contract |
| Guardian (wali) | In many schools of law, the bride's guardian takes part in the contract |
| Walimah | The wedding feast, given by the groom's family, to which the poor are also invited |

The marriage ceremony is simple. An imam or another Muslim leads the reading of the contract and usually gives a short sermon, and the couple and their guardians agree to its terms. Under the Constitution of Zimbabwe (section 78) a person may marry only from the age of 18, and no one may be forced into marriage; Muslims in Zimbabwe are expected to follow the law.

**Polygyny.** The Qur'an (4:3) permits a man to marry up to four wives on the condition that he treats them with justice, and Qur'an 4:129 observes that perfect fairness is very difficult. Many Muslims in the world and in Zimbabwe are in monogamous marriages, and many scholars argue that the conditions make polygyny rare. Under Zimbabwean law, a marriage registered under the civil law is monogamous, while a registered customary marriage may be polygamous.

**Divorce.** Islam allows divorce (**talaq**) but regards it as the last choice, to be preceded by efforts at reconciliation through family and community. There is a waiting period (*iddah*) after divorce. A wife may also ask for divorce through a judge or by agreement.

## Death

Muslims believe that death is the passing to the life after, and that this life is a test. When someone is dying, others stay with them and may recite the Shahadah near them. At the news of a death, Muslims say a phrase from Qur'an 2:156, meaning that we belong to God and to God we return.

The funeral takes place as soon as possible, usually within a day:

| Step | What happens | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ghusl | The body is washed, by people of the same sex (or by a spouse), with respect | Purification and honour |
| Kafan | The body is wrapped in plain white cloth | Equality: all are the same in death |
| Janazah prayer | The community prays for the dead, standing, with no bowing or prostration | Asking God's forgiveness for the person |
| Burial | The body is placed in a simple grave, on its right side, facing Makkah | Return to the earth, facing the qiblah |

Cremation is not allowed. The grave is simple, and many Muslims discourage elaborate memorials. Mourning lasts three days for most relatives, and a widow observes a longer period of four months and ten days (Qur'an 2:234). Neighbours bring food to the bereaved family, and the community visits to offer comfort. Loud wailing is discouraged, and patience is encouraged.

:::example{title="Describing an Islamic funeral"}
Farai's friend Ibrahim loses his grandfather. The next morning the body is washed by male relatives and wrapped in white cloth. The men and boys of the community gather outside the mosque and perform the janazah prayer, standing in rows. The body is then buried facing Makkah. A strong examination answer names each stage in order, uses the correct terms, and gives the meaning of at least one (for example, the plain white cloth shows that all are equal before God).
:::

:::example{title="Meaning of the aqiqah"}
Nokuthula asks Fatima why her family gave meat to the poor after the new baby was named. Fatima explains that aqiqah is a way of thanking God for the child by sharing blessings with others. This links the rite to the Muslim teaching of charity.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying that Muslim marriage can be arranged without consent"}
In Islam both partners must consent. Forced marriage is wrong in Islam and illegal in Zimbabwe.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing aqiqah and circumcision"}
Aqiqah is the naming ceremony with sacrifice on the seventh day; circumcision (khitan) is a separate rite for boys.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing Muslim burial as the same as other religions"}
Muslims bury the dead quickly, facing Makkah, wrapped in plain white cloth, without cremation. Give these specific features.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- At birth, the adhan is whispered to the baby, tahnik is done and the aqiqah is held on the seventh day.
- Boys are circumcised; harmful cutting of girls is not required by Islam.
- Muslim marriage (nikah) needs consent, mahr and witnesses, and is followed by a walimah.
- The Qur'an permits up to four wives on condition of justice; law in Zimbabwe regulates the forms of marriage; divorce is allowed but discouraged.
- At death the body is washed, wrapped in white cloth, prayed over (janazah) and buried promptly facing Makkah.
- Mourning lasts three days, longer for a widow, with the community offering comfort.
:::
