## Introduction

In a classroom in Mutare, a Muslim student named Fatima is excused from the lesson for a short time on Fridays. At home she is learning to recite long passages from memory, and she keeps her copy of the Qur'an on a high shelf, wrapped in a clean cloth. When her classmate Chipo asks why, Fatima says: "It is the word of God for us. We treat it with respect."

This lesson explains what the Qur'an is for Muslims, how it was revealed and preserved, how it is arranged, and how Muslims use it. You will also learn about the Hadith and the Sunnah, which Muslims use alongside the Qur'an to understand how to live. You should already know the life of the Prophet and the cave of Hira, because the first revelation is the starting point of the Qur'an.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Qur'an"}
The holy book of Islam, which Muslims believe is the word of God revealed to the Prophet Muhammad through the angel Jibril. The word means "recitation".
:::

:::definition{term="Hadith"}
A report of what the Prophet Muhammad said, did or approved. The plural is *ahadith*.
:::

:::definition{term="Sunnah"}
The example or way of the Prophet, known through the Hadith and used as a model for Muslim life.
:::

:::definition{term="Hafiz"}
A Muslim who has memorised the whole Qur'an.
:::

:::keyfact
**The Qur'an:** 114 surahs (chapters) made up of ayat (verses); revealed over about 23 years; first surah **Al-Fatihah** ("the Opening"). **Major Hadith collections (Sunni):** Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, compiled in the ninth century CE.
:::

## Revelation of the Qur'an

Muslims believe that the Qur'an was revealed to the Prophet over about twenty-three years, from about 610 CE until near the end of his life, through the angel **Jibril**. The first words revealed are traditionally identified with the opening of Surah 96, which calls on the Prophet to recite and speaks of God as the one who created. The revelations came in parts, in answer to events and questions in the community, and the Prophet recited each part to his Companions. Many of them memorised it, and scribes wrote it down on palm leaves, pieces of leather and flat stones.

## Compilation and preservation

Muslim tradition says that after the Prophet's death, **Abu Bakr** had the written parts and the memories of the Companions gathered into one collection, so that nothing would be lost. Later, **Uthman** had a standard copy made, from which copies were sent to the main cities, so that Muslims everywhere used the same text. Muslims therefore hold that the Qur'an has been preserved in its original Arabic. For this reason a translation into English, Shona or Ndebele is understood as a translation of the meaning, and not the Qur'an itself. Muslims recite and pray in Arabic.

## Structure of the Qur'an

The Qur'an has **114 surahs** (chapters), each made of **ayat** (verses; the singular is ayah). Surahs are arranged roughly from the longest to the shortest, not in the order in which they were revealed. Most surahs begin with the words "In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate".

Scholars speak of **Makkan** surahs, revealed before the Hijrah, which are usually shorter and stress belief in one God, judgement and the afterlife, and **Madinan** surahs, revealed after it, which are usually longer and deal with the laws and life of the community.

The first surah, **Al-Fatihah**, is a short prayer of seven verses praising God and asking for guidance. Muslims recite it in every cycle (rak'ah) of the daily prayer. Surah 112 (Al-Ikhlas) is a very short surah on the oneness of God. When citing the Qur'an we write the surah and the ayah, for example Qur'an 2:183 for the command about fasting.

## Recitation, memorisation and respect

Recitation is a form of worship. Muslims learn **tajwid**, the rules for reciting correctly, and Qur'an schools (madrasahs) teach children to read it in Arabic. Many Muslims memorise it entirely and are called a *hafiz*. In Ramadan, mosques often recite one thirtieth of the Qur'an each night.

Respect for the book is shown in many ways. Many Muslims make wudu before touching the Arabic text, keep it above other books and on a clean surface, wrap it in cloth and avoid carrying it into unclean places. Practice varies among Muslims, but all agree that the Qur'an should be treated with honour.

## Hadith and Sunnah

The Qur'an gives guidance on belief and conduct, but it does not explain every detail, such as how the five daily prayers are done. Muslims learn this from the **Sunnah**, the example of the Prophet, which is preserved in the **Hadith**. Each report has two parts: the chain of people who passed it on and the text itself. Scholars studied the chain to judge how reliable a report was, and classified Hadith as sound, good or weak.

Sunni Muslims especially use six collections, of which the two most trusted are **Sahih al-Bukhari** and **Sahih Muslim**, compiled in the ninth century CE. Shi'a Muslims have their own collections as well, which include reports from the family of the Prophet and the imams. All Muslims agree that the Hadith ranks below the Qur'an in authority.

| | Qur'an | Hadith and Sunnah |
|---|---|---|
| What is it? | Word of God revealed to Muhammad | Reports of the Prophet's sayings, actions and approvals |
| Written down | Gathered under Abu Bakr; standard copy under Uthman | Collected and checked by scholars, e.g. Bukhari and Muslim |
| Authority | The first and highest source | Second source, explaining and applying the Qur'an |
| Example of use | Commands to pray, give zakat and fast | Shows how to pray, how to wash for prayer and how to give zakat |

:::example{title="Qur'an and Sunnah working together"}
Qur'an 2:183 commands fasting in Ramadan. The Qur'an gives the principle, and the Sunnah shows the details: when to start eating before dawn, and the practice of breaking the fast at sunset. A good answer to "How do Muslims use the Hadith?" explains that the Hadith help Muslims to apply the Qur'an in daily life.
:::

:::example{title="Why respect for the Qur'an matters"}
Rudo visits a Muslim friend, Aisha, and sees her father pick up the Qur'an only after he has washed, and place it on a stand. Aisha explains that this shows respect for what Muslims believe is the word of God. In an examination, a description of respectful treatment should name specific acts (wudu, a high place, a cloth, a stand) and give the reason.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling the Qur'an the words of the Prophet"}
Muslims believe the Qur'an is the word of God revealed to the Prophet. The Prophet's own sayings are in the Hadith.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing Hadith and Sunnah"}
The Sunnah is the Prophet's example; the Hadith are the reports in which that example is recorded.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the Qur'an is arranged in the order it was revealed"}
The surahs are arranged roughly from longest to shortest. The Makkan and Madinan labels show when they were revealed, not where they stand.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Muslims believe the Qur'an is the word of God, revealed to the Prophet Muhammad through Jibril over about 23 years.
- It was gathered under Abu Bakr and a standard text was sent to the cities under Uthman.
- It has 114 surahs of ayat; the first is Al-Fatihah; surahs are Makkan or Madinan.
- Muslims show respect by recitation, memorisation (hafiz), wudu and careful handling.
- The Hadith record the Sunnah, the Prophet's example; the collections of Bukhari and Muslim are the most trusted by Sunni Muslims.
- The Qur'an is the first source of authority and the Hadith the second.
:::
