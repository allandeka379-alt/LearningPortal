## Introduction

On a Friday in a Harare suburb, a boy named Yusuf walks to the mosque with his father. In his school bag is a calendar showing dates in two ways: the usual one and the Islamic one. When his classmate Tendai asks why the Islamic year number is so different, Yusuf explains that Muslims count their years from a journey made in 622 CE, the Hijrah. To understand that journey, and why it matters to over a billion Muslims, you need to know the story of the Prophet Muhammad and the Arabia in which he lived.

This lesson presents the life of the Prophet as Muslim tradition relates it. The Prophet is never shown in any picture in Islam; when Muslims speak of him they usually add a blessing such as "peace be upon him". You should already know that Islam is a monotheistic religion and that Muhammad is its last prophet.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Jahiliyyah"}
The Arabic word Muslims use for the period in Arabia before Islam. It is usually translated "the time of ignorance", meaning ignorance of God's guidance, not a claim that the people were unintelligent.
:::

:::definition{term="Hijrah"}
The migration of the Prophet and his followers from Makkah to Madinah in 622 CE. It marks year 1 of the Islamic calendar.
:::

:::definition{term="Wahy (revelation)"}
The message that Muslims believe God sent to the Prophet Muhammad through the angel Jibril (Gabriel), later written down as the Qur'an.
:::

:::keyfact
**Born:** about 570 CE, Makkah. **First revelation:** about 610 CE, cave of Hira (Laylat al-Qadr). **Hijrah:** 622 CE (year 1 of the Islamic calendar). **Return to Makkah:** 630 CE. **Death:** 632 CE, Madinah.
:::

## Arabia before Islam

Makkah lay in the dry west of the Arabian peninsula. It was a trading town because it stood on caravan routes and held a very old shrine, the **Ka'bah**, a cube-shaped building which Muslims believe was first built by the prophet Ibrahim and his son Ismail for the worship of the one God. By the sixth century CE many Arabs had filled the area around it with images of tribal gods and worshipped them.

Society was organised by **tribes**. The leading tribe in Makkah was the Quraysh, whose leaders controlled trade and looked after the pilgrims who came to the shrine. Loyalty to the tribe came before everything else, and disputes could lead to long feuds. Muslim tradition describes social problems of the time: the poor and enslaved people had few protections, some people were cheated in trade, and some families practised the killing of newborn girls. The Qur'an refers to this in Surah 81:8-9, where the girl who was buried alive is asked about the wrong done to her.
Not everyone followed the tribal gods. Jewish and Christian communities lived in parts of Arabia, and Muslim tradition speaks of some individuals, called *hanifs*, who looked for the religion of Ibrahim. The Jahiliyyah is therefore a Muslim description of a period, and not a judgement on every person who lived in it.

## Early life of the Prophet

Muslim tradition says that Muhammad was born in Makkah about 570 CE into a branch of the Quraysh. His father, Abdullah, died before or soon after his birth, and his mother, Aminah, died when he was about six. He was cared for first by his grandfather, Abd al-Muttalib, and then by his uncle, Abu Talib. As a young man he worked in the caravan trade and became known for honesty. People called him **al-Amin**, "the trustworthy".

At about twenty-five he married **Khadijah**, a respected businesswoman who had employed him in her trade. Their marriage was happy, and they had children. Khadijah supported him throughout the early years of his mission. Muslims honour her as the first person to accept Islam.

## The first revelation and the early preaching

Muhammad liked to spend time alone in thought. Muslims believe that about 610 CE, when he was in a cave on Mount Hira near Makkah, the angel **Jibril** appeared and told him to recite. This is the first revelation, remembered on **Laylat al-Qadr**, the Night of Power, in Ramadan. Muhammad was frightened, and went home to Khadijah, who comforted him and believed him. Over about twenty-three years the rest of the Qur'an was revealed.

The first Muslims included Khadijah, Muhammad's young cousin Ali, his close friend Abu Bakr, and Zayd ibn Harithah. After some years of quiet preaching he began to speak publicly. His central message was **Tawhid**: there is only one God, who must be worshipped alone. He also taught justice, honesty, care for orphans and the poor, and that all people will be answerable to God on the Day of Judgement.

## Opposition in Makkah

Many Quraysh leaders opposed the message. The reasons given in Muslim tradition are that it rejected the gods whose shrines brought pilgrims and income, that it treated rich and poor, free and enslaved, as equal before God, and that it challenged the authority of the clan leaders. The early Muslims suffered insults, boycotts and violence. **Bilal**, an enslaved African man who had become a Muslim, was tortured by his owner and was freed by Abu Bakr. He later became the first muezzin, the one who calls Muslims to prayer.

To escape persecution a group of Muslims migrated for a time across the Red Sea to Abyssinia, where the Christian ruler gave them protection. In about 619 CE Khadijah and Abu Talib both died, and the Prophet lost his two greatest supporters. Muslims call this period a time of great sorrow.

## The Hijrah and the community at Madinah

Visitors to Makkah from the town of Yathrib, later called **Madinah**, accepted Islam and invited the Prophet to come and settle disputes between their tribes. In 622 CE he and Abu Bakr slipped out of Makkah, and the other Muslims also moved in small groups. This is the **Hijrah**. The caliph Umar later chose it as the starting point of the Islamic calendar because it marked the birth of a free Muslim community.

In Madinah the Prophet built a mosque, set up a community of both the Makkans who had migrated and the local helpers, and arranged an agreement between the Muslims, the Jewish tribes and others, often called the Constitution of Madinah, setting out shared duties of defence and the protection of each group's religion. The Makkans later fought the Muslims in battles such as Badr (624 CE) and Uhud (625 CE). In 628 CE a treaty at Hudaybiyyah made a pause in the fighting.

## Return to Makkah and the last years

In 630 CE the Prophet returned to Makkah with a large force. According to Muslim tradition the city was taken with very little fighting and he forgave most of his former enemies. The Ka'bah was cleared of its images and dedicated again to the worship of one God. In 632 CE he performed the Farewell Pilgrimage and gave a sermon that reminded Muslims of the equality of all people, the rights of women, and the duty to hold fast to the Qur'an and his example (Sunnah). He died in Madinah soon afterwards and was buried there.

:::example{title="Why the Hijrah is a turning point"}
Before 622 CE the Muslims in Makkah were a persecuted minority with no safe place to worship openly. In Madinah they became a community with a mosque, laws for their daily life and an agreement with their neighbours. This is why Muslims date their calendar from the Hijrah and not from the birth of the Prophet or the first revelation. An examination answer should name the date, say what changed, and state why it is remembered.
:::

:::example{title="Reading the life of the Prophet as a model"}
Muslims call the Prophet's example the Sunnah. Nyasha, a Muslim pupil in Bulawayo, says that his honesty as a trader reminds her to be truthful in her shop work, and his forgiveness at the conquest of Makkah reminds her to forgive in a quarrel with a neighbour. For Muslims the life of the Prophet is not only history; it guides everyday behaviour.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the Prophet Muhammad founded a new God"}
Muslims believe that Muhammad is the last of the prophets in a long line, including Ibrahim, Musa and Isa, and that he taught the worship of the same one God. He is not worshipped.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the first revelation with the Hijrah"}
The first revelation (about 610 CE, in Makkah) and the Hijrah (622 CE, to Madinah) are different events. Only the Hijrah starts the Islamic calendar.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing the story as proven history or as an opinion"}
Write "Muslims believe" or "according to Muslim tradition" where the point is about revelation. Dates and events are given as the tradition records them.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (a): describe or narrate"}
For *describe the life of the Prophet Muhammad up to the Hijrah*, put the events in order: birth and early life, marriage to Khadijah, the cave of Hira, preaching and opposition, the Hijrah. Give a date or place with each stage to earn the marks.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (b): explain the significance"}
For *explain the importance of the Hijrah to Muslims*, give separate reasons: it saved the community from persecution, it created the first Muslim community, it began the Islamic calendar and it shows trust in God. Develop each reason in a full sentence.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Before Islam, Makkah was a tribal trading town with a shrine, the Ka'bah, filled with images; Muslims call this the Jahiliyyah.
- The Prophet Muhammad was born about 570 CE, was known as al-Amin and married Khadijah.
- About 610 CE, in the cave of Hira, he received the first revelation through Jibril.
- Quraysh leaders opposed his teaching of one God and equality, and the early Muslims were persecuted.
- The Hijrah to Madinah in 622 CE began the Islamic calendar; he returned to Makkah in 630 CE and died in 632 CE.
:::
