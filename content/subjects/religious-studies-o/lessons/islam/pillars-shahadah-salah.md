## Introduction

At a school in Chinhoyi, a bell rings for lunch. A Muslim student named Yusuf quietly finds a clean corner of the library, lays a small mat on the floor and prays for a few minutes. His friend Thandeka asks what he is doing. "It is the midday prayer," he says. "I pray five times a day." The practice is one of the **Five Pillars of Islam**, the five duties that Muslims see as the framework of worship.

In this lesson you learn the five Pillars in outline and then study the first two in detail: the Shahadah and Salah. The other three Pillars are taught in the next lesson. You should already know the articles of faith, which are what Muslims believe, because the Pillars are what Muslims do.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Five Pillars"}
The five basic acts of worship in Islam: Shahadah, Salah, Zakat, Sawm and Hajj.
:::

:::definition{term="Shahadah"}
The declaration of faith: that there is no god but God (Allah), and that Muhammad is God's messenger.
:::

:::definition{term="Salah"}
The obligatory prayer performed five times a day in a set form, facing the Ka'bah.
:::

:::definition{term="Wudu"}
The ritual washing that Muslims perform before prayer.
:::

:::keyfact
**The Five Pillars:** 1 Shahadah, 2 Salah, 3 Zakat, 4 Sawm, 5 Hajj. **Prayer times:** Fajr (dawn), Dhuhr (midday), Asr (afternoon), Maghrib (sunset), Isha (night). **Qiblah:** the direction of the Ka'bah in Makkah. **Jumu'ah:** Friday congregational prayer.
:::

Sunni Muslims call these the Five Pillars. Shi'a Muslims also keep these practices, together with some additional duties which are described in a later lesson.

## Shahadah: the declaration of faith

The **Shahadah** has two parts. In the first a Muslim declares that there is no god but the one God, which affirms Tawhid. In the second a Muslim declares that Muhammad is God's messenger, which affirms the Prophet's role. A person becomes a Muslim by saying the Shahadah sincerely and with understanding. It is whispered into the ear of a newborn, repeated in the call to prayer and in each prayer, and ideally spoken as the last words in life. It is the centre of Muslim identity: everything else in Islam depends on it.

## Salah: the five daily prayers

Muslims pray five times between dawn and night. Each prayer has a fixed time window, shown on mosque timetables:

| Prayer | Time of day | Obligatory cycles (rak'ahs, usually) |
|---|---|---|
| Fajr | Dawn, before sunrise | 2 |
| Dhuhr | Midday, after noon | 4 |
| Asr | Afternoon | 4 |
| Maghrib | Just after sunset | 3 |
| Isha | Night | 4 |

### The call to prayer

The **adhan** is the call to prayer, spoken aloud by the **muezzin** (traditionally from the minaret). It declares that God is great, repeats the Shahadah and invites people to prayer. The first muezzin was Bilal, a Companion of the Prophet. A shorter call (iqamah) is said just before the congregation begins.

### Preparing for prayer

A Muslim prays with a clean body, clean clothes and a clean place. Before prayer comes **wudu**: washing the hands, rinsing the mouth and nose, washing the face and arms to the elbows, wiping the head and washing the feet. Many Muslims use a **prayer mat**. In prayer they face the **qiblah**, the direction of the Ka'bah in Makkah, which for Muslims in Zimbabwe is roughly north, a little east of north.

### The form of the prayer

Each prayer is made up of cycles called **rak'ahs**. In each rak'ah the worshipper stands and recites Al-Fatihah and another passage from the Qur'an, bows from the waist, stands again, and then places the forehead on the ground in prostration, which is the position of the greatest humility before God. The worshipper sits and recites a closing blessing and ends by turning the head to the right and left in greeting. The prayers are in Arabic, whatever a person's mother tongue.

### Jumu'ah: Friday prayer

On Friday at midday, Muslims, especially men, are expected to gather at the mosque for the **Jumu'ah** prayer, which replaces the usual midday prayer. It includes a sermon (**khutbah**) given by the imam, in which he speaks about faith and daily life. Women are welcome to attend, and many mosques have a special area for them, although attendance is not required of them in the same way.

## The significance of prayer

Muslims say that prayer keeps God in mind through the day. It teaches discipline, because prayer must be at the proper time. It shows equality, since rich and poor, young and old stand shoulder to shoulder in the same rows. It unites Muslims worldwide, because they face the same direction. The Qur'an (29:45) teaches that prayer helps to keep a person away from wrongdoing. When Muslims cannot pray at the mosque they may pray at home, at school or at work.

:::example{title="Praying at school"}
Tafadzwa is asked what Yusuf does at midday. A full answer follows the order of prayer: he makes wudu to be clean, faces the qiblah on a mat, stands, recites from the Qur'an, bows and prostrates, then ends the prayer with greetings. Explaining the purpose (to remember God, to show humility) turns a description into an explanation.
:::

:::example{title="Why Friday matters"}
Rachel, who is Jewish, notes that her community gathers on Saturday and asks about Friday for Muslims. Yusuf explains that Friday is not a day of rest in the way that the Sabbath is; Muslims may work before and after the congregational prayer. Jumu'ah is a day to gather, listen to the khutbah and pray together. Different religions keep their special days differently.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Muslims pray to the Prophet or to the Ka'bah"}
Muslims pray to God alone. The Ka'bah is the direction (qiblah) they face, not an object of worship.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing Friday with a Muslim Sabbath"}
Jumu'ah is a congregational prayer at midday on Friday. Muslims are not required to stop work for the whole day.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing the prayers without times"}
Name each prayer and its time, and explain that the times keep prayer spread across the day.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Five Pillars are Shahadah, Salah, Zakat, Sawm and Hajj.
- The Shahadah declares belief in one God and in Muhammad as God's messenger.
- Salah is performed five times a day: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha.
- Muslims prepare with wudu, face the qiblah, and pray in rak'ahs with standing, bowing and prostration.
- The adhan is the call to prayer, given by the muezzin.
- Jumu'ah is the Friday congregational prayer with a sermon; prayer builds discipline, humility, equality and unity.
:::
