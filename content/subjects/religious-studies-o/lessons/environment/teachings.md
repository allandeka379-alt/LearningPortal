## Introduction

After a long dry season, villagers near Masvingo watch the clouds and wonder when the rains will come. A farmer in Chinhoyi plants trees on the edge of his field to hold the soil. A pupil in Hwange sees a plastic bag in a river and picks it up. People act this way for many reasons, and one of the strongest is belief. Every major religion has something to say about the natural world: where it came from, whether it belongs to us, and what we should do with it.

This lesson explains what Indigenous Religion (IR), Judaism, Christianity and Islam teach about creation and humanity's place in it, and compares the views. You should know the ideas of creation in the earlier topics: God as creator in IR, the creation accounts in Genesis (Judaism and Christianity), and the Qur'an (Islam). The next lessons look at how IR conserves the environment and at environmental problems and religious responses. As always, report beliefs as beliefs: "Muslims believe ...", "Many Christians understand dominion as ...".

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Stewardship"}
Caring responsibly for something that belongs to someone else. In religious teaching about the environment, humans are seen as caretakers of creation, not as owners who may use it without limit.
:::

:::definition{term="Dominion"}
The word used in Genesis 1:26-28 for humanity's rule over other living things. Many Jews and Christians understand it as responsible care, as a good ruler cares for those in their charge, and not as permission to destroy.
:::

:::definition{term="Khalifah"}
An Arabic word meaning a deputy or vicegerent. Muslims believe that human beings are God's khalifah on earth, with the duty to look after it.
:::

:::definition{term="Amanah"}
An Arabic word for a trust. Muslims teach that the earth and its resources are an amanah from God, for which people will be accountable.
:::

:::keyfact
Learn: IR: land belongs to **God and the ancestors**; nature is sacred. Jews and Christians: **Genesis 1:26-28** (dominion), **Genesis 2:15** (to work and keep the garden), **Psalm 24:1** (the earth is the Lord's), **Leviticus 25** (a Sabbath year of rest for the land). Muslims: **khalifah** (**Qur'an 6:165**; also 2:30), **amanah**, **Qur'an 30:41** (corruption appears on land and sea because of human actions).
:::

## Indigenous Religion

In IR, the earth is the creation of **God** (Mwari, Musikavanhu, Nyadenga; uMlimo, uNkulunkulu), and the land is held in trust by the **ancestors**. The people of a village believe that they do not own the land absolutely: the chief allocates it, and the ancestral spirits (midzimu/amadlozi) and the territorial spirits (mhondoro) protect it. Because the land is the place where the ancestors are buried and where God's gifts of rain and harvest are received, it is sacred.

Several features of the belief protect the environment:

- **Sacred places.** Certain forests, groves, hills, pools and rivers are believed to be homes of spirits or places of ritual and are not to be harmed.
- **Taboos (zviera).** Rules forbid cutting some trees, killing some animals or fishing in some pools.
- **Totems (mutupo/isibongo).** A clan that has an animal as its totem will not kill or eat it; this protects the species.
- **Days of rest.** The rest day (chisi) was a day on which farming was not done, which gave the land a rest in some communities.
- **Rain ceremonies.** Rain-making rituals such as mukwerera/ukucela izulu express dependence on God and the ancestors for the rains.

The next lesson looks at these methods in detail.

## Judaism and Christianity

Jews and Christians share the creation accounts in Genesis. In **Genesis 1** God creates the world in an ordered sequence and calls it good; humans are made in God's image and given **dominion** over fish, birds and animals (1:26-28). In **Genesis 2:15** the first man is placed in the garden of Eden to cultivate it and look after it. **Psalm 24:1** declares that the earth and everything in it belong to the Lord; **Psalm 104** praises God for the beauty and provision of creation.

Most Jewish and Christian writers on the environment understand dominion as **stewardship**: because the earth belongs to God, humans are caretakers who must answer to Him. Some critics have argued that dominion has been misread as permission to exploit; many Jews and Christians reject that reading.

### The Sabbath year

**Leviticus 25** commands that every seventh year the land is to have a Sabbath of rest, when it is not sown or harvested in the usual way, so that the poor and wild animals may eat what grows. This teaches that land is not simply a resource to be used without pause. The same chapter says that the land cannot be sold permanently, for it belongs to God (25:23). Jewish teaching also includes the principle of *bal tashchit*, "do not destroy", based on Deuteronomy 20:19-20, which forbids cutting down fruit trees when besieging a city.

### Differences within

Christians differ in emphasis. Some stress that the world will be renewed by God and that Christians should care for creation now; others give less attention to the environment. Many churches, including Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant and African Independent churches, teach creation care. Jews also differ, but most agree on the duty to protect the earth.

:::example{title="Stewardship in a church garden"}
A church in Mutare has a large plot behind its hall. The minister reads Genesis 2:15 and tells the congregation that the first people were placed in a garden to look after it. Tendai's youth group plants indigenous trees, builds a compost heap, and sets aside a part of the plot to rest each year, which the group says reminds them of Leviticus 25. They also gather the rubbish from the church grounds. The example shows how Christians can turn the teaching on stewardship into practice.
:::

## Islam

Muslims believe that God (Allah) created the heavens and the earth in balance and that every creature praises its Creator. Humans hold a special position as **khalifah**, a deputy who looks after the earth (Qur'an 2:30; 6:165). The earth is an **amanah** (trust). Qur'an 30:41 states that corruption has appeared on land and sea because of what people have done, a verse often quoted in teaching about pollution and waste. Qur'an 7:31 teaches that people should eat and drink but not waste.

Muslims are taught to avoid wastefulness, to use water carefully (even when making wudu, the washing before prayer), to treat animals kindly and to plant trees. Muslim organisations in many countries run campaigns on tree planting, clean water and reducing waste.

:::example{title="Wudu and water"}
Yusuf learns at his madrasah in Harare that Muslims are taught to avoid waste, including when using water for washing before prayer. His teacher says this shows that a Muslim should use resources carefully at all times. Yusuf starts to switch off taps firmly and to use a basin when washing for prayer. The example shows how teaching on the environment links to daily acts of worship.
:::

## Comparing the views

| Feature | Indigenous Religion | Judaism and Christianity | Islam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who owns the earth? | God and the ancestors; the chief allocates land | The Lord (Psalm 24:1) | God (Allah) |
| Human role | Users and guardians, answering to the ancestors | Stewards with dominion as responsible care (Genesis 1-2) | Khalifah, deputy who looks after the earth |
| Key teaching | Land is sacred; taboos protect places and species | Sabbath year (Leviticus 25); do not destroy | Earth is a trust (amanah); avoid waste and corruption (Qur'an 30:41) |
| Practice | Sacred groves, taboos, totems, chisi | Tree planting, recycling, creation care groups | Water saving, tree planting, anti-waste campaigns |

**Similarities**: all four teach that the earth is not simply ours to use as we please, that humans are accountable, and that harming creation is wrong. **Differences**: the language and sources are different; IR stresses the presence of ancestors and spirits; Judaism and Christianity stress creation and stewardship; Islam stresses trust and accountability.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying that dominion means humans can do whatever they like"}
Most Jews and Christians interpret dominion as stewardship. Explain this and mention other readings fairly.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing IR as 'worship of nature'"}
IR teaches belief in God and the ancestors, and treats nature as sacred because of that relationship. Use its own terms.
:::

:::mistake{title="Quoting scripture without a reference"}
Cite Genesis 1:26-28, Psalm 24:1, Leviticus 25, Qur'an 6:165 and 30:41 and paraphrase them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing views without comparing"}
When asked to compare, give at least one similarity and one difference, and link them.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- In IR the land belongs to God and the ancestors; nature is sacred and protected by taboos, totems and sacred places.
- Jews and Christians teach that the earth is the Lord's (Psalm 24:1) and that dominion (Genesis 1:26-28) means stewardship (Genesis 2:15); Leviticus 25 gives the land a Sabbath year.
- Muslims teach that humans are khalifah and that the earth is an amanah; Qur'an 30:41 links corruption to human actions.
- The religions agree that people are accountable for how they treat creation but express this in different ways.
:::
