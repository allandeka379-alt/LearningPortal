## Introduction

After a heavy storm in Chimanimani, soil from a bare hillside washes into the river and the water turns brown. In a town in Mutare, plastic bags choke a drain. Near Kwekwe, a river is silted up where gold panners have dug the banks. In the dry season a veld fire races across grassland, destroying grazing and killing animals. These are not distant events; they are problems that Zimbabwean communities face, and they raise a moral question as well as a practical one. Do the teachings that you studied in the earlier lessons on stewardship, trust and sacred land lead believers to act?

This lesson identifies the main environmental problems and their causes, and describes how religious groups respond. You should know the religious teachings from the first lesson of this topic and the Indigenous Religion methods from the second. A good answer connects each problem with a cause and each response with a religious teaching.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Deforestation"}
The clearing of forests or woodland, for example for fuelwood, farmland, building materials or mining, faster than trees grow back.
:::

:::definition{term="Land degradation"}
The loss of the quality of soil and land, through erosion, over-grazing, over-cultivation and loss of vegetation, so that it can no longer grow crops or grass well.
:::

:::definition{term="Climate change"}
Long-term change in the world's weather patterns, largely driven by the release of greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide from burning fuels and clearing forests; its effects include droughts, floods and stronger storms.
:::

:::definition{term="Interfaith"}
Involving people of different religions working together.
:::

:::keyfact
Learn the problems: **deforestation**, **land degradation**, **veld fires**, **gold panning and siltation**, **pollution and waste**, **poaching**, **climate change**. Law: the **Environmental Management Act**, enforced by the **Environmental Management Agency (EMA)**. Examples of religious response: **AZTREC** (traditional leaders and mediums) and the **AAEC** (African Independent churches) in outline.
:::

## Environmental problems, causes and effects

| Problem | Main causes | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| Deforestation | Cutting trees for fuelwood, farmland, building poles, tobacco curing, commercial timber and mining | Soil erosion, loss of habitats, less rainfall retention, fuelwood shortage |
| Land degradation and soil erosion | Over-grazing, farming on slopes and river banks, poor farming methods, loss of vegetation | Falling harvests, gullies, silting of dams |
| Veld fires | Careless burning, hunters, honey collectors, clearing land, children playing, lightning | Loss of grazing, trees and animals; air pollution; soil damage |
| Gold panning and mining damage | Digging in river banks, use of chemicals such as mercury, abandoned pits | Siltation, polluted water, health risks, dangerous holes |
| Pollution and waste | Plastics, litter, sewage, factory waste, burning rubbish | Blocked drains, disease, poisoned water, harm to animals |
| Poaching | Illegal killing of wildlife for meat, ivory, horn or trade, often linked to poverty or organised crime | Loss of species; loss of tourism income; breaks totem and conservation values |
| Climate change | Burning fossil fuels, deforestation worldwide, with effects felt locally | More frequent droughts, floods and violent storms, crop failure, hunger and displacement; the 2019 Cyclone Idai in the east of Zimbabwe is one example of extreme weather |

Many of these problems are linked: deforestation increases erosion and silting; poverty can push people to cut trees or pan for gold; weak enforcement allows pollution. A good answer shows these links.

## Religious responses

| Religious group | Examples of response |
|---|---|
| Churches (Catholic, Protestant, African Independent) | Tree-planting days and nurseries; clean-up campaigns; sermons and youth groups on caring for creation (Genesis 2:15; Psalm 24:1); support for gardens, boreholes and soil conservation |
| African Independent churches | The Association of African Earthkeeping Churches (AAEC), a network of African Independent churches, has used worship, tree-planting ceremonies and nurseries to restore woodland and teach that caring for the earth is part of faith (in outline) |
| Traditional leaders and spirit mediums | The Association of Zimbabwean Traditional Ecologists (AZTREC), a network of chiefs, mediums and elders, has worked to protect sacred forests and to plant indigenous trees, using traditional authority (in outline) |
| Muslims | Teachings on being khalifah and avoiding waste; mosque clean-ups, water saving and tree planting; community awareness |
| Jews | Teaching on the Sabbath year and *bal tashchit* ("do not destroy"); the festival of Tu BiShvat, the new year for trees, is a time when many Jews plant trees |
| Interfaith groups | Joint tree planting, joint statements by leaders, shared clean-up days, and cooperation with schools, councils and EMA |

:::example{title="A joint clean-up in Mutare"}
In Mutare, a church, a mosque and a traditional leaders' committee agree to hold a joint clean-up of a stream that has been blocked by waste. Aisha and Yusuf from the mosque bring bags and gloves, Nyasha leads the church youth group in sorting the plastics, and the chief explains why the stream is respected by the community. Afterwards, they plant indigenous trees on the bank. The example shows how different religious groups, each drawing on its own teaching, can cooperate on a shared problem.
:::

:::example{title="A church nursery"}
A congregation in Marondera opens a tree nursery behind its hall. Members take seedlings of indigenous and fruit trees to plant in their yards, on school grounds and along gullies. The minister preaches on Genesis 2:15 and says that planting is a way of serving God. Tendai, aged fifteen, runs the watering roster. In three years the gully has begun to heal. The example shows teaching leading to action with a practical result.
:::

## The law in outline

The **Environmental Management Act** sets out the duty to protect the environment and provides for environmental impact assessments, control of pollution and waste, and protection of wetlands, rivers and forests. The **Environmental Management Agency (EMA)** enforces the Act and works with communities, councils and religious groups. Laws also control veld fires, requiring fire guards (firebreaks) and penalising those who start fires. Freedom of religion does not allow people to harm the environment in ways that the law forbids.

## Assessing responses

Religious groups bring special strengths: large numbers of followers, regular meetings, moral authority, a message of responsibility, and long-lasting local presence. Their weaknesses include limited funds, differences between groups, and the fact that not all believers act on their teachings. The best results come when religious groups work with councils, schools, EMA and scientists.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing problems without causes"}
For each problem give a cause and an effect: "deforestation, caused by cutting trees for fuelwood, leads to soil erosion".
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing responses without linking them to belief"}
Say which teaching motivates the response (for example, stewardship in Genesis 2:15 or the trust in Islam).
:::

:::mistake{title="Naming organisations whose details you are unsure of"}
Give AZTREC and the AAEC in outline only, as networks of traditional leaders and African Independent churches. Do not invent founders or dates.
:::

:::mistake{title="Presenting religions as the only solution"}
Religious groups work with government, EMA, schools and scientists. Mention cooperation.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (a): identify and describe"}
Name four or five problems with causes, then describe at least two religious responses with examples. Use one Shona or Ndebele term where relevant.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (b): how effective are religious responses?"}
Give strengths (moral authority, numbers, community trust), limits (resources, uneven commitment), and a conclusion such as "religious groups motivate and mobilise people, but work best with law and science".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Major problems: deforestation, land degradation, veld fires, gold panning and siltation, pollution and waste, poaching and climate change.
- Causes include poverty, population growth, poor farming methods, weak enforcement and global emissions.
- Religious groups respond with tree planting, clean-ups, teaching on stewardship, and interfaith work such as AZTREC and the AAEC (in outline).
- The Environmental Management Act and EMA enforce environmental protection; religious groups work best with them.
- A good answer links each problem, cause, teaching and response.
:::
