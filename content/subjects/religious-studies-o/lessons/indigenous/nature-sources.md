## Introduction

When Tendai's grandmother pours a little water on the ground before drinking from a spring, she says a few words of thanks. When the family plans to open a new field, the elders first speak to the ancestors. When someone is sick, the family asks whether a custom has been neglected. None of these actions is found in a book, and nobody remembers a day when the religion was "founded". Yet this is a living religion, held and handed on by families and communities across Zimbabwe for generations.

This lesson introduces **Indigenous Religion (IR)**, sometimes called African Traditional Religion: its nature, its features and the ways in which it is passed on. Later lessons look at God and creation, the spirit world, practitioners, sacred places and rituals. The earlier lessons on the meaning and features of religion will help you to see how IR shares the features of other religions.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Indigenous Religion (IR)"}
The religion of the indigenous peoples of Africa, handed down through families and communities. In Zimbabwe it is the religion of the Shona, Ndebele, Tonga, Venda, Kalanga and other peoples, and it includes belief in God, the ancestors and the spirit world, and rules for living with others.
:::

:::definition{term="Oral tradition"}
Knowledge that is passed from one generation to the next by speaking, singing, storytelling, proverbs and ceremonies, and not by writing.
:::

:::definition{term="Myth and legend"}
A **myth** is a sacred story explaining origins or meaning. A **legend** is a traditional story about people from the past, usually with some historical basis.
:::

:::definition{term="Proverb"}
A short, wise saying that sums up a community's values and experience, such as *chara chimwe hachitswanyi inda* (Shona: one thumb cannot crush a louse, meaning that people need one another).
:::

:::definition{term="Veneration of the ancestors"}
Honouring and remembering the dead of a family or community and asking them for help and guidance. Many scholars and believers explain that the ancestors are respected as intermediaries and not worshipped as God.
:::

## Nature and features of Indigenous Religion

| Feature | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| No single founder | It was not started by one person at a known time | Islam is linked to the revelation given to the Prophet Muhammad, and Christianity to Jesus |
| No single written scripture | Teaching is handed down orally | Elders teach myths, proverbs and praises at the fireside |
| Community-based | Belonging is usually by birth into a family, clan and community | A person joins by being born into the lineage |
| Part of daily life | There is no sharp line between religious and everyday life | Prayers before planting and at harvest; taboos on farming and water use |
| Belief in God and the spirit world | God is supreme; ancestors and other spirits are part of the world | Mwari (Shona), uMlimo and uNkulunkulu (Ndebele) |
| Moral code | Rules about right conduct are taught as part of religion | Respect for elders; zviera (taboos); Unhu |
| Concern for the living | Rituals seek rain, health, fertility, harmony and protection | Mukwerera/ukucela izulu; bira |
| Does not usually seek converts | It is a community's inheritance and not a message to spread | Contrast with missionary religions |

:::keyfact
Indigenous Religion is **a religion**, with beliefs, worship, practitioners, sacred places and a moral code. It is not "just culture" or "superstition". It is a way of life in which the sacred and the everyday are closely joined.
:::

## Sources of knowledge about Indigenous Religion

Because IR has no scripture, we learn it from many living sources.

| Source | What it carries | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Myths and legends | Beliefs about God, creation and how things began | A story of how death came into the world |
| Proverbs and sayings | Moral teaching in few words | *Rume rimwe harikombi churu* (Shona: one man cannot surround an anthill, meaning cooperation is needed) |
| Songs, dances and praise poems | Beliefs and history in memorable form | Mbira songs; clan praises (madupe, izibongo) |
| Prayers and invocations | Words addressed to God and the ancestors | Prayers at the start of a ceremony |
| Rituals and ceremonies | Beliefs acted out | Bira, mukwerera, kurova guva/umbuyiso |
| Sacred places and objects | Belief made visible | Mountains, pools, groves, shrines, the mbira |
| Art, names and totems | Identity and belief in symbols | Totem animals; names with meaning |
| Elders and family teaching | Living memory | Grandparents teaching children at the fire |

Written sources, such as books by scholars and records of interviews, also help, but they were written by people who learnt the tradition from its practitioners.

:::example{title="How a child learns at a homestead in Bindura"}
Nyasha, aged ten, spends the school holidays with her grandmother.

1. **At the fire:** her grandmother tells the story of how the first people came into the world, and Nyasha learns that Mwari is the creator.
2. **Proverbs:** when Nyasha is rude to a visitor, her grandmother says a proverb about respect, and Nyasha understands the value.
3. **Totem:** her grandmother recites the family's totem praises and explains the totem taboo.
4. **Ceremony:** at the next family ceremony she watches the elders, hears the songs and learns what to do.
5. **Result:** without a textbook, Nyasha has learnt about God, morality, identity and ritual.
:::

:::example{title="Reading the sources of one belief"}
Take the belief that the rain comes from God and that the ancestors may plead for it.

1. **Myth:** a story tells how Mwari gave rain to the earth.
2. **Song:** the community sings rain songs at mukwerera.
3. **Ritual:** elders and a spirit medium lead the offering of beer and prayers.
4. **Sacred place:** in some regions the community sends petitions to a shrine such as Njelele in the Matopo Hills.
5. **Proverb:** sayings remind people that rain and harvest depend on more than human effort.

The same belief is therefore carried by a story, a song, a ritual, a place and a saying.
:::

## Labels to avoid

Some words used in the past carry unfair judgements.

| Loaded label | Why it is problematic | Fairer term |
|---|---|---|
| Paganism, heathenism | They were used by outsiders to suggest that the religion is inferior or false | Indigenous Religion |
| Primitive religion | It wrongly suggests that the religion is simple or undeveloped | Indigenous Religion |
| Superstition | It dismisses the beliefs without understanding them | Beliefs and practices |
| Ancestor worship | Many believers say that they honour, not worship, the ancestors; God is the supreme being | Veneration of the ancestors |
| Animism | A term from Tylor's theory; many believers do not use it | Describe the actual beliefs |

Using fair terms is part of the multi-faith, non-confessional approach of this subject. It does not mean that every belief must be accepted; it means describing what people believe in terms they would recognise.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Indigenous Religion has no beliefs or no morality"}
IR has beliefs about God, spirits and life after death, and a detailed moral code taught by the family and community.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing about 'African Traditional Religion' as though it is one identical religion"}
Peoples such as the Shona, Ndebele and Tonga share many features but have their own names, rituals and customs. Say which group you mean where you can.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using 'ancestor worship' or 'pagan'"}
Use "veneration of the ancestors" and "Indigenous Religion", and explain that the ancestors are honoured as intermediaries.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing IR in the past tense only"}
Indigenous Religion is alive today: many people practise it, and many Christians and Muslims also keep its family customs. Use the present tense unless you refer to history.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1, part (a)"}
When asked to *outline the features* of IR, give about seven features with a short explanation or example each (two marks each). Include no founder, oral tradition, community-based, daily life and belief in God and the spirit world.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1, part (b)"}
For *explain why oral tradition is important*, develop reasons: it carries teaching to the young, keeps history, links families and communities, and allows practice to adapt. Mention a limitation, such as the risk of details being forgotten or changed.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Indigenous Religion is the religion of the indigenous peoples of Zimbabwe: it has no single founder or written scripture and is handed down orally in families and communities.
- It is community-based, part of daily life, and includes belief in God (Mwari, uMlimo/uNkulunkulu), the ancestors and the spirit world, with a moral code.
- Its sources include myths, legends, proverbs, songs, prayers, rituals, sacred places, art, names, totems and the teaching of elders.
- Ancestors are venerated as intermediaries; loaded labels such as pagan, primitive and ancestor worship should be avoided.
- It is a living religion, practised today alongside other religions.
:::
