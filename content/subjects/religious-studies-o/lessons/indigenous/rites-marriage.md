## Introduction

Imagine a weekend in Bindura when a young man, Tafadzwa, and his uncles arrive at the home of Nyasha's family. They are not simply visiting. They are beginning a process in which two families will be joined. In Indigenous Religion (IR) marriage is understood as a union of families, not only of two individuals. The ancestors of both sides are informed, and the community witnesses and supports the couple.

This lesson describes marriage in IR: its meaning, the steps of the marriage process, the types of marriage found in customary practice and the harmful practices that are now illegal. You should already know the role of ancestors (midzimu/amadlozi) from earlier lessons. Christian, Jewish and Muslim marriage are studied in the lessons on those religions.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Marriage"}
A socially and religiously recognised union between a man and a woman (and, through them, between their families) that creates rights and duties and is the accepted setting for raising children.
:::

:::definition{term="Bride wealth"}
Gifts, traditionally cattle and today often money or goods, given by the groom's family to the bride's family as part of the marriage process. It is called **roora** in Shona and **lobola** in Ndebele.
:::

:::definition{term="Monogamy and polygamy"}
**Monogamy** is marriage to one spouse at a time. **Polygamy** is marriage to more than one spouse; in Zimbabwe it is usually **polygyny**, one man with more than one wife.
:::

:::definition{term="Go-between"}
A trusted relative or family friend who carries messages between the two families during courtship and negotiations: **munyai** in Shona, **umkhongi** in Ndebele.
:::

## The meaning and purpose of marriage

In IR, marriage has four main purposes.

- **To join two families.** Relationships between the families become permanent, and each side gains new relatives and obligations.
- **To have children.** Children continue the lineage and are the link between the living and the ancestors.
- **To continue the lineage and the family name.** Marriage protects the family's place in the community.
- **To involve the ancestors.** Both families inform their ancestors so that the couple and their children receive protection.

## The marriage process

The details differ between families and peoples, but the main steps are widely recognised.

| Step | What happens | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Courtship | Young people meet and show interest; families make quiet enquiries about each other | Shows respect and makes sure the families are suitable |
| Go-between | The groom's family sends a go-between to the bride's family | Shows respect; avoids direct confrontation |
| Introduction | The groom and his relatives visit the bride's family | Formal request to marry; agreement to start talks |
| Bride wealth | The families negotiate roora/lobola | Thanks the bride's family and seals the alliance |
| Role of aunts and uncles | The bride's paternal aunt (vatete/ubabakazi) and the uncles advise the couple | Passes on family values and responsibilities |
| Welcoming the bride | The bride is received at the groom's home and introduced to his relatives and ancestors | Incorporates her into her new family |

### Bride wealth

Bride wealth is not buying a person. Those who practise it describe it as a **token of appreciation** to the bride's family for raising her, and a **sign of respect** and a **seal** between families. It also makes the families responsible for supporting the marriage. In outline it may include a part set aside for the bride's mother, the main bride wealth and gifts for relatives. Amounts and forms (cattle or money) differ, and in the courts and in public debate today people discuss whether high demands make marriage unaffordable or put pressure on women.

:::example{title="A marriage process, step by step"}
Tafadzwa wishes to marry Nyasha, who lives in another district.

1. A family friend acts as go-between and tells Nyasha's family that Tafadzwa wishes to visit.
2. Tafadzwa and his uncles visit, are received, and ask formally for Nyasha's hand.
3. Both families agree on the roora and a date for its presentation.
4. Nyasha's paternal aunt advises her about married life.
5. Nyasha is welcomed at Tafadzwa's home, where relatives greet her and the ancestors are informed.

Each step builds trust, and the final welcome shows that she now belongs to two families.
:::

## Types of marriage

| Type | Description | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monogamy | One husband and one wife | Common today; recognised in customary and civil law |
| Polygamy (polygyny) | A man married to more than one wife | Accepted in customary law; more wives meant more children and labour; the arrangement can cause jealousy, conflict over inheritance and financial strain |
| Levirate / widow inheritance | A widow may marry a relative of her late husband | Traditionally kept the widow and children in the family; harmful when done **without the widow's consent** |
| Sororate | A wife's sister marries the husband when the wife has died or cannot have children | Traditionally kept the alliance between the families |
| Elopement | A couple leaves without the usual process; the families later settle matters, often with payment | Shows a couple's independence; families must still repair relations |
| Pledging a girl (kuzvarira) | A girl is promised or given in marriage to settle a debt or gain protection | **Harmful and illegal**: it violates the rights of a child |

:::example{title="Two views of polygamy"}
Sipho's grandfather says that in his youth a man with several wives had more hands to work the fields and more children to care for the elderly. Thandeka, his granddaughter, notes that a household with several wives can be a source of jealousy, can divide property after a death and can be expensive, especially when school fees and health costs must be shared. Both describe real experiences. In an exam answer, set out the advantages and the disadvantages and then give your own supported judgement.
:::

## Harmful practices and the law

Marriage under the law of Zimbabwe requires the **free and full consent** of both people. The Constitution of Zimbabwe (2013), section 78, sets the minimum age of marriage at 18, and the Marriages Act of 2022 also sets 18 as the minimum age for marriage. This means that **child marriage**, **forced marriage**, **pledging girls** and **wife inheritance without consent** are harmful practices and unlawful. Their effects include ending a girl's education, health risks from early pregnancy and loss of freedom.

If you or someone you know is at risk, a trusted adult, a teacher, the police Victim Friendly Unit or Childline Zimbabwe (116) can help.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Writing that roora means buying a wife"}
Bride wealth is described by those who practise it as a token of appreciation and a seal between families. Do not write that the woman is "sold".
:::

:::mistake{title="Presenting harmful practices as acceptable today"}
Practices such as kuzvarira, child marriage and inheriting a widow without her consent are harmful and illegal. Explain what they were and why they are now rejected.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing about 'all Africans'"}
Write "among the Shona" or "in many families". Customs differ between peoples and families.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Describe the marriage process"}
For a Paper 1 (a) *describe*, give the steps in order (courtship, go-between, introduction, bride wealth, welcome) with one point of meaning each. Use the Shona and Ndebele terms.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Discuss advantages and disadvantages"}
For *discuss polygamy* or *assess roora*, give reasons for and against, mention the law (section 78, age 18, consent) and finish with a short judgement.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- In IR, marriage joins families, continues the lineage and involves the ancestors.
- The process includes courtship, a go-between (munyai/umkhongi), bride wealth (roora/lobola), the advice of aunts and uncles and the welcome of the bride.
- Customary types include monogamy, polygamy, levirate, sororate and elopement.
- Child marriage, forced marriage, pledging girls and wife inheritance without consent are harmful and illegal; the minimum age to marry is 18.
- Bride wealth is described as appreciation and a seal between families; high demands are debated today.
:::
