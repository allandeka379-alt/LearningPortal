## Introduction

Look at the front page of almost any newspaper and you may find religion mentioned in two very different ways. One report speaks of violence "in the name of religion". Another shows an imam, a priest and a rabbi standing together at a memorial for the victims of a disaster. Religion is a powerful force in people's lives, because it deals with identity, values and what people hold most sacred. For that reason it can deepen divisions, and it can also bring people together. This lesson looks at both sides honestly. It does not rank religions or blame any group. The aim is to understand how religion can be involved in conflict, why religion is seldom the only cause, and how religions work for peace.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Religious intolerance"}
Refusing to respect people who hold different beliefs, or treating them unfairly because of their religion.
:::

:::definition{term="Extremism"}
Holding views so rigid and one-sided that a person is willing to harm others to impose them. Extremists represent a very small minority and are rejected by the great majority of believers in every religion.
:::

:::definition{term="Fundamentalism"}
A word with several meanings. It is used by some people for a strict and literal approach to scripture. Fundamentalism is not the same as extremism, because many strict believers are peaceful.
:::

:::definition{term="Interfaith dialogue"}
Respectful conversation and cooperation between people of different religions, to understand one another and to work for shared goals.
:::

:::keyfact
Examples (in outline only): the **Crusades** (from 1095, when Pope Urban II called for a military expedition to the Holy Land); the **Northern Ireland Troubles** (about 1968 to 1998; the Good Friday Agreement 1998); the persecution of **Jews in Europe**, ending in the **Holocaust** (1941-45, about six million Jews murdered by the Nazi regime). In Zimbabwe: the **Zimbabwe Heads of Christian Denominations** and interfaith councils.
:::

## How religion can contribute to conflict

| Factor | How it contributes | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Intolerance | Treating followers of another religion as inferior or as enemies | Often grows from ignorance and fear |
| Extremism | A small group uses violence to impose its views | Rejected by most believers |
| Competition for converts | Groups compete aggressively for members, or insult other faiths | Can cause resentment in families and schools |
| Disputes within a religion | Disagreements over leadership, doctrine, property or practice divide congregations | Occurs in every religion |
| Misuse of scripture | Texts are quoted out of context to justify hatred or violence | Leaders should teach the whole message |
| Persecution | A group is discriminated against or attacked because of its faith | Violates freedom of religion |

:::example{title="The Crusades in outline"}
In 1095 Pope Urban II called Christians in western Europe to a military expedition to take Jerusalem and the Holy Land from Muslim rule. Over the next two centuries a series of expeditions were launched. Many people were killed, including Muslims, Jews and Eastern Christians. Historians stress that religion was only one of the motives: power, land, trade and adventure also played a part. Today many Christian leaders express regret for the violence of those centuries. The example shows religion being used to support war, and also that historians seek several causes.
:::

:::example{title="The Northern Ireland Troubles in outline"}
From about 1968 to 1998, violence in Northern Ireland killed thousands of people. The two main communities were commonly labelled Catholic and Protestant, but the disputes were mainly about national identity and political power, whether the region should belong to the United Kingdom or to a united Ireland, along with fairness in housing, jobs and rights. Religion marked the dividing line between the communities. In 1998 the Good Friday Agreement set up a peace settlement, and church leaders on both sides took part in calls for peace. The example shows how religious labels can become attached to a political conflict.
:::

The persecution of Jews in Europe over many centuries, ending in the Holocaust of 1941-45, in which the Nazi regime murdered about six million Jews, is a terrible example of religious and racial hatred. It is taught so that such crimes are never repeated.

## Religion is seldom the only cause

In most conflicts, religion is mixed with politics, ethnicity, land, resources and history. People may use a religious label to describe a fight that is also about power or fairness. A student should always ask: *what else is happening?* It is also fair to remember that millions of believers in every religion have worked for peace.

## How religions build peace

- **Teaching peace and respect.** Sermons, madrasah, Sunday schools, synagogue classes and Indigenous Religion teaching about unhu teach forgiveness and respect for neighbours.
- **Interfaith dialogue and cooperation.** Leaders and members of different religions meet, learn about each other's beliefs and work together on joint projects such as caring for orphans or cleaning a town.
- **Church bodies and interfaith councils.** In Zimbabwe the **Zimbabwe Heads of Christian Denominations** (ZHOCD) is an umbrella group of church bodies that speaks and works together on national issues. Local interfaith councils bring together Christians, Muslims, Jews, Hindus and followers of Indigenous Religion.
- **Mediation.** Religious leaders are trusted to mediate in disputes between families, communities and even political groups.
- **Education.** Learning about other religions, as you do in this subject, helps to remove fear and stereotypes.
- **Relief and reconciliation.** Religious organisations help refugees and victims regardless of faith and support healing after conflict.

:::example{title="An interfaith council acts quickly"}
Rumours spread in a fictional town in Mashonaland that a mob will attack a small mosque after a quarrel between two families. The Interfaith Council meets that evening: a pastor, an imam, a Jewish community member and an elder who follows Indigenous Religion. They visit both families, go on local radio to ask for calm, and stand together outside the mosque on Friday. The rumours fade. The example shows how respected leaders from different religions can prevent violence by speaking and acting together.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Blaming one religion"}
Every religion has been misused in conflict and every religion has produced peacemakers. Never say that one religion is violent by nature.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying that religion is the sole cause of a war"}
Most conflicts have political, economic and social causes as well. Say how religion was involved and what else was involved.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing fundamentalism with extremism"}
Many strict believers are peaceful. Extremism means willingness to harm others to impose views.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving no examples of peace-building"}
Name at least one example, such as interfaith dialogue, the ZHOCD, an interfaith council or mediation by religious leaders.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (a): explain how religion can cause conflict"}
Give about seven developed points: intolerance, extremism, competition for converts, disputes within religions, misuse of scripture, persecution and one or two outline examples. Say that religion is seldom the only cause.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (b): discuss or assess"}
For "religion does more to cause conflict than to build peace", give the causes of conflict, the evidence of peace-building, the point that religion works together with politics and economics, and a reasoned conclusion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Religion can contribute to conflict through intolerance, extremism, competition for converts, internal disputes, misuse of scripture and persecution.
- Examples in outline: the Crusades, the Northern Ireland Troubles and the persecution of Jews in Europe ending in the Holocaust.
- Religion is seldom the only cause of conflict; politics, power, land and fairness are usually involved.
- Religions build peace through teaching, interfaith dialogue, the ZHOCD and interfaith councils, mediation, education and relief.
- A balanced answer never ranks religions or blames a whole community.
:::
