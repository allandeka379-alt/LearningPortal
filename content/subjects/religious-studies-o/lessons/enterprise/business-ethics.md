## Introduction

At a market in Mutare a trader weighs maize for a customer. A scale that shows slightly less than the true weight would earn her a little more, but the customer would never know. A few streets away, a shop owner decides whether to pay a worker on time, and a minibus operator is asked for a bribe at a roadblock. Each choice is about business ethics: how to earn a living honestly, fairly and with care for others.

In this lesson you learn what the four religions in your syllabus teach about honest trade, fair wages and workers' rights, lending and interest, bribery, consumer protection, care for the environment in business and social responsibility. You should already know the teachings on work, wealth and giving, and on corruption and honesty in general. This lesson applies them to business. The religions are described as their followers understand them.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Business ethics"}
The moral principles that guide how a business or trader behaves towards customers, workers, competitors and the community.
:::

:::definition{term="Usury and riba"}
Usury is charging interest on a loan, especially excessive interest. In Islam, *riba* is interest or any unfair gain on a loan, and it is forbidden.
:::

:::definition{term="Social responsibility"}
The duty of a business to benefit society as well as its owners, for example through fair employment, care for the environment and support for the community.
:::

:::definition{term="Consumer protection"}
Protecting buyers from cheating, unsafe goods or false claims.
:::

:::keyfact
**Honest trade:** Leviticus 19:35-36 (just scales and weights); Proverbs 11:1; Qur'an 83:1-3. **Fair wages:** Leviticus 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:14-15; James 5:4. **Interest:** Exodus 22:25; Qur'an 2:275. **Environment in business:** Qur'an 7:56; Genesis 2:15.
:::

## Honest trade and consumer protection

All the religions condemn cheating in trade. **Leviticus 19:35-36** commands the use of honest scales, weights and measures, and Proverbs 11:1 says that false scales displease God. **Qur'an 83:1-3** pronounces a warning against those who demand full measure when they buy, but give less when they sell. The Qur'an also tells the people of Madyan, through the prophet Shu'ayb, not to diminish the measure (Qur'an 11:84-85). In **Indigenous Religion** cheating in exchange of cattle, goods or labour breaks unhu and shames the family, and elders expect fair dealing. Modern consumer protection extends these teachings: honest labelling, safe food, fair prices and refusing to sell goods that harm. A trader who respects these teachings puts the customer's well-being before extra profit.

## Fair wages and workers' rights

The Torah commands employers not to hold back the wages of a hired worker overnight (Leviticus 19:13) and not to oppress a poor hired worker (Deuteronomy 24:14-15). In the New Testament, James 5:4 warns employers that unpaid wages cry out against them. Muslim teaching tells employers to pay workers promptly and fairly and not to burden them beyond their strength. The Sabbath law in the Torah gives rest to servants and workers as well as owners (Exodus 20:10). In Zimbabwe, labour law sets standards for pay and conditions, and religious leaders support the idea that workers have the right to be paid on time, to work in safety and to be treated with dignity.

## Lending and interest

| Religion | Teaching on lending and interest |
|---|---|
| Judaism | The Torah forbids charging interest to a fellow Israelite in need (Exodus 22:25; Deuteronomy 23:19-20), and lending to the poor is a duty; later Jewish law developed arrangements that allow business loans |
| Christianity | The Bible condemns exploitation of the poor through loans (Exodus 22:25; Luke 6:34-35); the medieval church forbade usury, but most Christians today accept reasonable interest and condemn exploitative lending |
| Islam | Riba (interest) is forbidden, and God has permitted trade (Qur'an 2:275); Islamic finance avoids interest |
| Indigenous Religion | Lending among kin is based on trust and mutual help; exploiting a person in need is condemned |

**Islamic finance in outline.** Islamic banks and financial institutions try to follow the rules of the faith. They do not charge or pay interest. Instead they use arrangements such as **profit-and-loss sharing**, in which the bank and the entrepreneur share the results of a project, and **cost-plus sale**, in which the bank buys an item and sells it to the customer at an agreed price. They also avoid businesses connected with alcohol or gambling and avoid excessive uncertainty in contracts. Many non-Muslims also use these services.

## Bribery and corruption in business

Bribery is condemned by all the religions (Exodus 23:8; Qur'an 2:188). A business that wins contracts by paying bribes cheats honest competitors and harms the public. Religious teachers urge business people to refuse to give or take bribes, even if refusing costs them. Honest business builds trust and, in the long run, a strong reputation.

## Care for the environment and social responsibility

The Bible teaches that people were placed in the garden to work it and care for it (Genesis 2:15), and the Qur'an warns against spreading corruption on earth after it has been set in order (Qur'an 7:56). In Indigenous Religion, the land and the water are under the care of the ancestors and the spirits. A business that pollutes a river or cuts down forests without replacing them breaks these teachings. A responsible business also gives fair jobs, shares some profit with the community, and supports good causes. Muslims pay zakat on business wealth, Jews and Christians give tithes and charity, and many businesses support local clinics and schools.

:::example{title="The trader and the scale"}
Chipo runs a stall selling groundnuts in Gwanda. A friend suggests that she should set her scale slightly low to earn more. Chipo remembers Leviticus 19:35-36, which her church teaches, and her grandmother's saying that dishonest dealing shames the family. She keeps the scale correct and posts a notice of her prices. Her customers return because they trust her. The example shows honest trade based on teaching and unhu.
:::

:::example{title="An Islamic way to buy a van"}
Yusuf, a Muslim in Bulawayo, wants a loan to buy a delivery van. His uncle explains that an Islamic finance institution can buy the van and sell it to him for an agreed price, paid in instalments, so that no interest is charged. Yusuf also learns that the institution does not lend to businesses that sell alcohol. The example shows how Islamic teaching on riba affects practical finance.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Writing that all Christians and Jews forbid all interest"}
The Bible warns against exploiting the poor with loans, but most Jews and Christians today accept reasonable interest. Islam is the religion in which riba is forbidden as a rule.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing Islamic finance as free money"}
Islamic finance still involves profit. It avoids interest and uses sharing of profit and loss or sale at an agreed price.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing teachings without applying them"}
After a reference such as Leviticus 19:35-36, say what it means for a trader: use accurate scales, state honest prices, and do not cheat customers.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (a): describe or explain"}
For *describe religious teachings on honesty in business*, give at least three teachings with references (Leviticus 19:35-36, Qur'an 83:1-3, Leviticus 19:13) and say what each means for a trader or employer.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (b): discuss or assess"}
For *how far can a business be both honest and profitable?*, give reasons why honesty builds trust and lasting success, ways in which dishonesty may bring short-term gains, and reach a supported conclusion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- All four religions condemn cheating in trade; Leviticus 19:35-36 and Qur'an 83:1-3 teach honest measures.
- Workers must be paid fairly and promptly (Leviticus 19:13; James 5:4) and treated with dignity.
- Islam forbids riba (Qur'an 2:275) and Islamic finance uses profit sharing and agreed-price sales; most Jews and Christians accept reasonable interest but condemn exploitation.
- Bribery is condemned, and honest business builds trust.
- Businesses are urged to care for the environment and to benefit the community.
:::
