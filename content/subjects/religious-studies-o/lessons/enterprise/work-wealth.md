## Introduction

On a Saturday morning in a village near Masvingo, neighbours gather at a field for a work party, a nhimbe, to plough for a widow. After the work there is food and beer, and everyone goes home having helped and been helped. In Bulawayo a young man opens a small tuck shop and gives a share of his profit to his church. In Harare a preacher on the radio promises that those who give generously will be blessed with riches. These scenes show how closely work, money and faith are linked in Zimbabwean life, and how people disagree about how they fit together.

In this lesson you learn what the four religions in your syllabus teach about work, wealth and poverty, including the debate about the prosperity gospel. You should already know that the religions teach that people are responsible to God and to each other. The lesson describes the range of views and does not tell you what to believe.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Stewardship"}
The belief that wealth and the earth belong to God, and that people are trustees who must use them responsibly and share them.
:::

:::definition{term="Tithe"}
A tenth of one's income or produce given to a religious cause. It is practised in Judaism and by many Christian churches.
:::

:::definition{term="Zakat and tzedakah"}
Zakat is the obligatory giving of a fixed share of qualifying wealth in Islam (usually 2.5% of qualifying savings held for a lunar year). Tzedakah is Jewish charitable giving, regarded as justice and duty.
:::

:::definition{term="Prosperity gospel"}
The teaching, found in some churches, that faith, positive confession and generous giving lead to health and wealth.
:::

:::keyfact
**Work:** Genesis 2:15; Proverbs 6:6-11; 2 Thessalonians 3:10; Qur'an 62:10. **Wealth and greed:** 1 Timothy 6:10; Luke 12:15-21; Proverbs 14:31. **Giving:** Deuteronomy 14:22 and 15:11; Malachi 3:10; Qur'an 2:177 and the pillar of Zakat. **Indigenous Religion:** nhimbe (Shona), ilima (Ndebele).
:::

## The value of work

In **Indigenous Religion**, work is part of life in the community. The **nhimbe** (Shona) or **ilima** (Ndebele) is a gathering in which neighbours help one another with ploughing or harvesting, and the host provides food. Hard work is praised and laziness is condemned, because a family that does not work cannot feed itself or help others. Chiefs have traditionally kept a communal field, the zunde ramambo, whose harvest is stored for the needy.

In **Judaism and Christianity** the Bible presents work as part of God's plan: the first man was put in the garden to work it and take care of it (Genesis 2:15), and the Sabbath commandment speaks of six days of work (Exodus 20:9). Proverbs 6:6-11 tells the lazy person to learn from the ant. In 2 Thessalonians 3:10 Paul says that anyone who refuses to work should not eat, and Colossians 3:23 encourages Christians to work wholeheartedly as for God. This does not mean that those who cannot work, because of age or disability, should be neglected. Jews also keep the Sabbath as a day of rest from work.

In **Islam** work is a form of worship when it is honest. The Qur'an tells believers that after the Friday prayer they should spread through the land and seek God's bounty (Qur'an 62:10). Muslims are encouraged to earn a living by lawful (*halal*) means and to avoid begging when they can work.

## Wealth, sharing and giving

The religions do not condemn wealth in itself. They teach that wealth is a **trust** to be used responsibly and shared.

| Religion | Attitude to wealth | Giving and sharing |
|---|---|---|
| Indigenous Religion | Wealth in the family and community is valued; cattle and land are signs of well-being | Sharing food, work and harvest; zunde ramambo; help for kin |
| Judaism | Wealth is a blessing if honestly earned and used for good | Tzedakah as a duty; the tithe (Deuteronomy 14:22); leaving the edges of fields for the poor (Leviticus 19:9-10) |
| Christianity | Wealth is not wrong, but love of money is (1 Timothy 6:10) | Tithes and offerings (Malachi 3:10); caring for the poor (Acts 4:32-35) |
| Islam | Wealth is a trust from God | Zakat, a pillar of the faith; voluntary sadaqah (Qur'an 2:177) |

## Greed and its dangers

All four religions warn about greed. In 1 Timothy 6:10 Paul teaches that the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. In Luke 12:15-21 Jesus tells of a rich man who stored up goods for himself and was not rich towards God, and in Matthew 19:21-24 he warns of the difficulty for the rich who trust in wealth. The Qur'an warns against wastefulness (Qur'an 17:26-27) and against being distracted from God by rivalry in gaining more (Qur'an 102). In Indigenous Religion, greed and selfishness break unhu, and a person who hoards while neighbours are hungry is condemned. Hard work, then, is encouraged, but it should not become greed.

## Poverty

Poverty has many causes, including drought, unemployment, illness and unfair systems, and poor people should not be blamed. Deuteronomy 15:11 says that there will always be poor people in the land, and so you should open your hand to them. Proverbs 14:31 teaches that whoever oppresses the poor insults their maker. Zakat is a duty for Muslims to support the needy, and Christians follow Jesus' concern for the poor. Religious bodies run feeding schemes, schools, clinics and skills training, and many also urge governments and employers to act justly.

## The prosperity gospel

The **prosperity gospel** teaches that God wants believers to be healthy and wealthy, and that faith, positive speech and giving, sometimes called "seed", bring blessing. It is popular in some Pentecostal and charismatic churches in Zimbabwe and elsewhere. Supporters point to texts such as Malachi 3:10 and 3 John 2 and say that it encourages hope, hard work, discipline and entrepreneurship. Critics, including many Christians, say that it misreads the Bible, gives false hope to the poor, may blame those who are sick or poor for lacking faith, and can enrich leaders while members give beyond their means. Many churches take a middle position: they teach that God blesses, but that blessing is not guaranteed by giving and that suffering does not show a lack of faith. The debate is about how to understand Christian teaching, and a fair answer should present both views.

:::example{title="A nhimbe and a church offering"}
Rudo's family call a nhimbe to harvest their groundnuts. Neighbours work until midday, and the family feeds them and sends some groundnuts to a widow. On Sunday, Rudo gives part of her pocket money as an offering at church. In both cases she is practising sharing: the first is based on community values, the second on Christian teaching about giving. In an examination, you could use this example to show that work and giving are linked.
:::

:::example{title="Two views of the prosperity gospel"}
Tafadzwa's cousin says that her church teaches that giving a tenth of her income opens the way to blessing. Tafadzwa's friend says that his pastor warns that no one can buy God's favour. They look at 1 Timothy 6:10 and Malachi 3:10 together. They agree that Christians should give, but disagree about whether giving guarantees wealth. This shows the range of views within Christianity.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying that religions condemn wealth"}
The religions condemn greed and the love of money, not wealth itself. They teach honest earning and sharing.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing the prosperity gospel as the teaching of all Christians"}
It is one teaching in some churches. Many Christians reject it or accept only part of it. Present both views.
:::

:::mistake{title="Blaming poor people for being poor"}
Religious teaching stresses compassion and justice, and poverty has many causes. Do not suggest that poverty always results from laziness.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (a): describe or explain"}
For *explain what two religions teach about work and wealth*, name the religion, give a text or practice (Genesis 2:15, 2 Thessalonians 3:10, zakat, nhimbe), and say what it means for believers.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (b): discuss or assess"}
For *to what extent is the prosperity gospel consistent with Christian teaching?*, give supporters' arguments, critics' arguments, refer to texts, and reach a supported conclusion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Indigenous Religion values work and sharing through nhimbe/ilima and the zunde ramambo, and condemns laziness and selfishness.
- Judaism and Christianity present work as part of God's plan (Genesis 2:15; 2 Thessalonians 3:10); Islam treats honest work as worship.
- Wealth is a trust to be shared through tithes, tzedakah and zakat; greed is condemned (1 Timothy 6:10).
- Religions teach compassion for the poor and recognise that poverty has many causes.
- The prosperity gospel is debated: supporters stress hope and enterprise, critics stress its risks.
:::
