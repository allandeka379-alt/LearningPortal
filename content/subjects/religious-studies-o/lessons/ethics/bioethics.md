## Introduction

Modern medicine can do things that earlier generations could hardly imagine: it can keep a very sick person alive on a machine, help a couple who cannot have children, and replace a failing kidney with a donor's. These powers raise hard questions. A family in Harare may have to decide whether to approve an operation for a relative; a young woman may face a crisis pregnancy; a hospital may ask whether a patient will accept a blood transfusion. People of faith turn to their religion for guidance, and they do not always agree.

This lesson presents the **range of views** within and between the four religions. It does not tell you what to think; in examinations you must describe what religions teach and show understanding of the reasons. You should already know what the religions teach about God, creation and the value of life. Where views differ inside a religion, the lesson says so.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Sanctity of life"}
The belief that human life is sacred, a gift of God, and so has a special value that must be protected.
:::

:::definition{term="Quality of life"}
The idea that the kind of life a person has, including pain, independence and dignity, matters when making medical decisions.
:::

:::definition{term="Abortion"}
The ending of a pregnancy before the baby is able to live outside the womb.
:::

:::definition{term="Euthanasia"}
Ending the life of a person who is suffering from an incurable illness, in order to end the suffering. Active euthanasia means doing something to end life; passive euthanasia means withholding or withdrawing treatment.
:::

:::keyfact
**Scripture:** Exodus 20:13 (do not murder); Psalm 139:13-16 and Jeremiah 1:5 (God knows a person before birth); Qur'an 5:32 (saving one life is like saving all humanity); Qur'an 17:33 (do not take a life God has made sacred except by right). **Zimbabwe:** the Termination of Pregnancy Act allows abortion only in limited circumstances; euthanasia is not legal.
:::

## Sanctity and quality of life

All four religions teach that life is a **gift** and should be respected. Judaism holds that each person is made in the image of God (Genesis 1:27), and that to save a life is to save a world. Christianity teaches that God knew and formed people before birth (Psalm 139:13-16; Jeremiah 1:5). Islam teaches that God gives and takes life, and that saving one life is like saving all humanity (Qur'an 5:32). In Indigenous Religion life comes from Mwari and is renewed through the ancestors, and children are a blessing for the family. The religions agree on the value of life but differ on how to apply it in hard cases, and some stress the **quality of life**, such as relief of pain, when treatment decisions are made.

## Abortion

Zimbabwean law, the Termination of Pregnancy Act, allows an abortion only in limited circumstances, such as danger to the life or health of the woman, a pregnancy caused by rape or incest, or a serious abnormality in the unborn child; the law requires medical authorisation.

| Religion | Range of views on abortion |
|---|---|
| Indigenous Religion | Children are a blessing and life comes from Mwari and the ancestors; abortion is generally disapproved of |
| Judaism | The unborn child is protected, but is not treated as a full person until birth in classical teaching; most agree that abortion is permitted if the mother's life is in danger, and views differ on other cases |
| Christianity | The Catholic Church opposes abortion from conception; many Protestant and other churches also oppose it, with some allowing exceptions such as danger to the mother; a few support wider choice |
| Islam | Most scholars permit it only for serious reasons such as saving the mother's life, and mainly in early pregnancy; views differ on when the unborn child is considered alive in the full sense |

## Contraception and infertility

Contraception (family planning) is accepted by most Protestant churches, many Muslims (with the agreement of both spouses), and many Jews. The Catholic Church teaches that couples should use only natural methods of family planning. In Indigenous Religion large families are valued, though spacing of births has long been practised, and today views vary.

For couples who cannot have children, **IVF** (fertilisation outside the body) and **surrogacy** (another woman carries the child) are options in some countries. The Catholic Church opposes IVF; many other Christians accept it. Jewish teachers generally support help for infertile couples. Many Muslim scholars accept IVF within marriage with the husband's and wife's own cells, but oppose donor cells and surrogacy. In Indigenous Religion, children are so valued that infertility causes great pressure on couples, and extended families often help through adoption or care for relatives' children.

## Euthanasia

Euthanasia is not legal in Zimbabwe. All four religions teach that life should be preserved and that it is wrong to kill, so they generally oppose **active** euthanasia. Christians cite Exodus 20:13, Muslims cite Qur'an 4:29 and 17:33, and Jews stress that life has infinite value. Many teachers in these religions accept that a dying person may refuse treatment that only prolongs dying, and support **palliative care** (care that eases pain and gives comfort). In Indigenous Religion death comes at the time set by Mwari and the ancestors, and a family cares for a dying relative until the end.

## Organ donation and blood transfusion

Many Jews, Christians and Muslims accept **organ donation** as an act of love and a way of saving life, while some within each religion are cautious. In Indigenous Religion many people feel that the body should be buried whole, so organ donation is less common. A **blood transfusion** is accepted by most religions, but Jehovah's Witnesses, who understand passages such as Acts 15:28-29 to forbid blood, refuse transfusions. Doctors respect the wishes of adult patients, but where a child's life is in danger a court may order treatment, because children have a right to health care.

## The death penalty

Laws on the death penalty differ between countries and have changed over time in Zimbabwe. Judaism's Torah lists capital crimes, but rabbinic courts made executions very rare, and many Jews today oppose the penalty. The Catholic Church teaches that it is not acceptable today, while some other Christians accept it for the gravest crimes. Islamic law allows the death penalty for murder, but the Qur'an also encourages forgiveness (Qur'an 2:178). Many people in Indigenous Religion hold that a murderer causes lasting spiritual harm, which required cleansing by the family. Because the question is debated, an answer should give views and reasons.

:::example{title="Rachel and Thandeka discuss a hospital case"}
Rachel, who is Jewish, and Thandeka, a Catholic, hear that a woman in a hospital in Bulawayo has a pregnancy that threatens her life. Rachel explains that Jewish teaching allows an abortion to save the mother. Thandeka explains that the Catholic Church opposes direct abortion but also teaches that doctors must try to save both lives. Both agree that the case is very hard and that the family and doctors should be supported. The example shows religious views that differ and a shared respect for life.
:::

:::example{title="A decision about a transfusion"}
Farai's father must have an operation, and the doctor says he may need blood. Farai's family belong to a church that refuses transfusions. The doctor explains the risks, and the father, an adult, makes the decision. If it were Farai who needed blood and a doctor judged his life to be in danger, the hospital could ask a court to order treatment. This shows how an adult's religious choice is respected but a child's right to health care is protected.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Presenting one view as the view of a whole religion"}
Write "the Catholic Church teaches" or "many Muslim scholars accept", not "Christianity says" or "Islam says", where views differ.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving your own opinion instead of the religions' views"}
In an examination, explain what religions teach and why. Give your own judgement only where the question asks for it and support it with reasons.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing active and passive euthanasia"}
Active euthanasia means acting to end life; passive means withholding or withdrawing treatment. Many religions treat them differently.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (a): describe or explain"}
For *describe the views of two religions on abortion*, name the religions, give each position, and add a reason (such as the sanctity of life) and any exception (such as danger to the mother). Mention that views differ within religions.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (b): discuss or assess"}
For *how far do religions agree on life issues?*, show where they agree (life is sacred, active euthanasia is wrong) and where they differ (contraception, IVF, organ donation), then reach a supported conclusion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- All four religions value life as a gift, but differ in applying this to hard medical questions.
- Zimbabwean law allows abortion only in limited circumstances; religions range from strong opposition to permission where the mother's life is in danger.
- Contraception and IVF are accepted by many, but the Catholic Church teaches differently on both.
- All four religions generally oppose active euthanasia and support palliative care.
- Organ donation and blood transfusion are accepted by most, with exceptions; the death penalty is debated within religions.
:::
