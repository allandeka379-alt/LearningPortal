## Introduction

Young people grow up surrounded by messages about love, bodies and relationships: from friends, from social media, from songs and, in many homes, from religious teaching. At a church youth camp, a school guidance lesson or a family gathering at which an aunt gives advice to the girls, the question is the same: how should a young person behave responsibly? Religions have a great deal to say on this, and the teaching is meant to protect people and to guide them towards respect and responsibility.

This lesson is age-appropriate and non-graphic. You learn what sexuality means, what the four religions in your syllabus teach about sex and marriage, and the problems of teenage pregnancy and sexual abuse. You should already know the family and marriage customs from earlier lessons. The religions are described as their followers understand them, and where views differ inside a religion the lesson says so. **If you or someone you know is being harmed, speak to a trusted adult or call Childline Zimbabwe on 116.**

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Sexuality"}
The whole of a person's identity as a male or female being, including feelings, relationships and the body, not only sexual activity.
:::

:::definition{term="Chastity"}
Keeping sexual relations for marriage, and so refraining from sex before marriage. Religions use the word for the standard they teach to the unmarried.
:::

:::definition{term="Fidelity"}
Faithfulness to a husband or wife in marriage. Adultery, sex between a married person and someone else, breaks it.
:::

:::definition{term="Sexual abuse"}
Any sexual act forced on a person, or done to a child, without true consent. It is a crime and a serious wrong.
:::

:::keyfact
**Judaism and Christianity:** Genesis 2:24 (a man joins his wife); Exodus 20:14 (the commandment against adultery). **Christianity:** 1 Corinthians 6:18-20 (the body is the temple of the Holy Spirit); Hebrews 13:4 (marriage should be honoured). **Islam:** Qur'an 17:32 (do not approach *zina*, unlawful sex); Qur'an 30:21 (spouses find tranquillity, love and mercy). **Help:** Childline Zimbabwe 116; the police Victim Friendly Unit; a clinic; a trusted adult.
:::

## What religions teach about sex and marriage

Although the four religions differ in detail, they share a broad view: **sex is a gift connected with marriage and family, and it carries responsibility**.

**Indigenous Religion.** Traditional society valued chastity before marriage and faithfulness within it. Aunts, grandmothers, uncles and elders gave guidance to young people as they grew up, and marriage with the payment of roora (Shona) or lobola (Ndebele) joined two families. Children were seen as a blessing and as the link between the living and the ancestors. The community expected respect and discipline, and it condemned adultery. Some practices once described as tradition, such as testing a girl to prove virginity, or pledging a girl in marriage (kuzvarira), harm the girl and are today widely condemned and, in the case of child marriage, illegal.

**Judaism.** The Torah describes sex as part of marriage (Genesis 2:24), and Jewish teachers regard it as holy within marriage, not shameful. Marriage and children are valued (Genesis 1:28), and adultery is forbidden by the commandments (Exodus 20:14). Jewish communities differ: Orthodox Jews teach that sex belongs only in marriage, while Reform and some other Jews place more emphasis on personal responsibility and commitment.

**Christianity.** Most Christians teach that sex is a gift of God for marriage, and that unmarried people should practise chastity and married people fidelity. They refer to Genesis 2:24, Exodus 20:14, Hebrews 13:4 and 1 Corinthians 6:18-20, which teaches that the body is the temple of the Holy Spirit. Churches run youth groups and teaching programmes that encourage abstinence and respect. Christians differ over other matters, such as contraception, which is discussed in the lesson on life issues.

**Islam.** Islam teaches that marriage (*nikah*) is the lawful setting for sex, and describes spouses as a source of tranquillity, love and mercy (Qur'an 30:21). Sex outside marriage (*zina*) is forbidden, and the Qur'an advises Muslims not to approach it, which includes avoiding situations that lead to it (Qur'an 17:32). Islam stresses modesty in dress and behaviour for both men and women, and protection of family honour and of the rights of both partners.

## Responsible behaviour and the law

Responsible sexual behaviour means making decisions that protect one's health, education and future, and respecting other people's right to say no. Many parents and teachers advise waiting until marriage, and teenagers are advised to talk to a trusted adult, to avoid pressure from peers, and to avoid risky situations such as lonely places at night or accepting gifts in return for favours. Religions also teach respect for others: a person is never an object.

The law protects children. The Constitution of Zimbabwe 2013 sets the minimum age for marriage at 18 (section 78), and, after a Constitutional Court ruling in 2022, the age of consent to sex was raised to 18. In plain terms, **the law protects children under 18**. Sexual contact with a child is a crime even if the child appears to agree.

## Teenage pregnancy and sexual abuse

**Teenage pregnancy** has many causes, including poverty, peer pressure, lack of accurate information, child marriage, and abuse. The effects may include health risks for a young mother and her baby, interruption of education and stigma. The Education Amendment Act of 2020 says that girls may not be excluded from school because of pregnancy, so schools must allow them to continue. Religious communities teach that a young mother and her child should be treated with compassion, not condemnation, and that the father too carries responsibility.

**Sexual abuse and exploitation** are serious wrongs. They may happen to boys and girls, within the family, at school or in the community, and are never the child's fault. A person who has been abused should tell a trusted adult, call Childline on 116, report to the police Victim Friendly Unit or go to a clinic as soon as possible, and within three days if possible, because health workers can give protective treatment. All the religions teach that abusers must answer for their acts, and that leaders must not cover up abuse to protect a reputation.

## Diverse views on sexual orientation

The four religions teach, in their traditional forms, that marriage is between a man and a woman. Some religious groups elsewhere in the world, such as some Reform Jewish and some Christian communities, hold different views, while many others do not. Zimbabwean law does not recognise same-sex marriage. In all of the religions, teachers say that every person has dignity and that bullying, hatred and violence against any person are wrong. This lesson states these facts without asking you to hold any particular view.

:::example{title="An aunt's guidance and a youth camp"}
Chipo, aged 15, has been told by her aunt that a girl should respect herself and wait until marriage, and her church youth camp teaches the same message with Hebrews 13:4. Her school adds a health lesson on how to say no to pressure. Chipo sees that the advice from her family, her church and her school point in the same direction. In an examination answer, you would show how religious teaching and health education support each other.
:::

:::example{title="Yusuf and modesty"}
Yusuf, a Muslim student in Gweru, explains to his classmates that in Islam modesty applies to men and women. His father taught him that Qur'an 17:32 warns against approaching *zina*, and that this includes avoiding situations that could lead to it. The class learns that the teaching aims to protect people and the family.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Describing chastity as only a rule for girls"}
The religions teach chastity and fidelity for both men and women. Do not write as though only girls are responsible.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating a harmful custom as the teaching of a religion"}
Virginity testing and pledging girls (kuzvarira) harm girls and are condemned. Distinguish them from the religion's teaching about marriage and respect.
:::

:::mistake{title="Blaming the victim of abuse"}
Abuse is never the child's fault. Say that the abuser is responsible and that the child should tell a trusted adult.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (a): describe or explain"}
For *describe the teaching of two religions on sex and marriage*, choose two religions, name a source (such as Genesis 2:24 or Qur'an 30:21), paraphrase it and add what believers do.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2 (b): discuss or assess"}
For *how far do religious teachings help young people to behave responsibly?*, give ways they help (guidance, chastity, community support), limits (not everyone follows them, or harmful customs), and reach a supported conclusion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Sexuality includes identity, feelings and relationships; responsible behaviour protects health, education and others.
- The four religions teach that sex belongs within marriage and that married people should be faithful.
- Judaism and Christianity cite Genesis 2:24 and Exodus 20:14; Islam cites Qur'an 30:21 and 17:32.
- The law protects children under 18; sexual abuse is a crime and a child should tell a trusted adult or call Childline 116.
- Teenage pregnancy has social causes and effects; girls cannot be excluded from school because of pregnancy.
:::
