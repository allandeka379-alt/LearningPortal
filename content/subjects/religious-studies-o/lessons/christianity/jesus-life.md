## Introduction

On a Sunday morning in Gweru, Tendai sits with his grandmother in a crowded church. The preacher reads a story from the Gospel of Mark about a storm on a lake, and then reminds the congregation that Christians believe the one who calmed the storm also died on a cross and rose again. Christianity is the largest religion in Zimbabwe, and its whole story begins with one person, Jesus of Nazareth. To understand the Christian faith, a student must first know what the Gospels say about his life.

In this lesson you will learn the world in which Jesus lived and then follow his life from birth to ascension. Remember the approach of this subject: you describe what the Gospels record and what Christians believe, and you do not need to state your own faith. His teachings (the Sermon on the Mount and the parables) have their own lesson, **The Teachings of Jesus**. You should already know from earlier lessons what a scripture is and what is meant by a rite or a festival.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Gospel"}
A book of the New Testament that tells the good news about Jesus. There are four: **Matthew, Mark, Luke and John**. The word means "good news".
:::

:::definition{term="Messiah and Christ"}
**Messiah** is the Hebrew word for "anointed one", the promised deliverer whom many Jews expected. **Christ** is the Greek word with the same meaning. Christians believe that Jesus is the Messiah, and "Christ" became part of his name.
:::

:::definition{term="Incarnation"}
The Christian belief that God became human in Jesus. It is explained further in the lesson on Christian beliefs.
:::

:::keyfact
The four Gospels are **Matthew, Mark, Luke and John**. The birth narratives are in **Matthew 1-2** and **Luke 1-2**; the baptism is in **Mark 1:9-11**; the temptations in **Matthew 4:1-11**; the entry into Jerusalem in **Mark 11:1-11**; the Last Supper in **Mark 14:22-25**; the empty tomb in **Mark 16:1-8**.
:::

## Palestine in the time of Jesus

Jesus was born and lived in Palestine, the land of the Jews, which at that time was ruled by the Roman Empire. Rome appointed governors and client rulers, collected taxes, and kept soldiers in the land. Many Jews resented the occupation, especially the taxes and the presence of foreign troops. Jesus grew up in the village of **Nazareth** in the region of **Galilee**, in the north. Judea, with the capital **Jerusalem** and its Temple, lay to the south.

Jews of the time were not all alike. Four groups appear in or around the Gospel story.

| Group | What they were known for | Attitude to Rome |
|---|---|---|
| Pharisees | Strict observance of the Torah and its interpretation; teachers in the synagogues | Lived under Roman rule without armed revolt |
| Sadducees | Wealthy priestly families who ran the Temple; accepted the written Torah | Co-operated with Rome to keep order |
| Zealots | Wanted to drive out the Romans, if necessary by force | Armed resistance |
| Essenes | Lived in strict, withdrawn communities; often linked with the Dead Sea Scrolls | Withdrew from public life |

These groups disagreed with one another, and they respond to Jesus in different ways in the Gospels. Note that Jesus himself was Jewish, as were his first disciples. He taught in synagogues, kept the festivals and used the scriptures that Jews read. Christians believe that his teaching fulfils the promises of those scriptures.

## Birth and early life

Two Gospels tell of his birth, each with its own emphasis.

In **Luke 1-2** the angel Gabriel visits **Mary** in Nazareth and tells her that she will bear a son called Jesus (Luke 1:26-38). Because of a census ordered by the emperor Augustus, Mary and her husband **Joseph** travel to **Bethlehem**, where Jesus is born and laid in a manger. Shepherds, told by angels, come to see the baby (Luke 2:1-20). When the baby is brought to the Temple, **Simeon** and **Anna** recognise him as the promised deliverer.

In **Matthew 1-2** Joseph is told in a dream to accept Mary and name the child Jesus. Wise men (magi) from the east follow a star to find the child; King **Herod** is troubled and plans to harm him; Joseph is warned in a dream and the family flees to Egypt, returning to Nazareth after Herod's death.

Both accounts say that Jesus was born in Bethlehem and grew up in Nazareth. Christians believe that Mary's child was conceived through the Holy Spirit; this is part of the doctrine of the incarnation.

:::example{title="Two accounts, one story"}
Tafadzwa notices that his Grade 7 Christmas play has shepherds but no wise men, while his church's Epiphany service tells of wise men but not shepherds. This is not a mistake. Luke's Gospel tells of the shepherds and Matthew's Gospel tells of the wise men. A good examination answer names the Gospel for each detail.
:::

## Baptism, temptations and the first disciples

When Jesus was about thirty years old, he was baptised by **John the Baptist** in the river Jordan (**Mark 1:9-11**). The Gospels say that the Spirit came down like a dove and a voice from heaven declared him God's beloved Son. Jesus then spent forty days in the wilderness, where, according to **Matthew 4:1-11**, he faced three temptations: to turn stones into bread, to test God by throwing himself from the Temple, and to take power over all kingdoms by worshipping the devil. He refused each, answering with the scriptures. Christians see in this story how he stayed faithful to God.

He then began his public work in Galilee and called disciples. In **Mark 1:16-20** he calls the fishermen **Simon (Peter)** and **Andrew**, and then **James** and **John**, who leave their nets to follow him. He chose **twelve** disciples (the apostles), a number Christians often link with the twelve tribes of Israel. Among them were Matthew, a tax collector, and Judas Iscariot.

## The ministry in Galilee

For about three years, according to the Gospels, Jesus travelled through Galilee teaching, healing and calling people to repent because the **Kingdom of God** was near (Mark 1:14-15). The Gospels record many miracles. In outline, they fall into groups.

| Type of miracle | Example | Where to read |
|---|---|---|
| Healing | A paralysed man is let down through a roof and healed | Mark 2:1-12 |
| Power over nature | A storm on the lake is calmed | Mark 4:35-41 |
| Feeding | Five loaves and two fish feed a crowd | Mark 6:30-44 |
| Restoring life | The daughter of Jairus is raised | Mark 5:21-43 |

Christians understand the miracles as signs of God's power and compassion. The Gospels say that they showed that the Kingdom was at work and that Jesus cared for the sick, the hungry and people whom others avoided. Some Christians read the miracles literally, others emphasise their meaning; either way a student should report them as "the Gospels record" and "Christians believe".

## The last week in Jerusalem

The Gospels give more space to the last week of Jesus than to any other period, and Christians observe it in **Holy Week**.

1. **Entry into Jerusalem.** Jesus rode into the city on a donkey while crowds spread cloaks and branches before him (**Mark 11:1-11**). Christians remember this on Palm Sunday.
2. **The Last Supper.** On the night before he died, he shared a meal with his disciples, took bread and wine, and spoke of his body and of a new covenant (**Mark 14:22-25**). This is the origin of the Eucharist.
3. **Gethsemane and arrest.** He prayed in the garden of Gethsemane, where he was betrayed by **Judas Iscariot** and arrested by the Temple authorities.
4. **Trial.** He was questioned by the Jewish council (the Sanhedrin) and then brought before the Roman governor **Pontius Pilate**, who gave the order for crucifixion (Mark 14-15).
5. **Crucifixion.** He was crucified at a place called Golgotha, between two criminals, and died. He was buried in a tomb.

## Resurrection and ascension

All four Gospels say that on the third day women who came to the tomb found it empty. In **Mark 16:1-8** they are told that he has risen. Matthew 28, Luke 24 and John 20 add accounts of the risen Jesus meeting his disciples, for instance on the road to Emmaus (Luke 24). Christians believe that Jesus rose from the dead, and this belief is the heart of the Christian faith, celebrated every Easter and every Sunday.

Luke 24:50-53 and Acts 1:9-11 describe the **ascension**: forty days after the resurrection, according to Acts, Jesus was taken up into heaven in front of his disciples. They were told to wait in Jerusalem for the coming of the Holy Spirit (see the lesson on the early Church).

:::example{title="Reading a Gospel story in order"}
Nyasha is asked to describe the last week of Jesus. She writes: Jesus enters Jerusalem on a donkey (Mark 11); at the Last Supper he gives bread and wine to his disciples (Mark 14); Judas betrays him and he is arrested; he is tried before the Sanhedrin and Pilate; he is crucified; on the third day the women find the tomb empty (Mark 16). She gives each stage a reference and explains why Christians remember it, so her answer is both ordered and developed.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Stating Christian belief as fact"}
Write "Christians believe that Jesus rose from the dead" rather than "Jesus rose from the dead". The subject describes beliefs and does not rank them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the birth accounts"}
Shepherds and the census are in Luke; wise men, Herod and the flight to Egypt are in Matthew. Name the Gospel.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting that Jesus was Jewish"}
Jesus and his disciples were Jews. The Pharisees, Sadducees, Zealots and Essenes were Jewish groups, not Christians or Romans.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing instead of explaining"}
A list of miracles earns few marks. Say what each shows (power over nature, compassion for the sick) and how Christians understand it.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (a): describe or narrate"}
For *describe the events of the last week of Jesus' life* or *outline the birth of Jesus*, keep to the order of events, name the Gospel and place (Bethlehem, Jordan, Jerusalem, Golgotha), and give one developed sentence for each stage. About seven or eight developed points reach full marks.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (b): significance"}
For *explain the significance of the resurrection for Christians* link it to belief in life after death, the forgiveness of sins, the Christian weekly worship on Sunday and the celebration of Easter. State whose belief it is: "Christians believe that ..."
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Jesus lived in Palestine under Roman rule; Pharisees, Sadducees, Zealots and Essenes were Jewish groups with different attitudes to the law and to Rome.
- Matthew 1-2 and Luke 1-2 tell of his birth in Bethlehem and his childhood in Nazareth, each with its own details.
- He was baptised by John in the Jordan, faced temptations in the wilderness and called twelve disciples.
- In Galilee he taught about the Kingdom of God, healed the sick and performed miracles that Christians see as signs of God's power and compassion.
- The last week includes the entry into Jerusalem, the Last Supper, arrest, trial before Pilate and crucifixion.
- Christians believe that he rose on the third day and ascended; this belief is the centre of their faith.
:::
