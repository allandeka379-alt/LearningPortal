## Introduction

In Zimbabwe, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Christmas Day and Boxing Day are public holidays, so most students know when they happen. Yet there is more to these days than a holiday. In a Marondera church, a congregation lights candles in the weeks before Christmas; in a Harare cathedral, ashes are placed on foreheads at the beginning of Lent; and on an open field near Mutare, thousands of apostolic church members gather at Easter in white robes. Each date belongs to a pattern that shapes the Christian year.

In this lesson you will learn the main Christian festivals and sacred days, what each means to Christians, and how they are observed, especially in Zimbabwe. You should know the story of the life of Jesus and the early Church, because the festivals remember its main events. Describe observances as they are done by different churches, and avoid assuming that every Christian marks every day in the same way.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Christian year"}
The yearly cycle of seasons and festivals through which Christians remember the life of Jesus and the history of the Church. It is also called the liturgical year.
:::

:::definition{term="Lord's Day"}
Sunday, the day on which Christians believe Jesus rose from the dead and the usual day of weekly worship (see Acts 20:7 and Revelation 1:10).
:::

:::definition{term="Lent"}
The forty days (not counting Sundays) before Easter, a time of prayer, fasting and repentance, beginning on Ash Wednesday.
:::

:::keyfact
**Sunday** is the Lord's Day for most Christians. **Seventh-day Adventists** keep Saturday as the Sabbath. **Ascension** is forty days after Easter; **Pentecost** is fifty days after Easter. The date of Easter changes each year.
:::

## Sunday and the Sabbath

Most Christians worship on **Sunday**, the first day of the week, the day when, according to the Gospels, the tomb was found empty. They call it the **Lord's Day**. Sunday is therefore a weekly remembrance of the resurrection, and many Christians treat it as a day of rest as well as worship. **Seventh-day Adventists** and some other Christians keep the **Saturday** as the Sabbath, following the commandment in Exodus 20:8-11, which Jews also observe from Friday evening. Do not confuse the two: Sunday is the Christian day of worship for most, and Saturday is the Sabbath, observed by Jews and Seventh-day Adventists.

## The Christian year

| Season or day | Approximate time | Meaning | How observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Advent | Four Sundays before Christmas | Preparation for the birth of Jesus and for his return | Advent wreaths, candles, hymns of waiting |
| Christmas | 25 December | Birth of Jesus (Luke 2) | Church services, carols, nativity plays, family meals |
| Epiphany | 6 January | The visit of the wise men and the showing of Christ to the nations (Matthew 2) | Services, in some churches blessing of water |
| Lent | From Ash Wednesday for forty days | Preparation for Easter; repentance | Fasting, prayer, giving to the poor |
| Palm Sunday | Sunday before Easter | The entry into Jerusalem (Mark 11:1-11) | Processions with palm branches |
| Maundy Thursday | Thursday before Easter | The Last Supper | Holy Communion, foot washing in some churches |
| Good Friday | Friday before Easter | The crucifixion | Solemn services, fasting, the way of the cross |
| Easter | Sunday after Good Friday (date changes) | The resurrection | Joyful services, often at dawn |
| Ascension | Forty days after Easter | Jesus taken into heaven (Acts 1:9-11) | Services on a Thursday or Sunday |
| Pentecost | Fifty days after Easter | The coming of the Holy Spirit (Acts 2) | Services, prayer for the Spirit |

Easter is the greatest festival because it celebrates the resurrection. In the Western churches the date of Easter is calculated from the spring full moon, so it falls on a different Sunday each year, and Orthodox churches often follow a different calendar and therefore celebrate Easter on another date. Lent is observed most strictly in Catholic, Orthodox and Anglican churches, though many other Christians also fast and pray in this season.

## Christmas

Christmas remembers the birth of Jesus, told in Luke 2 and Matthew 2. Christians believe it celebrates the incarnation, God becoming human. The date of 25 December was chosen by the Church in the early centuries; the Bible does not give the date of Jesus' birth, so you should not write that he was born on that day. In Zimbabwe many people travel to the family's rural home, attend a special service, and share a meal. Some Orthodox churches observe Christmas in January.

:::example{title="Christmas in a Zimbabwean family"}
Nyasha's family travels to her grandmother's home near Gweru. On Christmas morning they attend a service where the children act the nativity story, singing carols in Shona and English. Afterwards the extended family shares a meal and visits neighbours. The day combines the Christian message of God's gift in Jesus with the Zimbabwean value of gathering the family. A good answer names both what Christians remember and what people do.
:::

## Holy Week and Easter

**Holy Week** begins on Palm Sunday with the entry into Jerusalem. On **Maundy Thursday** Christians remember the Last Supper. On **Good Friday** they remember the crucifixion in sombre services, often reading the account of the trial and death of Jesus; in some churches the cross is venerated or the "stations of the cross" are followed. On **Holy Saturday**, many churches wait in silence, and **Easter Sunday** brings services that proclaim the resurrection, often starting before dawn. Easter is the central festival for Christians, because their faith rests on the belief that Jesus rose from the dead.

In Zimbabwe many churches hold special gatherings at Easter, including large open-air services. Members of apostolic churches in particular travel to gather for several days of prayer, preaching and singing. Others take part in Easter conventions and camps organised by their denominations.

## Ascension and Pentecost

**Ascension** remembers the moment when, according to Acts 1:9-11, Jesus was taken up into heaven forty days after the resurrection; Christians believe he reigns with God and will return. **Pentecost** remembers the coming of the Holy Spirit upon the disciples fifty days after Easter (Acts 2). It is often called the birthday of the Church, and Pentecostal churches in particular stress its meaning.

:::example{title="Lent in a school and a church"}
In February a Catholic church in Harare holds an Ash Wednesday service. The priest marks a cross of ashes on each forehead, a sign of sorrow for sin. During Lent Sipho gives up sweets and puts the money aside for a children's home, and his class collects clothing. The example shows the three Lenten practices: prayer, fasting and giving to the poor.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing Sunday with the Sabbath"}
Most Christians worship on Sunday. The Sabbath is Saturday, kept by Jews and Seventh-day Adventists.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing Pentecost with Easter"}
Easter remembers the resurrection; Pentecost remembers the coming of the Holy Spirit, fifty days later.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Jesus was born on 25 December"}
The date is the day on which Christmas is celebrated, and the Bible does not give a date.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing festivals without meanings"}
Link each festival to the event it remembers and to how it is observed.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Most Christians worship on Sunday, the Lord's Day, remembering the resurrection; Seventh-day Adventists keep Saturday as the Sabbath.
- The Christian year includes Advent, Christmas, Epiphany, Lent, Holy Week, Easter, Ascension and Pentecost.
- Christmas remembers the birth of Jesus and Epiphany the visit of the wise men.
- Lent is forty days of prayer, fasting and giving before Easter; Holy Week remembers the last days of Jesus.
- Easter celebrates the resurrection and is the central Christian festival; Ascension is forty days and Pentecost fifty days after Easter.
- In Zimbabwe, churches mark these days with services, gatherings and family celebrations.
:::
