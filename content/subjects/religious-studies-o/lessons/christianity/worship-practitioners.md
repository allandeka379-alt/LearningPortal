## Introduction

On Sunday morning in Harare, Tafadzwa walks with his mother to a Catholic church where a priest in a green robe leads a Mass with set prayers and a choir. His friend Nyasha walks the other way to a Pentecostal church where a pastor in a suit preaches and the whole congregation sings with drums. Two kilometres away, members of an apostolic church in white robes gather in an open field, led by their elders, to pray under the sky. All three are acts of Christian worship, yet they look and sound very different.

In this lesson you will study how Christians worship, where they worship, who leads them, and what they wear and use as symbols. The sacraments (baptism, Eucharist and others) have their own lesson. You should know the main denominations before you start, because many differences in worship follow from them. As always, describe practices as they are and avoid ranking them.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Worship"}
Acts of honour, praise, thanks and prayer offered to God, alone or together.
:::

:::definition{term="Liturgy"}
A set order of worship with fixed prayers, readings and actions, as in Catholic, Orthodox and Anglican churches.
:::

:::definition{term="Charismatic worship"}
Worship that is spontaneous, with an emphasis on the gifts of the Holy Spirit, such as healing and speaking in tongues, as in Pentecostal and charismatic churches.
:::

:::definition{term="Vestments"}
Special clothing worn by clergy to lead worship, such as the alb, stole and chasuble.
:::

:::keyfact
Forms of worship: **liturgical** (fixed order, for example the Catholic Mass), **non-liturgical** (more freedom, as in many Protestant churches), and **charismatic** (spontaneous, led by the Spirit). Leaders: **pope, bishop, priest, pastor, deacon, elder, evangelist, prophet**.
:::

## Forms of worship

| Form | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Liturgical | Set prayers and readings, a fixed order, vestments, often a lectionary and formal music | Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican |
| Non-liturgical | A simpler order with more freedom; emphasis on Bible reading and preaching | Many Methodist, Baptist and Brethren churches |
| Charismatic | Spontaneous prayer, long periods of singing, healing and prophecy | Pentecostal churches |
| African Independent | Song, dance and drumming, prophecy, healing, open-air services | Apostolic churches |

Most worship has certain elements in common: hymns or choruses, Bible readings, a sermon, prayers (of praise, thanks, confession and petition), an offering and a blessing. Christians gather on Sunday as the day of resurrection; some, such as Seventh-day Adventists, worship on Saturday.

## A typical Sunday service

The pattern differs by church, but a general order is:

1. **Gathering:** a call to worship and an opening hymn.
2. **Prayer:** praise, confession of sin and thanks.
3. **The word:** readings from the Bible, followed by a **sermon** that explains the passage.
4. **Response:** hymns, a creed in some churches, and prayers for others.
5. **Offering:** members give money or gifts for the work of the church and for the poor.
6. **Sacrament:** in many churches the Eucharist or Holy Communion.
7. **Blessing and sending out.**

:::example{title="Comparing two Sunday services"}
In a Catholic church in Bulawayo the Mass follows a fixed order. The priest greets the people, the Bible is read, the priest preaches, the bread and wine are shared and the people are blessed. At a Pentecostal service in the same city the order is freer: a long time of singing, testimonies, a lengthy sermon, and prayer for the sick. Both worship God through song, scripture, prayer and teaching. A good comparison names the shared elements as well as the differences.
:::

## Places of worship

Christians believe that God can be worshipped anywhere, but they usually gather in special places.

- **Church buildings:** Catholic and Anglican churches usually have an altar or holy table, a lectern for readings, a pulpit for preaching, seats or pews, a font for baptism and sometimes stained-glass windows. Many are built in the shape of a cross. Methodist and Baptist churches are plainer, with a pulpit or platform at the front and often a baptismal pool in Baptist churches.
- **Halls, schools, tents and homes:** many Pentecostal and new churches begin in rented halls, tents and homes. House churches are common where there are few buildings.
- **Open-air sites:** apostolic churches often worship on hills, in the bush or in fields, because they feel closer to God in the open air. Some sites are used regularly for large gatherings, such as at Easter.
- **Holy places:** pilgrims may visit places such as Jerusalem or Bethlehem, or church centres, for worship.

## Leaders and practitioners

| Leader | Church | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Pope | Catholic | Bishop of Rome and head of the Catholic Church worldwide |
| Bishop | Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican and others | Leads a diocese; ordains priests and confirms |
| Priest | Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican | Leads worship, celebrates the Eucharist, hears confession, serves a parish |
| Pastor or minister | Protestant, Pentecostal | Leads a congregation, preaches and cares for members |
| Deacon | Many churches | Assists in worship and serves the poor and sick |
| Elder | Many Protestant churches | Helps to lead and guide the congregation |
| Evangelist | Many churches | Preaches the Gospel to people outside the church |
| Prophet | African Independent churches | Receives and speaks messages believed to come from God; heals and guides |

Titles and duties differ between churches. In some, women may be ordained as pastors or bishops; in others, only men may be priests. In many churches women also lead through women's groups, as teachers and as elders.

## Attire and symbols

- **Cross and crucifix:** the cross is the main Christian symbol, reminding believers of the death and resurrection of Jesus. A **crucifix** shows the figure of Jesus on the cross and is common in Catholic and Orthodox churches; many Protestant churches use an empty cross to stress the resurrection.
- **Vestments:** clergy in liturgical churches wear robes such as the alb (a white robe), stole (a scarf-like band) and chasuble (an outer robe), with colours that change with the Christian season.
- **White garments of apostolic churches:** members wear white robes, often with a belt or sash. White stands for purity and for the clothing of heaven, and in many of these churches members also cover their heads or remove their shoes at holy places. Dress differs between churches and between women and men.
- **The dove:** a symbol of the Holy Spirit, based on the account of Jesus' baptism (Mark 1:10).
- **The fish:** an early Christian symbol. The Greek word for fish, *ichthys*, was used as a code for "Jesus Christ, Son of God, Saviour".
- **Candles, water and bread and wine:** used in baptism, Easter and the Eucharist.

:::example{title="An open-air apostolic service"}
On a Saturday near Mutare, men and women in white robes sit in groups on an open hillside. Elders lead the singing and clap in rhythm, a prophet preaches from the Bible, and members testify. Many have removed their shoes as a sign of respect for the place. The service shows how worship, place and dress work together: the open air, the white clothes and the song express devotion. Questions on this topic ask you to describe what happens and to explain why, in the believers' view.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Describing all Christian worship as the same"}
Liturgical, non-liturgical, charismatic and African Independent worship differ. Name the type.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing a cross with a crucifix"}
A cross is empty; a crucifix has the figure of Jesus on it.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing leaders without roles"}
Do not just list pope, bishop, priest and pastor. Give a short role for each and name the church.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling AIC worship less serious"}
Describe the worship of apostolic churches respectfully and explain the meaning of its practices.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (a): describe"}
For *describe a Christian service* or *describe the leaders of the Church*, use the order of a service or a table of leaders. Include at least one example of a specific church, such as a Catholic Mass or an apostolic service.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (b): explain"}
For *explain the importance of symbols or worship to Christians* give reasons such as expressing faith, reminding believers of the Gospel, uniting the congregation, and supporting daily life. Add an example, such as the cross or the white robes.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Christian worship can be liturgical (fixed order), non-liturgical (freer) or charismatic (spontaneous), and African Independent churches add song, dance and prophecy.
- Most services include hymns, Bible readings, a sermon, prayers and an offering.
- Christians worship in church buildings, halls, homes and open-air sites.
- Leaders include the pope, bishops, priests, pastors, deacons, elders, evangelists and, in African Independent churches, prophets.
- Symbols and attire include the cross and crucifix, vestments, white garments, the dove and the fish.
:::
