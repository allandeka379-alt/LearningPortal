## Introduction

In many Zimbabwean churches the congregation stands on Sunday and recites a short statement together, beginning with belief in God the Father as creator of heaven and earth. The statement is the Apostles' Creed, and in a few lines it holds the main beliefs of Christians across the world. A creed answers the question "What do Christians believe?" in a form that can be learned by heart.

In this lesson you will learn the main Christian beliefs, how they appear in the creeds, and where Christian groups differ. Remember that this subject describes what Christians believe, and does not ask you to accept it. Use words such as "Christians believe that" and "according to Catholic teaching" so that it is clear whose view is being given. You should know the life of Jesus and the early Church before this lesson, and you will see these beliefs again in worship and sacraments.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Trinity"}
The Christian belief that there is one God in three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
:::

:::definition{term="Incarnation"}
The belief that God became a human being in Jesus Christ. The word comes from the Latin for "in flesh".
:::

:::definition{term="Salvation"}
Being saved from sin and its consequences, and restored to a right relationship with God.
:::

:::definition{term="Grace"}
God's free and undeserved love and favour. Christians believe that salvation is a gift of grace, not something earned.
:::

:::definition{term="Atonement"}
The belief that Jesus' death on the cross put right the broken relationship between God and humanity. The word means being "at one" with God.
:::

:::keyfact
Two creeds: the **Apostles' Creed** (a short statement used in baptism and worship) and the **Nicene Creed** (from the Councils of Nicaea, 325, and Constantinople, 381). Catholic belief includes **purgatory**; most Protestants do not accept it.
:::

## God the Creator

Christians believe that there is one God, who created the universe and everything in it, and who cares for it. The first chapters of Genesis (Genesis 1-2) tell of God creating the world and humankind in God's image. Christians understand this as meaning that human life has value, and that people have a duty to look after the earth. Christians differ in how they read the creation stories: some read them literally, others as teaching about God's purpose and not as a scientific account.

## The Trinity and the incarnation

Christians believe in one God who is known as three persons: **Father, Son and Holy Spirit**. This is not three gods; it is one God, understood as a relationship of love. The Father is creator, the Son is Jesus Christ, who became human, and the Holy Spirit is the presence of God at work in believers and in the Church. The word "Trinity" does not appear in the Bible; Christians draw the teaching from passages such as Matthew 28:19, where baptism is in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

The **incarnation** is the belief that in Jesus God became human, as in the opening of John's Gospel (John 1:14). Christians believe that Jesus is fully God and fully human. This is why they give his life, death and resurrection such weight.

## Sin and salvation

Christians believe that people turn away from God and from one another; this is called **sin**. The story of Adam and Eve in Genesis 3 is often used to describe how sin entered human life, although Christians interpret it differently. Sin damages relationships with God and with other people. Christians believe that God offers **salvation** through Jesus. Romans 3:23-24 says that all have sinned and are made right through grace as a gift. Most Christians teach that salvation comes through **faith** (trust in God) and God's **grace**, and that faith shows itself in good works. Catholics place stress on faith together with works and the sacraments, while many Protestants stress faith alone. Both agree that good deeds matter.

## Atonement and resurrection

Christians believe that Jesus' death on the cross was a sacrifice that put right the relationship between God and humanity. Different Christians explain this in different ways: as a payment for sin, as a victory over evil, or as a demonstration of God's love. His **resurrection**, which Christians believe took place on the third day, is seen as God's victory over death. Paul wrote that if Christ has not been raised, then faith is empty (1 Corinthians 15). For Christians the resurrection gives hope of life after death and shows that Jesus' work was approved by God.

## The Holy Spirit and the Church

Christians believe that the Holy Spirit came at Pentecost (Acts 2) and continues to guide and strengthen believers. Paul describes fruits of the Spirit in Galatians 5:22-23, such as love, joy and peace. The **Church** is the community of believers. Christians use several images for it: the body of Christ, the people of God and the family of God. The Church worships, teaches and serves, and Christians believe it is the Spirit's work to unite them.

## Life after death and the second coming

Christians believe in life after death. The Apostles' Creed speaks of "the resurrection of the body and the life everlasting". Christians usually speak of **heaven** (life with God), **hell** (separation from God) and a final **judgement**, when, according to Matthew 25, people are judged by how they have lived. Catholics also teach **purgatory**, a state of purification for those who die in God's friendship but are not yet fully ready for heaven. Most Protestants do not accept the teaching on purgatory. Christians differ on how to understand hell, with some taking descriptions literally and others emphasising separation from God.

The **second coming** is the belief that Christ will return at the end of time to judge the living and the dead and to bring God's Kingdom in its fullness (Acts 1:11; 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17). Christians differ on when and how this will happen, and many say that no one knows the time.

## The creeds

A creed is a statement of belief. The **Apostles' Creed** is short, goes back to early baptismal statements, and is used by Catholic, Anglican and many Protestant churches. The **Nicene Creed** developed from the Council of Nicaea (325) and was finished at Constantinople (381). It says more about Jesus, the Son, being "of one being with the Father", and is used by Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican and many other churches. Some churches, such as some Baptist and Pentecostal churches, do not use creeds in worship, though they hold similar beliefs.

:::example{title="Reading a line of the creed"}
When the congregation reaches the lines about the Holy Spirit and the holy catholic Church, Sipho, a Methodist, understands "catholic" as meaning "universal", the whole Church throughout the world. A Roman Catholic neighbour understands the phrase in the same sense but also thinks of her own church. A student should know that the word in the creed is lower-case and means universal.
:::

:::example{title="How denominations differ on one belief"}
Question: *How do Catholics and Protestants differ on life after death?* Catholics teach purgatory, a stage of purification before heaven, and pray for the dead. Most Protestants do not accept purgatory and teach that those who trust in Christ go to be with God. Both believe in heaven, judgement and the resurrection. A good answer names the shared belief and the difference.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Christians believe in three gods"}
The Trinity is one God in three persons. Do not describe it as three gods.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing incarnation with resurrection"}
The incarnation is God becoming human in Jesus. The resurrection is Jesus rising from the dead.
:::

:::mistake{title="Presenting beliefs as facts"}
Write "Christians believe ..." and "Catholics teach ...". Do not write that something is true.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring differences between denominations"}
Purgatory is a Catholic teaching; salvation by faith alone is stressed by many Protestants. Name the group.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (a): describe or outline"}
For *outline the main beliefs of Christians* choose five or six beliefs from the table, give a clear statement of each, and add a reference or an example. Avoid long lists without explanation.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (b): explain"}
For *explain the importance of the resurrection for Christians* or *of the Trinity*, say what Christians believe, how it shapes worship (Easter, baptism in the name of the Trinity) and how it affects daily life.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Christians believe in one God, the creator, who is known as three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit (the Trinity).
- The incarnation is the belief that God became human in Jesus; the atonement and resurrection are central to salvation.
- Sin separates people from God; salvation is a gift of grace received through faith, shown in good works.
- The Holy Spirit empowers believers, and the Church is the community of believers.
- Christians believe in judgement, heaven and hell, and the second coming; Catholics also teach purgatory.
- The Apostles' and Nicene creeds summarise the faith, and denominations differ on some beliefs.
:::
