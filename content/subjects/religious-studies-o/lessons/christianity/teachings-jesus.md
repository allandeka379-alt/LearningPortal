## Introduction

At a school assembly in Chinhoyi, a Form 2 pupil reads the story of a traveller who was beaten by robbers and helped by a stranger from a people his neighbours despised. When the assembly ends, Chipo hears two students arguing about the story: one says it is about being kind, the other says it is about who counts as your neighbour. Both are close to what Christians find in it. Jesus taught mostly in short sayings and stories, and his teaching still shapes how many Christians in Zimbabwe treat family, neighbours, enemies and the poor.

In this lesson you will study what the Gospels record Jesus as teaching: the Kingdom of God, the Sermon on the Mount, four parables, the commandment to love, forgiveness and care for the outcast. You should already know the outline of the life of Jesus. As always, you report what the Gospels say and what Christians understand by it, and you cite and paraphrase rather than quote.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Kingdom of God"}
The rule of God in the world and in people's lives. Jesus began his work by announcing that the Kingdom was near (Mark 1:14-15). Christians understand it as already beginning and still to come in its fullness.
:::

:::definition{term="Parable"}
A short story from everyday life that carries a spiritual or moral meaning. Jesus often taught in parables, using farming, family and travel.
:::

:::definition{term="Beatitudes"}
The opening blessings of the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5:3-12), each saying that a certain kind of person is "blessed" or happy in God's sight.
:::

:::definition{term="Agape"}
The Greek word for self-giving love, used in the New Testament for love of God and love of neighbour.
:::

:::keyfact
The **Sermon on the Mount** is in **Matthew 5-7**. The **Beatitudes** are in **Matthew 5:3-12**, the **Lord's Prayer** in **Matthew 6:9-13**, the **Golden Rule** in **Matthew 7:12**. The **greatest commandment** is in **Mark 12:28-34**. The parables of the **Good Samaritan** (Luke 10:25-37), **Prodigal Son** (Luke 15:11-32), **Lost Sheep** (Luke 15:1-7) and **Sower** (Mark 4:1-20).
:::

## The Kingdom of God

Jesus began his public work by saying that the time was fulfilled, the Kingdom of God was near, and people should turn back to God and believe the good news (Mark 1:14-15). The Kingdom is not a country with borders. It is God's rule, seen where people live by God's will: where the sick are healed, the poor are cared for and sins are forgiven. Many of the parables begin with "The Kingdom of God is like ..." and compare it to a seed, to treasure or to a feast.

## The Sermon on the Mount

According to Matthew, Jesus taught a large crowd from a hillside. The sermon fills Matthew 5-7.

| Section | Reference | Main teaching |
|---|---|---|
| Beatitudes | Matthew 5:3-12 | Blessed are the poor in spirit, those who mourn, the gentle, those who hunger for justice, the merciful, the pure in heart, the peacemakers and those persecuted for doing right |
| Salt and light | Matthew 5:13-16 | Followers are to be an influence for good, so that others see their good deeds |
| The law fulfilled | Matthew 5:17-20 | Jesus did not come to abolish the law and the prophets but to fulfil them |
| Love for enemies | Matthew 5:43-48 | Love enemies and pray for those who persecute you |
| Prayer | Matthew 6:5-15 | Pray sincerely; the Lord's Prayer is the model |
| Treasure and trust | Matthew 6:19-34 | Do not put trust in wealth; do not be anxious, because God cares |
| Judging and the Golden Rule | Matthew 7:1-12 | Do not judge hypocritically; treat others as you would want them to treat you |

The **Lord's Prayer** (Matthew 6:9-13) addresses God as Father, asks that God's name be honoured and God's Kingdom come, and asks for daily bread, forgiveness and protection from evil. Christians of almost every denomination pray it in worship. The **Golden Rule** (Matthew 7:12) sums up the teaching on how to treat people: do for others what you would want them to do for you.

:::example{title="The Beatitudes in everyday life"}
Sipho's class is raising money to help a classmate whose father has lost his job. A Christian student might say that this reflects the blessing on the merciful, those who show care for people in need. Another might point to the blessing on the peacemakers when two friends are brought together after a quarrel. Notice that the Beatitudes are not rules but descriptions of the kind of people Jesus calls happy in God's sight, and the examples must show the quality, not just name it.
:::

## Four parables and their meanings

| Parable | Reference | Story in brief | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Good Samaritan | Luke 10:25-37 | A man is robbed and left half dead; a priest and a Levite pass by; a Samaritan, from a despised group, helps and pays for his care | Love of neighbour crosses boundaries of race and religion; a neighbour is anyone in need |
| The Prodigal Son | Luke 15:11-32 | A younger son takes his inheritance, wastes it, returns in shame, and is welcomed by his father; the elder brother resents it | God's forgiveness is generous, and those who stay faithful must not be bitter |
| The Lost Sheep | Luke 15:1-7 | A shepherd leaves ninety-nine sheep to find one that is lost, then celebrates | God values each person and rejoices when a sinner returns |
| The Sower | Mark 4:1-20 | A farmer scatters seed: some falls on the path, rocky ground, thorns and good soil | People respond to God's word in different ways; only some bear fruit |

Notice that each story has a setting familiar to a Zimbabwean listener: a road, a family, a flock, a field. When you explain a parable, tell the story briefly and then give the meaning.

## Love of God and neighbour

In **Mark 12:28-34** a teacher of the law asks Jesus which commandment is the first of all. Jesus answers by joining two commands from the Jewish scriptures: love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, mind and strength (Deuteronomy 6:4-5), and love your neighbour as yourself (Leviticus 19:18). He says that there is no commandment greater than these. Christians therefore treat love of God and love of neighbour as the centre of Christian morality, and they use the Good Samaritan to show how wide the word "neighbour" is.

## Forgiveness and care for the outcast

Jesus taught that forgiveness should be given repeatedly. In **Matthew 18:21-35** he tells Peter to forgive again and again and tells a story of a servant who was forgiven a large debt but refused to forgive a small one. He also welcomed people whom others avoided. In **Luke 19:1-10** he stays at the house of **Zacchaeus**, a chief tax collector disliked in his town, and Zacchaeus promises to give half his possessions to the poor and to repay those he cheated. In **Matthew 25:31-46** the story of the sheep and the goats says that feeding the hungry, welcoming strangers and visiting the sick is service to Christ himself.

## Relevance today

Christians in Zimbabwe draw on these teachings in many ways. Church-run clinics, schools and feeding programmes are often defended with the parable of the Good Samaritan and Matthew 25. Many Christian families stress forgiveness and reconciliation after disputes. Others point to the Golden Rule as a guide for how pupils should treat one another at school. Christians also differ in emphasis: some highlight personal prayer and faith, others stress social action, and a good answer allows for both.

:::example{title="Explaining a parable in an examination"}
Question: *Explain the meaning of the parable of the Prodigal Son.* Tatenda's answer: "A younger son asked for his share of the property, wasted it and returned in shame. His father ran to welcome him and held a feast (Luke 15:11-32). Christians see the father as God, who forgives those who return to him. The elder brother's anger warns believers not to be bitter when others are forgiven." The answer tells the story briefly, gives the meaning and names the lesson.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Retelling the story without the meaning"}
The question usually asks for the meaning or lesson. Spend one sentence on the story and the rest on what Christians learn from it.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the Good Samaritan with the Prodigal Son"}
The Good Samaritan (Luke 10) is about who our neighbour is. The Prodigal Son (Luke 15) is about forgiveness. Do not mix their details.
:::

:::mistake{title="Quoting at length or inventing sayings"}
Cite the reference and paraphrase. Do not invent words for Jesus and do not give a verse reference you are unsure of.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying that Jesus abolished the law"}
In Matthew 5:17 he says that he came to fulfil the law and the prophets, not to abolish them.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (a): describe or outline"}
For *describe the parable of the Good Samaritan* give the story in order (the robbery, the priest and Levite, the Samaritan's help), cite Luke 10:25-37, and name the characters. Do not stop at the story if the question asks about the lesson.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (b): relevance today"}
For *how is the teaching of Jesus on love relevant today?* give two or three developed examples from Zimbabwe, such as helping strangers, forgiving neighbours or caring for orphans, and link each to a reference.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Jesus announced the Kingdom of God, the rule of God that Christians see in lives lived by God's will (Mark 1:14-15).
- The Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7) includes the Beatitudes, teaching on the law, the Lord's Prayer and the Golden Rule.
- The parables of the Good Samaritan, Prodigal Son, Lost Sheep and Sower teach neighbourly love, forgiveness, God's care for each person and different responses to God's word.
- The greatest commandment joins love of God and love of neighbour (Mark 12:28-34).
- Jesus taught forgiveness and welcomed outcasts such as Zacchaeus; Matthew 25 links care for the needy with service to Christ.
- Christians apply these teachings through worship, charity, reconciliation and everyday conduct.
:::
