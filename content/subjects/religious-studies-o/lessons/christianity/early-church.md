## Introduction

When Nyasha's church in Marondera celebrates Pentecost, the preacher reminds the congregation that Christianity began as a small group of Jewish followers of Jesus in one city, Jerusalem, and within three centuries had spread across the Roman Empire and beyond, including into Africa. This is a remarkable story, and it explains why Christianity is a world religion today. It also shows that Christianity reached Africa long before European missionaries arrived in Zimbabwe.

In this lesson you will learn how the Gospels and the Book of Acts describe the first Christians, the work of Peter and Paul, the debate about Gentile believers, the persecution of Christians, the change under Constantine and the Council of Nicaea, and the spread into North Africa and Ethiopia. You should already know the outline of the life, death and resurrection of Jesus. Later divisions between churches are in the lesson on denominations.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Apostle"}
A person "sent out" with a message. The twelve apostles were the disciples chosen by Jesus; **Paul** is also called an apostle because he understood himself as sent by the risen Jesus.
:::

:::definition{term="Pentecost"}
The festival fifty days after Passover and Easter, when, according to Acts 2, the Holy Spirit came upon the disciples in Jerusalem. Christians often call it the birthday of the Church.
:::

:::definition{term="Gentile"}
A person who is not Jewish. The early Church had to decide how Gentiles could join.
:::

:::definition{term="Martyr"}
A person who dies rather than give up their faith. The word comes from a Greek word meaning "witness".
:::

:::keyfact
**Pentecost**: Acts 2. **Paul's conversion**: Acts 9. **Council of Jerusalem**: Acts 15. **Edict of Milan**: 313 CE. **Council of Nicaea**: 325 CE. Early African Christianity: Alexandria, Carthage and the Ethiopian (Aksum) church.
:::

## Pentecost and the first Christians

After the ascension the disciples waited in Jerusalem. On the day of **Pentecost**, Acts 2 says, they were together when a sound like wind and what looked like tongues of fire appeared, and they were filled with the Holy Spirit. They began to speak in languages understood by visitors from many lands, and **Peter** preached that Jesus was the Messiah who had risen. About three thousand people were baptised that day. The first Christians shared their belongings, met for prayer, teaching, the breaking of bread and fellowship (Acts 2:42-47). Christians treat Pentecost as the beginning of the Church and the coming of the Holy Spirit.

Peter was the leader of the apostles in the early chapters of Acts. Together with John he healed a lame man (Acts 3) and was brought before the Jewish council for preaching. In **Acts 8:26-40** the evangelist **Philip** meets an Ethiopian official on the road to Gaza and baptises him, a story that Ethiopian Christians treasure.

## Paul: conversion and journeys

**Saul**, a Pharisee from Tarsus, first opposed the Christians and approved the killing of Stephen, the first martyr (Acts 7-8). In **Acts 9** he was travelling to Damascus to arrest believers when, as Acts tells it, a light from heaven struck him down and the risen Jesus spoke to him. After this experience he was baptised and became a leading apostle, known by his Roman name, **Paul**.

| Journey | Reference | Main facts |
|---|---|---|
| First | Acts 13-14 | Paul and Barnabas travel from Antioch to Cyprus and the towns of Galatia; many Gentiles believe |
| Second | Acts 15:36-18:22 | Paul and Silas travel through Asia Minor to Philippi, Athens and Corinth; the Gospel enters Europe |
| Third | Acts 18:23-21:17 | Paul spends a long time in Ephesus and strengthens the churches |
| To Rome | Acts 27-28 | Paul is arrested in Jerusalem and sent to Rome as a prisoner |

Paul wrote many letters (epistles) to the churches, and these form a large part of the New Testament. He taught that people are saved by faith in Jesus and not only by keeping the Jewish law.

## The Council of Jerusalem

As more Gentiles joined, a question arose: must a non-Jew first be circumcised and keep the Jewish law before becoming a Christian? **Acts 15** describes a meeting in Jerusalem attended by Peter, Paul, Barnabas and James. After discussion the apostles and elders decided that Gentile believers did not have to be circumcised, though they were asked to avoid certain practices, such as idol worship and immorality. The decision made it possible for Christianity to become a worldwide faith and not only a Jewish movement.

## Persecution

For about three centuries, Christians in the Roman Empire were sometimes persecuted. They would not worship the emperor or the Roman gods, which made them suspect. Persecution came in waves and varied by place. Under the emperor **Nero** Christians in Rome were blamed for the great fire of 64 CE. Persecution was severe under the emperor **Diocletian**, from 303 CE. Many Christians were martyred, but the Church continued to grow, and some writers said that the blood of martyrs was the seed of the Church.

## Constantine and Nicaea

In 313 CE the emperors **Constantine** and **Licinius** issued the **Edict of Milan**, which gave Christians freedom to worship and the return of confiscated property. Constantine later supported the Church. In 380 the emperor Theodosius made Christianity the official religion of the Empire.

A dispute arose about the nature of Christ. A priest from Alexandria named **Arius** taught that the Son was created by the Father and was not fully equal to him. The **Council of Nicaea** met in 325 CE, called by Constantine, and rejected the teaching of Arius. The council affirmed that the Son shares the same being as the Father. The creed that Christians recite, the **Nicene Creed**, grew out of this debate and was completed at a later council at Constantinople in 381.

## Early African Christianity

Africa was part of the early Church from the beginning. **Alexandria** in Egypt was a great centre of learning where teachers such as Clement and Origen worked; tradition links the founding of the Church there with the evangelist Mark. In North Africa, **Carthage** produced writers such as **Tertullian** and **Cyprian**, and **Augustine** of Hippo (354-430) became one of the most influential Christian thinkers. In the fourth century the kingdom of **Aksum** in Ethiopia adopted Christianity under King **Ezana**, helped by a Christian teacher named Frumentius. The Ethiopian Orthodox Church is one of the oldest churches in the world.

:::example{title="Why Acts 15 mattered"}
Imagine a Gentile woman in Corinth who wants to join the Christians but is told that she must first follow the Jewish law in full. The decision at Jerusalem answers her: she may join without circumcision, and the Church welcomes her. This shows how the council opened the way to people of every nation. In an exam, explain the problem, the decision and the result.
:::

:::example{title="Persecution and growth"}
A Christian in Carthage in the third century refuses to burn incense to the emperor. She is arrested and may be put to death as a martyr. Other believers meet in secret, remember her, and the community continues to grow. A good answer on persecution gives the reason (refusing to worship the emperor), the effect (martyrs) and the outcome (growth).
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying that Christianity came to Africa only with missionaries"}
Christianity was in Egypt, North Africa and Ethiopia in the early centuries. The missionaries who came to Zimbabwe in the nineteenth century belong to a later story.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing Pentecost with Easter or the Jewish festival"}
Christian Pentecost remembers the coming of the Holy Spirit in Acts 2; Easter remembers the resurrection. Pentecost is fifty days after Easter.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving Paul's journeys without reasons"}
Do not just list towns. Say what Paul did (preached, founded churches, wrote letters) and why the journeys mattered (the Gospel reached Gentiles and Europe).
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying that Constantine made everyone a Christian in 313"}
The Edict of Milan gave freedom of worship. Christianity became the official religion later, in 380.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (a): describe or narrate"}
For *describe the events of Pentecost* or *describe Paul's conversion*, narrate in order, cite Acts 2 or Acts 9, and give names (Peter, Saul/Paul, Ananias). For *outline the journeys of Paul* choose three or four towns and say what happened.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (b): significance"}
For *explain the significance of the Council of Jerusalem* or *of Pentecost*, say what changed: Gentiles could join without circumcision, so the Church became worldwide; at Pentecost the Spirit empowered the disciples to preach. Link to the Church today.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- At Pentecost (Acts 2) the Holy Spirit came upon the disciples; Peter preached and about three thousand were baptised.
- Saul, a persecutor, became Paul the apostle after his experience on the road to Damascus (Acts 9); he made missionary journeys and wrote letters.
- The Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15) decided that Gentile believers need not be circumcised.
- Christians suffered persecution under Roman emperors; the Edict of Milan (313) gave freedom of worship and the Council of Nicaea (325) dealt with the teaching of Arius.
- Christianity reached Egypt, North Africa and Ethiopia in the early centuries: Alexandria, Carthage and Aksum.
:::
