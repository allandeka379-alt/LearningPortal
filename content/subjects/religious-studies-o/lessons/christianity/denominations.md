## Introduction

On one street in Harare you may pass a Catholic cathedral with a bell tower, a Methodist church with a choir, a hall where a Pentecostal congregation sings with drums, and, on a Saturday, a field where men and women in white robes gather for prayer under the open sky. All of them are Christian, yet they worship in different ways. Many students ask: how did one faith become so many churches, and do the differences matter?

In this lesson you will learn the main divisions in the history of the Church, the beliefs and practices that mark the major groups, and the work of the ecumenical movement that encourages co-operation. The subject describes differences without ranking one group above another. You should know the early Church story, including the Council of Nicaea, before you begin. The history of the churches in Zimbabwe is in its own lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Denomination"}
A particular church or group of churches within Christianity, with its own organisation and emphases, for example the Catholic Church, the Methodist Church or the Zion Christian Church.
:::

:::definition{term="Schism"}
A split in a church. The **Great Schism** of 1054 divided the Church of the East from the Church of the West.
:::

:::definition{term="Reformation"}
The movement in sixteenth-century Europe that began with Martin Luther's protest in 1517 and led to the Protestant churches.
:::

:::definition{term="Ecumenism"}
The movement for unity and co-operation among Christians of different churches.
:::

:::keyfact
**1054**: the Great Schism (Orthodox and Catholic). **1517**: Martin Luther's Ninety-five Theses begin the Reformation. **1948**: the World Council of Churches is founded. In Zimbabwe the main Christian bodies are the **Zimbabwe Council of Churches**, the **Zimbabwe Catholic Bishops' Conference** and the **Evangelical Fellowship of Zimbabwe**.
:::

## The Great Schism of 1054

In the early centuries the Church was one family with five great centres: Rome, Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch and Jerusalem. Over time the Church in the Greek-speaking East and the Latin-speaking West drifted apart. They disagreed about the authority of the bishop of Rome (the pope), about a phrase in the Nicene Creed, and about customs and language. In **1054** the leaders of Rome and Constantinople excommunicated one another, and the division became lasting. The Church of the West became the **Roman Catholic Church**, led by the pope. The Church of the East became the **Orthodox Church**, a family of self-governing churches (for example the Greek, Russian and Ethiopian Orthodox) that honour the Patriarch of Constantinople as first among equals.

## The Reformation and Protestant churches

By the sixteenth century many Christians in Europe believed the Church needed reform. In **1517** the German monk **Martin Luther** published ninety-five points for debate, criticising the sale of indulgences and stressing that people are saved by faith and grace, with the Bible as the final authority. His protest spread. In Switzerland **John Calvin** led reform in Geneva. In England, King Henry VIII broke with the pope in the 1530s and established the **Church of England**, from which the worldwide Anglican communion grew. Those who "protested" and reformed their churches became known as **Protestants**.

Later movements grew within Protestantism. **Methodists** followed John Wesley in the eighteenth century, with an emphasis on personal faith, hymn singing and works of service. **Baptists**, who arose in the seventeenth century, practise believers' baptism and stress local congregational authority.

## Pentecostal and African Independent Churches

The **Pentecostal** movement began in the early twentieth century and stresses the work of the Holy Spirit, healing, prayer and spiritual gifts such as speaking in tongues, with lively worship. Pentecostal and charismatic churches have grown rapidly in Africa, including Zimbabwe.

**African Independent (or Initiated) Churches (AICs)** were founded by Africans, not by European missions. They combine Christian faith with African forms of worship, music, dance, prophecy and healing. Examples in Zimbabwe are the Apostolic churches. They are described in the lesson on Christianity in Zimbabwe.

## Comparing the main groups

| Feature | Catholic | Orthodox | Protestant (for example Methodist, Anglican, Baptist) | Pentecostal | African Independent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Authority | Bible and tradition; the pope as leader | Bible and tradition; councils; bishops and patriarchs | The Bible as the final authority | The Bible and the guidance of the Spirit | The Bible, prophets and church leaders |
| Sacraments | Seven | Seven (called holy mysteries) | Usually two: baptism and Holy Communion | Usually two ordinances | Varies; baptism and communion are common |
| Worship | Liturgical (Mass) | Liturgical, with icons and chanting | Varies from liturgical (Anglican) to simple (Baptist) | Spontaneous, with music and prayer for healing | Often open-air, with song, dance and prophecy |
| Leadership | Pope, bishops, priests, deacons | Patriarchs, bishops, priests, deacons | Bishops, ministers or pastors, elders | Pastors and apostles | Prophets, bishops and elders |

The table gives a general picture only. Within each group there is variety, and a student should say "many" or "most" rather than "all". The Catholic and Orthodox churches keep seven sacraments, while most Protestant churches keep two.

:::example{title="Comparing two churches"}
Tendai attends a Catholic church and his cousin Sipho attends a Methodist church. In Tendai's church the service is the Mass, led by a priest in vestments under the authority of a bishop and the pope, with seven sacraments. Sipho's church has a pastor, a hymn-singing congregation and two sacraments, baptism and communion. Both groups say the Lord's Prayer, read the Bible and believe in the Trinity. A good comparison always names a shared belief as well as the differences.
:::

## Ecumenism

Many Christians feel that division weakens the witness of the Church, and they work for unity. The **World Council of Churches**, founded in 1948, brings together many Protestant, Orthodox and other churches for dialogue and joint action. In Zimbabwe the **Zimbabwe Council of Churches** (ZCC, not to be confused with the Zion Christian Church), the **Zimbabwe Catholic Bishops' Conference** and the **Evangelical Fellowship of Zimbabwe** represent different groups of churches and sometimes speak or act together on national matters such as health, education and peace. Not all churches join these bodies, and some prefer to keep their independence.

:::example{title="Churches working together"}
In a Gweru suburb, a Catholic parish, a Methodist circuit and an apostolic church agree to run a joint food-relief programme during a drought. Each keeps its own worship and leadership, yet together they gather donations and share them with families. This is an example of ecumenism in practice: unity in action without needing agreement on every belief.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying that Catholics are not Christians"}
Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant, Pentecostal and African Independent churches are all Christian denominations. Do not rank them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the two ZCCs"}
The **Zimbabwe Council of Churches** is an ecumenical body. The **Zion Christian Church** is a church. Write the full name.
:::

:::mistake{title="Generalising about a whole group"}
Not all Protestants worship in the same way, and not all African Independent churches share the same practices. Use "many", "most" or "some".
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving dates without events"}
Link each date to its event: 1054 to the split between Rome and Constantinople, 1517 to Luther and the start of the Reformation.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (a): describe or outline"}
For *describe the differences between Catholic and Protestant churches*, use headings that match the table: authority, sacraments, worship and leadership. Give both sides for each heading.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (b): explain"}
For *explain why Christians work together in the ecumenical movement*, give reasons such as unity in witness, joint action for the poor, and the prayer of Jesus for unity in John 17. Add an example from Zimbabwe if you can.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Great Schism (1054) divided the Church into the Catholic West and the Orthodox East.
- The Reformation began in 1517 with Martin Luther; Calvin, the Church of England and later Methodists and Baptists belong to the Protestant family.
- Pentecostal churches emphasise the Holy Spirit; African Independent Churches combine Christian faith with African forms of worship.
- Churches differ in authority, sacraments, worship and leadership, but share belief in God, Jesus, the Bible and the Trinity.
- Ecumenical bodies such as the World Council of Churches and, in Zimbabwe, the Zimbabwe Council of Churches, the Catholic Bishops' Conference and the Evangelical Fellowship of Zimbabwe encourage co-operation.
:::
