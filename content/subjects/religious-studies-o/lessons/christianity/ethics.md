## Introduction

When Tafadzwa finds a wallet on the way to school in Kariba, he faces a choice. He can keep the money, or he can hand it to the headmistress. The Christians in his family would say that the right answer is clear, but they would also ask where that conviction comes from: the commandment not to steal? the teaching of Jesus about honesty? conscience? In Christian ethics, such choices are guided by several sources and by principles such as love, justice and forgiveness.

In this lesson you will study where Christians find moral guidance, the key teachings (the Ten Commandments, love and the Golden Rule, forgiveness, justice, stewardship, honesty and work), and how Christians apply them in daily life and service. Specific issues, such as HIV and AIDS, gender and the environment, are studied in topics 6 to 15 and are only outlined here. Remember that Christians hold different views on some questions, and you should report the range fairly.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Ethics (morality)"}
Principles about what is right and wrong and how people should live.
:::

:::definition{term="Conscience"}
The inner sense of right and wrong. Many Christians believe that God speaks to people through conscience, and see the Bible and the Church as helping to form it.
:::

:::definition{term="Agape"}
Self-giving love, the love that seeks the good of others, as taught by Jesus.
:::

:::definition{term="Stewardship"}
The idea that humans are trusted by God to care for creation, resources and their own gifts.
:::

:::keyfact
Sources: **the Bible, the teaching of Jesus, the Church and tradition, and conscience**. The **Ten Commandments** are in **Exodus 20:1-17** and Deuteronomy 5. The **Golden Rule** is in **Matthew 7:12**. Love of God and neighbour: **Mark 12:28-34**.
:::

## Sources of Christian morality

Christians draw guidance from four main sources.

| Source | How it guides |
|---|---|
| The Bible | The Ten Commandments, the Prophets, the teaching of Jesus and the letters set out moral principles |
| The teaching and example of Jesus | The Sermon on the Mount, the parables and Jesus' care for the outcast are the model |
| The Church and tradition | Church leaders, councils and teaching (for example Catholic social teaching) interpret the Bible for today |
| Conscience | Believers use their inner sense of right and wrong, shaped by prayer and the Spirit (Romans 2:15) |

Christians do not all give these sources the same weight. Catholics and Orthodox Christians give weight to Church teaching and tradition; many Protestants emphasise the Bible; and many believers stress conscience guided by the Spirit.

## The Ten Commandments

The Ten Commandments, given to Moses at Sinai (Exodus 20:1-17), are accepted by Christians as a basic moral guide. They fall into two groups: duties to God (to have no other gods, not to make idols, not to misuse God's name, to keep the Sabbath) and duties to other people (to honour parents, and not to murder, commit adultery, steal, bear false witness or covet). Christians also teach that Jesus summed up the commandments in love of God and neighbour (Mark 12:28-34). The commandments are not only prohibitions: for example, "do not steal" leads many Christians to a duty to share with the needy.

## Love, forgiveness and reconciliation

The centre of Christian ethics is **agape**. Jesus taught his followers to love their neighbour as themselves, to love even their enemies (Matthew 5:43-48), and to treat others as they would want to be treated (the Golden Rule, Matthew 7:12). Paul describes the qualities of love in 1 Corinthians 13: love is patient and kind and does not keep a record of wrongs.

**Forgiveness** follows from love. In the Lord's Prayer Christians ask God to forgive them as they forgive others, and in Matthew 18:21-35 Jesus tells his followers to forgive again and again. **Reconciliation** means repairing a broken relationship. In Zimbabwe, churches have helped families and communities to reconcile after disputes and political conflict, as the lesson on churches in the liberation struggle and reconciliation shows.

## Justice and care for the poor

The Old Testament prophets call for justice: Amos 5:24 asks for justice to flow like a river, and Micah 6:8 asks the people to do justice, love kindness and walk humbly with God. Jesus cared for the sick, the hungry and the outcast, and in Matthew 25:31-46 he says that serving them is serving him. James 2:14-17 says that faith without good deeds is dead. As a result, Christian churches run schools, clinics, feeding schemes and orphan care, and speak against corruption and exploitation. Christians differ about methods. Some stress charity and personal giving; others stress changing unjust structures through advocacy and political action.

## Stewardship, honesty and work

According to Genesis 1:28-31 and 2:15, humans are asked to care for creation. Christians apply this to farming, water, forests and wildlife. Honesty is also central: the commandment against false witness, and Ephesians 4:25, call for truth. Christians teach that work is a calling: Ephesians 4:28 says that a former thief should work with his hands so as to share with those in need, and 2 Thessalonians 3:10-12 urges people to earn their own living. Business and the use of wealth are discussed in the topic on enterprise.

## Sexual and family ethics in outline

Most Christian churches teach that marriage between a man and a woman is a lifelong union in which sexual intimacy belongs, and that children should be raised with love. Many encourage faithfulness within marriage and abstinence before it. Churches differ on contraception, divorce and remarriage, and on questions of sexual orientation: Catholic teaching differs from the practice of some Protestant churches, and views within a single denomination can differ. Christians also condemn violence in the home and abuse. In Zimbabwe, the law protects the dignity of every person and sets the minimum marriage age at 18; Christians are called to treat every person with respect, whatever their views on contested questions.

:::example{title="Applying the Golden Rule to cyberbullying"}
Thandeka, a Form 4 pupil, sees classmates posting cruel messages about a girl on a phone group. She remembers the Golden Rule: do for others what you would want them to do for you. She leaves the group, tells the girl that she is on her side, and reports the messages to a teacher. This is a Christian application of love and justice in a modern setting. A good answer to such a case names the teaching, the action and the reason.
:::

:::example{title="Two Christians, one issue"}
A Catholic and a Methodist agree that Christians must care for the poor. The Catholic points to Catholic social teaching, which speaks of the dignity of every person and the common good. The Methodist points to John Wesley's concern for the poor and to Matthew 25. Both reach similar action, a church feeding scheme, from different sources of authority. The example shows that Christians can share moral conclusions while drawing on different sources.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying that all Christians agree on every moral question"}
On contested questions, Christians take different positions. Report the range ("some Christians ...; others ...").
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving the commandment without its application"}
Do not just list the commandments. Explain how they guide choices, for example how "do not steal" applies to corruption or to keeping found property.
:::

:::mistake{title="Presenting morality as only rules"}
For Christians love, forgiveness and conscience are central. Show that rules and love work together.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring Zimbabwean examples"}
Use examples from schools, families and communities in Zimbabwe, such as church feeding schemes or reconciliation efforts.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (a): describe or outline"}
For *outline the sources of Christian morality* or *describe the Ten Commandments*, give four sources or the two groups of commandments, with a reference (Exodus 20:1-17; Matthew 7:12; Mark 12:28-34) and a short example.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (b): explain or discuss"}
For *explain how love guides Christian behaviour* give developed points: love of neighbour (Good Samaritan), forgiveness (Lord's Prayer) and service (Matthew 25), each linked to a practice in Zimbabwe.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Christians draw moral guidance from the Bible, the teaching of Jesus, the Church and tradition, and conscience.
- The Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:1-17) are a basic guide; Jesus summed them up in love of God and neighbour (Mark 12:28-34).
- Agape, the Golden Rule, forgiveness and reconciliation are central to Christian ethics.
- Justice and care for the poor lead churches to run schools, clinics and feeding schemes.
- Stewardship, honesty and work are important moral duties; on sexual and family ethics churches share some teaching and differ on others.
:::
