## Introduction

On a Sunday morning in Bindura, Farai's grandmother takes her old Shona Bible from its cloth wrapping, finds the reading for the day and follows the words with her finger while the preacher reads aloud. Farai carries a small Ndebele Bible in his school bag; his cousin has a Bible application on her phone. The book is the same, but it comes in many forms and languages. For Christians the Bible is the most important book: the source of their beliefs, the text read in worship and a guide for life.

In this lesson you will learn how the Bible is arranged, how its books were collected, how Christians understand its authority, and how it is translated and used. You will recall that the Jewish scriptures are the Old Testament of the Christian Bible, so the lessons on Judaism are useful background. Cite and paraphrase the Bible, and avoid long quotations.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Bible"}
The sacred scriptures of Christians. The word comes from a Greek word for "books", and the Bible is a library of books written over many centuries.
:::

:::definition{term="Testament"}
A major division of the Bible: the **Old Testament**, which Christians share with Jews in the main, and the **New Testament**, which concerns Jesus and the early Church.
:::

:::definition{term="Canon"}
The list of books accepted as scripture by a religious community. The word comes from a Greek word for "rule" or "measure".
:::

:::definition{term="Inspiration"}
The belief that God guided the writers of scripture. In 2 Timothy 3:16 the writer says that scripture is inspired by God and useful for teaching.
:::

:::keyfact
The Protestant Bible has **66 books**: **39** in the Old Testament and **27** in the New Testament. The Catholic Bible has **73 books**, because it includes seven additional Old Testament books and parts of other books, called the deuterocanonical books.
:::

## The structure of the Bible

| Division | Group | Books (examples) | Number |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old Testament | Law (Pentateuch) | Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy | 5 |
| Old Testament | History | Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1 and 2 Samuel, Kings, Chronicles, Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther | 12 |
| Old Testament | Wisdom and poetry | Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Solomon | 5 |
| Old Testament | Prophets | Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Daniel and twelve shorter prophets from Hosea to Malachi | 17 |
| New Testament | Gospels | Matthew, Mark, Luke, John | 4 |
| New Testament | History | Acts of the Apostles | 1 |
| New Testament | Letters (epistles) | Romans to Jude, including Paul's letters | 21 |
| New Testament | Prophecy | Revelation | 1 |

The first five books of the Old Testament, the **Law**, tell of creation, the patriarchs, the Exodus and the covenant at Sinai. The Prophets call the people back to God and to justice. The Gospels tell the story of Jesus, Acts tells of the early Church, the letters give advice to churches, and Revelation uses symbols to speak of the end of time.

## The canon and the Apocrypha

The books of the Bible were written over many centuries and in several languages: the Old Testament mainly in Hebrew, and the New Testament in Greek. The books were gradually collected. Jews had their own scriptures (the Tanakh), and the early Church used them as the Old Testament. The New Testament canon of 27 books was widely recognised by the fourth century, and Christians accept the same twenty-seven.

The Old Testament canon is not identical in every church. The Catholic and Orthodox churches include extra books, such as Tobit, Judith, Wisdom and 1 and 2 Maccabees, which they call **deuterocanonical** ("second canon"). Protestants usually call these books the **Apocrypha** and may read them for background but not as scripture. This explains why a Catholic Bible has more books than a Protestant Bible, and a student should be able to say so without ranking either.

## Inspiration and interpretation

Christians believe that the Bible is inspired by God, but they understand this in different ways. Some Christians hold that it is the word of God in every detail and read it **literally**. Others say that God inspired human writers who used their own language, culture and style, so the Bible must be read in its context and the meaning drawn out by interpretation. Many read parts, such as poetry or parables, symbolically and other parts, such as historical books, as history. Churches also differ on authority: Catholics and Orthodox Christians give weight to tradition and church teaching alongside the Bible, while many Protestants emphasise the Bible as the final authority.

:::example{title="Two ways of reading Genesis 1"}
In a Form 3 class in Mutare, Tendai says that Genesis 1 tells that God made the world in six days and rested on the seventh. Aisha, a Muslim classmate, asks how Christians read it. Their teacher explains that some Christians take the days as ordinary days, while others read the account as teaching that God is creator and that creation is good, without insisting on a scientific timetable. This shows two interpretations within Christianity, and a good answer reports both without choosing between them.
:::

## Translation

Because the Bible was written in Hebrew and Greek, translation has been central to Christian life. The Bible has been translated into thousands of languages. In Zimbabwe, missionaries and later Zimbabwean Christians translated the Bible into Shona and Ndebele and into other local languages, and organisations such as the Bible Society of Zimbabwe continue this work. Translation has important effects: people can read and hear the scriptures in their mother tongue, it supported literacy and the writing of the languages, and it allowed Africans to interpret the Bible for themselves. Translators face questions of how to convey names, ideas and customs faithfully in another language.

## How Christians use the Bible

- **In worship:** passages are read aloud, the sermon explains a reading, and psalms and hymns come from the text. Many churches follow a calendar of readings.
- **In study:** groups meet to read, discuss and pray about a passage; Sunday schools teach children Bible stories.
- **In daily life:** individuals read a passage each day, learn verses by heart, and seek guidance for decisions in the Bible. Many Christians keep a Bible at home and bring it to worship.
- **In ceremonies:** the Bible is used at baptism, weddings and funerals. Oaths in some courts are taken on it.

:::example{title="A Bible study group"}
On Wednesday evenings in Gweru, a group of eight adults meets in Rudo's home. They read the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37), discuss who their neighbours are, and agree to visit an elderly woman who lives alone. This is the Bible in daily life: a text read together, discussed and put into action.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the Bible is one book written by one author"}
The Bible is a collection of books by many writers over many centuries. Christians believe that God inspired them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the Testaments"}
The Gospels, Acts, letters and Revelation are the New Testament. Genesis, Exodus, the Psalms and the Prophets are the Old Testament.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving the wrong number of books"}
Protestants: 66 (39 and 27). Catholics: 73. Do not say that the Bible has one set number for every church.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing only one way of interpreting scripture"}
Christians read the Bible literally and non-literally. Say which group holds which view.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Bible is a library of books with two parts: the Old Testament (39 books in Protestant Bibles) and the New Testament (27 books).
- The canon is the list of accepted books; Catholic and Orthodox Bibles include deuterocanonical books that Protestants call the Apocrypha.
- Christians believe the Bible is inspired, but some read it literally and others read it in context and symbolically.
- The Bible has been translated into Shona, Ndebele and many other languages, which helps people to read and interpret it for themselves.
- Christians use the Bible in worship, study, ceremonies and daily life.
:::
