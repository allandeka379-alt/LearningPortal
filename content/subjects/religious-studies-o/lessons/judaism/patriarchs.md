## Introduction

On a Saturday morning in Bulawayo, Miriam and her father walk to the synagogue. In the service the Torah is read aloud, and many of the first stories are about a man called Abraham, who left his home because he believed God had called him. Jews think of Abraham as the father of their people, and Christians and Muslims also honour him, each in their own way. When you study Abraham and his family you are studying the roots of Judaism, one of the world's oldest living religions.

In this lesson you learn the names that Jews are called, the story of the patriarchs (the founding fathers) and matriarchs (the founding mothers) as the Jewish scriptures tell it, and why the **covenant** matters. You do not need to decide whether the events happened exactly as told; you need to report accurately what Jews believe and what the scriptures say. The dates of the patriarchal period are uncertain, so this lesson gives no precise dates.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Judaism"}
The religion of the Jewish people, based on belief in one God, the covenant between God and the people, and the teaching of the Torah.
:::

:::definition{term="Covenant (brit)"}
A solemn agreement. In Judaism the covenant is the special relationship between God and the descendants of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, with promises from God and responsibilities for the people.
:::

:::definition{term="Patriarch and matriarch"}
A **patriarch** is a founding father of the people (Abraham, Isaac and Jacob). A **matriarch** is a founding mother (Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel and Leah).
:::

:::keyfact
Three names for the people: **Hebrews** (the earliest name, used in Genesis for Abraham's family), **Israelites** (the descendants of Jacob, whom God renamed **Israel**) and **Jews** (from Judah, one of Jacob's sons; the name for the people and their religion in later centuries).
:::

## Hebrews, Israelites and Jews

The three names belong to different stages of the story. In Genesis, Abraham is called a Hebrew. After Jacob received the name Israel, his twelve sons and their descendants were called the **children of Israel**, or Israelites. Later, when the kingdom of Judah was the home of the people and its survivors returned from exile, the word **Jew** became the common name, and today it describes all who belong to the Jewish people or practise Judaism. These are not three different peoples. They are three names used at different times for the same community.

## The call of Abraham and the covenant

According to Genesis, Abraham (at first called Abram) and his wife Sarah (at first Sarai) travelled with their family from Ur to Haran. In **Genesis 12:1-3** God tells Abraham to leave his country and his father's house and go to a land that God will show him. God promises to make him into a great nation, to bless him and to make him a blessing, so that through him all the families of the earth would be blessed. Abraham obeys. For Jews this obedience is a model of faith.

The covenant is renewed twice. In **Genesis 15** God promises Abraham descendants too many to count, as many as the stars, and the land of Canaan. In **Genesis 17** God gives the covenant its sign. The names Abram and Sarai are changed to **Abraham** and **Sarah**, and every male in the household is to be circumcised. **Circumcision (brit milah)** is performed on a baby boy on the eighth day, and Jewish families still keep this sign of the covenant. The Jewish scriptures therefore connect one event in the past with a practice that continues in every generation.

:::example{title="Reading the covenant promises"}
Genesis 12 and 15 contain three promises, which you can remember as **land, descendants and blessing**.

1. Land: Abraham's descendants will have the land of Canaan.
2. Descendants: they will become a great nation, as many as the stars.
3. Blessing: Abraham will be blessed and, through him, other peoples as well.

Jews also see responsibilities in the covenant: to walk in God's ways, to be faithful and to do what is right. A strong answer in an examination shows both sides: what God promises and what the people must do.
:::

## Isaac, Rebekah and the binding of Isaac

Sarah and Abraham were old when their son **Isaac** was born (Genesis 21). In **Genesis 22** God tests Abraham by asking him to offer Isaac. Abraham obeys, but at the last moment a messenger from God stops him and a ram is provided instead. Jews call this story the **Akedah** (the binding). They read it as a test of faith and trust, and they also note that the story ends with Isaac being spared. It is read in the synagogue at Rosh Hashanah, the Jewish new year.

In **Genesis 24** a servant of Abraham finds **Rebekah**, who offers water to him and his camels, and she becomes the wife of Isaac. Their twin sons were **Esau** and **Jacob**.

## Jacob, Israel and the twelve tribes

Jacob received his father's blessing in place of Esau, a story told in Genesis 27. He fled, dreamed at Bethel of a ladder between earth and heaven, and heard God repeat the covenant promises (Genesis 28). He married **Leah** and **Rachel** and had children with them and with their maids Bilhah and Zilpah. At the river Jabbok (**Genesis 32:22-32**) he wrestled through the night and was given the name **Israel**. His twelve sons became the ancestors of the **twelve tribes of Israel**.

| Mother | Sons (ancestors of the tribes) |
|---|---|
| Leah | Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun |
| Rachel | Joseph (whose sons Ephraim and Manasseh gave their names to two tribes), Benjamin |
| Bilhah (Rachel's maid) | Dan, Naphtali |
| Zilpah (Leah's maid) | Gad, Asher |

## Joseph in Egypt

The story of **Joseph** fills Genesis 37 to 50. Jacob loved Joseph, and his brothers, jealous, sold him to traders who took him to Egypt. There he worked in a household, spent time in prison, and, by explaining Pharaoh's dreams, was made a high official in charge of storing grain for a coming famine. When famine reached Canaan, his brothers came to buy grain; Joseph recognised them, tested them and forgave them. The family moved to Egypt, which sets the scene for the Exodus. Jews read the story as showing that God's purposes can bring good out of harm, and that forgiveness can heal a family.

## The people of the covenant

| Person | Main events | Where to read |
|---|---|---|
| Abraham | Called to leave home; covenant; circumcision; the binding of Isaac | Genesis 12, 15, 17, 22 |
| Sarah | Wife of Abraham; mother of Isaac | Genesis 17, 21 |
| Isaac | Son of the promise; spared at the Akedah | Genesis 21, 22 |
| Rebekah | Wife of Isaac; mother of Esau and Jacob | Genesis 24 |
| Jacob | Dream at Bethel; renamed Israel; father of twelve sons | Genesis 28, 32 |
| Leah and Rachel | Wives of Jacob; mothers of the tribes | Genesis 29-30 |
| Joseph | Sold to Egypt; rose to high office; forgave his brothers | Genesis 37-50 |

:::example{title="Why the story matters to Jews today"}
At a brit milah in Harare, the guests wish the baby a life of Torah, marriage and good deeds. The baby boy is circumcised on the eighth day, and Rachel's grandfather explains that Jewish boys have been welcomed this way since the time of Abraham. The ceremony links a newborn child to the ancient covenant, so the story is not only about the past: it explains why Jewish families keep this practice, and why they teach children the names of the patriarchs and matriarchs.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using Hebrew, Israelite and Jew as three different peoples"}
They are names for the same people at different times. Say which name suits which period.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving exact dates"}
The dating of the patriarchal period is uncertain. Write "according to Genesis" and avoid precise years.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing events without explaining the covenant"}
A question on Abraham needs the promises (land, descendants, blessing), the sign (circumcision) and the meaning for Jews today.
:::

:::mistake{title="Presenting the story as only Jewish"}
Abraham is honoured by Jews, Christians and Muslims, who tell his story differently. Describe the Jewish account as the Jewish account.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (a): describe or narrate"}
For *describe the call of Abraham* give the events in order with references (Genesis 12, 15, 17, 22), and use the names: Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Israel.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 (b): explain the significance"}
For *explain the significance of the covenant* link each point to Judaism today: circumcision, the idea of a chosen people with responsibilities, and Jewish identity.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Hebrews, Israelites and Jews are three names used at different times for the same people.
- In Genesis 12, 15 and 17 God calls Abraham and makes a covenant with promises of land, descendants and blessing.
- Circumcision on the eighth day (brit milah) is the sign of the covenant.
- Isaac was spared at the Akedah (Genesis 22); Jacob was renamed Israel (Genesis 32) and his twelve sons were ancestors of the twelve tribes.
- Joseph rose to high office in Egypt and forgave his brothers, which brought the family to Egypt.
- Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel and Leah are the matriarchs; dating of the period is uncertain.
:::
