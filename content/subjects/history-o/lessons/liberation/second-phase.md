## Introduction

On 21 December 1972 ZANLA fighters attacked **Altena Farm** in the Centenary district of the north-east. It was a small attack, but it marked a new kind of war. Instead of large groups crossing the Zambezi and meeting the Rhodesian army in battle, guerrillas now moved in small groups, lived among the people and built support village by village. Within seven years the war had spread across most of the countryside, and the Rhodesian government was forced to negotiate.

This phase of the struggle was fought by thousands of guerrillas and supported by hundreds of thousands of ordinary people. It also brought great suffering: historians estimate that about 30,000 people died in the war as a whole, most of them African civilians and fighters.

You should already know why the early phase (1966-1970) failed. Examiners ask you to **describe the main events**, **outline the role of the masses, chiefs, spirit mediums, war collaborators and churches**, and **explain the role of external support**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Politicisation"}
Teaching people the aims of the struggle and winning their support, for example through night meetings, songs and discussion.
:::

:::definition{term="Pungwe"}
An all-night political meeting held by guerrillas with villagers, with speeches, songs and discussion.
:::

:::definition{term="Mujibha and chimbwido"}
Young people who supported the guerrillas: **mujibhas** (boys) and **chimbwidos** (girls) carried messages, kept watch, gathered information and carried food and supplies.
:::

:::definition{term="Protected village"}
A fenced, guarded settlement into which the Rhodesian government moved rural people from about 1973 to cut them off from the guerrillas.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- 21 December 1972: Altena Farm attack opens the north-eastern front.
- 25 June 1975: Mozambique becomes independent; ZANLA bases along the eastern border follow.
- October 1976: ZANU and ZAPU form the Patriotic Front.
- 9 August 1976: Rhodesian raid on Nyadzonia camp in Mozambique.
- 23 November 1977: Rhodesian raid on Chimoio.
- 19 October 1978: Rhodesian air raid on Freedom Camp in Zambia.
:::

## A new strategy

The leaders of ZANLA, influenced by Chinese ideas of "people's war", argued that guerrillas must be supported by the people as fish are supported by water. The new strategy had several parts:

- **Small groups** that moved quickly, struck by surprise and avoided open battle with stronger forces.
- **Politicisation first:** before fighting in an area, guerrillas explained the aims of the struggle (land, freedom, majority rule) at pungwes and recruited local helpers.
- **Rural bases of support:** villagers provided food, shelter and information; the countryside became the main battlefield.
- **Spreading the war** across a wide area so that the Rhodesian forces were stretched.

The way into the north-east came through **Tete province of Mozambique**, where FRELIMO, which was fighting the Portuguese, allowed ZANLA to move through areas it controlled.

**After 1975**, when Mozambique became independent under FRELIMO, ZANLA could open bases along the whole eastern border, from Tete to Gaza, and the war spread into Manicaland, the Midlands and the south-east. ZIPRA operated mainly from **Zambia**, crossing into the west and north-west, and also through Botswana; in the late 1970s it built up a large, conventionally trained force in Zambia as well as its guerrilla units.

In **October 1976** ZANU and ZAPU formed the **Patriotic Front** to negotiate together, although their armies remained separate.

### Raids on camps

The Rhodesian forces attacked camps in neighbouring countries:

- **Nyadzonia (Mozambique), 9 August 1976:** a Rhodesian unit attacked the camp. Rhodesia claimed it was a guerrilla base; ZANU and others said it was mainly a refugee camp. Historians estimate that around a thousand people were killed, many of them unarmed.
- **Chimoio (Mozambique), 23 November 1977:** air and ground attack on ZANLA's headquarters and surrounding camps. Estimates of the dead vary widely, from over a thousand to several thousand, including many refugees and young recruits.
- **Freedom Camp (Zambia), 19 October 1978:** an air raid on a ZIPRA camp near Lusaka in which hundreds of people were killed.

Civilians suffered from all sides. Security forces killed civilians in raids, curfews and cross-border attacks; guerrillas killed people they accused of being informers ("sell-outs"); landmines killed travellers. In September 1978 and February 1979 ZIPRA shot down two Air Rhodesia civilian aircraft, killing most of those on board.

## The role of the masses

:::example{title="How villagers sustained the war, step by step"}
1. **Before fighting:** guerrillas held pungwes to explain the struggle and won the trust of villagers.
2. **Supplies:** families fed the fighters and hid weapons, often at great risk.
3. **Information:** mujibhas and chimbwidos watched roads, reported the movement of security forces and carried messages between groups.
4. **Recruitment:** young people from these villages crossed into Mozambique, Zambia or Botswana to train.
5. **Result:** the guerrillas could stay in an area for long periods, which was impossible in the 1960s. This support is the main reason the second phase succeeded where the first had failed.
:::

- **Peasants** gave food, shelter and information and suffered heavily from both sides.
- **Mujibhas and chimbwidos** were the eyes and ears of the guerrillas.
- **Workers and students** joined in large numbers; whole groups of school pupils crossed the borders to join the struggle.
- **Chiefs** were in a difficult position: they were paid officials of the government, and some supported it, while others secretly helped the guerrillas.
- **Spirit mediums**, especially in the north-east, gave guerrillas guidance and spiritual authority and linked the struggle to the First Chimurenga.
- **Churches** gave medical care at mission hospitals; the **Catholic Commission for Justice and Peace** (from 1972) documented abuses by the security forces, and some missionaries were deported.

## External support

- **The Frontline States** (Tanzania, Zambia, Botswana, and from 1975-76 Mozambique and Angola) hosted bases, refugees and offices, and suffered Rhodesian raids as a result.
- **The OAU Liberation Committee** channelled money and weapons.
- **The Soviet Union** and its allies armed and trained ZIPRA; **China** armed and trained ZANLA.
- **The United Nations** maintained sanctions and, through its agencies, helped refugees.
- Churches and some Western governments and organisations gave humanitarian aid to refugees.

## Rhodesian counter-measures

Rhodesia expanded conscription, created special units such as the **Selous Scouts** (1973), used **collective fines** and curfews, moved people into **protected villages**, declared martial law over most of the country by 1979, raided camps abroad, and later armed "auxiliaries" linked to the internal settlement parties. These measures killed many guerrillas but also drove more people to support them.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Describing only battles"}
The second phase was won as much by politics as by fighting. Always include the role of the masses and the strategy of politicisation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling the camps purely military"}
Nyadzonia and Chimoio held large numbers of refugees as well as fighters; state that their status was disputed and that many unarmed people died.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting ZIPRA"}
ZANLA operated mainly from Mozambique and ZIPRA mainly from Zambia. A balanced answer covers both armies.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- From 21 December 1972 (Altena Farm) the war became a guerrilla war based on politicising the people.
- Mozambique's independence (1975) gave ZANLA bases along the eastern border; ZIPRA operated mainly from Zambia; the Patriotic Front was formed in 1976.
- Rhodesian raids on Nyadzonia (1976), Chimoio (1977) and Freedom Camp (1978) killed many, including refugees.
- Peasants, mujibhas and chimbwidos, spirit mediums, churches and some chiefs sustained the struggle.
- The Frontline States, the OAU, the USSR and China supported the movements; Rhodesian counter-measures such as protected villages and martial law failed to stop the war.
:::
