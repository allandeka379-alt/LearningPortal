## Introduction

Between 1861 and 1865 the United States fought a civil war that killed more Americans than any other war in the country's history. At its heart was the enslavement of about four million people of African descent, the descendants of men and women carried across the Atlantic in the slave trade. Their freedom was the greatest result of the war.

For students in Zimbabwe and across Africa this history matters directly. The Atlantic slave trade took millions of Africans to the Americas, and the struggle over slavery in the United States was part of the long fight against that system. This lesson explains how disagreements between North and South grew from 1820 until the Southern states left the Union in 1860-61.

This is Cambridge 0470 Option A, key question 4: *Why was there a civil war in the United States and what were its results?* You should know that the United States was a federal republic in which power was shared between the national (federal) government and the states.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Slave state and free state"}
A slave state allowed slavery by law; a free state did not. The balance between them in the Senate, where each state had two senators, was a constant political issue.
:::

:::definition{term="Abolitionist"}
A person who campaigned for slavery to be ended (abolished) completely.
:::

:::definition{term="Popular sovereignty"}
The idea that the settlers of a territory should vote on whether to allow slavery there, rather than Congress deciding.
:::

:::definition{term="Secession"}
The act of a state leaving (seceding from) the Union. Southern leaders claimed states had the right to do so; Lincoln and most Northerners denied it.
:::

:::definition{term="States' rights"}
The argument that the states, not the federal government, should decide important questions. Before 1861 Southern politicians used it above all to defend slavery.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **1820:** Missouri Compromise.
- **1850:** Compromise of 1850, including a stricter Fugitive Slave Act.
- **1854:** Kansas-Nebraska Act; the Republican Party is founded.
- **1857:** Dred Scott decision.
- **November 1860:** Abraham Lincoln elected President.
- **20 December 1860:** South Carolina secedes.
- **12 April 1861:** Confederate forces fire on Fort Sumter.
:::

## North and South

By the 1850s North and South had developed in different ways.

| | North | South |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | Farming, but also fast-growing industry, trade and banking | Mainly agriculture; cotton grown for export was the most valuable crop |
| Labour | Free wage labour; many European immigrants | Enslaved labour on plantations and farms |
| Population | Larger and growing faster | Smaller; about a third of the people in the slave states were enslaved |
| Tariffs | Many favoured tariffs to protect industry | Opposed tariffs, which raised the cost of imported goods |

The invention of the cotton gin in 1793 had made cotton growing very profitable and increased the demand for enslaved labour. Southern society was dominated by planters, although most white Southern families owned no enslaved people. Enslaved people resisted in many ways, from slowing work and running away to open revolt, such as Nat Turner's rebellion in Virginia in 1831.

## Slave states and free states, 1820-1854

As the country expanded westward, every new territory raised the same question: would it be slave or free?

- **Missouri Compromise (1820):** Missouri entered as a slave state and Maine as a free state, keeping the balance. Slavery was banned in the rest of the Louisiana Purchase north of latitude 36°30'.
- **The Mexican War (1846-48)** added vast lands in the south-west and reopened the question.
- **Compromise of 1850:** California entered as a free state; the people of the new territories of New Mexico and Utah would decide for themselves; the slave trade (but not slavery) was ended in the capital, Washington DC; and a much stricter **Fugitive Slave Act** forced Northerners to help return people who had escaped slavery. This law angered many Northerners who had never thought much about slavery before.
- **Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854):** Senator Stephen Douglas proposed that settlers in Kansas and Nebraska decide by **popular sovereignty**. This overturned the Missouri line. Supporters and opponents of slavery rushed into Kansas and fought each other ("Bleeding Kansas"). Opponents of the spread of slavery formed the **Republican Party** in 1854.

## Abolitionism and Dred Scott

**Abolitionists** were a minority in the North, but a vocal one. William Lloyd Garrison published the newspaper *The Liberator* from 1831. Frederick Douglass, who had escaped slavery, became one of the most powerful speakers and writers against it. Harriet Tubman, also formerly enslaved, led others to freedom along the secret routes of the Underground Railroad. Harriet Beecher Stowe's novel *Uncle Tom's Cabin* (1852) sold hundreds of thousands of copies and turned many readers against slavery. Southern whites saw abolitionism as an attack on their society and property.

In the **Dred Scott** case (1857) the Supreme Court ruled that people of African descent could not be citizens of the United States and that Congress had no power to ban slavery in the territories. Southerners welcomed the decision. Many Northerners concluded that slavery might spread everywhere. In 1859 the abolitionist John Brown led a raid on the federal arsenal at Harpers Ferry, Virginia, hoping to start a revolt of enslaved people. He was captured and hanged, but many Southerners believed Northerners sympathised with him.

## The election of 1860 and secession

In 1860 the Democratic Party split into Northern and Southern wings, each with its own candidate. The Republican, **Abraham Lincoln**, won with about 40% of the popular vote, carrying almost every Northern state but no Southern state; in most of the South he was not even on the ballot. Lincoln promised not to interfere with slavery where it already existed, but he was firmly opposed to its **spread into the territories**.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why Lincoln's election led to secession"}
1. **A purely Northern victory:** Lincoln won without a single Southern electoral vote, showing that the North could now control the presidency alone.
2. **Fear for slavery:** Southern leaders believed that if slavery could not expand, it would eventually die, and that Republicans would attack it.
3. **Secession:** South Carolina seceded on 20 December 1860, and six more states of the Deep South followed by February 1861, forming the Confederate States of America with Jefferson Davis as President.
4. **Result:** the Union was divided before Lincoln even took office in March 1861.
:::

Lincoln declared secession illegal but promised not to attack first. When he tried to resupply **Fort Sumter**, a federal fort in Charleston harbour, Confederate forces bombarded it on **12 April 1861** and it surrendered. Lincoln called for volunteers to put down the rebellion, and four more slave states (Virginia, Arkansas, North Carolina and Tennessee) joined the Confederacy, making eleven. Four border slave states stayed in the Union.

## How far did slavery cause the war?

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: slavery or states' rights?"}
**Slavery was the central cause:** every major crisis from 1820 to 1860 was about slavery and its expansion. The declarations of several seceding states named the protection of slavery as their reason for leaving, and the Confederate constitution protected slavery.
**Other factors mattered:** some argue that economic differences, tariffs and the principle of states' rights caused the war, and that most Northerners fought to save the Union, not to free enslaved people.
**Judgement:** most historians today conclude that slavery was the root cause. Arguments about states' rights and the economy were mainly arguments about slavery, while the Union's aim in 1861 was to stop secession; freedom became a war aim later.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Lincoln promised to abolish slavery in 1860"}
In 1860 Lincoln promised not to interfere with slavery where it existed. He opposed its spread into the territories.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the two compromises"}
The Missouri Compromise (1820) drew the 36°30' line; the Compromise of 1850 admitted California as free and passed the Fugitive Slave Act. The Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854) overturned the 1820 line.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating states' rights as separate from slavery"}
If you discuss states' rights, explain which right the Southern states were defending: above all, the right to hold people in slavery.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- North and South developed different economies, with the South depending on the labour of about four million enslaved people by 1860.
- Westward expansion forced repeated crises over slave and free states: the Missouri Compromise (1820), the Compromise of 1850 and the Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854).
- Abolitionists, *Uncle Tom's Cabin*, the Dred Scott decision (1857) and John Brown's raid (1859) deepened mistrust.
- Lincoln's election in November 1860, opposed to the spread of slavery, led eleven Southern states to secede, and war began at Fort Sumter in April 1861.
- Most historians see slavery as the root cause of the war.
:::
