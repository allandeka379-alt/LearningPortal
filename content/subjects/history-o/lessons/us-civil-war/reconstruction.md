## Introduction

When the Civil War ended in April 1865, about four million people were free for the first time. What would freedom mean? Would they own land, vote, go to school and be equal before the law? And how should the defeated Southern states be brought back into the Union? The period from 1865 to 1877, when these questions were fought over, is called **Reconstruction**.

Zimbabweans know from their own history that independence or liberation does not end a struggle: questions about land, rights and reconciliation continue for decades. In the United States, Reconstruction produced great changes in the Constitution, but white Southerners regained power and many of the gains of formerly enslaved people were taken away after 1877.

This is Cambridge 0470 Option A, key question 4: whether the war changed anything. You should know the causes of the war and why the North won.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Reconstruction"}
The period 1865-1877 in which the federal government tried to bring the Southern states back into the Union and decide the rights of formerly enslaved people.
:::

:::definition{term="Freedmen's Bureau"}
A federal agency set up in March 1865 to help formerly enslaved people (freedpeople) with food, medical care, schools, labour contracts and legal disputes.
:::

:::definition{term="Black Codes"}
Laws passed by Southern state governments in 1865-66 to restrict the freedom of Black people, for example by forcing them to sign labour contracts and punishing "vagrancy".
:::

:::definition{term="Radical Republicans"}
Members of Congress who wanted to protect the rights of freedpeople, punish Confederate leaders and use federal power to transform the South.
:::

:::definition{term="Sharecropping"}
A system in which a family farmed part of a landowner's land in return for a share of the crop. It often trapped tenants in debt.
:::

:::keyfact{title="The three amendments"}
- **13th Amendment (ratified 1865):** abolished slavery throughout the United States.
- **14th Amendment (ratified 1868):** made everyone born in the United States a citizen and promised all citizens equal protection of the laws.
- **15th Amendment (ratified 1870):** said the right to vote could not be denied because of race, colour or previous condition of servitude.
:::

## Presidential Reconstruction, 1865-1866

Lincoln had planned a lenient approach to bring the South back quickly, but he was assassinated in April 1865. His successor, **Andrew Johnson**, a Southern Democrat from Tennessee who had stayed loyal to the Union, pardoned most former Confederates and allowed Southern states to form new governments once they accepted the end of slavery.

These governments, controlled by white Southerners, passed **Black Codes**. Freedpeople who could not prove they had a job could be arrested for vagrancy and hired out to employers; some codes limited where Black people could own or rent land. Many former Confederate leaders were elected to Congress. Northern voters were angered: it seemed that the South was trying to restore slavery in all but name.

## Radical Reconstruction, 1867-1877

Congress, led by the **Radical Republicans**, took control.

- It passed the **Civil Rights Act of 1866** over Johnson's veto and proposed the **14th Amendment**.
- The **Reconstruction Acts of 1867** divided most of the South into five military districts. To return to Congress, each state had to write a new constitution giving Black men the vote and ratify the 14th Amendment.
- Johnson opposed Congress at every step. In 1868 the House of Representatives impeached him, but the Senate acquitted him by a single vote.
- The **15th Amendment** (1870) protected the vote from racial discrimination.

The **Freedmen's Bureau** helped to set up thousands of schools, and Black churches and colleges grew rapidly. Black men voted in large numbers, and for the first time hundreds were elected to state legislatures and some to Congress; Hiram Revels of Mississippi became the first Black US senator in 1870. New state governments built public schools and hospitals for both races.

## Violence and the end of Reconstruction

White Southerners who opposed Black rights formed secret organisations. The **Ku Klux Klan**, founded in Tennessee in 1865-66, used threats, beatings and murder to stop Black people voting and to drive out their white allies. Congress passed **Enforcement Acts** in 1870-71, and federal action broke up the first Klan, but other armed groups continued the violence in the 1870s.

Northern support for Reconstruction weakened. Many Northerners were tired of the "Southern question", an economic depression began in 1873, and corruption scandals damaged the Republicans. One by one, Southern state governments were taken over by white conservative Democrats, who called themselves "Redeemers". The disputed presidential election of 1876 was settled by an agreement under which the Republican, Rutherford B. Hayes, became President and the remaining federal troops were withdrawn from Southern state capitals in **1877**. Reconstruction was over.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why Reconstruction ended in 1877"}
1. **Southern resistance:** violence by the Klan and other groups intimidated Black voters and Republicans.
2. **Northern weariness:** after the depression of 1873 and corruption scandals, Northern voters cared more about the economy than the South.
3. **Redeemer governments:** white Democrats regained control of state after state.
4. **The 1876 election:** the compromise that made Hayes President ended the use of federal troops to protect Republican governments.
5. **Result:** Southern states were left to white control, and Black rights were steadily removed.
:::

## Did the war and Reconstruction change anything?

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: success or failure?"}
**Real change:** slavery was abolished for ever by the 13th Amendment. The Union was preserved, and the federal government became stronger. The 14th and 15th Amendments wrote citizenship and equal rights into the Constitution; in the twentieth century the civil rights movement used them to win equality in law. Freedpeople built families, churches and schools, and literacy rose.
**Limited change:** most freedpeople received no land, and many became trapped in sharecropping and debt. After 1877 Southern states used poll taxes, literacy tests and violence to stop most Black men voting, and passed segregation ("Jim Crow") laws. The promises of the amendments were not kept for almost a century.
**Judgement:** the war ended slavery and changed the Constitution, a revolutionary result, but Reconstruction failed to secure equality, so its success was partial.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the amendments"}
13th: abolition of slavery. 14th: citizenship and equal protection. 15th: voting rights regardless of race. Learn them in order.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Reconstruction gave freedpeople land"}
There was no general redistribution of land. Most freedpeople remained landless and many became sharecroppers.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Johnson was removed from office"}
Johnson was impeached by the House of Representatives in 1868 but acquitted by the Senate, so he stayed President until 1869.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (b): 'Explain why Reconstruction came to an end'"}
Give developed reasons: Southern violence, Northern loss of interest, economic depression, the Redeemers and the 1877 compromise. Use dates to show the sequence.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (c): 'Did the Civil War change anything?'"}
Balance lasting changes (abolition, amendments, a stronger federal government) against what did not change (land, voting rights after 1877, segregation). A supported judgement that distinguishes legal change from everyday life scores well.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Reconstruction (1865-1877) aimed to restore the Union and decide the rights of about four million freedpeople.
- Andrew Johnson's lenient policy allowed Southern states to pass Black Codes, which provoked Radical Reconstruction from 1867.
- The 13th (1865), 14th (1868) and 15th (1870) Amendments abolished slavery and wrote citizenship and voting rights into the Constitution.
- Klan violence, Northern weariness and the 1877 compromise ended Reconstruction, and Black rights in the South were steadily removed.
- The war ended slavery permanently, but equality was not achieved for almost a century.
:::
