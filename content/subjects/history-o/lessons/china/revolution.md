## Introduction

On 1 October 1949 Mao Zedong stood in Tiananmen Square in Beijing and proclaimed the People's Republic of China. The Communists had won a long struggle against the Nationalist Guomindang, largely by winning the support of China's peasants. Their ideas about guerrilla warfare and peasant support influenced liberation movements across the world, including ZANU and ZANLA, whose fighters were trained by Chinese instructors in the 1960s and 1970s.

This lesson covers the **causes** of the revolution, the **Guomindang** and the **Communist Party**, the **struggle** between them from 1925 to 1949, how the Communists **won the peasants' support**, and **why the Communists won**. China under Mao after 1949 is the next lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Qing dynasty"}
The last imperial dynasty of China, which ruled from 1644 until the emperor abdicated in 1912.
:::

:::definition{term="Warlord"}
A military commander who controlled a region with his own army, independent of any national government. Warlords dominated China from 1916 to 1928.
:::

:::definition{term="Guomindang (GMD) or Kuomintang (KMT)"}
The Nationalist Party, founded by Sun Yat-sen and later led by Chiang Kai-shek (Jiang Jieshi).
:::

:::definition{term="Chinese Communist Party (CCP)"}
The party founded in 1921 that, under Mao Zedong's leadership from the mid-1930s, won power in 1949.
:::

:::definition{term="Guerrilla warfare"}
Fighting by small, mobile groups that avoid large battles, attack weak points and rely on the support of the local population.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- 1911-1912: revolution ends the Qing dynasty; China becomes a republic.
- 1921: Chinese Communist Party founded in Shanghai.
- 1927: the Guomindang turns on the Communists (Shanghai, April).
- 1934-1935: the Long March.
- 1937-1945: war with Japan.
- 1946-1949: civil war.
- 1 October 1949: People's Republic of China proclaimed.
:::

## Causes of the revolution

1. **The weakness and fall of the Qing:** defeats in the Opium Wars (1839-1842 and 1856-1860) and by Japan (1894-1895) humiliated the dynasty. A revolution that began in October 1911 forced the boy emperor Puyi to abdicate in February 1912.
2. **Foreign domination:** Britain, France, Germany, Russia, Japan and others forced "unequal treaties" on China, controlled ports and trade, and had special legal privileges.
3. **Warlords:** after the death of Yuan Shikai in 1916, China broke up into regions ruled by warlords whose armies taxed and plundered the people.
4. **Peasant poverty:** most Chinese were peasants. Many rented land from landlords who took a large share of the harvest; they also faced high taxes, debt, famine and floods.
5. **New ideas:** the May Fourth Movement of 1919, a protest against the Treaty of Versailles giving Germany's rights in Shandong to Japan, spread nationalist and radical ideas among students and workers, including Marxism after the Russian Revolution.

## The two parties

| | Guomindang (Nationalists) | Communist Party |
|---|---|---|
| Founded | 1912 (from earlier revolutionary groups) | 1921, Shanghai |
| Leaders | Sun Yat-sen; from 1925 Chiang Kai-shek | Several leaders; Mao Zedong became dominant from 1935 |
| Ideas | Sun's Three Principles of the People: nationalism, democracy, people's livelihood | Marxism adapted to China: revolution led by peasants as well as workers |
| Support | Landlords, businessmen, the army, the middle class | Peasants, some workers and students |

From 1923 the two parties co-operated in the **First United Front**, with Soviet advice, to defeat the warlords.

## The struggle, 1925-1949

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1925 | Sun Yat-sen dies; Chiang Kai-shek rises to lead the Guomindang |
| 1926-1928 | Northern Expedition against the warlords |
| April 1927 | Chiang turns on the Communists in Shanghai; thousands killed |
| 1928 | Guomindang government established at Nanjing |
| 1931 | Chinese Soviet Republic proclaimed in Jiangxi; Japan seizes Manchuria |
| 1930-1934 | Guomindang encirclement campaigns against the Jiangxi Soviet |
| October 1934-October 1935 | The Long March to Shaanxi |
| December 1936 | Xi'an Incident: Chiang forced to agree to a united front against Japan |
| July 1937 | Full-scale war with Japan begins |
| 1945 | Japan surrenders |
| 1946-1949 | Civil war |
| 1 October 1949 | People's Republic of China proclaimed |

- **The 1927 split:** during the Northern Expedition Chiang, backed by businessmen and landlords, turned on the Communists, killing thousands in Shanghai and other cities. The Communists' attempts to seize cities failed.
- **The Jiangxi Soviet (1931-1934):** Mao and Zhu De built a base in the countryside, redistributed land and formed the Red Army. Chiang launched encirclement campaigns; the fifth used a ring of blockhouses and forced the Communists to break out.
- **The Long March (1934-1935):** the Red Army marched for about a year through some of China's hardest terrain to Shaanxi in the north-west. Of roughly 80,000 to 90,000 who set out from Jiangxi, historians estimate that fewer than 10,000 completed the march. At the **Zunyi Conference** (January 1935) Mao gained a leading position. The march became a powerful legend of Communist endurance.
- **Yan'an and the war with Japan:** from their base at Yan'an the Communists fought a guerrilla war behind Japanese lines and expanded their territory. After the **Xi'an Incident** (December 1936) the Guomindang agreed to a second united front against Japan, but the alliance was always uneasy. The Guomindang bore the main conventional fighting and suffered heavy losses.
- **The civil war (1946-1949):** after Japan's surrender, talks failed and war resumed. Despite US aid to the Nationalists, the People's Liberation Army (PLA) won great victories in Manchuria and central China in 1948-1949, took Beijing in January 1949 and crossed the Yangzi. Chiang and his government withdrew to Taiwan.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: how the Communists won peasant support"}
1. **Need:** peasants were the vast majority, crushed by rents, taxes and debt.
2. **Policy:** in Communist areas landlords' land was redistributed (or, during the war with Japan, rents and interest were cut), and peasants joined local councils.
3. **Behaviour:** the Red Army's rules of discipline told soldiers to pay for what they took, return what they borrowed and treat peasants with respect, unlike warlord and many Guomindang troops.
4. **Patriotism:** the Communists' guerrilla war against Japan presented them as defenders of the nation.
5. **Result:** peasants supplied food, recruits and intelligence, which a guerrilla army needed to survive and grow.
:::

## The role of the peasants

Peasants were the base of the revolution. They provided soldiers for the Red Army and later the PLA, food and shelter for guerrillas, information about enemy movements, and labour to carry supplies in the civil war. Mao's idea that a revolution in China would come from the countryside, not mainly from city workers as in Russia, was one of his key contributions.

## Why the Communists won

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: Communist strengths or Guomindang weaknesses?"}
1. **Communist strengths:** peasant support through land reform and good discipline; Mao's leadership and guerrilla strategy; a reputation for fighting Japan; effective organisation and propaganda.
2. **Guomindang weaknesses:** corruption; hyperinflation in the late 1940s that ruined the middle class; dependence on landlords and failure to carry out land reform; harsh conscription; heavy losses against Japan; poor generalship in the civil war.
3. **Outside factors:** the war with Japan weakened the Guomindang and gave the Communists room to grow; Soviet forces in Manchuria in 1945 helped the Communists obtain captured Japanese weapons; US aid could not save the Nationalists.
4. **Judgement:** historians weigh these differently; many argue that Guomindang weaknesses and the war with Japan gave the Communists their opportunity, and Communist strengths allowed them to take it.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Treating the Long March as a victory"}
The Long March was a retreat with enormous losses. It became a powerful symbol, and Mao rose during it, but militarily it was an escape.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing Sun Yat-sen and Chiang Kai-shek"}
Sun founded the Guomindang and died in 1925. Chiang led it from then and fought the Communists.
:::

:::mistake{title="Narrating instead of explaining"}
For "why did the Communists win?", organise the answer by factors and explain each, rather than telling the story year by year.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Causes: the fall of the Qing (1911-1912), foreign domination, warlords, peasant poverty and new ideas.
- The Guomindang (Sun Yat-sen, then Chiang Kai-shek) and the Communists co-operated until 1927, then fought.
- Key stages: Jiangxi Soviet, the Long March (1934-1935), Yan'an and the war with Japan (1937-1945), and the civil war (1946-1949).
- The Communists won peasant support through land reform, discipline and resistance to Japan; peasants supplied soldiers, food and intelligence.
- The People's Republic was proclaimed on 1 October 1949; the Nationalists withdrew to Taiwan.
:::
