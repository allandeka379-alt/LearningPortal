## Introduction

In 1949 the Communists took over a country exhausted by decades of war. Over the next 27 years Mao Zedong tried to turn China into a strong, industrial, socialist country. His government achieved real changes in land ownership, the position of women, education and health. It also launched campaigns that caused enormous suffering: the famine after the Great Leap Forward was one of the deadliest in history. Both sides of this record matter, and examiners expect you to weigh them.

You should already know how the Communists won power in 1949. This lesson covers **China's problems in 1949** and the **policies** used to deal with them, **social changes**, and an **assessment** of Mao's rule.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Collectivisation"}
Combining small private farms into large collective farms owned and worked jointly, under state control.
:::

:::definition{term="Five-Year Plan"}
A government plan setting production targets for industry and agriculture over five years, copied from the Soviet Union.
:::

:::definition{term="People's commune"}
A huge unit created in 1958 that combined many villages; it organised farming, industry, schools, militia and communal kitchens.
:::

:::definition{term="Red Guards"}
Groups of students and young people who, from 1966, attacked teachers, officials and traditional culture in Mao's name during the Cultural Revolution.
:::

:::definition{term="Cult of personality"}
The use of propaganda to present a leader as a heroic, almost god-like figure who must not be criticised.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- 1950: Agrarian Reform Law and Marriage Law.
- 1953-1957: First Five-Year Plan.
- 1956-1957: Hundred Flowers campaign, followed by the Anti-Rightist Campaign.
- 1958-1962: Great Leap Forward; famine about 1959-1961.
- 1966-1976: Cultural Revolution.
- 9 September 1976: death of Mao.
:::

## China's problems in 1949

- **Political:** the Nationalists still held Taiwan; parts of the country were unsettled, with bandits and opponents of the new regime.
- **Economic:** industry, railways and farms had been wrecked by war; hyperinflation; most land was owned by landlords.
- **Social:** most people could not read; disease, opium addiction and poverty were widespread; women had few rights.
- **International:** the United States refused to recognise the new government; from 1950 China fought in the Korean War.

### Land reform (1950-1952)

Under the **Agrarian Reform Law (1950)** landlords' land, animals and tools were taken and given to poor peasants. Work teams held village "speak bitterness" meetings where peasants accused landlords. Many landlords were beaten or killed; historians' estimates of the number killed vary widely, commonly from about one million to two million or more. Some 300 million peasants gained land.

### The First Five-Year Plan (1953-1957)

With Soviet loans, advisers and equipment, the plan concentrated on **heavy industry**: steel, coal, electricity, machinery and railways. Output grew fast, and new industrial cities developed. At the same time peasants were moved step by step into **co-operatives** and then larger collectives.

### The Hundred Flowers campaign (1956-1957)

Mao invited intellectuals to criticise the Party ("Let a hundred flowers bloom"). The criticism was much sharper than expected. In the **Anti-Rightist Campaign (1957)** hundreds of thousands of critics were labelled "rightists" and lost their jobs or were sent to labour camps. Historians still debate whether Mao planned this as a trap or changed course when the criticism alarmed him.

### The Great Leap Forward (1958-1962)

Mao aimed to overtake Britain's industrial output within about fifteen years by mobilising the people.

- **People's communes** replaced the collectives; private plots were abolished and families ate in communal kitchens.
- **Backyard furnaces:** millions of people tried to make steel in small furnaces; much of it was useless, and farm work was neglected.
- **Unscientific farming methods,** such as very close planting and deep ploughing, and a campaign that killed sparrows (allowing insects to multiply), damaged harvests.
- **Exaggerated reports:** officials reported impossible harvests, so the state took grain that villages needed, and grain was even exported.

The result was a terrible **famine** from about 1959 to 1961. Historians' estimates of excess deaths range from about 15 million to more than 40 million; many cite figures of around 30 million. When Defence Minister Peng Dehuai criticised the policy at the **Lushan Conference (1959)**, he was dismissed. In the early 1960s Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping led a recovery, allowing private plots and incentives again.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why the Great Leap led to famine"}
1. **Policy:** peasants were moved into communes and many were sent to make steel or work on projects instead of farming.
2. **Methods:** close planting, deep ploughing and the sparrow campaign reduced yields.
3. **False reporting:** officials who feared being called "rightists" reported huge harvests.
4. **Procurement:** the state took grain based on these false figures, leaving villages without food.
5. **Result:** mass starvation, worst in provinces such as Anhui, Henan and Sichuan; bad weather in some areas made it worse, but historians agree that policy was the main cause.
:::

### The Cultural Revolution (1966-1976)

Mao, sidelined after the famine, launched the **Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution** in 1966 to regain control and to purge the Party of people he accused of following a "capitalist road".

- **Red Guards**, waving the "Little Red Book" of Mao's quotations, attacked the "Four Olds" (old customs, culture, habits and ideas), destroying temples and books and humiliating teachers and officials.
- Liu Shaoqi was purged and died in custody in 1969; Deng Xiaoping was sent to work in a factory.
- Schools and universities were closed for years; millions of young people were sent to the countryside to learn from the peasants.
- The army restored order from 1968; struggles at the top continued, including the death of Lin Biao in 1971 and the rise of the radical "Gang of Four".
- Estimates of deaths range from several hundred thousand to well over a million, and millions more were persecuted.

## Social changes

- **Women:** the **Marriage Law (1950)** banned arranged and child marriages and concubinage and allowed women to seek divorce; women entered paid work in large numbers, although inequality remained.
- **Education:** literacy campaigns and simplified characters greatly raised literacy, though the Cultural Revolution disrupted schooling for a decade.
- **Health:** campaigns against diseases and opium; from the late 1960s "barefoot doctors" brought basic care to villages. Life expectancy rose substantially, according to most estimates from the mid-thirties around 1949 to the mid-sixties by the late 1970s.

## Assessment of Mao's rule

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: achievements and costs"}
1. **Achievements:** China was unified and independent; land reform ended landlord power; industry grew; women's legal status, literacy and life expectancy improved; China tested an atomic bomb (1964), took the UN seat in 1971 and gained international recognition.
2. **Costs:** millions died in land reform, the Great Leap famine and the Cultural Revolution; free speech was crushed; the cult of personality allowed disastrous mistakes to go unchecked.
3. **Official verdict:** in 1981 the Communist Party itself judged that Mao's achievements outweighed his mistakes, while condemning the Cultural Revolution.
4. **Historians' views:** many historians judge the human cost far more harshly. A balanced answer sets the gains against the very high human costs and reaches its own reasoned judgement.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Giving death tolls as exact facts"}
Write "historians estimate between about 15 and 45 million excess deaths in the famine", not one exact number.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution"}
The Great Leap (1958-1962) was an economic campaign that caused famine. The Cultural Revolution (1966-1976) was a political and cultural campaign led by Red Guards.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 2: evaluate how the Communists dealt with China's problems"}
Organise by problem (political, economic, social), give the policy used for each, then judge how far it worked and at what cost.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Use balance"}
"How successful was Mao?" needs achievements and costs with evidence, and a judgement that weighs them; one-sided praise or condemnation scores lower.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- In 1949 China faced war damage, inflation, landlord power, illiteracy, disease and international isolation.
- Land reform (1950) and the First Five-Year Plan (1953-1957) transformed land ownership and heavy industry.
- The Hundred Flowers campaign ended in the Anti-Rightist Campaign (1957).
- The Great Leap Forward (1958-1962) caused a famine in which historians estimate tens of millions died.
- The Cultural Revolution (1966-1976) brought chaos and persecution; social changes for women, education and health were real but uneven; Mao's rule combined major achievements with very high human costs.
:::
