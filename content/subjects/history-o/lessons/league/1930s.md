## Introduction

In the 1930s the League of Nations faced aggression by two great powers that sat on its own Council: Japan and Italy. Each time it investigated, condemned and debated, and each time the aggressor kept what it had seized. By 1939 the League was ignored by almost everyone.

For Africans, the most important of these crises was the Italian invasion of **Abyssinia** (Ethiopia) in 1935. Ethiopia was one of the very few African states not under colonial rule and had defeated an Italian army at Adwa in 1896. Its Emperor, **Haile Selassie**, appealed to the League in person in Geneva in June 1936. The League's failure to protect him angered Africans and people of African descent across the world and strengthened the growing Pan-African movement.

You should already know how the League was organised and its record in the 1920s.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Great Depression"}
The worldwide economic slump that followed the Wall Street Crash of October 1929, with collapsing trade, falling prices and mass unemployment.
:::

:::definition{term="Puppet state"}
A state that appears independent but is controlled by another power. Manchukuo was a Japanese puppet state.
:::

:::definition{term="Lytton Report"}
The report of the League's commission of inquiry into Manchuria, led by Britain's Lord Lytton and published in October 1932.
:::

:::definition{term="Hoare-Laval Pact"}
A secret plan of December 1935 by the British and French foreign ministers, Samuel Hoare and Pierre Laval, to give Italy much of Abyssinia in return for ending the war.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- October 1929: Wall Street Crash.
- 18 September 1931: Mukden incident; Japan begins to occupy Manchuria.
- February-March 1933: League accepts the Lytton Report; Japan announces it is leaving.
- December 1934: Walwal incident.
- 3 October 1935: Italy invades Abyssinia.
- May 1936: Italian troops take Addis Ababa; sanctions lifted in July.
:::

## How the Depression made the League's work harder

- **Aggressive nationalism:** in Japan, Germany and Italy, economic hardship strengthened leaders who promised to solve problems through expansion and military power.
- **Self-interest:** Britain and France were struggling with unemployment and were unwilling to impose sanctions that would hurt their own trade or to pay for military action.
- **Trade barriers:** countries raised tariffs to protect their industries, undermining the League's aim of co-operation.
- **Disarmament failed:** the World Disarmament Conference (1932-1934) achieved nothing; Germany left it and the League in October 1933.

## The Manchurian crisis, 1931-1933

**Causes:** Japan's economy depended on exports, especially silk, and the Depression hit it hard. Japan already controlled the South Manchuria Railway and had troops (the Kwantung Army) guarding it. Manchuria offered raw materials, land and markets, and the army had growing influence over the government.

**Events:** On **18 September 1931** an explosion damaged the railway near Mukden. Japanese officers blamed Chinese saboteurs, though most historians agree that Japanese officers staged it. The Kwantung Army occupied the whole of Manchuria and in 1932 set up the puppet state of **Manchukuo** under Puyi, the last Chinese emperor. China appealed to the League, which sent the **Lytton Commission**. Its report, published in October 1932, found that Japan had acted wrongly and that Manchuria should be returned to Chinese rule. In February 1933 the Assembly accepted the report; the Japanese delegation walked out, and in March 1933 Japan announced that it was leaving the League.

**Why no action?** Sanctions were not imposed. Britain and France did not want to lose trade with Japan or risk a war in Asia, the USA (Japan's main trading partner) was not a member, and the League took a year to report.

**Consequences:** Japan kept Manchuria and invaded the rest of China in 1937. The League had shown that a great power could commit aggression and suffer nothing worse than criticism.

## The Abyssinian crisis, 1935-1936

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| March 1896 | Ethiopia defeats Italy at Adwa |
| December 1934 | Clash at Walwal between Italian and Ethiopian troops |
| April 1935 | Stresa Front: Britain, France and Italy agree to oppose German rearmament |
| 3 October 1935 | Italy invades Abyssinia |
| October-November 1935 | League votes for economic sanctions, excluding oil |
| December 1935 | Hoare-Laval Pact leaked; Hoare resigns |
| March 1936 | Hitler remilitarises the Rhineland |
| May 1936 | Italian forces capture Addis Ababa |
| June-July 1936 | Haile Selassie addresses the League; sanctions lifted |

**Causes:** Mussolini wanted an empire to show Italian greatness, to avenge Adwa and to link Italy's colonies of Eritrea and Italian Somaliland. Abyssinia seemed weak and lightly armed.

**Events:** After the Walwal clash, Haile Selassie appealed to the League, while Mussolini built up his forces. Britain and France, anxious to keep Italy as an ally against Hitler after the **Stresa Front**, delayed. Italy invaded on **3 October 1935**. The League quickly condemned Italy and imposed sanctions, but these did not include oil, coal or steel, and Britain did not close the Suez Canal to Italian supply ships. Italian forces used aircraft, tanks and poison gas. The leaked **Hoare-Laval Pact** showed that Britain and France were prepared to reward the aggressor. Addis Ababa fell in May 1936, and in July the League lifted its sanctions.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why sanctions against Italy failed"}
1. **Oil was not banned.** Members feared that an oil ban would provoke Mussolini to war, and the USA, a major supplier, was not a member.
2. **The Suez Canal stayed open,** so Italian troops and supplies kept flowing to East Africa.
3. **Britain and France wanted Italy as an ally** against Germany, so they sought a deal (Hoare-Laval) rather than a showdown.
4. **The sanctions were too slow and too weak** to stop a war that lasted only seven months.

Result: the aggressor won, and the League's main weapon was shown to be ineffective.
:::

**Consequences:** Abyssinia was annexed to Italy. Mussolini moved closer to Hitler (the Rome-Berlin Axis, 1936) and left the League in 1937. Hitler used the distraction to remilitarise the Rhineland in March 1936. Collective security was finished.

## Why had the League collapsed by 1939?

- The Depression encouraged aggression and made members unwilling to act.
- Great powers left: Japan (1933), Germany (1933), Italy (1937).
- Britain and France put national interests first and turned to **appeasement** outside the League; the Munich Agreement of 1938 was made without it.
- The League had no army, and its sanctions failed when they were tested.
- The USA never joined, and the USSR was expelled in December 1939.

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: Manchuria or Abyssinia?"}
Some historians see **Manchuria** as the turning point, since it was the first time a great power got away with aggression. Others argue that **Abyssinia** was more damaging, because it was close to Europe, sanctions were actually tried and failed, and Britain and France were caught secretly bargaining with the aggressor. A strong answer explains why each could be seen as decisive and reaches a judgement.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the League did nothing over Manchuria"}
It investigated, published the Lytton Report and condemned Japan. The failure was that it imposed no sanctions.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting why oil was left out"}
Oil sanctions were not applied because members feared war with Italy and the USA would have kept selling oil.
:::

:::mistake{title="Wrong dates"}
Manchuria began in September 1931; Italy invaded Abyssinia in October 1935, not 1936.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Explain why the League failed over Abyssinia"}
In a Cambridge Paper 1 (b) or ZIMSEC (b) answer, give several reasons, each developed: weak sanctions, the Suez Canal, Hoare-Laval, Britain and France's fear of Germany, the absence of the USA.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="How far was the Depression to blame?"}
For a "how far" question, show how the Depression caused aggression and weakened the will to act, then weigh it against structural weaknesses and the choices of Britain and France.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Depression encouraged aggression and made Britain and France unwilling to act.
- Japan seized Manchuria (1931); the Lytton Report condemned it, but there were no sanctions and Japan left the League (1933).
- Italy invaded Abyssinia (1935); sanctions excluded oil, the Suez Canal stayed open and the Hoare-Laval Pact discredited the League.
- Italy took Addis Ababa (May 1936); sanctions were lifted and Mussolini joined Hitler.
- By 1939 aggressors had left, appeasement had replaced collective security, and the League was ignored.
:::
