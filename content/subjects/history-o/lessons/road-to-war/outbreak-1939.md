## Introduction

On 1 September 1939 German forces invaded Poland. Two days later Britain and France declared war on Germany. Within weeks Southern Rhodesia, as part of the British Empire, was at war too; Rhodesian airmen and soldiers, black and white, would serve in East Africa, North Africa, Italy and Burma.

Why did a crisis over Danzig and the Polish Corridor become a world war, when Britain and France had given way over Austria and Czechoslovakia only a year earlier? This lesson explains the events of 1939 and then asks the big question: **who, or what, was to blame?**

You should already know Hitler's aims, the remilitarisation of the Rhineland, the Anschluss and the Munich Agreement.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Guarantee"}
A public promise to defend a country if it is attacked. Britain and France guaranteed Poland on 31 March 1939.
:::

:::definition{term="Non-aggression pact"}
An agreement between two states not to attack each other.
:::

:::definition{term="Secret protocol"}
A hidden part of a treaty. The secret protocol of the Nazi-Soviet Pact divided Poland and eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres of influence.
:::

:::definition{term="Ultimatum"}
A final demand with a deadline, after which the sender will take action, such as declaring war.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- 15 March 1939: German troops occupy Bohemia and Moravia.
- 31 March 1939: British and French guarantee to Poland.
- 23 August 1939: Nazi-Soviet Pact.
- 1 September 1939: Germany invades Poland.
- 3 September 1939: Britain and France declare war.
- 17 September 1939: Soviet forces invade eastern Poland.
:::

## The end of appeasement: March 1939

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 15 March 1939 | German troops occupy Bohemia and Moravia; Slovakia becomes a German-dependent state |
| March 1939 | Germany takes Memel from Lithuania |
| 31 March 1939 | Britain and France guarantee Poland's independence |
| April 1939 | Italy invades Albania; guarantees to Romania and Greece |
| May 1939 | Pact of Steel between Germany and Italy |
| August 1939 | Anglo-French military talks with the USSR fail |
| 23 August 1939 | Nazi-Soviet Pact signed |
| 25 August 1939 | Britain signs a formal alliance with Poland |
| 1 September 1939 | Germany invades Poland |
| 3 September 1939 | Britain and France declare war |

On **15 March 1939** German troops entered Prague. For the first time Hitler had seized land where few Germans lived, so he could no longer claim to be uniting German speakers. Chamberlain now accepted that Hitler could not be trusted. On **31 March** Britain and France guaranteed Poland, which Hitler was now pressing over **Danzig** and a German road and railway across the **Polish Corridor**. Britain introduced conscription for young men in spring 1939 and speeded up rearmament.

## The Nazi-Soviet Pact, 23 August 1939

Britain and France opened talks with the USSR in 1939, but they moved slowly. The Anglo-French military mission travelled to Moscow by ship in August and had limited powers. Poland refused to allow Soviet troops onto its territory, even to fight Germany.

Meanwhile Germany offered Stalin a deal. On **23 August 1939** the foreign ministers Ribbentrop and Molotov signed a **non-aggression pact**. A **secret protocol** divided Poland between Germany and the USSR and placed the Baltic states and Bessarabia in the Soviet sphere.

:::example{title="A step-by-step explanation: why did Stalin sign?"}
1. **Distrust of the West:** Stalin had been left out of Munich and suspected that Britain and France wanted Germany to turn east against him.
2. **The talks went badly:** the Western mission was slow and junior, and Poland would not let Soviet troops in.
3. **Time:** the Red Army had been weakened by Stalin's purges, and peace with Germany bought time to rebuild.
4. **Territory:** the secret protocol offered eastern Poland and a sphere of influence in the Baltic.

**For Hitler** the pact meant he could attack Poland without fearing a war on two fronts.
:::

## Invasion and declarations of war

Hitler expected that, without Soviet help, Britain and France would not fight for Poland. On 25 August Britain turned its guarantee into a formal alliance with Poland. German forces invaded Poland on **1 September 1939**. Britain sent Germany an ultimatum to withdraw; when it expired on the morning of **3 September**, Britain declared war, and France followed later that day. On 17 September Soviet forces invaded eastern Poland, as the secret protocol had planned.

**Why did Britain and France fight for Poland after giving way over Czechoslovakia?**

- After Prague, it was clear that Hitler's aims were not limited.
- Britain and France had publicly guaranteed Poland; breaking the promise would destroy their credibility.
- Public opinion had changed: most British people now accepted that war was necessary.
- Britain was better prepared in 1939, especially in air defence.

## Who was to blame?

| Factor | Case for blaming it | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Hitler's aims and actions | Lebensraum, rearmament, the occupation of Prague and the invasion of Poland | Others made his actions easier |
| The Treaty of Versailles | Created German grievances and gave Hitler a cause | Germany was not bound to choose war; the treaty had been revised in Germany's favour by 1939 |
| The failure of the League | Manchuria and Abyssinia showed aggression went unpunished | The League depended on its members, especially Britain and France |
| Appeasement | Encouraged Hitler to believe that the West would not fight | Britain was not ready in 1938; appeasement ended in March 1939 |
| The Nazi-Soviet Pact | Removed the threat of a two-front war, so Hitler felt free to attack | Hitler had already ordered plans for the invasion of Poland in April 1939 |

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: Hitler's war?"}
In 1961 the British historian A. J. P. Taylor argued that Hitler was largely an opportunist who took chances offered by others, and that war came partly through miscalculation. Most historians since have given more weight to Hitler's aims of expansion in the east, while accepting that the mistakes of other powers made his path easier. A strong answer explains both views and judges which factor turned a dangerous situation into war in September 1939.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the USSR was Germany's ally throughout the war"}
The pact was a non-aggression pact. Germany invaded the USSR in June 1941.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the secret protocol"}
The public pact promised peace; the secret protocol divided Poland. Both parts matter for explaining the invasion.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing causes without judging them"}
"Who was to blame" questions need a judgement. Explain how the factors are linked, for example how appeasement and the Pact both encouraged Hitler to think he could attack Poland safely.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="How important was the Nazi-Soviet Pact? (Cambridge Paper 1 (c))"}
Explain what the Pact changed (no two-front war, Poland isolated), then compare it with long-term factors such as Hitler's aims and appeasement, and reach a judgement on its importance.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Explain why Britain and France declared war in September 1939"}
For an explain question, link each reason to the situation in 1939: the occupation of Prague, the guarantee to Poland, changed public opinion and better preparation.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Germany's occupation of Bohemia and Moravia (15 March 1939) ended appeasement; Britain and France guaranteed Poland (31 March).
- Anglo-French talks with the USSR failed; the Nazi-Soviet Pact (23 August 1939) secretly divided Poland.
- Germany invaded Poland on 1 September; Britain and France declared war on 3 September; the USSR invaded on 17 September.
- Blame is debated: Hitler's aims, Versailles, the League's failure, appeasement and the Pact all played a part.
- Most historians see Hitler's expansionist aims as the main cause, made easier by the mistakes of others.
:::
