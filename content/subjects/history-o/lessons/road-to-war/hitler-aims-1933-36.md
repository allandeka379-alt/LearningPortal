## Introduction

Adolf Hitler became Chancellor of Germany on 30 January 1933. Within three years he had rebuilt the German armed forces, recovered the Saar, sent troops into the Rhineland and found allies in Italy and Japan, all in defiance of the Treaty of Versailles and without firing a shot against the Western powers.

Why should a student in Zimbabwe study this? Because the war that followed drew in the whole world, including Africa. Thousands of Africans from Southern Rhodesia and the rest of the continent served in the Second World War, and the war weakened the colonial powers so much that it speeded up the end of empire. Understanding how it began starts with Hitler's aims.

You should already know the terms of the Treaty of Versailles and why the League of Nations failed in the 1930s.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Lebensraum"}
"Living space": Hitler's aim of gaining land in eastern Europe, especially Poland and the Soviet Union, for German settlers, food and raw materials.
:::

:::definition{term="Rearmament"}
Building up armed forces again. Germany's rearmament broke the military terms of the Treaty of Versailles.
:::

:::definition{term="Remilitarisation"}
Sending armed forces back into an area that had been demilitarised, such as the Rhineland in March 1936.
:::

:::definition{term="Anti-Comintern Pact"}
An agreement between Germany and Japan (November 1936), later joined by Italy (1937), to oppose the Communist International (Comintern) and the spread of communism.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- 30 January 1933: Hitler becomes Chancellor.
- October 1933: Germany leaves the Disarmament Conference and the League.
- January 1935: Saar plebiscite.
- March 1935: conscription announced and the Luftwaffe revealed.
- 7 March 1936: troops enter the Rhineland.
- July 1936: Spanish Civil War begins.
- November 1936: Rome-Berlin Axis announced; Anti-Comintern Pact signed.
:::

## Hitler's aims

Hitler set out his ideas in *Mein Kampf*, written in the mid-1920s. His foreign policy aims were:

1. **To overturn the Treaty of Versailles**: rebuild the armed forces, recover lost land and end reparations.
2. **To unite all German speakers** in one Greater Germany, including those in Austria, the Sudetenland and Poland.
3. **To win Lebensraum** in the east at the expense of Poland and the Soviet Union.
4. **To destroy communism**, which he linked to his antisemitic beliefs.

Historians debate whether Hitler followed a step-by-step plan or was an opportunist who took chances as they came. Many conclude that his aims were consistent but his methods and timing were flexible.

## Early steps, 1933-1935

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| October 1933 | Germany leaves the Disarmament Conference and the League |
| January 1934 | Ten-year non-aggression pact with Poland |
| July 1934 | Austrian Nazis murder Chancellor Dollfuss; Mussolini moves troops to the Austrian border |
| 13 January 1935 | Saar plebiscite: about 90 per cent vote to rejoin Germany |
| March 1935 | Conscription reintroduced; the Luftwaffe revealed |
| April 1935 | Stresa Front: Britain, France and Italy protest |
| June 1935 | Anglo-German Naval Agreement |
| 7 March 1936 | Remilitarisation of the Rhineland |
| November 1936 | Rome-Berlin Axis; Anti-Comintern Pact |

- **Leaving the League (1933):** Hitler claimed that Germany was being denied equal treatment at the Disarmament Conference.
- **Pact with Poland (1934):** this reassured the West that Hitler was peaceful and weakened France's eastern alliances.
- **Austria (1934):** an attempted Nazi coup failed when Mussolini sent troops to the Brenner Pass. Hitler backed down; he was not yet strong enough.
- **The Saar (1935):** under the Treaty of Versailles the Saar had been run by the League for fifteen years. In the plebiscite of January 1935 about 90 per cent of voters chose to rejoin Germany, a legal and very popular success.

### Rearmament

Rearmament began secretly in 1933. In March 1935 Hitler announced that Germany had an air force and that conscription would be introduced to build an army of 36 divisions, roughly 550,000 men. Rearmament also reduced unemployment.

Britain, France and Italy formed the **Stresa Front** in April 1935 to protest. Just two months later Britain signed the **Anglo-German Naval Agreement**, allowing Germany a surface fleet of up to 35 per cent of Britain's. Britain had accepted a breach of Versailles without consulting France or Italy, and the Stresa Front collapsed when Italy invaded Abyssinia that October.

:::example{title="Why did Britain accept German rearmament? A step-by-step explanation"}
1. Many British people believed the Treaty of Versailles had been too harsh, so German rearmament seemed understandable.
2. Britain was more worried about the naval threat than about the German army, so an agreed limit on the German navy looked like a gain.
3. Britain could not afford an arms race during the Depression.
4. Some saw a strong Germany as a barrier against Soviet communism.

Result: Britain chose to manage German rearmament rather than stop it, which encouraged Hitler to take further risks.
:::

## The Rhineland, March 1936

On **7 March 1936** German troops marched into the demilitarised Rhineland. Hitler justified the move by pointing to the new Franco-Soviet treaty, which he claimed threatened Germany. It was a gamble: his army was not ready for war, and he is reported to have said later that the following forty-eight hours were the most nerve-racking of his life.

France and Britain did not act. France was about to hold an election and was unwilling to fight without British support. Many in Britain felt that Germany was only moving troops into its own territory. The League was busy with the Abyssinian crisis.

**Consequences:** Hitler's confidence grew and his generals' doubts weakened. Germany began building fortifications in the west, making it harder for France to help its allies in eastern Europe.

## Allies: the Axis and the Anti-Comintern Pact

The Abyssinian crisis pushed Mussolini away from Britain and France. In October 1936 Germany and Italy reached an agreement, and on 1 November Mussolini described it as an **axis** around which Europe would turn. In November 1936 Germany and Japan signed the **Anti-Comintern Pact**; Italy joined in 1937.

## The Spanish Civil War, 1936-1939

In July 1936 nationalist generals, led by **Francisco Franco**, rebelled against Spain's elected Republican government. Germany and Italy sent help to Franco; the Soviet Union aided the Republicans. Britain and France set up a **Non-Intervention Committee**, but Germany and Italy ignored it.

- **Germany** sent the **Condor Legion**, an air force unit whose bombing of the Basque town of **Guernica** in April 1937 shocked the world. Germany tested aircraft and tactics and obtained raw materials such as iron ore.
- **Italy** sent tens of thousands of troops as well as aircraft and weapons.
- Franco won in 1939.

:::example{title="Weighing significance: what did Germany gain from Spain?"}
**Military gains:** combat experience for pilots and tests of new aircraft and bombing tactics.
**Diplomatic gains:** closer ties with Mussolini, and proof that Britain and France would not act firmly.
**Limits:** Franco's Spain stayed neutral in the Second World War, so Hitler gained no ally in the fighting.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the Saar and the Rhineland"}
The Saar returned to Germany legally by plebiscite (January 1935). The Rhineland stayed German throughout; in March 1936 Hitler broke the treaty by sending troops into it.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Hitler took Austria in 1934"}
The 1934 Nazi coup in Austria failed. The Anschluss came in March 1938.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating the Axis and the Anti-Comintern Pact as the same"}
The Axis linked Germany and Italy (1936); the Anti-Comintern Pact linked Germany and Japan (1936), and Italy joined later.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Hitler aimed to overturn Versailles, unite German speakers, win Lebensraum in the east and destroy communism.
- Germany left the League in 1933, regained the Saar by plebiscite in 1935 and openly rearmed from March 1935.
- The Anglo-German Naval Agreement (June 1935) undermined the Stresa Front.
- The Rhineland was remilitarised on 7 March 1936 without resistance.
- The Rome-Berlin Axis, the Anti-Comintern Pact and intervention in Spain gave Hitler allies and military experience.
:::
