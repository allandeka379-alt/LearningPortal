## Introduction

At midnight on 17-18 April 1980, at Rufaro Stadium in Salisbury, the British flag was lowered and the flag of Zimbabwe was raised. After ninety years of colonial and minority rule and a war that had cost tens of thousands of lives, Zimbabwe was independent. The new government's first major policy was **reconciliation**: an appeal to former enemies, black and white, to work together to build the nation.

You should already know the terms of the Lancaster House Agreement (December 1979). Examiners ask you to **describe the events leading to independence** and **explain the policy of reconciliation**. Political developments after 1980, including the disturbances in Matabeleland and the Midlands and the Unity Accord, are covered in the next topic.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Reconciliation"}
The policy of the first independent government of bringing together former enemies (Africans and whites, and the different liberation movements) by setting aside past wrongs and working together for national development.
:::

:::definition{term="Integration (of armies)"}
Combining ZANLA, ZIPRA and the former Rhodesian forces into one national army.
:::

:::definition{term="Demobilisation"}
Releasing fighters from military service and helping them return to civilian life.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Key facts"}
- Late December 1979: ceasefire; guerrillas move to assembly points.
- 27-29 February 1980: common-roll elections (the white-roll seats were decided earlier in February).
- 4 March 1980: results announced; ZANU-PF wins 57 of the 80 common-roll seats.
- 18 April 1980: independence; Robert Mugabe is Prime Minister and Canaan Banana President.
- 18 April 1982: Salisbury renamed Harare.
:::

### The ceasefire and the return

After the agreement, a ceasefire came into force at the end of December 1979. Thousands of ZANLA and ZIPRA fighters moved into **assembly points**, supervised by the **Commonwealth Monitoring Force**, while the Rhodesian forces returned to their bases. Exiled leaders, including Joshua Nkomo and Robert Mugabe, returned home to huge crowds, and refugees began to come back from Mozambique, Zambia and Botswana with help from the United Nations refugee agency.

### The elections of February 1980

Elections were held under British supervision with Commonwealth observers. The campaign was tense, with complaints of intimidation from several parties. The results announced on **4 March 1980** were:

| Party | Common-roll seats (of 80) |
|---|---|
| ZANU-PF (Robert Mugabe) | 57 |
| PF-ZAPU (Joshua Nkomo) | 20 |
| UANC (Abel Muzorewa) | 3 |

The 20 white-roll seats were all won by Ian Smith's Rhodesian Front. Robert Mugabe became Prime Minister, and the Reverend **Canaan Banana** became the ceremonial President.

### Independence Day, 18 April 1980

Independence was celebrated at Rufaro Stadium, attended by Prince Charles for Britain and leaders from many countries. Zimbabwe soon joined the United Nations, the OAU and the Commonwealth, and became a founding member of the Southern African Development Coordination Conference (SADCC).

## The policy of reconciliation

### Aims

- To end the bitterness of the war and prevent revenge killings.
- To keep the skills and capital of white farmers, business people and civil servants, so that the economy kept working.
- To unite the supporters of different parties and the former armies.
- To reassure investors and the international community.

In a broadcast after the election results, Mugabe called on all Zimbabweans to forget the past and work together; whites were told they had a place in the new nation.

### Examples

- **A broad cabinet:** ZAPU leaders, including Joshua Nkomo as Minister of Home Affairs, and two white ministers from outside ZANU-PF were included in the government.
- **Continuity in the state:** most civil servants, judges and police kept their jobs; the former Rhodesian army commander at first oversaw the integration of the armies.
- **Property respected:** as agreed at Lancaster House, private property, including commercial farms, was protected.
- **No revenge trials:** there were no trials of Rhodesian leaders for actions during the war.

### Integration of the armies

ZANLA, ZIPRA and the former Rhodesian forces were brought together into the **Zimbabwe National Army**, with help from a British Military Advisory and Training Team. Former enemies were placed in the same battalions. Fighters who were not needed were demobilised and offered small allowances, training or places in cooperatives. The process was difficult: in 1980-81 there were serious clashes between former ZANLA and ZIPRA fighters at **Entumbane** in Bulawayo, which the new army had to bring under control.

:::example{title="Assessing a policy: achievements and limits of reconciliation"}
**Achievements:** there was no mass revenge against whites; many white farmers and professionals stayed and the economy grew strongly in 1980-81; aid and investment arrived; three hostile armies were merged into one.

**Limits:** reconciliation was mainly between black and white, and relations between ZANU-PF and ZAPU soon broke down, leading to the dismissal of Nkomo from the cabinet in 1982 and the disturbances in Matabeleland and the Midlands. Economic inequality remained, because whites kept most commercial farmland and business ownership. Some critics argued that reconciliation asked the victims of colonial rule to forgive without receiving land or justice.

**Judgement:** reconciliation succeeded in its immediate aims of stability and continuity, but it did not resolve the deeper divisions of land and wealth, or the political rivalry between the two former liberation movements.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Wrong election results"}
ZANU-PF won 57, PF-ZAPU 20 and the UANC 3 of the 80 common-roll seats. The 20 white seats were won by the Rhodesian Front.
:::

:::mistake{title="Presenting reconciliation as a complete success"}
Reconciliation had real achievements, but examiners expect you to show its limits as well, especially the land question and the breakdown between ZANU-PF and ZAPU.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing President and Prime Minister in 1980"}
In 1980 the President (Canaan Banana) was ceremonial; the Prime Minister (Robert Mugabe) led the government. The executive presidency came at the end of 1987.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- After the Lancaster House Agreement a ceasefire began in late December 1979, with guerrillas in assembly points under Commonwealth monitoring.
- In the February 1980 elections ZANU-PF won 57, PF-ZAPU 20 and the UANC 3 common-roll seats; Mugabe became Prime Minister and Banana President.
- Zimbabwe became independent on 18 April 1980.
- Reconciliation aimed at peace, unity and economic continuity: a broad cabinet, continuity in the civil service, protected property and integration of the armies.
- It achieved stability but left land and wealth inequality and political rivalry unresolved.
:::
