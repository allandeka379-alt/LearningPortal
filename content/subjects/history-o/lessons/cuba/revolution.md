## Introduction

On 1 January 1959 the Cuban dictator **Fulgencio Batista** fled the country, and a guerrilla army led by **Fidel Castro** took power. A small island ninety miles (about 145 kilometres) from the United States soon became a communist state allied to the Soviet Union, and it remained one long after the Cold War ended.

The Cuban Revolution matters for Africa. Cuba sent soldiers, doctors and teachers to Angola, Ethiopia and many other countries, and trained students from across the continent. Cuban troops fought in Angola from 1975 to 1991, and their role in the late 1980s is linked to the negotiations that led to Namibia's independence in 1990.

ZIMSEC Paper 2 asks you to describe the nature of the Batista regime, outline the stages of the revolution, and describe its results. US reactions, the Bay of Pigs and the Missile Crisis are covered in the next lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Guerrilla warfare"}
Fighting by small, mobile groups who use surprise attacks, know the local terrain and rely on the support of the local people, rather than fighting large battles.
:::

:::definition{term="26th of July Movement"}
Castro's revolutionary movement, named after the date of the attack on the Moncada barracks (26 July 1953).
:::

:::definition{term="Nationalisation"}
Taking privately owned businesses or land into state ownership.
:::

:::definition{term="Agrarian reform"}
Changing who owns farmland, usually by breaking up large estates and giving land to peasants or the state.
:::

:::keyfact{title="People to know"}
- **Fulgencio Batista**: dictator who seized power in a coup on 10 March 1952.
- **Fidel Castro**: leader of the 26th of July Movement; Prime Minister from February 1959.
- **Raúl Castro**: Fidel's brother and fellow commander.
- **Ernesto "Che" Guevara**: Argentine doctor who became a leading guerrilla commander.
:::

## Cuba under Batista

Cuba won independence from Spain in 1898 with American help, but the USA kept great influence. Under the **Platt Amendment** (1901-1934) the USA could intervene in Cuban affairs, and it kept a naval base at Guantánamo Bay. American companies owned much of the sugar industry, the railways, the utilities and the mines.

Batista, a former army sergeant, had been elected president in 1940. In **March 1952**, facing likely defeat in an election, he seized power in a military coup. His regime was:

- **Dictatorial:** the constitution was suspended, opponents were imprisoned, tortured or killed, and the press was censored.
- **Corrupt:** officials enriched themselves, and Havana's casinos and hotels were linked to American organised crime.
- **Unequal:** a wealthy minority lived well in Havana while many rural workers were poor, often illiterate and without healthcare. Sugar workers had jobs for only part of the year.
- **Dependent on the USA:** the economy relied on selling sugar to the American market, and the USA sold Batista weapons until 1958.

## The stages of the revolution

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 10 March 1952 | Batista seizes power in a coup |
| 26 July 1953 | Castro leads a failed attack on the Moncada barracks in Santiago de Cuba |
| 1955 | Castro released in an amnesty; goes into exile in Mexico |
| 2 December 1956 | Castro and 81 others land from the yacht Granma |
| 1957-1958 | Guerrilla war in the Sierra Maestra mountains |
| March 1958 | The USA stops arms sales to Batista |
| Summer 1958 | Batista's offensive against the guerrillas fails |
| December 1958 | Guevara's forces win the battle of Santa Clara |
| 1 January 1959 | Batista flees; the rebels take power |

### Moncada, 1953

On 26 July 1953 Castro led about 130-160 young rebels (estimates vary) in an attack on the Moncada army barracks in Santiago de Cuba, hoping to seize weapons and spark a rising. The attack failed; many rebels were killed, several after capture. At his trial Castro made a famous defence speech, later published as *History Will Absolve Me*, attacking Batista's dictatorship and setting out a programme of reform. He was sentenced to fifteen years in prison.

### Exile and the Granma, 1955-1956

Released in an amnesty in 1955, Castro went to Mexico, where he founded the **26th of July Movement** and met **Che Guevara**. On 2 December 1956, 82 men landed on the coast of eastern Cuba from the overcrowded yacht **Granma**. Batista's forces attacked them within days, and only a small group of survivors reached the **Sierra Maestra** mountains.

### The Sierra Maestra, 1957-1958

From the mountains the guerrillas built up their strength:

- They won peasant support by paying for food, treating the sick, teaching reading and promising land reform.
- They used hit-and-run raids on army posts to capture weapons.
- An urban underground organised sabotage and strikes in the cities.
- Rebel radio and foreign journalists spread their message.

Batista's army was large but poorly motivated, and its brutality turned more people against the regime. In March 1958 the USA stopped selling arms to Batista. A major army offensive in the summer of 1958 failed, and the rebels advanced westwards. When Guevara's column won the battle of **Santa Clara** in late December 1958, Batista fled on **1 January 1959**.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why did a small guerrilla force defeat Batista?"}
1. **Batista was unpopular:** corruption, inequality and repression meant few Cubans would fight for him.
2. **The guerrillas won rural support:** peasants supplied food and information, so the rebels could survive in the mountains.
3. **The army collapsed from within:** many soldiers were unwilling to fight, and brutal reprisals created more rebels.
4. **Batista lost US support:** the 1958 arms embargo damaged his army and his authority.
5. **Result:** by late 1958 the regime collapsed when the rebels won key battles, rather than being destroyed in one great battle.
:::

## Consolidation and domestic results, 1959-1961

After victory the revolution moved quickly to the left:

- **Revolutionary justice:** many Batista officials were tried and executed.
- **Agrarian Reform Law (May 1959):** large estates were broken up; land went to co-operatives, state farms and peasants.
- **Nationalisation (1960):** American and Cuban-owned companies, banks and sugar mills were taken over.
- **Literacy campaign (1961):** young volunteers went into the countryside to teach adults to read; illiteracy fell sharply.
- **Health and education:** free schooling and healthcare were extended to the whole population.
- **Political control:** elections were not held, opposition newspapers were closed, and critics were imprisoned. Hundreds of thousands of Cubans, many from the middle classes, left for the USA.
- **Communism:** Castro declared the revolution socialist in April 1961 and later announced that he was a Marxist-Leninist; the Communist Party of Cuba became the only party.

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: the results for Cubans"}
**Supporters** point to near-universal literacy, free healthcare and education, and the end of foreign control of the economy.
**Critics** point to the lack of free elections, the imprisonment of opponents, shortages and dependence on the USSR, and the many Cubans who emigrated.
**Judgement:** historians weigh social achievements against political freedom differently; a good answer shows both sides.
:::

## Effects on the world

- **Alignment with the USSR:** in 1960 Cuba signed a trade agreement exchanging sugar for Soviet oil, and the USA responded with an embargo. Cuba became a Soviet ally ninety miles from the USA.
- **Cold War crises:** the Bay of Pigs invasion (1961) and the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962).
- **Latin America:** the revolution inspired guerrilla movements across the continent; Guevara was killed in Bolivia in 1967.
- **Africa:** Guevara fought briefly in the Congo (1965); Cuban troops helped the MPLA government in Angola from 1975 and Ethiopia in 1977-1978; Cuba sent doctors and teachers and gave scholarships to African students.
- **The Non-Aligned Movement:** Cuba hosted its summit in Havana in 1979.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking the revolution was communist from the start"}
Castro's early programme stressed democracy, reform and national independence. He declared the revolution socialist only in April 1961.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing Moncada and the Granma"}
Moncada (26 July 1953) was the failed attack on a barracks; the Granma landing (2 December 1956) began the guerrilla war.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing about the Missile Crisis in a revolution question"}
The Missile Crisis is a result of the revolution's alignment with the USSR; mention it briefly and focus on the question asked.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Batista seized power in 1952 and ran a corrupt, repressive regime dependent on the USA.
- Castro's attack on Moncada (26 July 1953) failed, but gave his movement its name.
- After the Granma landing (December 1956) the guerrillas fought from the Sierra Maestra; Batista fled on 1 January 1959.
- The new government carried out land reform, nationalisation and a literacy campaign, but ended political freedom and turned to communism by 1961.
- Cuba allied with the USSR and supported revolutionary and liberation movements in Latin America and Africa, especially Angola.
:::
