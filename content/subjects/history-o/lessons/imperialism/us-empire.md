## Introduction

The United States had been born in a war against an empire. In 1898 it fought a short war against Spain, which an American diplomat famously called a "splendid little war", and ended it with an overseas empire of its own: Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines, as well as control over Cuba. Filipinos and Cubans who had been fighting for independence from Spain found that they had exchanged one master for another.

This raises questions familiar across Africa: can a power that claims to bring freedom also rule others without their consent? Many Americans at the time asked the same question, and their debate is part of this topic.

This is Cambridge 0470 Option A, key question 5: why, and with what effects, the United States gained former Spanish colonies. You should know the motives for US imperialism (markets, naval bases, ideas of destiny and racial superiority).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Yellow journalism"}
Sensational, exaggerated newspaper reporting designed to sell papers, used by rival New York papers in the 1890s to stir up anger against Spain.
:::

:::definition{term="Teller Amendment"}
A resolution of Congress in April 1898 promising that the United States would not annex Cuba.
:::

:::definition{term="Platt Amendment"}
Conditions that the United States required Cuba to include in its constitution in 1901: the USA could intervene in Cuba, would have naval bases there, and could restrict Cuba's treaties and debts.
:::

:::definition{term="Anti-imperialist"}
An American who opposed the annexation of overseas colonies, for example members of the Anti-Imperialist League founded in 1898.
:::

## Cuba and the Philippines before 1898

By the 1890s Cuba and Puerto Rico in the Caribbean and the Philippines in the Pacific were among the last remnants of the Spanish Empire.

In **Cuba**, a war for independence broke out in 1895, led by the writer **José Martí**, who was killed early in the fighting, and generals such as Máximo Gómez and Antonio Maceo. The Spanish commander, General Weyler, forced rural civilians into fortified camps (*reconcentración*) to cut off the rebels from support. Disease and hunger in the camps killed very large numbers of Cubans. American businesses had invested heavily in Cuban sugar, and American public opinion strongly sympathised with the rebels.

In the **Philippines**, the secret society Katipunan began a revolution against Spain in 1896. **Emilio Aguinaldo** became its leader.

## The Spanish-American War, 1898

Several factors pushed the United States to war:

- **Yellow journalism:** newspapers owned by William Randolph Hearst and Joseph Pulitzer printed sensational stories of Spanish cruelty.
- **The USS Maine:** on 15 February 1898 the American battleship exploded in Havana harbour, killing over 250 sailors. Many Americans blamed Spain. The cause has never been proved; an American naval investigation in the 1970s concluded that an internal explosion was the most likely cause.
- **Humanitarian sympathy** for the Cubans, **economic interests** in Cuban sugar and trade, and **strategic** ambitions for naval power.

In April 1898 Congress declared war, and with the **Teller Amendment** promised not to annex Cuba. The war lasted less than four months:

- On 1 May 1898 Commodore George Dewey destroyed the Spanish fleet in **Manila Bay** in the Philippines.
- In July American forces, including Theodore Roosevelt's volunteer "Rough Riders", fought near **Santiago de Cuba**, and the Spanish fleet there was destroyed.
- Puerto Rico was occupied, and an armistice was signed in August.

In the **Treaty of Paris** (10 December 1898), Spain gave up Cuba and ceded **Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines** to the United States, which paid Spain 20 million dollars. In the same year the USA also annexed **Hawaii**.

## The debate over imperialism

The Treaty of Paris caused a fierce debate. The Senate ratified it in February 1899 by only one vote more than the two-thirds majority needed.

**Supporters of empire** argued that the Philippines would give access to the markets of China and Asia and a naval base in the Pacific; that if the USA left, another power such as Germany or Japan would take the islands; and, in the racist language of the time, that Filipinos were not ready to govern themselves and that the United States had a duty to "civilise" them. President **William McKinley** called his policy "benevolent assimilation".

**Anti-imperialists**, including the writer Mark Twain and the industrialist Andrew Carnegie, argued that ruling people without their consent betrayed the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution, that colonies would be costly and draw the USA into foreign wars, and, in some cases, from racist fears, that they did not want non-white peoples to become Americans.

## The Philippine-American War

Aguinaldo had declared Philippine independence in June 1898 and expected American support. When the USA decided to keep the islands, fighting broke out in **February 1899**. The Filipinos turned to guerrilla warfare. American forces responded harshly, burning villages, moving civilians into camps and, in some cases, using torture, which caused scandal in the United States. Aguinaldo was captured in 1901, and the USA declared the war over in July 1902, although resistance continued for years in some areas.

About 4,000 American soldiers died. Historians estimate that around 20,000 Filipino fighters were killed, and that Filipino civilian deaths, mostly from disease and famine caused by the war, were at least 200,000; some estimates are much higher.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why the Philippine-American War broke out"}
1. **Filipino aims:** Aguinaldo's forces had fought Spain for independence and declared it in June 1898.
2. **American decision:** McKinley decided to keep the islands for markets, a naval base and to prevent rival powers taking them.
3. **Treaty of Paris:** Spain ceded the Philippines to the USA without consulting the Filipinos.
4. **Result:** tension between US and Filipino troops around Manila turned into war in February 1899.
:::

American rule then brought schools taught in English, roads and public health programmes, and from 1907 an elected Philippine Assembly with limited powers, but final authority stayed in American hands. The Philippines became independent only in 1946.

## Cuba to 1906

American troops occupied Cuba from 1898 to 1902. Cubans wrote a constitution, but the United States insisted that it include the **Platt Amendment** (1901). It allowed the USA to intervene to protect Cuban independence and order, gave it naval stations (including **Guantánamo Bay**), and limited Cuba's power to make treaties and borrow money. Cuba became formally independent on 20 May 1902. When a revolt broke out after disputed elections in **1906**, the Cuban president asked for help and US troops returned, occupying the island until 1909. Cuba was independent in name but under strong American control.

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: liberation or a new empire?"}
**Liberation:** the war ended Spanish rule and the camps in Cuba; Cuba became independent in 1902; American rule in the Philippines built schools and roads and promised eventual self-government.
**A new empire:** the USA annexed Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines without consent, fought a brutal war against Filipino independence, and limited Cuban independence through the Platt Amendment and intervention in 1906.
**Judgement:** for Cubans and Filipinos the war replaced Spanish rule with American control, so the result was more a new empire than liberation, even though American rule differed from Spain's.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the USA annexed Cuba"}
The Teller Amendment ruled out annexation. Cuba became independent in 1902, but under the Platt Amendment.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Spain blew up the Maine"}
The cause of the explosion has never been proved. State that many Americans blamed Spain at the time.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ending the story in 1898"}
For Filipinos the war against the USA (1899-1902) was longer and deadlier than the war against Spain in 1898.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Cuba (from 1895) and the Philippines (from 1896) were fighting for independence from Spain.
- Yellow journalism, the explosion of the USS Maine, sympathy for Cubans and economic and strategic interests led to war in April 1898.
- By the Treaty of Paris (December 1898) Spain gave up Cuba and ceded Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines to the USA.
- Americans debated imperialism fiercely, and the Philippine-American War (1899-1902) cost many thousands of Filipino lives.
- Cuba became independent in 1902 under the Platt Amendment, and US troops returned in 1906.
:::
