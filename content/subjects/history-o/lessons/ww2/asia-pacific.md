## Introduction

On the morning of 7 December 1941 Japanese aircraft attacked the American naval base at **Pearl Harbor** in Hawaii. Within hours Japanese forces were also landing in Malaya and attacking Hong Kong, the Philippines and other targets. The European war became a world war, and in six months Japan built an empire stretching from Burma to the central Pacific.

The war in Asia later involved Africans directly: soldiers from East and West Africa, and the Rhodesian African Rifles from Southern Rhodesia, fought in Burma in 1944-45.

This lesson covers Cambridge Depth study E, key question 2: **how did the Second World War in the Asia-Pacific develop?**

You should know that Japan had seized Manchuria in 1931 and had been at war with China since 1937.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Embargo"}
An official ban on trade in certain goods with a particular country. The American oil embargo of 1941 threatened to bring Japan's war machine to a halt.
:::

:::definition{term="Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere"}
Japan's name for the empire it planned in East and Southeast Asia, presented as freeing Asia from Western rule but run in Japan's interests.
:::

:::definition{term="Aircraft carrier"}
A warship with a flight deck from which aircraft take off and land. In the Pacific war carriers replaced battleships as the most important ships.
:::

:::definition{term="Codebreaking"}
Reading the enemy's secret messages. American codebreakers' work on Japanese naval codes was vital at Midway.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **September 1940:** Tripartite Pact of Germany, Italy and Japan.
- **July-August 1941:** Japan occupies southern Indochina; the USA freezes Japanese assets and stops oil exports.
- **7 December 1941:** attack on Pearl Harbor; landings in Malaya.
- **15 February 1942:** surrender of Singapore.
- **4-7 June 1942:** Battle of Midway.
:::

## How US-Japanese relations broke down

1. **Japan's war in China** (from 1937) was condemned in the USA, which gave aid to China.
2. **Japan's alliance with Germany and Italy** (the Tripartite Pact, September 1940) made the USA see it as part of a hostile bloc.
3. **Expansion into Indochina:** after France's defeat, Japan moved into northern French Indochina (1940) and then southern Indochina (July 1941), bringing its forces within reach of Malaya, the Dutch East Indies and the Philippines.
4. **Economic pressure:** in July 1941 the USA froze Japanese assets, and oil exports stopped; Britain and the Dutch did the same. Japan imported most of its oil, much of it from the USA, and its reserves would last only a limited time.
5. **Failed talks:** in November 1941 the USA demanded that Japan withdraw from China and Indochina. Japan's leaders, now headed by General **Tojo**, would not give up China. They decided to seize the oil and raw materials of Southeast Asia, especially the Dutch East Indies, and to strike first at the American fleet that could stop them.

## Pearl Harbor, 7 December 1941

Planned by Admiral **Yamamoto**, the attack was carried out by aircraft from six Japanese carriers in two waves. They sank or damaged eight battleships and many other ships, destroyed about 180 aircraft, and killed about 2,400 Americans.

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: how successful was the attack on Pearl Harbor?"}
**A tactical success:** surprise was complete, the US Pacific battle fleet was crippled for months, and Japan's other attacks in Southeast Asia went ahead without American interference.

**A strategic failure:** the American aircraft carriers were at sea and escaped; the oil storage tanks, repair yards and submarine base were not destroyed; six of the eight battleships were later repaired. Above all, the attack united a divided American public behind the war. The USA declared war on Japan on 8 December, and on 11 December Germany and Italy declared war on the USA.

**Judgement:** most historians see Pearl Harbor as a short-term triumph that brought into the war an enemy whose industrial power Japan could never match.
:::

## Japanese success in Southeast Asia: Malaya and Singapore

On 8 December 1941 (local time, just before the attack on Pearl Harbor because of the time difference), Japanese troops under General **Yamashita** landed in northern Malaya and southern Thailand. Two days later Japanese aircraft sank the British battleship *Prince of Wales* and the battlecruiser *Repulse*, which had been sent to defend Singapore.

The Japanese advanced down the Malayan peninsula in about eight weeks, often using bicycles on the roads and moving through jungle and plantations to get behind British positions. Kuala Lumpur fell in January 1942. The defenders withdrew to **Singapore** island, which the British had long called a fortress. The Japanese crossed the strait on 8 February, captured the reservoirs, and on **15 February 1942** General **Percival** surrendered. Tens of thousands of British, Indian and Australian soldiers became prisoners; it was one of the worst defeats in British military history.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why were the Japanese so successful in Malaya?"}
1. **Command of the air and sea:** Japanese aircraft destroyed much of the RAF in Malaya and sank the two capital ships, so the British could not defend the coast or attack the Japanese advance.
2. **Training and tactics:** experienced Japanese troops, many with years of war in China behind them, outflanked British positions through jungle and by small landings along the coast.
3. **British weaknesses:** many Allied troops were poorly trained, had no tanks, and were under commanders who reacted slowly; Britain's best forces and equipment were needed in Europe and North Africa.
4. **Singapore's defences:** they had been planned mainly against attack from the sea, and the island depended on water from the mainland.
5. **Result:** the Japanese, although outnumbered, took Malaya and Singapore in seventy days.
:::

By May 1942 Japan had also taken Hong Kong, the Philippines, the Dutch East Indies and Burma.

## Midway, June 1942

Admiral Yamamoto planned to capture **Midway** island and draw the remaining American carriers into a battle in which they would be destroyed. But American codebreakers had broken much of the Japanese naval code and identified Midway as the target. Admiral **Nimitz** placed three carriers in ambush.

On 4 June American dive-bombers caught the Japanese carriers while they were rearming and refuelling their aircraft and set three of them on fire within minutes; the fourth was fatally damaged later that day and sank the next morning. The USA lost one carrier, the *Yorktown*.

Midway was a turning point because:

- Japan lost **four fleet carriers**, together with many experienced pilots and skilled mechanics who could not quickly be replaced.
- Japan's expansion in the Pacific was halted; from then on it was mainly on the defensive.
- American industry could build new carriers and train pilots far faster than Japan. In August 1942 the USA began its first major offensive, at **Guadalcanal**.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Pearl Harbor destroyed the US Navy"}
The US carriers were not there, and the base and most battleships were repaired. The attack was a tactical success but a strategic error.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Singapore's guns all faced the wrong way"}
This is an oversimplification. The defences had been planned mainly against a naval attack; what decided the battle was Japanese control of the air and sea and the speed of their advance through Malaya.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting why Japan needed Southeast Asia"}
The oil embargo explains the timing: Japan needed the oil of the Dutch East Indies.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 part (b)"}
"Explain why the Japanese were successful in the early stages of the war": give reasons linked to Malaya and Singapore, not a general narrative of the whole Pacific.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 4 part (b): how important was Midway?"}
Judge importance by its military losses, its effect on Japanese strategy and its long-term consequences, and compare it with Coral Sea and Guadalcanal.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- War in China, the Tripartite Pact, expansion into Indochina and the 1941 oil embargo broke US-Japanese relations.
- Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941) crippled the US battle fleet but missed the carriers and united the USA for war.
- Air and sea control, experienced troops and British weaknesses let Japan take Malaya and Singapore (15 February 1942).
- At Midway (4-7 June 1942), helped by codebreaking, the USA sank four Japanese carriers.
- After Midway Japan was on the defensive, facing America's far greater industrial power.
:::
