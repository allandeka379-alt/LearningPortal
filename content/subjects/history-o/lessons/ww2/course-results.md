## Introduction

The Second World War began with Germany's invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939 and ended with Japan's surrender in 1945. It was fought on every continent except Antarctica and killed more people than any war in history. On one side stood the **Axis** powers (Germany, Italy and Japan); on the other the **Allies**, led from 1941 by Britain, the USSR and the USA.

Southern Rhodesia declared war alongside Britain in September 1939. Rhodesian airmen flew with the Royal Air Force, the **Rhodesian African Rifles** fought in Burma, and the colony became a major training ground for Allied pilots. Across the continent, hundreds of thousands of Africans served as soldiers, carriers and labourers, and the war's results helped to open the way to African independence.

You should already know the causes of the war, from the Treaty of Versailles to the invasion of Poland.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Blitzkrieg"}
"Lightning war": fast attacks by tanks and motorised infantry supported by aircraft, designed to break through and encircle the enemy before it could react.
:::

:::definition{term="Axis"}
The alliance of Germany, Italy and Japan (from 1940).
:::

:::definition{term="Turning point"}
An event after which the course of a war changes decisively, such as Stalingrad or Midway.
:::

:::definition{term="Superpower"}
A state with enough military, economic and political power to influence events across the whole world. After 1945 the USA and the USSR were the two superpowers.
:::

:::definition{term="Decolonisation"}
The process by which colonies won independence from European empires, which speeded up after 1945.
:::

## The course of the war

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 September 1939 | Germany invades Poland |
| April-June 1940 | Germany conquers Denmark, Norway, the Low Countries and France; Dunkirk evacuation |
| July-October 1940 | Battle of Britain |
| 22 June 1941 | Operation Barbarossa: Germany invades the USSR |
| 7 December 1941 | Japan attacks Pearl Harbor; the USA enters the war |
| June 1942 | Battle of Midway |
| October-November 1942 | Battle of El Alamein |
| August 1942-February 1943 | Battle of Stalingrad |
| July-September 1943 | Allies invade Sicily and Italy; Mussolini overthrown; Italy surrenders |
| 6 June 1944 | D-Day landings in Normandy |
| 8 May 1945 | German surrender takes effect (VE Day) |
| 6 and 9 August 1945 | Atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki |
| 2 September 1945 | Japan formally surrenders |

### Blitzkrieg and the fall of France

Poland was defeated within weeks; the USSR invaded from the east on 17 September 1939, as agreed in the Nazi-Soviet Pact. After a quiet winter (the "Phoney War"), Germany attacked in the west in spring 1940. German tanks broke through the Ardennes forest and raced to the Channel. More than 300,000 British and French soldiers were evacuated from **Dunkirk** (late May-early June 1940), but France signed an armistice on 22 June 1940. Germany occupied the north and west; a collaborating French government ruled the rest from Vichy.

### The Battle of Britain

To invade Britain (Operation Sea Lion), Germany needed control of the air. From July to October 1940 the Luftwaffe attacked RAF airfields and then British cities. The RAF, helped by radar and by fighter aircraft such as the Spitfire and Hurricane, inflicted losses the Luftwaffe could not sustain, and Hitler postponed the invasion. Britain, led by **Winston Churchill**, fought on.

### Barbarossa and Stalingrad

On **22 June 1941** Germany invaded the USSR. German forces reached the outskirts of Moscow but were halted in December 1941 by Soviet counter-attacks and the bitter winter. In 1942 they attacked towards the oilfields of the Caucasus and the city of **Stalingrad**. After months of street fighting, a Soviet encirclement trapped the German Sixth Army, which surrendered in February 1943. From then on the Red Army advanced westwards.

### North Africa and Italy

Italy entered the war in June 1940. Its attack on Egypt failed, and German forces under **Rommel** arrived in 1941. In October-November 1942 the British Eighth Army under **Montgomery** won the **Battle of El Alamein**, and American and British forces landed in Morocco and Algeria (Operation Torch). Axis forces in Africa surrendered in Tunisia in May 1943. In 1941 British and Commonwealth forces, including African troops, defeated the Italians in East Africa, and **Haile Selassie** returned to Addis Ababa. The Allies invaded Sicily in July 1943; Mussolini was overthrown and Italy surrendered in September 1943.

### Pearl Harbor and the Pacific

On **7 December 1941** Japan attacked the US fleet at **Pearl Harbor** in Hawaii, bringing the USA into the war; Germany declared war on the USA days later. Japan swiftly conquered Malaya, Singapore, the Philippines, the Dutch East Indies and Burma. At **Midway** (June 1942) the US Navy sank four Japanese aircraft carriers, a turning point. The Americans then advanced towards Japan by "island-hopping", while British, Indian and African troops fought the long campaign in Burma.

### The defeat of Germany and Japan

On **D-Day, 6 June 1944**, Allied forces landed in Normandy, opening a second front in the west. Paris was liberated in August 1944. A German counter-attack in the Ardennes (the Battle of the Bulge, December 1944) failed. Soviet troops reached Berlin in April 1945; Hitler killed himself on 30 April, and the German surrender took effect on **8 May 1945**. Japan fought on until the USA dropped atomic bombs on **Hiroshima** (6 August) and **Nagasaki** (9 August) and the USSR declared war on Japan (8 August). Japan announced its surrender on 15 August and signed it formally on 2 September 1945.

:::example{title="A turning point explained step by step: why was Stalingrad so important?"}
1. **Losses:** Germany lost an entire army: about 90,000 German soldiers surrendered at the end of the battle (an estimate), and many more had been killed.
2. **Momentum:** after Stalingrad the Red Army took the offensive and, apart from brief setbacks, never lost it.
3. **Morale:** the defeat shook German confidence and encouraged the Allies and resistance movements.
4. **Resources:** Germany could not replace its losses of men and equipment as fast as the USSR, supported by American supplies, could.

Together with El Alamein and Midway, Stalingrad marks the period in 1942-1943 when the tide turned against the Axis.
:::

## African and Rhodesian participation

- **Southern Rhodesia** sent white servicemen to many fronts and raised the **Rhodesian African Rifles** (1940), which fought in Burma in 1944-1945.
- The **Rhodesian Air Training Group** trained thousands of Allied aircrew at bases in Southern Rhodesia.
- The **King's African Rifles** and the **Royal West African Frontier Force** fought in East Africa and Burma.
- French colonial troops from West and Equatorial Africa fought for the Free French.
- Africa supplied minerals, food and labour; in some colonies men were conscripted for labour.

## Results of the war

**Human cost:** historians estimate that between 70 and 85 million people died, most of them civilians. The USSR suffered the greatest losses, estimated at over 20 million. About six million Jews were murdered in the Holocaust.

**Political results:**

- The **United Nations** was founded on 24 October 1945 to keep the peace.
- The **USA and the USSR** emerged as superpowers; Britain and France were weakened.
- Germany was divided, and Europe split between East and West, leading to the **Cold War**.
- War crimes trials were held at Nuremberg and Tokyo.

**Decolonisation:** the war weakened the European empires and spread ideas of self-determination (the Atlantic Charter of 1941). African servicemen returned with new experiences and expectations. India and Pakistan became independent in 1947 and Ghana in 1957.

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: the war and African nationalism"}
**Important:** ex-servicemen, new towns and industries, and the language of freedom used by the Allies all strengthened nationalist movements; a weakened Britain could not hold its empire for long.
**Limits:** independence came mostly in the 1950s and 1960s, through African organisation and struggle; in settler colonies such as Southern Rhodesia, white minority rule became more entrenched after the war.
**Judgement:** the war speeded up decolonisation, but African political action decided its timing and form.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the dates of the two surrenders"}
Germany surrendered in May 1945 (VE Day, 8 May); Japan announced its surrender in August and signed it on 2 September 1945.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the USA was in the war from 1939"}
The USA joined after Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941).
:::

:::mistake{title="Using precise casualty figures as facts"}
Casualty totals are estimates. Write "historians estimate about…" and give a range.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="ZIMSEC Paper 2: describe the events"}
Organise by theatre (Western Europe, the Eastern Front, North Africa and Italy, the Pacific) with a key battle, date and result for each.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Evaluate the results"}
Group results as political, economic, social and colonial, and include Africa. In a "to what extent" part, weigh which results were most lasting.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Blitzkrieg defeated Poland (1939) and France (1940); the Battle of Britain stopped a German invasion of Britain.
- Barbarossa (June 1941) failed before Moscow; Stalingrad (1942-43), El Alamein (1942) and Midway (1942) turned the war.
- Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941) brought the USA in; D-Day (6 June 1944) opened the second front.
- Germany surrendered on 8 May 1945; Japan surrendered after the atomic bombs and Soviet entry, signing on 2 September 1945.
- Africans and Rhodesians served widely; the war created the UN, two superpowers and the Cold War, and speeded decolonisation.
:::
