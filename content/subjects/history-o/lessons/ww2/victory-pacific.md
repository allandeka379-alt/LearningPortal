## Introduction

After Midway in June 1942 the war in the Pacific turned into a long Allied advance across thousands of kilometres of ocean, island by island, towards Japan. It ended in August 1945 with the first and only use of nuclear weapons in war, followed by Japan's surrender. Then the victors had to decide what to do with the defeated nations and their leaders.

Africans fought in this war against Japan too: the 11th East African Division and two West African divisions fought in Burma, and the **Rhodesian African Rifles**, raised in Southern Rhodesia in 1940, served in the Burma campaign.

This lesson covers Cambridge Depth study E, key question 4 (the defeat of Japan and the consolidation of victory).

You should already know why Midway was a turning point.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Island-hopping"}
The American strategy of capturing selected islands to use as air and naval bases, while bypassing and isolating other strongly defended Japanese islands.
:::

:::definition{term="Kamikaze"}
Japanese pilots who deliberately crashed their aircraft, loaded with explosives, into Allied ships, from October 1944.
:::

:::definition{term="War crimes trial"}
A trial of individuals for crimes such as waging aggressive war, breaking the laws of war, and crimes against humanity.
:::

:::definition{term="Occupation"}
Control of a defeated country by the armed forces of the victors, as in Germany (divided into four zones) and Japan (under American control) after 1945.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **August 1942-February 1943:** Guadalcanal campaign.
- **1 April-June 1945:** Battle of Okinawa.
- **6 August 1945:** atomic bomb on Hiroshima.
- **8 August 1945:** the USSR declares war on Japan.
- **9 August 1945:** atomic bomb on Nagasaki.
- **2 September 1945:** Japan's formal surrender.
:::

## Guadalcanal and island-hopping

In August 1942 US Marines landed on **Guadalcanal** in the Solomon Islands to capture an airfield the Japanese were building. Six months of fighting on land, at sea and in the air followed. Japan finally withdrew in February 1943. Guadalcanal was Japan's first major defeat on land, and the battle wore down Japanese ships, aircraft and experienced pilots.

The Americans then advanced on two lines: **General MacArthur** through New Guinea towards the Philippines, and **Admiral Nimitz** across the central Pacific. Using **island-hopping**, they captured key islands for airfields and bypassed strong points such as the base at Rabaul, leaving their garrisons cut off. The capture of the **Marianas** in 1944 put Japan's cities within range of the new B-29 bombers. At **Leyte Gulf** (October 1944), one of the largest naval battles in history, the Japanese navy was largely destroyed as a fighting force.

## Okinawa

**Okinawa**, only about 550 kilometres from Japan's main islands, was invaded on 1 April 1945. The fighting lasted almost three months. Kamikaze attacks sank or damaged many American ships. More than 12,000 Americans were killed, and historians estimate Japanese military deaths at roughly 75,000 to 110,000. Very large numbers of Okinawan civilians also died; estimates range widely, from about 40,000 to over 100,000. To American leaders, Okinawa suggested how costly an invasion of Japan itself would be.

## The bombing of Japan

From late 1944 B-29s bombed Japan from the Marianas. In March 1945 the Americans switched to low-level night raids with incendiary bombs on cities built largely of wood. The raid on **Tokyo** on 9-10 March 1945 started a firestorm; historians estimate that 80,000 to over 100,000 people died. Dozens of other cities were burned. At the same time an American submarine campaign and mining of Japan's waters cut off imports of oil, food and raw materials.

## The atomic bombs

The USA, with British help, had developed the atomic bomb in the secret **Manhattan Project**; it was first tested in New Mexico on 16 July 1945. On 26 July the **Potsdam Declaration** called on Japan to surrender unconditionally or face destruction. Japan's government did not accept it.

- On **6 August 1945** an atomic bomb destroyed **Hiroshima**.
- On **8 August** the **Soviet Union** declared war on Japan and invaded Manchuria the next day.
- On **9 August** a second bomb was dropped on **Nagasaki**.

Historians estimate that by the end of 1945 about 140,000 people had died at Hiroshima and about 70,000 at Nagasaki, from the blasts, fires and radiation; many more suffered illness for years afterwards. **Emperor Hirohito** intervened to accept surrender, announced by radio on 15 August, and the formal surrender was signed on 2 September 1945.

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: why did the USA use the atomic bomb, and was it justified?"}
**To end the war quickly and save lives:** President **Truman** and his advisers argued that an invasion of Japan would cost huge numbers of American and Japanese lives, as Okinawa had suggested, and that Japan's leaders showed no sign of surrendering.

**Other motives:** some historians argue that the USA also wanted to end the war before the USSR could play a large part in Asia, and to show Soviet leaders the new weapon's power. Others point to the momentum of a vast project, and to wartime attitudes that made bombing cities seem normal.

**Was it necessary?** Critics argue that Japan was already close to defeat because of the blockade and bombing, that the Soviet declaration of war may have been as important in forcing surrender, and that a warning demonstration or clearer terms about the Emperor could have been tried. Supporters reply that Japan's army leaders wanted to keep fighting even after both bombs.

**Your judgement:** decide which arguments the evidence supports best, and remember that the moral question and the historical question (why it was used) are different.
:::

## Consolidating victory

**Germany:** at Yalta (February 1945) and Potsdam (July-August 1945) the Allies agreed to divide Germany into four zones, run by the USA, Britain, France and the USSR, with Berlin also divided into four sectors. The aims were to demilitarise, denazify and democratise Germany and to collect reparations.

**Japan:** Japan was occupied by mainly American forces under **General MacArthur** from 1945 to 1952. The army and navy were abolished, war leaders were purged, and land reform broke up large estates. A new constitution (in force from 1947) made the Emperor a symbol with no political power and renounced war. The Emperor himself was not put on trial.

**The trials:**

- At **Nuremberg** (November 1945-October 1946) an international military tribunal of the USA, Britain, France and the USSR tried leading Nazis for conspiracy, crimes against peace, war crimes and crimes against humanity. Twelve were sentenced to death, others were imprisoned, and three were acquitted.
- In **Tokyo** (1946-1948) Japanese leaders, including the former prime minister **Tojo**, were tried; seven were sentenced to death.
- Supporters saw the trials as establishing that leaders are personally responsible for atrocities; critics called them "victors' justice", because Allied actions were not judged. One judge at Tokyo, Radhabinod Pal of India, dissented from the verdicts.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Reversing the order of the bombs"}
Hiroshima was bombed first (6 August), then Nagasaki (9 August). The Soviet declaration of war came in between (8 August).
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing VE Day with the end of the war"}
Germany surrendered in May 1945; the war with Japan continued until August-September 1945.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating the atomic bomb debate as settled"}
Present the main views fairly and support your own judgement with evidence.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 part (b): explain why the Allies defeated Japan"}
Link several causes: American industrial power, island-hopping, the destruction of the Japanese navy, the blockade and bombing, the atomic bombs and Soviet entry.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 4: how important was the atomic bomb?"}
Judge importance by its effect on the timing of surrender, compare it with Soviet entry and the blockade, and consider its long-term significance (the nuclear age, the Cold War).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Guadalcanal (1942-43) was Japan's first major land defeat; island-hopping and Leyte Gulf brought the Allies closer to Japan.
- Okinawa (April-June 1945) was very costly and shaped plans for invading Japan; firebombing and blockade devastated Japan.
- Atomic bombs destroyed Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August); the USSR declared war on 8 August; Japan surrendered on 2 September 1945.
- The decision to use the bomb is debated: saving lives, influencing the USSR, and whether it was necessary.
- Germany was divided into four zones and Japan occupied by the USA; leaders were tried at Nuremberg and Tokyo.
:::
