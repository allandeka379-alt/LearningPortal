## Introduction

The Korean War was the first time the Cold War turned into a large-scale shooting war. Troops from the United States and many other countries fought under the flag of the **United Nations**, and Chinese forces entered the war on the other side. The war ended in 1953 with an armistice, not a peace treaty, and Korea is still divided today.

Korea is the first of three case studies (with Cuba and Vietnam) for the Cambridge key question "How effectively did the United States contain the spread of communism?". It also shows how the UN, an organisation that Zimbabwe joined at independence in 1980, could be used in a Cold War crisis.

You should already know what containment and the Truman Doctrine meant, and that China became communist in 1949.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="38th parallel"}
The line of latitude along which Korea was divided in 1945, with Soviet forces occupying the north and American forces the south.
:::

:::definition{term="Security Council"}
The UN body responsible for peace and security. Its five permanent members (USA, USSR, Britain, France and China) can each veto a decision.
:::

:::definition{term="Armistice"}
An agreement to stop fighting. It is not a peace treaty, so a state of war technically continues.
:::

:::definition{term="Rollback"}
Going beyond containment to push communism back out of a country where it already ruled, as the UN forces tried to do in North Korea in October 1950.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and names to learn"}
- **25 June 1950:** North Korea invades South Korea.
- **15 September 1950:** UN landing at Inchon.
- **October-November 1950:** Chinese forces enter the war.
- **April 1951:** Truman dismisses General MacArthur.
- **27 July 1953:** armistice signed at Panmunjom.
- Leaders: **Kim Il-sung** (North Korea), **Syngman Rhee** (South Korea), **Harry Truman** (US President to January 1953), **Dwight Eisenhower** (from January 1953).
:::

## Background: a divided Korea

Korea had been ruled by Japan from 1910 to 1945. When Japan surrendered in 1945, Soviet troops occupied the north and American troops the south, divided at the **38th parallel**. The division was meant to be temporary, but the superpowers could not agree on elections for the whole country. In 1948 two states were set up: the communist **Democratic People's Republic of Korea** in the north under **Kim Il-sung**, and the **Republic of Korea** in the south under **Syngman Rhee**, who was strongly anti-communist and also authoritarian. Both leaders wanted to unite Korea under their own rule, and there were frequent clashes along the border.

## Why did North Korea invade, and why did the USA react?

On **25 June 1950** North Korean forces crossed the 38th parallel. Documents released after the Cold War show that Kim Il-sung had asked Stalin for permission and Soviet support, and that Stalin agreed in early 1950, on condition that Mao's China would help if needed.

Several factors encouraged the invasion:

- In 1949 the communists had won in China, and the USSR had tested its first atomic bomb. Communism seemed to be advancing.
- In January 1950 the US Secretary of State, Dean Acheson, described an American "defensive perimeter" in the Pacific that did not include Korea, which suggested that the USA might not fight for it.

The USA reacted quickly. Truman believed that if South Korea fell, other countries in Asia might follow, and that failing to act would repeat the mistake of appeasement in the 1930s. A secret report of April 1950, **NSC-68**, had already recommended a large increase in American military spending to resist communism everywhere.

### The role of the United Nations

The USA took the issue to the **Security Council**. The USSR was boycotting the Council at the time in protest at the UN's refusal to give China's seat to the new communist government. So there was no Soviet veto, and the Council called on member states to help South Korea. A UN force was formed under the American general **Douglas MacArthur**. The USA provided most of the troops, ships and aircraft, but soldiers came from many countries, including Britain, Turkey, Canada and Australia.

## The course of the war

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1945 | Korea divided at the 38th parallel |
| 1948 | Two Korean states set up |
| 25 June 1950 | North Korea invades the South |
| September 1950 | UN forces hold the Pusan perimeter; landing at Inchon (15 September) |
| October 1950 | UN forces cross the 38th parallel and take Pyongyang |
| October-November 1950 | Chinese forces enter the war and drive the UN back |
| January 1951 | Seoul falls for the second time |
| April 1951 | Truman dismisses MacArthur |
| July 1951 | Armistice talks begin; the front settles near the 38th parallel |
| 27 July 1953 | Armistice signed at Panmunjom |

**Phase 1 (June-September 1950).** The North Korean army captured Seoul and pushed the South Korean and UN forces into a small area around the port of **Pusan** in the south-east.

**Phase 2 (September-November 1950).** On 15 September MacArthur landed troops at **Inchon**, near Seoul, far behind the North Korean lines. The North Korean army collapsed and retreated. With UN approval, UN forces crossed the 38th parallel, captured the northern capital **Pyongyang** and advanced towards the **Yalu River**, the border with China. The aim had changed from containment to rollback.

**Phase 3 (November 1950-mid-1951).** China had warned that it would not accept hostile forces on its border. Large Chinese forces attacked and drove the UN troops back south of the 38th parallel; Seoul fell again in January 1951. MacArthur publicly called for attacks on China itself and hinted at using atomic weapons. Truman, fearing a wider war with China and perhaps the USSR, dismissed him in April 1951.

**Phase 4 (1951-1953).** The UN forces recovered Seoul, and the front settled near the 38th parallel. Two years of bitter fighting followed while armistice talks dragged on, partly over whether prisoners of war could refuse to go home. After Eisenhower became President and Stalin died (March 1953), the armistice was signed on **27 July 1953**. A demilitarised zone was created near the 38th parallel.

The cost was enormous. Historians estimate that well over two million people died, most of them Korean civilians; about 36,000 Americans were killed.

:::example{title="A turning point explained step by step: why the Chinese entered the war"}
1. **UN forces crossed the 38th parallel** in October 1950 and advanced towards the Yalu River.
2. **China felt threatened:** a hostile, American-led army on its border might attack the new communist state.
3. **China had warned** that it would intervene, but MacArthur did not take the warning seriously.
4. **Result:** the war became a long and costly stalemate, and the chance of uniting Korea under the South was lost.
:::

## How far did containment succeed?

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: a success or a failure for containment?"}
**Success:** South Korea survived as a non-communist state; the UN had acted against aggression; the USA showed it would fight to contain communism.
**Failure or limited success:** the attempt at rollback failed and North Korea remained communist; China was strengthened and became an enemy of the USA; the war cost millions of lives and ended only in an armistice.
**Judgement:** many historians argue that containment succeeded, because its aim was to stop communism spreading, while rollback failed. Your answer should make clear which aim you are judging.
:::

The war also had wider effects. American defence spending rose sharply, as NSC-68 had proposed. The USA strengthened NATO and signed new alliances in Asia, such as SEATO (1954). Relations between the USA and communist China stayed hostile for twenty years.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the USSR vetoed the UN action"}
The USSR was boycotting the Security Council in June 1950, which is why the UN could act. Had it been present, it would almost certainly have used its veto.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the war ended with a peace treaty"}
It ended with an armistice in July 1953. No peace treaty has been signed, and Korea is still divided.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing containment with rollback"}
Containment meant defending South Korea; crossing the 38th parallel to conquer the North was rollback, and it failed.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (c): 'How successful was containment in Korea?'"}
Separate the aims: defending South Korea (achieved) and uniting Korea (failed). Use specific evidence such as Inchon, the Chinese intervention and the armistice line, then judge.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (b): 'Explain why the USA became involved'"}
Explain the domino-style fear after China fell in 1949, the lesson of appeasement, NSC-68, and the chance to act through the UN. Each reason needs a "because" that links it to American action.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Korea was divided at the 38th parallel in 1945; two hostile states were set up in 1948.
- North Korea invaded on 25 June 1950 with Stalin's approval; the USA acted through the UN, helped by the Soviet boycott of the Security Council.
- Inchon (September 1950) turned the war; the UN advance to the Yalu brought China in, and the war became a stalemate.
- Truman dismissed MacArthur in April 1951; the armistice was signed on 27 July 1953.
- Containment of South Korea succeeded, but rollback failed and the human cost was enormous.
:::
