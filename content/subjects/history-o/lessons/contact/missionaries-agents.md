## Introduction

Many of Zimbabwe's best-known schools and hospitals, such as those at Inyati, Morgenster, Empandeni and Old Mutare, began as mission stations. Missionaries brought literacy, Western medicine and Christianity, but some of them also helped open the way for colonial rule. At the same time hunters, traders, explorers and concession seekers carried news of gold, ivory and land back to the south and to Europe.

This lesson looks at the early missionaries in Zimbabwe, what they did, what difficulties they faced and what difference they made, and then at the wider group of Europeans historians call **agents of colonisation**. You should know about the Ndebele state, because the first lasting missions were in Mzilikazi's kingdom.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Missionary"}
A person sent by a church to spread the Christian faith, often also running schools, clinics and farms.
:::

:::definition{term="Evangelism"}
Preaching and teaching in order to convert people to Christianity.
:::

:::definition{term="Agent of colonisation"}
A person or group whose activities, whether intended or not, prepared the way for colonial rule: missionaries, hunters, traders, explorers and concession seekers.
:::

:::definition{term="Concession"}
A grant of rights (for example to mine, trade or use land) given by a ruler to an individual or company.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
The London Missionary Society (LMS) opened its mission at **Inyati** in 1859 and at **Hope Fountain**, near Bulawayo, in 1870. The Jesuit **Zambezi Mission** arrived in Lobengula's kingdom in 1879.
:::

## The early missionary groups

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1561 | The Portuguese Jesuit Gonçalo da Silveira reaches the Mutapa court and is killed |
| 1859 | LMS mission opened at Inyati, with Mzilikazi's permission, after Robert Moffat's visits |
| 1870 | LMS mission at Hope Fountain, near Bulawayo |
| 1879 | Jesuit Zambezi Mission arrives in Lobengula's kingdom; Empandeni follows in 1887 |
| 1888 | LMS missionary Charles Helm interprets for Lobengula at the signing of the Rudd Concession |
| 1891 | After the occupation, the Dutch Reformed Church opens Morgenster near Fort Victoria; Anglican and Methodist missions begin in Mashonaland |

- **London Missionary Society (LMS)**: Robert Moffat, a missionary at Kuruman in present-day South Africa, had become a friend of Mzilikazi. Through this friendship the LMS was allowed to open **Inyati** in 1859, staffed by missionaries including Moffat's son John Smith Moffat, William Sykes and Thomas Morgan Thomas. **Hope Fountain** followed in 1870.
- **Jesuits (Catholic)**: the Zambezi Mission reached Bulawayo in 1879, and later opened **Empandeni** (1887).
- **After the occupation of 1890**, many more churches came: the **Dutch Reformed Church** at **Morgenster** (1891), the **Anglicans** under Bishop George Knight-Bruce, the **Methodists** (Wesleyans), the American Methodists at Old Mutare, the Seventh-day Adventists at Solusi and others. Many received land from the British South Africa Company.

## Missionary activities

- **Evangelism**: preaching, translating parts of the Bible and hymns into African languages, and building churches.
- **Education**: teaching reading, writing and arithmetic; mission schools later trained teachers, clerks and nurses.
- **Health**: treating the sick and later building clinics and hospitals.
- **Agriculture and crafts**: introducing new crops, the plough, fruit trees, irrigation, carpentry and building methods.
- **Diplomacy and advice**: missionaries acted as interpreters, letter-writers and advisers to African rulers, especially Lobengula.

## Challenges faced by the early missionaries

1. **Few converts**: in Matabeleland, the LMS made very few converts in its first decades. The Ndebele social system, built on the king, the regiments and cattle, left little room for a new faith, and people feared the king's disapproval.
2. **Control by the king**: Mzilikazi and Lobengula allowed missionaries to stay but limited their activities and where they could go.
3. **Disease**: malaria and other illnesses killed missionaries and their families.
4. **Language and culture**: missionaries had to learn new languages and often misunderstood African customs and beliefs.
5. **Distance and transport**: supplies came by ox-wagon over hundreds of kilometres from the south.
6. **Competition** between different churches, and lack of money.
7. **Strong African religion**: Mwari, the ancestors and spirit mediums remained central to people's lives.

:::example{title="Explaining why the LMS made so few converts in Matabeleland"}
1. **Political control:** the king decided who could live where and how people should behave; becoming Christian could look like disloyalty.
2. **Social system:** young men belonged to regiments and the economy depended on raiding and cattle, which the missionaries criticised.
3. **Religion:** the Ndebele had their own beliefs and the Inxwala ceremony; Christianity asked them to give these up.
4. **Few benefits:** at first, missionaries offered little that people needed, while the costs of converting were high.

Only after the conquest of 1893 and the destruction of the old political order did mission churches grow quickly.
:::

## Impact of missionary activities

**Positive**
- Literacy and education; many later nationalist leaders were educated at mission schools.
- Medical care; mission hospitals still serve rural communities.
- Written forms of African languages, grammars and dictionaries.
- New farming methods, crops and skills.

**Negative**
- Undermined African religion and customs (polygamy, ancestral rites, initiation), often by condemning them.
- Divided communities between converts and non-converts.
- Some missionaries helped colonisation directly, for example by interpreting and advising on concessions or encouraging British rule.
- Many missions received large areas of land from the Company after the occupation.

## The agents of colonisation

| Agent | Examples | How they prepared the way |
|---|---|---|
| Missionaries | Robert and John Smith Moffat, Charles Helm | Gained the trust of rulers; interpreted and witnessed treaties; reported on the country; some urged British rule |
| Hunters | Frederick Courteney Selous | Mapped routes, reported on the land, later guided the Pioneer Column |
| Traders | Many ivory and goods traders | Brought guns, cloth and trade goods; spread knowledge of the interior |
| Explorers | David Livingstone, Karl Mauch | Livingstone's travels (he saw the Victoria Falls in 1855) drew British attention to central Africa; Mauch's reports of gold in 1867 led to a gold rush to Tati and spread the idea of rich goldfields |
| Concession seekers | Charles Rudd, Rochfort Maguire, Francis Thompson | Obtained the Rudd Concession (1888), which Rhodes used to win the Royal Charter |

:::example{title="Weighing the role of missionaries in colonisation"}
**Claim:** "Missionaries were the most important agents of colonisation in Zimbabwe."

1. **For:** missionaries had lived at the Ndebele court for decades; John Smith Moffat negotiated the Moffat Treaty (1888) and Charles Helm interpreted the Rudd Concession and assured Lobengula about promises that were not in the written document.
2. **Against:** the concession seekers backed by Rhodes and his money, and the gold reports of hunters and explorers such as Mauch, did more to bring the occupation; missionaries had few converts and limited influence on the king.
3. **Judgement:** missionaries were important because they had the king's trust, but concession seekers backed by Rhodes's money and the British government were decisive.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying missionaries arrived with the Pioneer Column"}
The LMS was at Inyati from 1859 and the Jesuits from 1879. Many other churches came after 1890.
:::

:::mistake{title="Only listing positive effects"}
Questions on impact expect both benefits (education, health) and harm (undermining culture, links with colonisation).
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the two Moffats"}
Robert Moffat was Mzilikazi's friend at Kuruman. His son John Smith Moffat worked at Inyati and later negotiated the Moffat Treaty of 1888.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The LMS opened Inyati (1859) and Hope Fountain (1870); the Jesuits came in 1879; many more churches arrived after 1890.
- Missionaries preached, taught, healed, introduced new farming methods and advised rulers.
- They faced few converts, royal control, disease, language barriers, distance and strong African religion.
- Their impact included education, health care and written languages, but also cultural damage and help to colonisation.
- Missionaries, hunters, traders, explorers and concession seekers were all agents of colonisation; concession seekers backed by Rhodes were decisive.
:::
