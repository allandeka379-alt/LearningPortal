## Introduction

After 1866 Germany was half united. Prussia led the North German Confederation, but Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden and southern Hesse remained outside, and many southern Germans, especially Catholics, distrusted Protestant Prussia. Four years later a war with France brought them in, and on **18 January 1871** the King of Prussia was proclaimed German Emperor in the palace of the French kings at Versailles.

The new German Empire changed the balance of power in Europe. Its later rivalry with France and Britain helped lead to the First World War, which was fought in Africa too, including in German East Africa and South West Africa.

This lesson completes Cambridge Option A, key question 3. You need the story of 1862-66 and the outcome of the Treaty of Prague.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Hohenzollern candidature"}
The offer of the vacant Spanish throne to Prince Leopold of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen, a relative of the Prussian king, in 1870. France saw it as a threat of encirclement.
:::

:::definition{term="Ems telegram"}
The telegram of 13 July 1870 reporting King Wilhelm's meeting with the French ambassador at Ems. Bismarck shortened it before publication so that it read like an insult.
:::

:::definition{term="Kaiser"}
The German word for emperor. Wilhelm I became *Deutscher Kaiser* (German Emperor) in 1871.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **1867:** the Luxembourg crisis.
- **13 July 1870:** the Ems telegram.
- **19 July 1870:** France declares war on Prussia.
- **1-2 September 1870:** battle of Sedan; Napoleon III captured.
- **18 January 1871:** German Empire proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors, Versailles.
- **10 May 1871:** Treaty of Frankfurt.
:::

## Prussia and France after 1866

Napoleon III had expected the war of 1866 to be long, weakening both German powers and allowing France to gain territory. Instead Prussia won in seven weeks, and France gained nothing. French opinion felt that Prussia's rise was a defeat for France.

- In **1867** Napoleon tried to buy **Luxembourg** from the King of the Netherlands. Bismarck stirred up German opinion against the sale, and a conference in London made Luxembourg neutral instead. France had been humiliated again.
- Bismarck also revealed French requests for compensation, which made the southern German states afraid of France.

The **southern states** were a problem for Bismarck. They had military alliances with Prussia and were in the Zollverein, but in 1868 elections to a new customs parliament returned many anti-Prussian members from the south. Bismarck seems to have concluded that only a national war against France would unite north and south quickly.

## The Spanish throne and the Ems telegram

In 1868 Queen Isabella II of Spain was overthrown, and the Spanish government looked for a new king. It offered the throne to **Prince Leopold of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen**. Bismarck secretly encouraged the candidature, and in July 1870 news of Leopold's acceptance became public.

France reacted furiously: a Hohenzollern on the throne of Spain would place Prussian influence on two of France's borders. Under heavy pressure, Leopold's father withdrew the candidature on **12 July 1870**. France had won a diplomatic victory, but its government wanted more. The French ambassador, **Benedetti**, met King Wilhelm at the spa town of **Ems** on 13 July and demanded a promise that the candidature would never be renewed. Wilhelm politely refused and sent Bismarck a telegram describing the meeting.

Bismarck, with the generals Moltke and Roon, **edited the telegram** to make it shorter and sharper, so that it seemed the king had snubbed the ambassador, and released it to the press. French opinion was outraged, and on **19 July 1870** France declared war on Prussia.

:::example{title="A source analysed: the edited Ems telegram"}
**Content:** the original reported a courteous refusal and that the king would not see the ambassador again on the matter. The edited version left out the courtesy and made the refusal sound final and insulting.
**Purpose:** Bismarck wanted France to declare war, so that France would appear the aggressor. This mattered because the southern German alliances applied only if Prussia was attacked.
**Result:** the southern states joined Prussia, and the other great powers stayed out.
**Note on reliability:** Bismarck's own later memoirs describe his editing of the telegram, but they were written years afterwards to present him as the master planner, so historians use them with care.
:::

## The Franco-Prussian War, 1870-71

The German armies, with the southern states' forces alongside Prussia's, mobilised faster than France, using railways and Moltke's planning. They also had better artillery. One French army was surrounded at Metz. Another, with **Napoleon III** himself, was defeated and forced to surrender at **Sedan** on 1-2 September 1870.

When the news reached Paris, a **republic** was proclaimed on 4 September 1870. The new government fought on, and Paris was besieged from September 1870 until an armistice at the end of January 1871.

No great power helped France. Russia used the war to break the Black Sea clauses of the 1856 treaty, Austria-Hungary did not want another war with Prussia, Italy took Rome when French troops left it, and Britain was suspicious of Napoleon III.

## The German Empire

During the war Bismarck negotiated separately with each southern state. Bavaria was given special rights, such as control over its own army in peacetime, and Bismarck persuaded King Ludwig II of Bavaria to write a letter inviting Wilhelm to take the imperial title. On **18 January 1871**, in the **Hall of Mirrors** at Versailles, Wilhelm I was proclaimed German Emperor in front of princes and officers.

By the **Treaty of Frankfurt** (10 May 1871):

- France gave **Alsace** and much of **Lorraine** to Germany.
- France paid an indemnity of **5 billion francs**, with German troops occupying parts of France until it was paid.

The empire was a federation of states dominated by Prussia. The emperor was the King of Prussia, Bismarck became imperial Chancellor, and the Reichstag was elected by all adult men but had limited power over the government.

## How far was Bismarck responsible for unification?

This is the most common evaluation question on this topic, and historians disagree.

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: master planner or opportunist?"}
**Bismarck's role:** he provoked and isolated each enemy in turn, kept the wars short, offered Austria a mild peace, and used the Ems telegram to make France the aggressor. Some historians, following Bismarck's own memoirs, see a long-term plan.
**Other factors:** Prussia's economic strength (coal, iron, railways and the Zollverein) paid for and moved its army; Roon's reforms and Moltke's generalship won the battles; nationalism made southern Germans rally against France; and the mistakes of Austria and Napoleon III helped him.
**Judgement:** many historians now see Bismarck as a skilled opportunist with a clear aim, Prussian power, who used circumstances he did not create. Without him unification might have come later or in a different form, but he could not have done it without Prussian strength.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Prussia declared war on France"}
France declared war on Prussia on 19 July 1870. This mattered, because it brought the southern German states in under their alliances.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the Treaty of Frankfurt (1871) with the Frankfurt Parliament (1848)"}
They are 23 years apart. The treaty ended the Franco-Prussian War; the parliament was the failed attempt at unification in 1848-49.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the Empire was proclaimed in Berlin"}
It was proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, near Paris, while the war was still going on, which deeply offended the French.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (c): 'How far was Bismarck responsible for German unification?'"}
Write one section on Bismarck's actions with examples from 1864, 1866 and 1870, and another on other factors (economy, army, nationalism, others' mistakes). Finish with a judgement that weighs them, not a list.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 2 (Germany prescribed for March 2027)"}
Source questions often use cartoons or reports about Ems or Bismarck. Use dates and provenance: a French newspaper in July 1870, a German newspaper after Sedan and Bismarck's memoirs all had reasons to tell the story differently.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- France gained nothing from 1866 and was humiliated again over Luxembourg in 1867.
- Bismarck used the Hohenzollern candidature and the edited Ems telegram (13 July 1870) to make France declare war.
- German victory at Sedan (September 1870) and the siege of Paris won the war; no great power helped France.
- The German Empire was proclaimed at Versailles on 18 January 1871; France lost Alsace-Lorraine and paid 5 billion francs.
- Bismarck's diplomacy was crucial, but Prussian economic and military power, nationalism and others' mistakes also explain unification.
:::
