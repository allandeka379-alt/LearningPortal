## Introduction

Every person alive today, in Harare, Beijing, London or Lima, shares ancestors who lived in Africa. Some of the most important evidence for the human story was found in southern and eastern Africa: in caves near Johannesburg, at a lime quarry at Taung, and in the gorges and ash beds of Tanzania and Ethiopia. Africa is the place where humankind began.

This lesson looks at how people have explained human origins, at the scientific theory of evolution, and at the fossil evidence that makes Africa the **cradle of humankind**. You need no earlier knowledge, but the lesson on sources helps: the story of human origins is built almost entirely from archaeology and fossils. Dates in this lesson are approximate, given in "million years ago" (mya); scientists revise them as new finds are made.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Evolution"}
The gradual change in living things over many generations, by which new species develop from earlier ones.
:::

:::definition{term="Natural selection"}
Darwin's explanation of how evolution happens: individuals with features that help them survive in their environment live longer and have more offspring, so those features become more common over time.
:::

:::definition{term="Fossil"}
The preserved remains or traces (such as bones, teeth or footprints) of a living thing from the distant past, usually turned to stone.
:::

:::definition{term="Hominin"}
A member of the human family: modern humans and the extinct species more closely related to us than to chimpanzees, such as Australopithecus and earlier species of Homo.
:::

:::definition{term="Bipedalism"}
Walking upright on two legs, one of the earliest features that separated hominins from apes.
:::

## Two kinds of explanation

People have explained human origins in two very different ways. They answer different kinds of question, and many people hold both a religious faith and an understanding of science.

### Religious creation accounts

Most religions teach that a creator made the world and the first people. In the Bible's Book of Genesis, God creates Adam and Eve. Many African societies also have creation accounts: in Shona belief, for example, Mwari is the creator. These accounts explain **why** humans exist and how they should live; they are matters of faith and are not tested by excavation.

### The scientific theory of evolution

Science asks **how** humans developed, using evidence that can be tested: fossils, stone tools, dating methods and, more recently, genetics. Its central idea is evolution by natural selection.

## Charles Darwin and natural selection

Charles Darwin (1809–1882) was an English naturalist. On a five-year voyage on HMS *Beagle* in the 1830s he studied plants and animals in many lands, including the Galápagos Islands. In 1859 he published *On the Origin of Species*, which argued that:

1. living things produce more offspring than can survive;
2. offspring differ slightly from one another;
3. those with useful differences survive and reproduce more (natural selection, often summed up as "survival of the fittest");
4. over very long periods these small changes produce new species.

In *The Descent of Man* (1871) Darwin applied these ideas to humans and suggested that Africa was the most likely home of our earliest ancestors, because our closest living relatives, chimpanzees and gorillas, live there. At the time almost no human fossils had been found to test his idea; the African finds of the twentieth century supported it.

## Stages of human development

The table gives the main stages in outline. The dates are scientists' approximate ranges.

| Stage | Approximate time | Main features |
|---|---|---|
| Australopithecus ("southern ape") | about 4 to 2 mya | Walked upright; small brain (about a third of ours); lived in eastern and southern Africa |
| Homo habilis ("handy man") | about 2.4 to 1.4 mya | Larger brain; made simple stone tools (choppers and flakes) |
| Homo erectus ("upright man") | about 1.9 mya to roughly 100,000 years ago | Taller, larger brain; made hand axes; used fire; the first hominin to spread out of Africa into Asia |
| Homo sapiens ("wise man") | from about 300,000 years ago | Modern humans; large brain; language, art, complex tools; spread from Africa across the world |

:::keyfact{title="Learn the order"}
Australopithecus → Homo habilis → Homo erectus → Homo sapiens. Scientists now think human evolution was more like a branching bush than a single ladder, with several species sometimes alive at the same time, but this order is the outline you need.
:::

## The great fossil sites

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1859 | Darwin publishes *On the Origin of Species* |
| 1871 | Darwin's *The Descent of Man* suggests Africa as the birthplace of humans |
| 1924 | The Taung child skull is found in South Africa; Raymond Dart names *Australopithecus africanus* (1925) |
| 1947 | Robert Broom finds the skull known as "Mrs Ples" at Sterkfontein, South Africa |
| 1959 | Mary Leakey finds an early hominin skull at Olduvai Gorge, Tanzania |
| 1974 | The skeleton known as "Lucy" (*Australopithecus afarensis*) is found at Hadar, Ethiopia |
| 1978 | Mary Leakey's team uncovers fossil footprints at Laetoli, Tanzania |

- **Olduvai Gorge (Tanzania):** studied for decades by Louis and Mary Leakey; many hominin fossils and huge numbers of stone tools. *Homo habilis* was named from fossils found here.
- **Taung (South Africa):** the skull of a young Australopithecus, the "Taung child", the first early hominin recognised in Africa.
- **Sterkfontein (South Africa):** caves in the area now called the Cradle of Humankind World Heritage Site, with many Australopithecus fossils.
- **Laetoli (Tanzania):** footprints preserved in volcanic ash about 3.6 million years ago, proving that hominins walked upright very early.
- **Hadar (Ethiopia):** "Lucy", a partial skeleton about 3.2 million years old.

:::example{title="Why the Laetoli footprints matter: reasoning from evidence"}
1. **The evidence:** a trail of footprints left by two or more individuals in damp volcanic ash, which then hardened and was buried.
2. **Dating:** the ash layers can be dated to about 3.6 million years ago.
3. **Inference:** the prints show a heel strike and a big toe in line with the foot, like ours, so the makers walked upright on two legs.
4. **Conclusion:** upright walking came long before large brains or stone tools. This changed earlier ideas that a big brain came first.
:::

## Why Africa is the cradle of humankind

1. **The oldest hominin fossils** have all been found in Africa.
2. **Every major stage** (Australopithecus, Homo habilis, early Homo erectus, the earliest Homo sapiens) is represented by African fossils.
3. **The oldest stone tools** come from eastern Africa.
4. **Genetic studies** show that all living humans descend from populations that lived in Africa; people outside Africa descend from groups that left it within roughly the last 100,000 years.
5. **Environment:** a warm climate, open grasslands and rich animal life suited early hominins, and conditions in places such as the Rift Valley and limestone caves preserved fossils well.

:::example{title="Weighing the evidence for the 'cradle' idea"}
A sceptic might ask: "Perhaps fossils are only found in Africa because more people have searched there."

1. Scientists have searched hard in Asia and Europe too, and have found Homo erectus and later fossils there, but nothing as old as the African Australopithecus finds.
2. The genetic evidence is independent of fossils, yet it points to the same continent.
3. Two independent kinds of evidence agreeing makes the conclusion strong. This is the same cross-checking historians use with written and oral sources.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="'Humans evolved from chimpanzees'"}
Evolution does not say this. Humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor that lived millions of years ago; each line then evolved separately.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the sites and countries"}
Olduvai and Laetoli are in Tanzania; Hadar (Lucy) is in Ethiopia; Taung and Sterkfontein are in South Africa.
:::

:::mistake{title="Exact dates for fossils"}
Write "about 3.2 million years ago", not a precise year. Fossil dates are estimates and are revised as methods improve.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Identify and describe theories"}
For "Describe two theories on the origins of humankind", give one religious creation account and the scientific theory of evolution, and describe each fairly. Do not argue that one belief is foolish.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Explain why Africa is the cradle"}
Give developed reasons: "The oldest fossils of every stage, such as Lucy at Hadar, have been found in Africa, so humans most probably evolved there." Naming a site earns credit; explaining what it proves earns more.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Religious creation accounts explain human origins through a creator; the scientific theory of evolution explains them through gradual change.
- Charles Darwin's *On the Origin of Species* (1859) explained evolution by natural selection.
- The main stages are Australopithecus, Homo habilis, Homo erectus and Homo sapiens.
- Key African sites: Olduvai and Laetoli (Tanzania), Hadar (Ethiopia), Taung and Sterkfontein (South Africa).
- Africa is the cradle of humankind because the oldest fossils of every stage and the oldest tools are African, and genetics points to African ancestry for all people.
:::
