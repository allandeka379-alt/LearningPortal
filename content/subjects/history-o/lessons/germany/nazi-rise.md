## Introduction

In 1928 the Nazi Party won just 2.6 per cent of the vote in Germany's national election. Less than five years later its leader, Adolf Hitler, was Chancellor, and within eighteen months he was dictator of Germany. How a small, violent fringe party came to power in a democracy is one of the most important questions in modern history.

Zimbabwe's ZIMSEC syllabus studies this as part of the rise of European dictators (Paper 2), and Cambridge as Depth study B, key question 2: *Why was Hitler able to dominate Germany by 1934?* You should know about the problems of the Weimar Republic and the recovery under Stresemann.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Nazi"}
Short for the National Socialist German Workers' Party (NSDAP), the name taken in 1920 by the German Workers' Party that Hitler had joined in 1919.
:::

:::definition{term="SA (Sturmabteilung)"}
The Nazi "storm troopers" or "Brownshirts": the party's private army, used to protect meetings, attack opponents and march in the streets.
:::

:::definition{term="Lebensraum"}
"Living space": the Nazi idea that Germany needed more land in Eastern Europe for its people.
:::

:::definition{term="Volksgemeinschaft"}
"People's community": the Nazi idea of a united national community of racially "pure" Germans, above class and party divisions.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Names and dates to learn"}
- **1919:** Hitler joins the German Workers' Party (led by Anton Drexler); renamed the NSDAP in **1920**; Hitler leader from **1921**.
- **8-9 November 1923:** the Munich (Beer Hall) Putsch.
- **1924:** Hitler's trial and imprisonment; he writes *Mein Kampf*.
- **October 1929:** the Wall Street Crash begins the Depression.
- **July 1932:** the Nazis become the largest party in the Reichstag.
- **30 January 1933:** Hitler appointed Chancellor by President Hindenburg.
:::

## Nazi ideas

The party's 25-point programme of 1920 and Hitler's later writings set out its main ideas:

- **Nationalism:** destroy the Treaty of Versailles and unite all German-speaking people in one Greater Germany.
- **Racism and anti-Semitism:** Germans were an "Aryan" master race; Jews were blamed for Germany's defeat and problems and were to be excluded from citizenship.
- **Lebensraum:** expansion into Eastern Europe.
- **Anti-communism and anti-democracy:** Germany needed one strong leader, not quarrelling parties.
- **"Socialist" promises:** some points attacked big business and promised help for workers and small traders, although Hitler later played these down to win support from industrialists.

## The Munich Putsch, 1923

In November 1923, during the crisis of hyperinflation and the Ruhr occupation, Hitler decided to seize power in Bavaria and then march on Berlin, copying Mussolini's March on Rome. On the evening of **8 November** he and the SA took over a meeting in a Munich beer hall where the leaders of the Bavarian government were speaking. The war hero General **Ludendorff** supported him. But the Bavarian leaders, released during the night, called out the police and army. On **9 November** the Nazis marched through Munich; police opened fire, and sixteen Nazis and four police officers were killed. Hitler fled and was arrested.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: how a failed putsch helped Hitler"}
1. **The trial gave him a platform:** newspapers across Germany reported his speeches attacking the republic and Versailles.
2. **The judges were sympathetic:** he received the minimum five-year sentence and served only about nine months.
3. **He wrote *Mein Kampf*** in prison, setting out his ideas.
4. **He changed tactics:** he decided that the Nazis must win power legally, through elections, while still using the SA for intimidation.
5. **Result:** the putsch failed, but it made Hitler a national figure and led to the strategy that brought him to power.
:::

## Reorganisation and failure, 1924-29

After his release in December 1924, Hitler refounded the party in 1925 and rebuilt it:

- He made himself the undisputed **Führer** (leader) and set up a national organisation with regional leaders (*Gauleiter*).
- New groups attracted different supporters: the **Hitler Youth** (1926), organisations for students, teachers and women, and the **SS**, at first Hitler's bodyguard.
- **Joseph Goebbels** became the party leader in Berlin and built up its propaganda.

Yet in the 1928 election the Nazis won only **2.6%** of the vote and 12 seats. Germany was prosperous and stable under Stresemann, so most voters had no reason to listen to extremists. The Nazis did, however, begin to win support among farmers suffering from falling prices.

## The impact of the Depression, 1929-33

After the Wall Street Crash of October 1929, American banks recalled their loans. German businesses collapsed, world trade shrank, and unemployment rose to **over six million** by 1932. The coalition government fell in March 1930 because its parties could not agree on cutting unemployment benefits.

From 1930 Chancellor **Heinrich Brüning** governed by **presidential decree** under Article 48, rather than with a Reichstag majority. He cut spending and wages, which made the Depression worse and earned him the name "the hunger chancellor". Democracy had begun to break down before Hitler came to power.

| Reichstag election | Nazi share of the vote | Nazi seats |
|---|---|---|
| May 1928 | 2.6% | 12 |
| September 1930 | 18.3% | 107 |
| July 1932 | 37.3% | 230 |
| November 1932 | 33.1% | 196 |

### Why people voted Nazi

- **Despair and fear:** the Nazis promised work and bread. The middle classes, who remembered 1923, feared ruin again.
- **Fear of communism:** the Communist (KPD) vote also rose. Business leaders, farmers and the middle classes saw the Nazis as a barrier against revolution.
- **Propaganda:** Goebbels used posters, rallies, films, loudspeakers and simple slogans. Hitler flew from city to city to speak in the 1932 campaigns.
- **Hitler's leadership:** he presented himself as a strong leader above the quarrelling parties.
- **The SA:** street violence against communists made the Nazis look strong and suggested disorder only they could end.
- **Promises to everyone:** jobs for workers, protection for small shopkeepers, higher prices for farmers, the destruction of Versailles for nationalists.

Nazi support was strongest among Protestants, in rural and small-town areas and among the middle classes. It was weaker among Catholics and industrial workers, many of whom stayed loyal to the Centre Party, the SPD or the KPD.

## How Hitler became Chancellor

In the presidential election of 1932 Hitler stood against Hindenburg and won about 37% in the second round, but Hindenburg was re-elected. In July 1932 the Nazis became the **largest party** in the Reichstag, yet Hindenburg refused to make Hitler Chancellor.

Instead, a small group of conservatives around the President made the decisions:

- **Franz von Papen** was Chancellor from June to November 1932 but had almost no support in the Reichstag.
- **Kurt von Schleicher**, a general, replaced him in December 1932 but also failed to build a majority.
- Papen, wanting revenge on Schleicher, persuaded Hindenburg to appoint Hitler as Chancellor with Papen as Vice-Chancellor. The conservatives believed that with only three Nazis in the cabinet they could control Hitler.

On **30 January 1933** Hindenburg appointed Hitler Chancellor. The Nazi vote had actually fallen in November 1932, so Hitler came to power through a deal at the top, not by winning a majority. (How he then turned the chancellorship into a dictatorship, starting with the Reichstag Fire of February 1933, is covered in the next lesson.)

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1919 | Hitler joins the German Workers' Party |
| 1920 | Party renamed NSDAP; 25-point programme |
| November 1923 | Munich Putsch |
| 1924 | Trial; *Mein Kampf* written in prison |
| 1928 | Nazis win 2.6% of the vote |
| October 1929 | Wall Street Crash |
| 1930 | Brüning rules by decree; Nazis win 107 seats |
| July 1932 | Nazis largest party (230 seats) |
| 30 January 1933 | Hitler becomes Chancellor |

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: did Hitler win power or was he given it?"}
**Won:** the Nazis built a mass movement, and over a third of voters chose them in 1932; no other party could match their propaganda and organisation.
**Given:** they never won a majority in a free election, their vote was falling in November 1932, and Hitler was appointed by Hindenburg because Papen and other conservatives thought they could use him.
**Judgement:** both are needed. The Depression and Nazi campaigning made Hitler impossible to ignore, but the final step was the decision of a few conservative politicians.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Hitler was elected Chancellor"}
Germans voted for parties, not for a Chancellor. Hitler was appointed by President Hindenburg on 30 January 1933.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the Nazis won a majority"}
Their best result in a free election was 37.3% in July 1932. Even in March 1933 they won 43.9% and needed allies.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing reasons without linking them to the Depression"}
Nazi propaganda and ideas existed in the 1920s but brought little success. Explain why they worked after 1929.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Cambridge Paper 1 (b): 'Explain why Hitler became Chancellor'"}
Develop three or four reasons, such as the Depression, propaganda and Hitler's appeal, fear of communism, and the intrigues of Papen and Hindenburg. Link each one to the result.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (c): 'How important was the Depression?'"}
Compare the Depression with other factors and use the election figures: the jump from 2.6% to 37.3% shows its importance, while the appointment of January 1933 shows the role of the conservatives.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Nazi ideas combined nationalism, racism and anti-Semitism, Lebensraum and hostility to democracy and communism.
- The Munich Putsch (1923) failed, but the trial made Hitler famous and he turned to a legal route to power.
- In the prosperous years 1924-29 the Nazis had little success (2.6% in 1928).
- The Depression, propaganda, fear of communism and Hitler's appeal made them the largest party by July 1932.
- Hitler was appointed Chancellor on 30 January 1933 through a deal with Papen and Hindenburg.
:::
