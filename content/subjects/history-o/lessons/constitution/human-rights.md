## Introduction

Human rights belong to every person simply because they are human. Under colonial rule most Zimbabweans were denied basic rights: the vote, free movement, equal access to land and education. The struggle for independence was therefore also a struggle for rights. Today rights are protected by international agreements, such as the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, and by Chapter 4 of the Constitution of Zimbabwe, which has special sections for children.

You should know what a constitution is and the outline of the 2013 Constitution. This lesson covers **human rights** in the Universal Declaration and the Constitution, **children's rights and responsibilities**, **limitations** on rights, and **government efforts** to promote rights.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Human rights"}
Basic rights and freedoms that belong to every person, without discrimination, because they are human: for example the right to life, equality, freedom of expression and education.
:::

:::definition{term="Child"}
In the Constitution of Zimbabwe and the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, a person under the age of 18.
:::

:::definition{term="Responsibility"}
A duty that comes with rights, such as respecting the rights of others and obeying the law.
:::

:::definition{term="Limitation of rights"}
A legal restriction on a right, allowed only where it is fair, reasonable and necessary, for example to protect public safety, public health or the rights of others.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- 10 December 1948: Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR), adopted by the UN General Assembly.
- 1981: African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights (in force 1986).
- 20 November 1989: UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC).
- 1990: African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child.
- 2013: Constitution of Zimbabwe with a Declaration of Rights (Chapter 4).
:::

## Human rights in the Universal Declaration

The UDHR has 30 articles. Its first article states that all human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights. Rights are often grouped as:

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Civil and political rights | Life, liberty and security; freedom from slavery and torture; equality before the law; fair trial; freedom of thought, religion, expression and assembly; the right to vote and take part in government |
| Economic, social and cultural rights | Work and fair pay; rest and leisure; an adequate standard of living (food, housing, health care); education; taking part in cultural life |

The UDHR is not itself a binding treaty, but it inspired binding treaties and the bills of rights of many countries.

## Human rights in the Constitution of Zimbabwe

Chapter 4, the **Declaration of Rights**, protects among others:

- the right to life, personal liberty and human dignity;
- equality and non-discrimination;
- freedom from torture and from slavery;
- freedom of expression, of the media, of assembly and association, and of conscience;
- political rights, including the right to vote and stand for office;
- a fair trial and the rights of arrested and detained persons;
- property rights;
- the right to education and to health care, and rights to food and water;
- environmental rights, and language and culture.

It also has sections on the rights of particular groups: **women**, **children**, **the elderly**, **persons with disabilities** and **veterans of the liberation struggle**.

## Children's rights

The Constitution says that every child has the right:

- to equal treatment before the law;
- to be given a name and family name and to have the birth registered promptly;
- to family or parental care, or appropriate care when removed from the family;
- to education, health care, nutrition and shelter;
- to be protected from economic and sexual exploitation, child labour, maltreatment, neglect and abuse;
- not to be recruited into the armed forces or used in armed conflict;
- to have the child's best interests treated as paramount in every matter concerning the child.

The **UN Convention on the Rights of the Child** (1989) and the **African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child** (1990) set out similar rights, often grouped as survival, development, protection and participation (for example the right to be heard).

### Children's responsibilities

Rights come with responsibilities. Children are expected to:

- respect parents, elders and teachers, and help at home;
- attend school and work hard;
- respect the rights of other children, for example by not bullying;
- obey the law and school rules;
- look after their health and avoid drug and alcohol abuse;
- care for the environment and respect national symbols;
- help in the community.

:::example{title="Applying rights and responsibilities: a school case"}
1. **Situation:** Tatenda, aged 14, is kept at home by a relative to herd cattle instead of attending school.
2. **Rights involved:** his right to education and his right to be protected from child labour.
3. **Best interests:** the decision should be made in Tatenda's best interests, not for the relative's convenience.
4. **Responsibilities:** once back at school, Tatenda has a responsibility to attend regularly, respect teachers and other pupils, and work hard.
5. **Lesson:** rights protect children, and responsibilities help everyone's rights to be respected.
:::

## Limitations on rights

Most rights are not absolute. The Constitution allows rights to be limited by a law of general application where the limitation is **fair, reasonable, necessary and justifiable in a democratic society**, taking into account, for example:

- public safety, public order and public health;
- the rights and freedoms of others;
- national security and the prevention of crime.

Examples: freedom of expression does not allow hate speech or false statements that damage a person's reputation; freedom of movement can be limited during an epidemic; the right to liberty is limited when a court sends a convicted person to prison. The Constitution says some rights may not be limited at all, such as freedom from torture and from slavery and the right to a fair trial.

## Government efforts to promote human and children's rights

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1980 | Rapid expansion of schools and rural clinics begins after independence |
| 1990 | Zimbabwe ratifies the Convention on the Rights of the Child |
| 2007 | Domestic Violence Act |
| 2013 | Constitution with a Declaration of Rights and independent commissions, including the Zimbabwe Human Rights Commission |
| 2016 | Constitutional Court rules that no one may marry before the age of 18 |

Measures include:

- **Laws and institutions:** the Declaration of Rights, the Zimbabwe Human Rights Commission, the Gender Commission and the courts.
- **Education:** the expansion of schools after 1980 and assistance with school fees for vulnerable children.
- **Health:** free or low-cost health care for young children, immunisation campaigns with UNICEF and the WHO.
- **Child protection:** birth registration, child protection officers and laws against child labour and abuse; the 2016 Constitutional Court ruling against child marriage.
- **International commitments:** ratifying the UN and African treaties on rights and reporting on them.

:::example{title="Weighing government efforts"}
1. **Achievements:** a strong Declaration of Rights; a high literacy rate after the expansion of education in the 1980s; the ruling against child marriage; immunisation programmes.
2. **Limits:** economic difficulties have made it hard to provide free education and health care for all; birth registration and child marriage remain problems in some areas; human rights groups have reported violations of civil and political rights at times.
3. **Judgement:** good laws and institutions exist, but putting rights fully into practice depends on resources and on how laws are enforced.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the responsibilities"}
Questions on children often ask for **rights and responsibilities**. Give both.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying all rights are absolute"}
Most rights can be limited by law for fair and necessary reasons; only a few, such as freedom from torture, can never be limited.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Human rights belong to every person; the UDHR (10 December 1948) lists civil, political, economic, social and cultural rights.
- Chapter 4 of the Constitution of Zimbabwe is the Declaration of Rights, with sections for women, children, the elderly, persons with disabilities and veterans.
- Children (under 18) have rights to a name, care, education, health, protection from abuse and exploitation, and to have their best interests put first; they also have responsibilities.
- Most rights can be limited by fair, reasonable and necessary laws; some, such as freedom from torture, cannot.
- Government efforts include laws, the Human Rights Commission, education and health programmes and child protection, but resources and enforcement limit their impact.
:::
