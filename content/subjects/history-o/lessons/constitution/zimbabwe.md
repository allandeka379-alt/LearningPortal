## Introduction

Every Zimbabwean is affected by the Constitution, even if few have read it. It decides how the President and Parliament are chosen, what the courts can do, who is a citizen and which rights every person has. The present Constitution was approved by voters in a referendum on 16 March 2013 and replaced the Lancaster House Constitution that had been in force since independence in 1980.

You should already know the main events since independence. This lesson covers the **meaning and functions** of a constitution, the **making** of the 2013 Constitution and its main **provisions** in outline. Democracy and human rights have their own lessons.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Constitution"}
The supreme law of a country, which sets out how the country is governed, the powers and limits of the organs of the state, and the rights and duties of citizens.
:::

:::definition{term="Separation of powers"}
The division of state power between three arms, the **executive** (which governs), the **legislature** (which makes laws) and the **judiciary** (which interprets laws and settles disputes), so that no single arm holds all power.
:::

:::definition{term="Checks and balances"}
Ways in which each arm of the state can limit the others, for example Parliament questioning ministers or the courts striking down unlawful actions.
:::

:::definition{term="Rule of law"}
The principle that everyone, including the government, is subject to the law and that laws are applied fairly and equally.
:::

:::definition{term="Citizenship"}
Membership of a state, with the rights and duties that go with it.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Key facts"}
- 1979: Lancaster House Constitution agreed; in force at independence on 18 April 1980.
- February 2000: a proposed new constitution is rejected in a referendum.
- 16 March 2013: referendum approves the new Constitution.
- 22 May 2013: the Constitution is signed into law.
- The Declaration of Rights is Chapter 4 of the Constitution.
:::

## Functions of a constitution

1. **Supreme law:** any law or action that conflicts with it is invalid.
2. **Sets up the organs of state:** it creates the executive, legislature and judiciary and defines their powers.
3. **Limits government:** it prevents abuse of power through the separation of powers and checks and balances.
4. **Protects rights:** it lists the rights and freedoms of every person.
5. **Defines citizenship:** it says who is a citizen and how citizenship is gained or lost.
6. **Sets out national values and symbols:** for example the flag, anthem and official languages.
7. **Provides for change:** it explains how it can be amended.

## How the 2013 Constitution was made

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1979 | Lancaster House Constitution agreed in London |
| 1980 | Independence under the Lancaster House Constitution |
| 1980s-2000s | The Lancaster House Constitution is amended many times |
| February 2000 | A government-backed draft constitution is rejected in a referendum |
| 2008 | The Global Political Agreement commits the parties to a new constitution |
| 2009-2012 | A parliamentary committee (COPAC) holds public outreach meetings and drafts the text |
| 16 March 2013 | Referendum approves the new Constitution by a large majority |
| 22 May 2013 | The Constitution is signed into law |

The Lancaster House Constitution was a compromise made to end the war. Among other things it reserved seats for whites for seven years and protected property rights. It was amended many times; for example, an amendment created the executive presidency from the end of 1987. After the political crisis of 2008, the Global Political Agreement between ZANU-PF and the two MDC formations set up a parliamentary committee, **COPAC**, which held meetings across the country to hear people's views before drafting the new text.

## The Constitution in outline

### Founding values and principles

The Constitution states that Zimbabwe is a unitary, democratic and sovereign republic. Its founding values include the supremacy of the Constitution, the rule of law, fundamental human rights and freedoms, the equality of all people, gender equality, good governance and recognition of the liberation struggle. It recognises **sixteen** officially recognised languages, including Shona, Ndebele, English and sign language.

### Citizenship

A person can be a citizen of Zimbabwe:
- **by birth** (born in Zimbabwe to a parent who is a citizen, or born outside to a citizen parent in certain cases);
- **by descent**; or
- **by registration** (for example through marriage or long residence, under conditions set by law).

Citizens have rights such as voting and holding a passport, and responsibilities such as obeying the law, respecting the Constitution and defending the country.

### The Declaration of Rights

Chapter 4 lists fundamental rights and freedoms: for example the right to life, personal liberty, human dignity, equality and non-discrimination, freedom from torture, freedom of expression, assembly and association, political rights, a fair trial, education and health care. It also has sections on the rights of children, women, the elderly, persons with disabilities and war veterans. Some rights may be limited by law where this is fair, reasonable and necessary in a democratic society.

### The executive

- The **President** is head of state and government and commander-in-chief of the Defence Forces. The President is elected by voters for a **five-year term** and may serve at most **two terms**.
- **Vice-Presidents** and **Cabinet** ministers help the President run the government.
- Functions: carrying out laws and policies, running government departments, conducting foreign relations and proposing laws and the budget.

### The legislature

- **Parliament** has two houses: the **Senate** and the **National Assembly**.
- Functions: making laws, approving taxes and the budget, overseeing the executive (questions, committees), and representing the people.

### The judiciary

- The **Constitutional Court** is the highest court on constitutional matters; the **Supreme Court** is the final court of appeal in other cases; then the **High Court**, specialised courts (Labour and Administrative Courts), magistrates' courts and local courts.
- The **Chief Justice** heads the judiciary.
- Functions: interpreting the law, settling disputes, protecting rights and checking that laws and actions agree with the Constitution.

### Independent commissions and devolution

Independent commissions support democracy, including the Zimbabwe Electoral Commission, the Zimbabwe Human Rights Commission, the Zimbabwe Gender Commission, the Zimbabwe Media Commission and the National Peace and Reconciliation Commission. The Constitution also provides for the **devolution** of some powers to provincial and metropolitan councils and local authorities.

:::example{title="Separation of powers in action, step by step"}
1. **Legislature:** Parliament passes a law.
2. **Executive:** the President signs it, and ministries put it into effect.
3. **Judiciary:** a citizen believes the law breaks a right in the Declaration of Rights and goes to court.
4. **Check:** the Constitutional Court can declare the law invalid if it conflicts with the Constitution.
5. **Why it matters:** no single arm can make, enforce and judge a law on its own, which protects citizens against abuse of power.
:::

:::example{title="Weighing a debate: has the Constitution limited power in practice?"}
1. **Supporters point to:** presidential term limits, a strong Declaration of Rights, independent commissions and devolution, approved by a large majority of voters.
2. **Critics point to:** delays in passing laws to put some provisions into effect, amendments since 2017 (for example on how some senior judges are appointed), and slow progress on devolution.
3. **Judgement:** the text is widely regarded as a strong framework; how fully it is put into practice is debated.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the three arms of the state"}
The legislature (Parliament) **makes** laws; the executive (President and Cabinet) **carries them out**; the judiciary (courts) **interprets** them and settles disputes.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the referendums"}
The draft of 2000 was **rejected**; the Constitution of 2013 was **approved** (16 March 2013).
:::

:::mistake{title="Inventing section numbers"}
Examiners reward accurate content, not section numbers. If you are not sure of a number, describe the provision instead.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1: define and explain"}
"Define a constitution and explain its functions" needs a clear definition (supreme law setting out how a country is governed and the rights of citizens), then several functions, each explained.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Describe the three arms"}
For each arm, name who forms it and give two or three functions. Finish by explaining how the separation of powers and checks and balances protect citizens.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A constitution is the supreme law that sets up and limits government and protects rights.
- Zimbabwe's 2013 Constitution replaced the Lancaster House Constitution after the COPAC process and a referendum on 16 March 2013.
- It sets out founding values, sixteen recognised languages, citizenship by birth, descent and registration, and a Declaration of Rights (Chapter 4).
- The executive (President, Vice-Presidents, Cabinet), legislature (Senate and National Assembly) and judiciary (courts led by the Chief Justice) are separated, with checks and balances.
- Independent commissions and devolution are meant to strengthen democracy and accountability.
:::
