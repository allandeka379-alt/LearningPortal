## Introduction

Two international groups shaped Zimbabwe's place in the world after 1980. The **Commonwealth**, an association of mostly former British colonies, helped bring about the Lancaster House Conference of 1979; Harare later hosted its summit in 1991, but Zimbabwe left in 2003. The **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)** brought together countries that refused to take sides in the Cold War; Harare hosted its summit in 1986, when Prime Minister Robert Mugabe became its chairman.

You should already know about decolonisation and the Cold War in outline. This lesson covers the **origins**, **aims**, **organs** and **activities** of the Commonwealth, Zimbabwe's **membership and withdrawal**, and the **origins**, **aims** and **activities** of the Non-Aligned Movement.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Commonwealth (of Nations)"}
A voluntary association of independent states, most of them former British colonies, that co-operate on the basis of shared values such as democracy, human rights and development.
:::

:::definition{term="Dominion"}
A self-governing country of the British Empire, such as Canada, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa in the early twentieth century.
:::

:::definition{term="Non-alignment"}
A policy of not joining either of the Cold War blocs led by the United States and the Soviet Union.
:::

:::definition{term="CHOGM"}
Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting: the summit of Commonwealth leaders, normally held every two years.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- 1931: Statute of Westminster recognises the independence of the dominions.
- 1949: London Declaration allows republics to remain members.
- 1955: Bandung Conference of Asian and African states.
- 1961: first Non-Aligned summit, Belgrade.
- 1965: Commonwealth Secretariat set up in London.
- 1979: Lusaka CHOGM agrees on a conference on Rhodesia.
- 1986: NAM summit in Harare. 1991: CHOGM in Harare (Harare Declaration).
- 2002: Zimbabwe suspended from the Commonwealth; December 2003: Zimbabwe leaves.
:::

## The Commonwealth

### Origins

The Commonwealth grew out of the British Empire. As the dominions gained self-government, Britain recognised them as equal partners; the **Statute of Westminster (1931)** gave legal form to this. After India became independent in 1947 and decided to become a republic, the **London Declaration (1949)** allowed republics to remain members, with the British monarch as the symbolic **Head of the Commonwealth**. As African, Asian and Caribbean colonies became independent, most joined, turning the Commonwealth into a multiracial association of equals.

### Aims

- To promote democracy, good governance, the rule of law and human rights.
- To promote economic and social development and reduce poverty.
- To encourage co-operation in education, health, trade and sport.
- To oppose racism (the Singapore Declaration of 1971) and support world peace.

### Organs

| Organ | Function |
|---|---|
| Head of the Commonwealth | The British monarch; a symbol of the free association |
| CHOGM | Leaders' summit, normally every two years; sets policy |
| Commonwealth Secretariat (Marlborough House, London) | Set up in 1965; organises meetings and programmes, led by the Secretary-General |
| Commonwealth Ministerial Action Group (CMAG) | Set up in 1995; deals with serious violations of Commonwealth values and can recommend suspension |
| Commonwealth Foundation and Commonwealth of Learning | Support civil society and distance education |

### Activities

- **Against apartheid and minority rule:** South Africa left the Commonwealth in 1961 when its racial policies were condemned; the Gleneagles Agreement (1977) discouraged sporting contacts with apartheid South Africa.
- **Zimbabwe's independence:** at the **Lusaka CHOGM (August 1979)** leaders agreed that Britain should hold a conference leading to majority rule, which became the Lancaster House Conference. A Commonwealth force monitored the 1980 ceasefire and Commonwealth observers watched the elections.
- **Democracy:** the **Harare Declaration (1991)** committed members to democracy, human rights and good governance.
- **Development:** technical help, scholarships, education programmes and the Commonwealth Games.

:::example{title="A role explained step by step: the Lusaka CHOGM, 1979"}
1. **Situation:** in 1979 Britain's new government was considering recognising the Muzorewa government that came from the internal settlement.
2. **Pressure:** at Lusaka, African Commonwealth leaders such as Kaunda and Nyerere, supported by others, argued that only an agreement including the Patriotic Front could end the war.
3. **Agreement:** the meeting agreed that Britain should call an all-party constitutional conference and hold free elections under British supervision.
4. **Result:** the Lancaster House Conference opened in September 1979, leading to independence on 18 April 1980.
:::

### Zimbabwe's membership and withdrawal

Zimbabwe joined the Commonwealth at independence in 1980 and hosted CHOGM in 1991. After the disputed presidential election of March 2002 and criticism of violence and land seizures, the Commonwealth **suspended Zimbabwe from its councils** in March 2002. When the Abuja CHOGM in December 2003 kept the suspension in place, Zimbabwe **withdrew from the Commonwealth** in December 2003. The government argued that the Commonwealth was being used by Britain and its allies to punish land reform; critics of the government argued the suspension upheld the Harare Declaration. In 2018 Zimbabwe applied to rejoin.

## The Non-Aligned Movement

### Origins

After 1945 the world was split between the Western bloc led by the United States and the Eastern bloc led by the Soviet Union. Many newly independent countries did not want to be drawn into either side.

- **Bandung Conference (April 1955):** 29 Asian and African countries met in Indonesia to oppose colonialism and promote co-operation and peace.
- **Belgrade Conference (September 1961):** the first summit of the Non-Aligned Movement, with 25 member states. Its leading founders were Josip Broz Tito of Yugoslavia, Jawaharlal Nehru of India, Gamal Abdel Nasser of Egypt, Sukarno of Indonesia and Kwame Nkrumah of Ghana.

### Aims

- To stay independent of the Cold War blocs and military alliances.
- To end colonialism, racism and apartheid.
- To promote world peace and disarmament.
- To promote economic development and a fairer world economy for poorer countries.
- To defend the sovereignty of member states and the right of peoples to self-determination.

### Activities

- Summits of leaders, usually every three years, and a voice for developing countries at the UN.
- Support for liberation movements and pressure against apartheid.
- Campaigns for a fairer international economic order in the 1970s.
- **The Harare summit (September 1986):** Robert Mugabe became chairman of NAM, and the summit set up the AFRICA Fund (Action for Resisting Invasion, Colonialism and Apartheid) to help the Frontline States resist South African destabilisation.

### Assessment

NAM gave newly independent countries a collective voice and kept anti-colonial and anti-apartheid issues on the world agenda. But it had no power to enforce decisions, and some members were in practice close to one bloc (for example Cuba to the Soviet Union). After the Cold War ended about 1991, many asked whether non-alignment still had a purpose, although NAM continued to speak for developing countries.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the two Harare meetings"}
1986 was the **Non-Aligned Movement** summit (AFRICA Fund). 1991 was the **Commonwealth** summit (Harare Declaration on democracy and human rights).
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking non-aligned meant neutral in everything"}
NAM members took strong positions against colonialism and apartheid; non-alignment meant not joining the Cold War blocs.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Commonwealth grew from the British Empire into a free association of equal states (1931, 1949), with CHOGM and a Secretariat (1965).
- It opposed apartheid, helped bring about Lancaster House (Lusaka 1979) and set democratic standards (Harare Declaration 1991).
- Zimbabwe joined in 1980, was suspended in 2002 and left in December 2003.
- NAM began with Bandung (1955) and Belgrade (1961) to keep new states out of the Cold War blocs and oppose colonialism.
- Harare hosted the NAM summit in 1986, which set up the AFRICA Fund.
:::
