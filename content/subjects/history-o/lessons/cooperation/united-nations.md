## Introduction

The United Nations (UN) was created at the end of the Second World War to prevent another world war, after the League of Nations had failed. Zimbabwe joined it in 1980, but the UN had been involved in Zimbabwe's history long before: in 1966 its Security Council imposed its first ever mandatory economic sanctions, against the Rhodesian regime that had declared UDI. Today UN agencies such as UNICEF, the World Health Organization and the World Food Programme work in Zimbabwe on child health, disease and food security.

You should know why the League of Nations failed and how the Second World War ended. This lesson covers the **formation** and **aims** of the UN, its **organs** and **agencies**, its **work and effectiveness**, and its role in **human rights**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="United Nations Charter"}
The founding treaty of the UN, signed at San Francisco on 26 June 1945, which sets out its aims, principles and organs.
:::

:::definition{term="Veto"}
The power of each of the five permanent members of the Security Council to block a decision by voting against it.
:::

:::definition{term="Peacekeeping"}
The use of UN troops, police and observers, with the consent of the parties, to help keep a ceasefire or carry out a peace agreement.
:::

:::definition{term="Specialised agency"}
An international organisation linked to the UN that works in one field, such as health (WHO), food and agriculture (FAO) or labour (ILO).
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- August 1941: Atlantic Charter (Roosevelt and Churchill).
- April-June 1945: San Francisco Conference; Charter signed 26 June 1945.
- 24 October 1945: the UN comes into existence (now United Nations Day).
- 10 December 1948: Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
- 1966: first mandatory sanctions, against Rhodesia.
- 25 August 1980: Zimbabwe joins the UN.
:::

## Formation

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1941 | Atlantic Charter sets out war aims including a system of general security |
| 1944 | Dumbarton Oaks talks (USA, Britain, USSR, China) plan the new organisation |
| February 1945 | Yalta Conference agrees voting arrangements in the Security Council |
| April-June 1945 | San Francisco Conference; 50 states sign the Charter on 26 June |
| 24 October 1945 | The Charter comes into force: the UN is founded |
| 1946 | The League of Nations is formally dissolved |

The founders tried to avoid the League's weaknesses: the great powers, including the USA and the USSR, were members from the start, and the Security Council was given power to order sanctions and military action.

## Aims

1. To maintain international peace and security.
2. To develop friendly relations among nations based on equal rights and self-determination of peoples.
3. To co-operate in solving economic, social, cultural and humanitarian problems and to promote respect for human rights.
4. To be a centre for harmonising the actions of nations.

## Structure: the main organs

| Organ | Membership | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| General Assembly | All member states, one vote each | Discusses any world issue; makes recommendations; approves the budget; elects non-permanent Security Council members; admits members on the Council's recommendation |
| Security Council | 15 members: 5 permanent (USA, Britain, France, Russia, China) with a veto, 10 elected for two years | Main responsibility for peace and security; can order sanctions, authorise force and set up peacekeeping missions |
| Secretariat | Staff led by the Secretary-General | Runs the UN day to day; the Secretary-General can bring threats to peace to the Council's attention |
| International Court of Justice (The Hague) | 15 judges | Settles legal disputes between states and gives advisory opinions |
| Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) | 54 members | Co-ordinates economic and social work and the specialised agencies |
| Trusteeship Council | The permanent members | Supervised trust territories until independence; suspended its work in 1994 |

### Specialised agencies and programmes

- **WHO** (World Health Organization): health; led the campaign that eradicated smallpox (declared in 1980).
- **UNICEF:** children's health, nutrition and education.
- **UNESCO:** education, science and culture; lists World Heritage Sites such as Great Zimbabwe.
- **FAO:** food and agriculture; **WFP** (World Food Programme): food aid.
- **ILO** (International Labour Organization): workers' rights.
- **UNHCR:** refugees.
- **IMF** and **World Bank:** loans and economic stability.

## The work and effectiveness of the UN

### Peace and security

- **Korea (1950-1953):** the Security Council authorised a UN force to resist North Korea's invasion of South Korea, possible only because the USSR was boycotting the Council.
- **Suez (1956):** the first armed UN peacekeeping force (UNEF) supervised the withdrawal of foreign troops from Egypt.
- **The Congo (1960-1964):** a large UN force helped end the Katanga secession; Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld died in a plane crash during the crisis.
- **Southern Africa:** sanctions against Rhodesia (1966 and 1968); an arms embargo against South Africa (1977); a UN mission oversaw Namibia's elections and independence (1989-1990).
- **Failures:** the UN could not stop the genocide in Rwanda (1994) or the massacre at Srebrenica in Bosnia (1995); the veto often paralysed the Security Council during the Cold War.

:::example{title="A weakness explained step by step: the veto"}
1. **The rule:** any one of the five permanent members can block a Security Council decision.
2. **Why it exists:** the founders believed the UN would only work if the great powers agreed; they did not want them to leave, as some had left the League.
3. **The problem:** in the Cold War, the USA and USSR used the veto to protect themselves and their allies.
4. **Result:** the Council could not act in many conflicts, so the UN often seemed powerless when great-power interests were involved.
:::

### Development and humanitarian work

- Agencies have improved health (smallpox eradication, vaccination), food security and education across the world.
- UNHCR has helped millions of refugees, including Zimbabweans in camps in Mozambique and Zambia during the liberation war.
- The Millennium Development Goals (2000) and Sustainable Development Goals (2015) set targets for reducing poverty.

## Human rights

The UN Charter promised to promote human rights. On **10 December 1948** the General Assembly adopted the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)**, drafted by a commission chaired by Eleanor Roosevelt. It sets out civil and political rights (life, liberty, fair trial, freedom of speech and religion) and economic, social and cultural rights (work, education, health). It is a declaration, not a binding treaty, but it inspired later treaties such as the two International Covenants (1966) and the Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989), as well as national constitutions, including Zimbabwe's.

Today the **Human Rights Council** (2006) reviews every member's record, and the High Commissioner for Human Rights reports on abuses. Critics point out that the UN cannot force powerful states to respect rights.

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: has the UN been a success?"}
1. **For:** there has been no third world war; peacekeeping has helped end many conflicts; agencies have saved millions of lives; it helped decolonisation and the end of apartheid.
2. **Against:** the veto blocks action; it failed in Rwanda and Srebrenica; it depends on members for money and troops.
3. **Judgement:** most historians see the UN as more effective than the League, especially in humanitarian work, but limited in stopping wars when great powers disagree.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the General Assembly and the Security Council"}
The General Assembly includes every member and makes recommendations. The Security Council (15 members) can make binding decisions, and the five permanent members have a veto.
:::

:::mistake{title="Dating the UN to 26 June 1945"}
The Charter was **signed** on 26 June 1945; the UN came into existence when it came into force on **24 October 1945**.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The UN was planned during the Second World War and came into existence on 24 October 1945; Zimbabwe joined in 1980.
- Its aims are peace and security, friendly relations, co-operation and human rights.
- Its main organs are the General Assembly, Security Council (with the veto), Secretariat, ICJ, ECOSOC and the Trusteeship Council; specialised agencies include WHO, UNICEF, UNESCO, FAO and ILO.
- It has had successes (Korea, Suez, Namibia, smallpox) and failures (Rwanda, Srebrenica, Cold War vetoes).
- The UDHR (10 December 1948) is the foundation of the UN's human rights work.
:::
