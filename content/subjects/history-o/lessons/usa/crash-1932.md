## Introduction

In October 1929 share prices on the New York Stock Exchange, in Wall Street, collapsed. Within three years about a quarter of American workers had no job, thousands of banks had failed, and families were living in shacks made of scrap. The **Great Depression** spread across the world: prices for copper, tobacco and other exports from southern Africa fell sharply, hitting mines, farms and workers in Southern Rhodesia and its neighbours.

In this lesson you will study why the Crash happened, how it turned into a depression, how President Herbert Hoover responded, and why Franklin D. Roosevelt won the presidential election of 1932. Cambridge examines these as part of Depth study D.

You should already know about the economic boom of the 1920s and its weaknesses.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Shares"}
Parts of the ownership of a company. Shareholders hope to receive part of the profits and to sell the shares later for more than they paid.
:::

:::definition{term="Speculation"}
Buying shares (or other things) not to keep them, but to sell them quickly at a higher price for a profit.
:::

:::definition{term="Buying on the margin"}
Buying shares by paying only part of the price (sometimes as little as 10 per cent) and borrowing the rest from a broker, expecting to repay the loan when the shares were sold at a profit.
:::

:::definition{term="Depression"}
A long and severe fall in economic activity, with falling production, prices and trade and high unemployment.
:::

:::definition{term="Hooverville"}
A shanty town of homeless people, built from scrap materials, named mockingly after President Hoover.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **24 October 1929:** "Black Thursday", panic selling begins.
- **29 October 1929:** "Black Tuesday", the worst day of the Crash.
- **1930:** the Hawley-Smoot Tariff.
- **1932:** the Reconstruction Finance Corporation; the Bonus Army (July).
- **November 1932:** Roosevelt defeats Hoover in the presidential election.
:::

## Causes of the Crash

### Speculation and buying on the margin

During the 1920s share prices rose steadily, and from about 1927 they rose much faster. More and more people, not only the rich, bought shares hoping to make quick profits. Many bought **on the margin**, borrowing most of the price. Banks also speculated with their customers' savings. Share prices rose far above the real value of the companies, because people bought shares expecting prices to rise, which made prices rise further. As long as prices kept rising, everyone seemed to win.

### Weaknesses in the economy

The speculation was built on a weakening economy. By 1929 there was **overproduction**: factories had made more cars and consumer goods than people could afford, and farmers had been in difficulty for years. Wealth was very unequally shared, so demand could not keep rising. Other countries' tariffs made it hard to export surplus goods. There were thousands of small, poorly regulated **banks**, which had lent heavily.

### The Crash

In 1929 some investors began to suspect that prices could not keep rising and started to sell. Once prices began to fall, people who had bought on the margin had to sell to repay their loans, which pushed prices down further. On **24 October 1929** ("Black Thursday") panic selling began. Leading bankers tried to support the market by buying shares, but on **29 October** ("Black Tuesday") about 16 million shares were sold and prices collapsed. Over the following weeks billions of dollars of value disappeared, and share prices went on falling, with some recoveries, until 1932.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: how buying on the margin turned a fall into a crash"}
1. **Borrowed money:** many investors had paid only a small part of the price of their shares and owed the rest.
2. **Prices began to fall:** brokers demanded that investors repay their loans.
3. **Forced selling:** investors who could not pay had to sell their shares at any price.
4. **A spiral:** each wave of selling pushed prices lower, forcing more people to sell.
5. **Result:** a fall in prices became a panic, and those who had borrowed were ruined.
:::

## Effects of the Crash

### Financial effects

Many investors lost everything, and people who had borrowed to buy shares could not repay. Banks that had lent money to speculators, or speculated themselves, lost heavily. When people feared their bank might fail, they rushed to withdraw their savings, and many banks collapsed. Thousands of banks failed between 1929 and 1933, and millions of families lost their savings.

### Economic effects

With savings gone and banks failing, people and businesses stopped spending and investing. Demand fell, so factories cut production and dismissed workers; unemployed workers spent even less, so demand fell further. Industrial production fell sharply. Foreign trade collapsed, especially after the **Hawley-Smoot Tariff** (1930) raised American tariffs and other countries retaliated. American banks called in loans from abroad, spreading the depression to Germany and the rest of the world. Farm prices fell even lower, and many farmers lost their land.

### Social effects

- **Unemployment** rose to about a quarter of the workforce by 1932-33: historians estimate about 12 to 13 million people at its peak.
- Families who could not pay rent or mortgages were evicted. Homeless people built **Hoovervilles** on waste ground in many cities.
- Breadlines and soup kitchens fed the hungry; charities could not cope with the numbers.
- Hundreds of thousands of people travelled the country looking for work, often riding illegally on freight trains.
- Black Americans, Mexican Americans and other minorities were often the first to lose their jobs.

## Hoover's response

**Herbert Hoover** was President from 1929 to 1933. He believed in **"rugged individualism"**: people should solve their problems by their own efforts, and direct federal relief would weaken their independence. He believed the economy would recover by itself.

But Hoover did act, more than his critics often say:

- He persuaded business leaders to keep up wages and investment (voluntary agreements).
- He cut income tax briefly at the end of 1929 and increased spending on public works, such as the dam on the Colorado River later named the Hoover Dam.
- The **Federal Farm Board**, set up in 1929, bought surplus crops to support prices.
- In 1932 he set up the **Reconstruction Finance Corporation**, which lent money to banks, railways and large businesses, and in 1932 an act allowed loans to states for relief.

These measures were too small and too late, and some made things worse: the **Hawley-Smoot Tariff** damaged world trade. Hoover still refused direct federal payments to the unemployed.

### The Bonus Army

In the summer of 1932 thousands of unemployed First World War veterans, the **Bonus Army**, marched to Washington and camped there, demanding early payment of a bonus promised for 1945. In July troops under General Douglas MacArthur drove them out of their camp with tear gas and burned their shelters. Pictures of soldiers attacking veterans badly damaged Hoover's reputation.

## The 1932 election

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 24 October 1929 | Black Thursday |
| 29 October 1929 | Black Tuesday |
| 1930 | Hawley-Smoot Tariff |
| January 1932 | Reconstruction Finance Corporation set up |
| July 1932 | The Bonus Army is driven out of Washington |
| November 1932 | Roosevelt wins the presidential election |
| March 1933 | Roosevelt becomes President |

The Democratic candidate, **Franklin D. Roosevelt**, Governor of New York, promised Americans a **"New Deal"**. He gave few details, but he promised action: government help for the unemployed, public works, help for farmers, the regulation of banks and the end of Prohibition. He was a confident, warm speaker who travelled the country, despite being unable to walk unaided after polio.

Roosevelt won a landslide, carrying 42 of the 48 states. Voters blamed Hoover and the Republicans for the Depression and for doing too little, and Roosevelt offered hope.

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: did Roosevelt win, or did Hoover lose?"}
**Hoover lost:** the Depression happened on his watch, his measures failed, he seemed cold and uncaring, and the Bonus Army destroyed his reputation.
**Roosevelt won:** he offered energy, optimism and a promise of action; his record as Governor of New York, where he had organised relief, made the promise believable.
**Judgement:** most historians think that any Republican would have lost in 1932, but Roosevelt's personality and promises explain the size of his victory.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the Crash alone caused the Depression"}
The Crash was a trigger. The underlying causes were overproduction, inequality, weak banks and falling trade, which turned a stock-market collapse into a long depression.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Hoover did nothing"}
Hoover took several actions, including the Reconstruction Finance Corporation and public works, but he refused direct federal relief and his measures were too limited.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Roosevelt became President in November 1932"}
He won the election in November 1932 but took office on 4 March 1933.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (c): 'How far was speculation responsible for the Crash?'"}
Explain how speculation and buying on the margin made the market unstable, then weigh them against overproduction, inequality and weak banks. Reach a judgement about which was most important.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (b): 'Explain why Roosevelt won in 1932'"}
Give reasons from both sides: Hoover's unpopularity (the Depression, his beliefs, the Bonus Army) and Roosevelt's appeal (the New Deal promise, his personality and record).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Speculation and buying on the margin pushed share prices far above real values; overproduction, inequality and weak banks undermined the boom.
- Panic selling on 24 and 29 October 1929 caused the Crash; forced selling turned falling prices into collapse.
- Bank failures, falling demand and collapsing trade led to the Depression; unemployment reached about a quarter of the workforce.
- Hoover believed in rugged individualism; his measures (RFC, public works) were too limited, and the Bonus Army ruined his reputation.
- Roosevelt promised a New Deal and won a landslide in November 1932.
:::
