## Introduction

When **Franklin D. Roosevelt** became President on 4 March 1933, the American banking system was close to collapse, about a quarter of the workforce was unemployed, and many people had lost faith in government. Over the next few years Roosevelt's government created dozens of new agencies, spent billions of dollars and changed the relationship between Americans and their federal government. This programme was called the **New Deal**.

The New Deal is a key part of Cambridge Depth study D. It is also a major example in world history of a government using public works, welfare and regulation to fight a depression, ideas that many governments, including in Africa after independence, later used in their own development plans.

You should already know about the Wall Street Crash, the Depression and the 1932 election.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="New Deal"}
Roosevelt's programme, from 1933, to bring relief, recovery and reform to the USA during the Depression.
:::

:::definition{term="The three Rs"}
The aims of the New Deal: **relief** (immediate help for the unemployed and poor), **recovery** (getting the economy working again) and **reform** (changing the system so that a depression would not happen again).
:::

:::definition{term="Alphabet agencies"}
The new federal organisations of the New Deal, known by their initials, such as the CCC, AAA and TVA.
:::

:::definition{term="Fireside chats"}
Roosevelt's informal radio talks to the American people, explaining his policies; the first was in March 1933.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **4 March 1933:** Roosevelt's inauguration.
- **March-June 1933:** the "Hundred Days".
- **1935:** the Second New Deal (WPA; Wagner Act; Social Security Act).
- **May 1935:** the Supreme Court rules the NIRA unconstitutional.
- **1936:** Roosevelt re-elected with a huge majority.
:::

## The inauguration and the banking crisis

In his inaugural speech Roosevelt told Americans that "the only thing we have to fear is fear itself" (Franklin D. Roosevelt, inaugural address, 4 March 1933). He promised firm action and asked Congress for broad powers to deal with the emergency.

His first task was the banks. He declared a **bank holiday**, closing all banks. Congress quickly passed the **Emergency Banking Act**: government inspectors checked the banks, and only those judged sound were allowed to reopen, with government support. In his first **fireside chat** Roosevelt explained the plan on the radio and urged people to trust the reopened banks. When the banks reopened, more money was deposited than withdrawn. Confidence began to return.

## The Hundred Days, March-June 1933

In his first hundred days Roosevelt sent a flood of laws to Congress, which passed them quickly. The most important were:

- **Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC):** gave young unemployed men work in the countryside, planting trees, fighting soil erosion and building paths in national parks. They lived in camps and sent most of their wages home. About three million young men served in it before it ended in 1942.
- **Federal Emergency Relief Administration (FERA):** gave federal money to the states for relief, such as food, clothing and soup kitchens, and for work projects.
- **Agricultural Adjustment Act (AAA):** paid farmers to produce less, so that prices would rise. Crops were ploughed in and animals slaughtered, which angered many people while others were hungry. Farm incomes did rise, but many sharecroppers and tenants, a large number of them Black Americans, lost their land when owners took land out of production.
- **Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA):** built dams on the Tennessee River and its tributaries to control floods, produce cheap electricity and improve farming in one of the poorest regions of the USA, which stretched across seven states.
- **National Industrial Recovery Act (NIRA):** set up the **National Recovery Administration (NRA)**, which encouraged employers to agree codes setting fair prices, minimum wages and maximum hours, and allowed workers to join unions. Businesses that joined displayed a blue eagle symbol. The act also set up the **Public Works Administration (PWA)**, which built roads, bridges, schools, hospitals and dams.

Other measures regulated the stock market and banks; the Banking Act of 1933 set up federal insurance of bank deposits. Prohibition was ended in 1933.

In the winter of 1933-34 the **Civil Works Administration (CWA)** gave temporary jobs to about four million people.

:::example{title="A policy explained step by step: how the TVA worked"}
1. **The problem:** the Tennessee Valley suffered from floods, soil erosion and deep poverty; few farms had electricity.
2. **The plan:** a single federal agency built a series of dams across the region.
3. **The effects:** the dams controlled floods, created jobs and produced cheap hydroelectric power; farmers were taught to protect the soil.
4. **Significance:** the TVA showed the government planning the development of a whole region, which supporters praised as modernisation and critics attacked as unfair competition with private power companies.
:::

## The Second New Deal, 1935

By 1935 the economy had improved but unemployment was still very high, and Roosevelt faced criticism both from conservatives and from radicals who wanted more. In May 1935 the **Supreme Court** declared the NIRA unconstitutional. Roosevelt responded with a new wave of laws, often called the **Second New Deal**, which put more emphasis on reform and on the welfare of ordinary people:

- **Works Progress Administration (WPA):** the largest work-creation agency, employing millions of people on projects such as roads, schools, airports and parks, and also employing artists, writers and musicians.
- **Wagner Act (National Labor Relations Act):** gave workers the right to join trade unions and bargain collectively, and stopped employers from punishing union members.
- **Social Security Act:** set up old-age pensions, unemployment insurance and help for disabled people and for children in poor families. It became the foundation of the American welfare system.
- Higher taxes on the rich (the Revenue Act of 1935).

In the 1936 election Roosevelt won every state except two, showing the popularity of the New Deal. Later measures included the **Fair Labor Standards Act** (1938), which set a national minimum wage and maximum working hours.

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: how did the New Deal change after 1933?"}
**The First New Deal (1933-34)** concentrated on relief and recovery: saving the banks, raising farm prices and helping business through the NRA, in cooperation with business leaders.
**The Second New Deal (1935 onwards)** concentrated more on reform and social justice: welfare (Social Security), workers' rights (the Wagner Act) and taxing the rich.
**Weighing it:** some historians see a sharp change of direction in 1935; others see continuity, with Roosevelt experimenting all the time and responding to events. A good answer uses specific laws to support either view.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the PWA and the WPA"}
The PWA (1933) funded large construction projects through private contractors. The WPA (1935) employed millions of people directly on many kinds of projects, including the arts.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the AAA helped all farmers"}
It raised prices and helped landowners, but many sharecroppers and tenants lost their land and work.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing agencies without explaining them"}
Naming agencies earns few marks. Explain what each did and which problem it was meant to solve: relief, recovery or reform.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (a): 'Describe the work of the alphabet agencies'"}
Choose specific agencies and give precise details: who they helped and what they did. For example, the CCC gave young men work in conservation and paid wages to their families.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 4: 'How important was the TVA?' or 'Write an account of the Hundred Days'"}
An account should be in clear chronological order with accurate detail. For importance, compare the agency's effects with others' and consider different ways of judging importance: jobs created, long-term change, symbolism.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Roosevelt took office on 4 March 1933, closed and reopened the banks and restored confidence through fireside chats.
- The Hundred Days created the CCC, FERA, AAA, TVA and NIRA (NRA and PWA), aimed at relief, recovery and reform.
- Agencies gave work to millions, raised farm prices and built dams, roads and schools, though some groups were left out.
- After the Supreme Court struck down the NIRA in 1935, the Second New Deal brought the WPA, the Wagner Act and the Social Security Act.
- Roosevelt's landslide re-election in 1936 showed the New Deal's popularity.
:::
