## Introduction

On 1 October 1923 Southern Rhodesia became a self-governing colony. For the next thirty years a parliament elected almost entirely by white settlers made the laws. Some of those laws, above all the **Land Apportionment Act of 1930**, divided the country by race in ways that shaped Zimbabwe's land question right up to the land reform programme after 2000.

You should already know how Company rule ended and why settlers voted for Responsible Government in 1922. Examiners ask you to **describe Responsible Government**, **explain its policies** and **assess their impact on the indigenous people**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Responsible Government"}
A system in which a colony is governed by ministers responsible to an elected parliament in the colony, while Britain keeps some reserved powers. In Southern Rhodesia the parliament was elected almost entirely by white settlers.
:::

:::definition{term="Reserved powers"}
Powers kept by the British government after 1923, including the right to refuse laws that discriminated against Africans (except in certain areas). Britain never used this veto.
:::

:::definition{term="Segregation"}
The separation of people by race in land, housing, jobs, schools, hospitals and public places.
:::

:::definition{term="Destocking"}
Compulsory reduction of the number of cattle kept by African farmers in the reserves.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Laws and dates to learn"}
- 1 October 1923: Responsible Government begins; Charles Coghlan is the first Premier.
- 1930: Land Apportionment Act.
- 1931: Maize Control Act.

- 1933-1953: Godfrey Huggins is Prime Minister.
:::

## Why Responsible Government was granted

- **Settler demands:** settlers wanted to govern themselves rather than be ruled by a profit-making company.
- **The Company's wish to leave:** governing was costly and brought it no profit.
- **British policy:** Britain did not want the cost of ruling the colony directly and accepted settler self-rule.
- **The 1922 referendum:** settlers rejected union with South Africa and chose Responsible Government.

## Features of Responsible Government

- A **Governor** represented the British Crown.
- An elected **Legislative Assembly** made laws; a **Premier** (later Prime Minister) and cabinet ran the government.
- The vote was based on income, property and education qualifications. In theory these were non-racial; in practice almost all voters were white and only a handful of Africans qualified.
- Britain kept **reserved powers** over laws discriminating against Africans and over some constitutional matters, but never used them.

## Policies and their impact

### Land: the Land Apportionment Act (1930)

The Act divided the country's land by race. Roughly half, including most of the best-watered land and all the towns, was reserved for Europeans, who were only a small minority of the population. Africans were allocated the **Native Reserves** and smaller **Native Purchase Areas** where a few could buy farms; the rest was left unassigned or kept as forest.

**Impact:** Africans living on land now classed as European were moved, often to crowded reserves. They could not own land in towns, so urban Africans were treated as temporary workers. Crowding caused overgrazing and soil erosion in the reserves, and the Act became the foundation of colonial land inequality.

### Agriculture: the Maize Control Act (1931)

The Act set up a board to control maize marketing. Its pricing system gave a better price for maize sold in the local market, which was dominated by white farmers, and a lower price for maize sold through the pool for export, where most African maize ended up.

**Impact:** African farmers who had sold maize successfully lost income and were discouraged from commercial farming, protecting white farmers from competition during the Depression.

:::example{title="A law analysed step by step: the Maize Control Act"}
1. **Context:** in the Depression of the early 1930s maize prices fell and white farmers faced ruin.
2. **What the law did:** it controlled maize sales and paid different prices in different markets.
3. **Who gained:** white farmers, who supplied most of the better-paid local market.
4. **Who lost:** African producers, who received lower prices and often sold through traders.
5. **Judgement:** the Act was presented as an economic measure, but its effect was to protect one racial group at the expense of another.
:::

### Labour: the Industrial Conciliation Act (1934)

The Act set up procedures for trade unions and employers to negotiate wages and conditions, but it **excluded African workers from the definition of "employee"**. Africans could not join registered unions or take part in industrial agreements, and apprenticeships for skilled trades were effectively reserved for whites.

**Impact:** African workers were kept in low-paid unskilled jobs, white skilled workers were protected from competition, and Africans had no legal way to bargain collectively.

### Conservation and control: the Native Land Husbandry Act (1951)

The Act aimed to stop soil erosion in the reserves. It enforced **destocking**, required contour ridges and other conservation works, and gave individual farming and grazing rights in place of the traditional allocation of land by chiefs.

**Impact:** many families lost cattle, the main form of wealth. Men working in towns, and young men, could lose their right to land at home. Chiefs lost authority over land. The Act caused anger across the reserves and gave nationalists in the late 1950s a cause that united rural and urban Africans.

### Social policies

- **Segregation** in towns: Africans lived in separate townships and needed passes to move and seek work.
- **Education:** mostly left to missionaries; government spent far more per white pupil than per African pupil, and the first government secondary school for Africans opened only in the 1940s.
- **Health:** separate and unequal hospitals and services.
- Huggins described a "two-pyramid" policy of separate development, in which Africans would advance only within their own areas.

## Early African associations and protest

Before mass nationalism, African protest was mostly peaceful and led by small educated groups or workers:

- The **Rhodesia Bantu Voters Association** (1923) worked for the interests of the few African voters.
- The **Industrial and Commercial Workers' Union (ICU)** organised workers in the late 1920s and 1930s.
- The **Southern Rhodesia Bantu Congress** (1934) petitioned against discriminatory laws; it later became the African National Congress.
- The **1945 railway workers' strike** and the **1948 general strike** showed the growing power of urban workers.
- **Benjamin Burombo** organised protest in Bulawayo and the reserves in the late 1940s, including against destocking.

:::example{title="Weighing impact: who benefited from Responsible Government?"}
Supporters at the time claimed the period brought economic growth: railways, mines, settler farming and, during and after the Second World War, new industry. A balanced answer accepts that the economy grew, then asks who gained. Most of the benefits went to the white minority, while laws on land, maize, labour and cattle limited African opportunities. Your judgement should rest on evidence about both groups.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling 1923 independence"}
Responsible Government was self-government within the British Empire. Britain kept reserved powers and the colony was not independent.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the Land Apportionment Act with the Land Tenure Act"}
The Land Apportionment Act was passed in 1930 under Responsible Government; the Land Tenure Act of 1969 replaced it during UDI.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing laws without impact"}
"Explain its policies and assess their impact" needs each law linked to its effect on Africans, not just its name and date.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 structure"}
A **state** part may ask for five laws or five features: give the name and date only. An **explain** part needs each policy, what it did and its effect. A **how far / to what extent** part (for example, "To what extent did the Land Apportionment Act harm Africans?") needs both sides and a judgement.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Use the dates you are sure of"}
Give the dates of the four main acts (1930, 1931, 1934, 1951) accurately. If you are unsure of another detail, describe it in general terms rather than guessing.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Responsible Government (from 1 October 1923) meant settler self-rule through an elected parliament, with British reserved powers that were never used.
- The Land Apportionment Act (1930) divided land by race and gave Europeans about half, including the best land and the towns.
- The Maize Control Act (1931) and Industrial Conciliation Act (1934) protected white farmers and workers at African expense.
- The Native Land Husbandry Act (1951) enforced destocking and individual land rights, causing widespread anger.
- Africans protested through associations, unions and strikes (1945, 1948), preparing the way for mass nationalism.
:::
