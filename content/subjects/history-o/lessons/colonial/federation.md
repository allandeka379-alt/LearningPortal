## Introduction

For ten years the countries we now call Zimbabwe, Zambia and Malawi were joined in the **Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland**, often called the Central African Federation. It built the Kariba Dam, a university and a federal capital in Salisbury, and its supporters promised "partnership" between the races. Yet Africans in all three territories opposed it from the start, and by 1963 it had collapsed.

You should already know about Responsible Government in Southern Rhodesia and the rise of mass nationalism in Africa after 1945. Examiners ask you to **outline the reasons for federation**, **trace the steps** to its formation, **describe its policies**, **explain its successes and failures** and **explain why it collapsed**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Federation"}
A union of states or territories under a central (federal) government that handles shared matters such as defence and the economy, while each territory keeps its own government for local matters.
:::

:::definition{term="Partnership"}
The Federation's official racial policy: Europeans and Africans were said to advance together, but in practice Europeans kept political and economic control.
:::

:::definition{term="Secession"}
The withdrawal of a territory from a federation or union.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Key facts"}
- Territories: Southern Rhodesia, Northern Rhodesia and Nyasaland.
- April 1953: Southern Rhodesian (mainly white) voters approve federation in a referendum.
- 1953: the Federation is established, with Salisbury as federal capital.
- Federal Prime Ministers: Godfrey Huggins (1953-1956), Roy Welensky (1956-1963).
- 1959: states of emergency in Southern Rhodesia (February) and Nyasaland (March).
- 1960: Monckton Commission reports.
- 31 December 1963: the Federation is dissolved.
:::

## Reasons for federation

### Economic reasons

- **Combining strengths:** Northern Rhodesia had rich copper mines; Southern Rhodesia had industry, farming and the largest settler population; Nyasaland could supply labour.
- **A larger market** would attract investment and allow big projects such as a hydro-electric dam.
- Copper revenue could help pay for development, much of it in Southern Rhodesia.

### Political reasons

- **Settler control:** settlers in the Rhodesias wanted to join together to strengthen white rule and move towards dominion status, free of British Colonial Office interference in the northern territories.
- **Fear of Afrikaner nationalism:** after the National Party won power in South Africa in 1948, British settlers and the British government wanted a strong British-led bloc in central Africa.
- **Fear of African nationalism:** a larger settler-led state might hold back the growing demand for majority rule.

### British motives

Britain hoped federation would make the region economically stronger, reduce the costs of colonial rule, keep central Africa out of South Africa's orbit and, it claimed, build a multiracial "partnership".

## Steps to federation

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1949 | Settler leaders meet at Victoria Falls to discuss closer union |
| 1951 | Officials' conference in London and second Victoria Falls conference |
| 1952-1953 | London conferences draft the federal constitution |
| April 1953 | Referendum in Southern Rhodesia approves federation |
| 1953 | Federation established; Huggins becomes Federal Prime Minister |
| 1955-1959 | Kariba Dam built on the Zambezi |
| February-March 1959 | States of emergency in Southern Rhodesia and Nyasaland |
| 1960 | Monckton Commission reports |
| 1962-1963 | Britain accepts the right of Nyasaland and Northern Rhodesia to secede |
| 31 December 1963 | Federation dissolved |

Africans in Nyasaland and Northern Rhodesia, and African leaders in Southern Rhodesia, protested strongly. They feared that Southern Rhodesia's racial laws would spread north and that federation would end any hope of majority rule. Their petitions were ignored, and only a tiny number of Africans could vote in the referendum.

## Policies and developments

- **Government:** a federal parliament in Salisbury controlled defence, the economy, railways, European education and health; the territorial governments kept African affairs, land and African education. Africans had only a handful of federal seats.
- **Partnership:** some petty segregation was relaxed (for example in some hotels and post offices), but land laws, pass laws and job colour bars remained, especially in Southern Rhodesia.
- **Kariba Dam:** built across the Zambezi between 1955 and 1959 to supply electricity, mainly to the Copperbelt and Southern Rhodesian industry. About 57,000 Tonga people (a widely cited estimate) were moved from the valley to make way for the lake, many of them against their will.
- **Education:** the University College of Rhodesia and Nyasaland in Salisbury, open to students of all races, opened in the 1950s.
- **Economic growth:** industry and towns grew, especially in Southern Rhodesia, and foreign investment came in during the 1950s.

:::example{title="Weighing success and failure: who gained from federal growth?"}
1. **Evidence of success:** the federal economy grew in the 1950s, Kariba was built and investment flowed in.
2. **Where the gains went:** most new industry, the federal capital and the university were in Southern Rhodesia, while much of the revenue came from Northern Rhodesian copper.
3. **Who gained:** mainly Europeans, especially in Southern Rhodesia; Africans gained few political rights and continued to face colour bars.
4. **Judgement:** the Federation was an economic success for some but a political failure, because it never won African consent. This is why it could not survive once African nationalism grew.
:::

## Successes and failures

| Successes | Failures |
|---|---|
| Economic growth and investment in the 1950s | Africans opposed it from the start and never consented |
| Kariba Dam and hydro-electric power | Partnership was largely talk; colour bars remained |
| Multiracial university college | Benefits concentrated in Southern Rhodesia |
| Improved railways, roads and industry | Displacement of the Tonga for Kariba |

## Reasons for the collapse

1. **African nationalism:** the Nyasaland African Congress, led from 1958 by Dr Hastings Kamuzu Banda, and nationalist movements in Northern Rhodesia (later led by Kenneth Kaunda) demanded secession and majority rule.
2. **The 1959 emergencies:** in February 1959 Southern Rhodesia banned the African National Congress and detained hundreds; in March Nyasaland declared an emergency, arrested Banda and many others, and dozens of Africans were killed. A British inquiry (the Devlin Report) criticised the Nyasaland government severely.
3. **The Monckton Commission (1960):** it found strong African opposition to federation and recommended that territories should be able to leave.
4. **British policy changed:** after Harold Macmillan's "wind of change" speech (February 1960), Britain moved towards decolonisation and accepted that it could not hold Nyasaland and Northern Rhodesia in the Federation by force.
5. **Constitutional change in the north:** new constitutions gave Africans majorities in Nyasaland and then Northern Rhodesia, and their governments demanded secession.
6. **Southern Rhodesian politics:** the Rhodesian Front, which rejected partnership, won the Southern Rhodesian election of December 1962.

The Federation was dissolved on **31 December 1963**. Nyasaland became independent as **Malawi** (July 1964) and Northern Rhodesia as **Zambia** (October 1964). Southern Rhodesia kept most of the federal armed forces, which strengthened the settler government before UDI.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking all three territories wanted federation"}
Federation was wanted mainly by settlers in the Rhodesias and by Britain. Africans, especially in Nyasaland and Northern Rhodesia, opposed it.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the dates"}
The Federation lasted from 1953 to 1963. UDI (1965) came after it collapsed. Do not confuse the 1959 emergency with the 1965 emergency declared before UDI.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing Kariba only as a success"}
Kariba supplied power, but it displaced tens of thousands of Tonga people. Balanced answers show both.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Explain the collapse with linked factors"}
For "Explain the factors that led to its collapse", show how African nationalism, the 1959 emergencies, the Monckton Commission and British policy were connected: nationalist pressure led to the emergencies, the emergencies led to inquiries, and the inquiries changed British policy.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Successes and failures: be specific"}
Support each success or failure with a fact: Kariba, the university college, Copperbelt revenue, colour bars, the Tonga removals, African seats in parliament.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Federation (1953-1963) joined Southern Rhodesia, Northern Rhodesia and Nyasaland under a federal government in Salisbury.
- It was formed for economic, political and strategic reasons, mainly in the interests of settlers and Britain, against African opposition.
- Its successes were economic (growth, Kariba, investment); its failures were political (partnership was hollow, Africans never consented).
- African nationalism, the 1959 emergencies, the Monckton Commission and Britain's turn to decolonisation brought collapse.
- It was dissolved on 31 December 1963; Malawi and Zambia became independent in 1964.
:::
