## Introduction

In 1847 the Austrian chancellor Metternich is said to have called Italy "a geographical expression". He meant that there was no Italian nation, only a collection of states. Within twenty-five years, a Kingdom of Italy with its capital in Rome had proved him wrong. This lesson covers the first, failed attempt at unity in 1848-49 and the ideas behind it.

The story has echoes in African history. Nationalists across the continent had to decide whether a new nation should be built by a mass popular movement or by an existing state and its army, and whether to trust foreign allies. The same arguments divided Italian nationalists in the 1840s.

This is Cambridge 0470 Option A, key question 2: *How was Italy unified?* You should already know why revolutions broke out across Europe in 1848.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Risorgimento"}
"Resurgence" or "revival": the name given to the movement for Italian national revival and unification in the nineteenth century.
:::

:::definition{term="Young Italy"}
The secret society founded by Giuseppe Mazzini in 1831 to create a united, independent Italian republic through popular risings.
:::

:::definition{term="Carbonari"}
Secret revolutionary societies, active in Italy after 1815, which organised risings for constitutions in the 1820s and 1830s.
:::

:::definition{term="Statuto"}
The constitution granted by King Charles Albert of Piedmont in March 1848. It was the only Italian constitution of 1848 still in force after 1849.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **1815:** Congress of Vienna restores the old rulers of Italy.
- **1831:** Mazzini founds Young Italy.
- **1846:** Pius IX becomes pope.
- **March 1848:** the Five Days of Milan; Piedmont declares war on Austria.
- **July 1848:** Austria defeats Piedmont at Custoza.
- **23 March 1849:** Austria defeats Piedmont at Novara; Charles Albert abdicates.
- **1849:** French troops end the Roman Republic; Venice surrenders in August.
:::

## Italy after 1815

The Congress of Vienna divided Italy into several states:

| State | Ruler or influence in 1815 |
|---|---|
| Kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia | House of Savoy (the only native Italian dynasty), capital Turin |
| Lombardy and Venetia | Ruled directly by Austria |
| Parma, Modena and Tuscany | Dukes related to or allied with the Austrian royal family |
| Papal States | Ruled by the pope from Rome |
| Kingdom of the Two Sicilies (Naples and Sicily) | Bourbon kings |

**Austria** dominated the peninsula. It ruled Lombardy and Venetia, the richest region of the north; its relatives ruled the central duchies; and its armies were ready to restore order. When risings for constitutions broke out in Naples and Piedmont in 1821, and in the Papal States, Modena and Parma in 1831, Austrian troops crushed them.

Other obstacles to unity were just as real. Most Italians spoke regional dialects, communications were poor, and northern and southern economies were very different. Most peasants were illiterate and loyal to their local area and the Church.

## Nationalist ideas before 1848

**Giuseppe Mazzini**, from Genoa, founded **Young Italy** in exile in 1831. He believed that Italians formed a nation with a mission, that Italy should become a single independent **republic**, and that it should be achieved by the people themselves through education and armed risings. The risings Young Italy organised in the 1830s and 1840s all failed, and many members were executed or imprisoned, but Mazzini's writings inspired a generation of young nationalists, including Giuseppe Garibaldi.

Moderates offered other plans. The priest Vincenzo Gioberti argued in 1843 for a federation of Italian states under the pope. Others looked to **Piedmont**, the strongest independent Italian state, to drive out Austria. The election of **Pope Pius IX** in 1846, who began with reforms, seemed to make a papal-led Italy possible.

## The revolutions of 1848-49

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| January 1848 | Rising in Palermo; the King of Naples grants a constitution |
| March 1848 | Piedmont's Statuto granted; Milan's Five Days drive out Radetzky; Venice declares a republic |
| March 1848 | Charles Albert of Piedmont declares war on Austria |
| April 1848 | Pius IX refuses to support war against Austria |
| July 1848 | Radetzky defeats Charles Albert at Custoza |
| February 1849 | The Roman Republic is proclaimed |
| 23 March 1849 | Piedmont defeated at Novara; Charles Albert abdicates |
| 1849 | French troops take Rome; Venice surrenders in August |

The year began with a rising in **Palermo**, and rulers in Naples, Tuscany, Piedmont and the Papal States granted constitutions. When news arrived of the rising in Vienna, the people of **Milan** rose. In the **Five Days** (18-22 March 1848) they forced the Austrian commander, Field Marshal **Radetzky**, to retreat to the fortresses of the Quadrilateral. **Venice** declared the Republic of St Mark under Daniele Manin.

King **Charles Albert** of Piedmont declared war on Austria. Troops came from other states, but support soon fell away. In April **Pius IX** announced that he could not make war on Catholic Austria, and the King of Naples recalled his troops. Charles Albert wanted to enlarge Piedmont more than to cooperate with the republicans of Milan and Venice. Radetzky, reinforced, defeated him at **Custoza** in July 1848, and Piedmont made an armistice.

In Rome, after the pope's minister was murdered in November 1848, Pius IX fled. A **Roman Republic** was proclaimed in February 1849, with Mazzini as one of its leaders and Garibaldi commanding its defence. In March 1849 Charles Albert renewed the war but was crushed at **Novara** (23 March). He abdicated in favour of his son, **Victor Emmanuel II**.

The end came quickly. Louis Napoleon, President of France, sent an army to restore the pope, partly to win Catholic support at home; Rome fell in summer 1849 and Garibaldi led a retreat. Austria restored the dukes, the King of Naples reconquered Sicily, and Venice, besieged and stricken with cholera, surrendered in August 1849.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why Piedmont lost the war of 1848-49"}
1. **Austria's army was intact:** Radetzky withdrew to strong fortresses and waited for reinforcements.
2. **Allies withdrew:** the pope and the King of Naples pulled out, so Piedmont fought largely alone.
3. **Nationalists were divided:** Charles Albert distrusted the republicans of Milan and Venice, and they distrusted him.
4. **Result:** Piedmont was defeated at Custoza (1848) and Novara (1849).
:::

## Why Italy was not unified in 1848-49

- **Austrian military strength.** Radetzky's army defeated Piedmont twice.
- **Divisions among nationalists.** Republicans (Mazzini) and monarchists (Charles Albert) wanted different Italies; regional rivalries remained strong.
- **The pope's refusal** in April 1848 to fight Austria ended hopes of a papal-led Italy and cost the movement Catholic support.
- **Lack of mass support.** Peasants gained little and most did not join the risings.
- **Foreign intervention.** French troops destroyed the Roman Republic.
- **Rulers withdrew their constitutions**, except in Piedmont.

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: was 1848-49 a complete failure for Italian nationalism?"}
**Yes:** every revolution was crushed, Austria was back in control, and the old rulers returned.
**No:** Piedmont kept the Statuto and became the hope of moderate nationalists; the defence of Rome made Garibaldi a hero; and nationalists learned that Italy could not "do it alone" against Austria, but would need a strong state and a foreign ally.
**Judgement:** the attempt failed, but it pointed the way to the successful methods of the 1850s.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Mazzini wanted a kingdom under Piedmont"}
Mazzini wanted a united republic created by the people. It was Cavour and the moderates who later united Italy as a kingdom under Piedmont's king.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing Custoza and Novara"}
Custoza was the defeat of July 1848; Novara was the decisive defeat of 23 March 1849, after which Charles Albert abdicated.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting France"}
France, not Austria, ended the Roman Republic in 1849. Louis Napoleon sent the troops.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- After 1815 Italy was divided into several states, and Austria ruled Lombardy and Venetia and dominated the rest.
- Mazzini's Young Italy (1831) called for a united republic made by the people; moderates looked to the pope or to Piedmont.
- In 1848 Milan and Venice rose and Piedmont declared war, but Radetzky won at Custoza and Novara.
- The Roman Republic of 1849 was ended by French troops, and Venice surrendered in August 1849.
- Italy was not unified because of Austrian strength, divided nationalists, the pope's refusal, little mass support and foreign intervention; only Piedmont kept its constitution.
:::
