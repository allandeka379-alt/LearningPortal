## Introduction

In May 1860 about a thousand volunteers in red shirts sailed from near Genoa to Sicily. Within five months their leader, Giuseppe **Garibaldi**, had conquered the largest state in Italy, the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, and then handed it to King Victor Emmanuel II. It was one of the most remarkable military campaigns of the century, and it turned Cavour's northern kingdom into a Kingdom of Italy.

Garibaldi is a good example of how one person can change events, but also of how individuals depend on circumstances. Liberation leaders in Africa, such as those who led guerrilla armies, often became national heroes in a similar way, while arguments continued about how much they achieved alone.

This is Cambridge 0470 Option A, key question 2. You should already know about Mazzini, the failure of 1848-49 and Cavour's war of 1859.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="The Thousand (I Mille)"}
The force of about a thousand volunteers, many from northern Italy, who sailed with Garibaldi to Sicily in May 1860. They wore red shirts.
:::

:::definition{term="Kingdom of the Two Sicilies"}
The southern kingdom of Naples and Sicily, ruled by the Bourbon dynasty. In 1860 its king was Francis II.
:::

:::definition{term="Roman Question"}
The problem, after 1861, of how Italy could make Rome its capital while the pope ruled it with the protection of French troops.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **May 1860:** the Thousand land at Marsala, Sicily.
- **7 September 1860:** Garibaldi enters Naples.
- **26 October 1860:** Garibaldi meets Victor Emmanuel II near Teano.
- **17 March 1861:** the Kingdom of Italy is proclaimed.
- **1866:** Italy gains Venetia.
- **20 September 1870:** Italian troops enter Rome.
:::

## Garibaldi before 1860

Garibaldi was born in **Nice** in 1807. He joined Mazzini's Young Italy and, after a failed rising, fled to South America, where he spent more than ten years fighting in local wars and learned guerrilla warfare. His Italian Legion in Uruguay first wore the famous red shirts. He returned in 1848, became a hero by defending the Roman Republic in 1849, and in 1859 commanded volunteers in the war against Austria. By 1860 he had accepted that unity would come under Victor Emmanuel II, but he remained a democrat who distrusted Cavour, especially after his birthplace, Nice, was given to France.

## The expedition of the Thousand, 1860

In April 1860 a rising broke out in **Sicily** against the Bourbon king. Garibaldi decided to support it. Cavour did not stop the expedition, but he did not openly support it either: he feared both a failure and a success that might lead Garibaldi to attack Rome and bring French troops against Italy.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| April 1860 | Rising in Sicily against Bourbon rule |
| 5-6 May 1860 | The Thousand sail from Quarto, near Genoa |
| 11 May 1860 | Landing at Marsala in western Sicily |
| 15 May 1860 | Victory at Calatafimi |
| Late May-June 1860 | Palermo taken |
| August 1860 | Garibaldi crosses to the mainland |
| 7 September 1860 | Garibaldi enters Naples |
| September 1860 | Piedmontese army invades the Papal States (Marches and Umbria) |
| October 1860 | Battle of the Volturno; plebiscites in the south; meeting at Teano |

The Thousand landed at **Marsala** on 11 May and won a hard fight at **Calatafimi**. Sicilian peasants and townspeople joined them, and they took **Palermo**. Garibaldi governed Sicily as dictator in the name of Victor Emmanuel. In August he crossed to the mainland, and the Bourbon army, poorly led and demoralised, melted away. Garibaldi entered **Naples** on 7 September 1860, after King Francis II had left.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why Garibaldi's expedition succeeded"}
1. **Local support:** Sicilians had already risen and many joined the volunteers, who were seen as liberators.
2. **Weak enemy:** the Bourbon army was large but poorly led, and many of its officers were unwilling to fight.
3. **Leadership:** Garibaldi's guerrilla experience and personal courage inspired his men and impressed the population.
4. **No foreign intervention:** Britain was friendly, and Cavour did not stop the expedition.
5. **Result:** a small force conquered a kingdom of millions in about four months.
:::

## Cavour, Garibaldi and Teano

Cavour now feared that Garibaldi would march on **Rome**, which was defended by French troops. That could bring war with France and Austria and could leave the democrats, not the king, in charge of the south. Cavour persuaded Napoleon III to accept a Piedmontese advance, and in September the Piedmontese army invaded the **Papal States**, defeating the papal army at Castelfidardo and occupying the Marches and Umbria while leaving Rome alone. It then entered the south.

Garibaldi defeated the Bourbon army on the **Volturno** in early October. Plebiscites in Naples and Sicily voted overwhelmingly to join Victor Emmanuel's kingdom. On **26 October 1860** Garibaldi met the king near **Teano** and hailed him as King of Italy. He handed over his conquests, refused rewards and retired to his island home of Caprera.

The **Kingdom of Italy** was proclaimed on **17 March 1861**. Cavour died in June 1861.

## Completing unification: Venetia and Rome

Two areas remained outside the kingdom.

**Venetia (1866).** Italy allied with Prussia in April 1866. In the war that followed, Italy was defeated by Austria on land at Custoza and at sea at Lissa, but Prussia crushed Austria at Königgrätz (Sadowa). Austria handed Venetia to Napoleon III, who passed it to Italy, and a plebiscite confirmed the transfer.

**Rome (1870).** Garibaldi tried twice to seize Rome by force: in 1862 he was stopped and wounded by Italian troops at Aspromonte, and in 1867 he was defeated by French and papal troops at Mentana. Only when the **Franco-Prussian War** forced France to withdraw its garrison in 1870 could Italy act. Italian troops entered Rome on **20 September 1870**, and it became the capital in 1871. The pope refused to accept the loss of his lands.

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: how important was Garibaldi?"}
**Very important:** he conquered the south, which no one else was willing or able to do, and by handing it to Victor Emmanuel he turned a northern kingdom into Italy. His fame won support for the national cause in Italy and abroad.
**Less important:** he depended on the Sicilian rising, a weak Bourbon army and Cavour's tolerance. Cavour's diplomacy and the Piedmontese army secured the Papal States and the plebiscites, and Venetia and Rome were won because of Prussian victories, not Garibaldi's campaigns, which failed in 1862 and 1867.
**Judgement:** Garibaldi was essential in 1860, but the completion of unification depended on Piedmont's state and on the wars of other powers.
:::

## The role of other European countries

- **France:** fought Austria in 1859, accepted Piedmont's gains in 1860 for Nice and Savoy, but protected the pope in Rome until 1870.
- **Austria:** its defeats in 1859 and 1866 cost it Lombardy and Venetia.
- **Britain:** gave diplomatic support to the Italians' right to choose their own government and did not interfere with Garibaldi's landing.
- **Prussia:** its victories over Austria (1866) and France (1870) allowed Italy to gain Venetia and Rome.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Garibaldi wanted to become king or president"}
Garibaldi handed his conquests to Victor Emmanuel II at Teano in 1860 and refused rewards.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Italy won Venetia by defeating Austria"}
Italy lost the battles of Custoza and Lissa in 1866. It gained Venetia because Prussia defeated Austria.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Italy was complete in 1861"}
The Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in 1861, but Venetia joined in 1866 and Rome in 1870.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (c): 'How important was Garibaldi's contribution to unification?'"}
Show what Garibaldi achieved, then weigh it against Cavour, Victor Emmanuel, Napoleon III and Prussia. A judgement such as "essential in 1860 but less important for 1866-70" earns credit when it is supported.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 2 (Italy is prescribed in 2028)"}
Sources on Garibaldi and Cavour are often written by admirers or enemies of one of them. Ask what each writer wanted readers to think about who deserved the credit.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- In May 1860 Garibaldi and the Thousand landed in Sicily, and by 7 September he had taken Naples.
- Cavour, fearing an attack on Rome, sent the Piedmontese army through the Papal States, avoiding Rome itself.
- At Teano (26 October 1860) Garibaldi handed the south to Victor Emmanuel II, and the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed on 17 March 1861.
- Venetia was gained in 1866 through alliance with Prussia, and Rome in 1870 when French troops left during the Franco-Prussian War.
- Garibaldi's role was decisive in 1860, but France, Britain and especially Prussia shaped the completion of unification.
:::
