## Introduction

On 12 September 1890 a small force of British South Africa Company pioneers and police raised the British flag on a hill they called Fort Salisbury, the beginning of present-day Harare. Less than two years earlier, King Lobengula had put his mark on a document he believed gave a few white men the right to dig for gold. Rhodes's company turned that document into a royal charter and an occupation. How this happened is one of the most important stories in Zimbabwe's history.

This lesson explains why Zimbabwe was colonised, the treaties and concessions of 1887–1888, Lobengula's attempts to resist by diplomacy, the Royal Charter and the occupation of 1890. You should know about the Ndebele state, the agents of colonisation and the Scramble for southern Africa. The Anglo-Ndebele War of 1893–94 is covered in the next topic.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Treaty"}
A formal agreement between rulers or states.
:::

:::definition{term="Concession"}
A grant of rights, for example to mine, given by a ruler to an individual or company.
:::

:::definition{term="Royal Charter"}
A document issued by the British Crown giving a company the right to govern and develop a territory on Britain's behalf.
:::

:::definition{term="British South Africa Company (BSAC)"}
The company formed by Cecil Rhodes and his partners, which received the Royal Charter in 1889 and ruled Southern Rhodesia until 1923.
:::

:::definition{term="Pioneer Column"}
The armed expedition of pioneers and Company police that occupied Mashonaland in 1890.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
Grobler Treaty 1887; Moffat Treaty February 1888; Rudd Concession 30 October 1888; Royal Charter 29 October 1889; Pioneer Column at Fort Salisbury 12 September 1890.
:::

## The scramble for Zimbabwe

Four powers were interested in the land between the Limpopo and the Zambezi:

- **Britain**, through the Cape Colony and above all Cecil Rhodes;
- **the South African Republic (Transvaal)**, whose Boers wanted land and to expand north;
- **Portugal**, which claimed the land as part of its plan to link Mozambique and Angola;
- **Germany**, whose colonies in South West Africa and East Africa made Britain fear a German link across the continent.

## Why Zimbabwe was colonised

1. **The "second Rand" myth**: after gold was found on the Witwatersrand in 1886, many believed even richer goldfields lay north of the Limpopo, an idea fed by earlier reports of ancient gold workings.
2. **Rhodes's ambitions**: Rhodes dreamed of British territory from the **Cape to Cairo** and of using northern gold to make the Cape the leading power in southern Africa.
3. **Land** for white settlers and farming.
4. **Strategic rivalry**: Britain wanted to stop the Transvaal, Portugal and Germany from taking the territory and blocking the road north.
5. **Missionary pressure** and the belief that British rule would protect missions and "civilise" Africans.
6. **Labour and markets**: settlers and companies hoped for African labour and new markets.

## Treaties and concessions

### The Grobler Treaty (1887)

Pieter Grobler, an agent of the South African Republic, obtained a treaty of friendship with Lobengula in July 1887. It renewed friendly relations and, according to the Transvaal, gave it rights such as a consul at Lobengula's court. Its terms and Lobengula's consent were disputed, but it alarmed the British, who feared a Boer takeover.

### The Moffat Treaty (February 1888)

The missionary **John Smith Moffat**, then working for the British government, persuaded Lobengula to sign a treaty in which he promised **not to make any treaty with another foreign power, or give away any of his land, without the approval of the British High Commissioner**. This placed the Ndebele state within the British **sphere of influence** and shut out the Boers, Portuguese and Germans.

### The Rudd Concession (30 October 1888)

Rhodes's agents **Charles Rudd, Rochfort Maguire and Francis Thompson** came to Lobengula's capital to obtain mining rights. After weeks of negotiations, and with the LMS missionary **Charles Helm** as interpreter, Lobengula put his mark on a concession.

| What Lobengula granted (written) | What he was to receive (written) |
|---|---|
| The sole right to all metals and minerals in his kingdom and the lands he ruled | £100 a month |
| The power to do all things necessary to win and process the minerals | 1,000 Martini-Henry rifles and 100,000 rounds of ammunition |
| The power to exclude other concession seekers | A steamboat on the Zambezi, or £500 instead |

Lobengula was also given **verbal promises**, reported by Helm: that no more than ten white men would come to dig in his country and that they would obey its laws. **None of these promises appeared in the written document.**

:::example{title="Analysing the Rudd Concession as a source"}
1. **Content:** it gave away mining rights over a huge area in return for money, rifles and a steamboat.
2. **Provenance:** it was written in English by Rhodes's agents; Lobengula could not read it and relied on Helm's interpretation.
3. **What it leaves out:** the verbal promises about ten men and Ndebele law.
4. **Use by Rhodes:** he used it to persuade the British government to grant his company a Royal Charter.
5. **Judgement:** as evidence of what Lobengula agreed to, the written text is unreliable, because the king's understanding depended on the verbal promises that were left out.
:::

### Lobengula's response

When Lobengula learned what the concession really meant, he tried to undo it by diplomacy:

- He sent two izinduna, **Babayane and Mshete**, to London in 1889 to see Queen Victoria and protest.
- He issued a public notice, published in a newspaper in the south in early 1889, saying he had suspended the concession pending an investigation.
- His induna **Lotshe**, who had advised him to sign, was put to death in 1889.
- He played rival concession seekers against each other, later granting a land concession to Eduard Lippert (1891).
- He avoided open war with the British, knowing the strength of their weapons.

## The Royal Charter (29 October 1889)

Rhodes combined his interests into the **British South Africa Company** and used the Rudd Concession to win a **Royal Charter** from the British government. The Charter allowed the Company to:

- make treaties and laws, and keep order;
- maintain its own police force;
- grant land and mining rights, build roads and railways, and trade;
- govern the territory north of British Bechuanaland and the Transvaal, west of the Portuguese territories.

For Britain, the Charter was empire **on the cheap**: a company, not the British taxpayer, would pay for occupation and government.

## The occupation of Mashonaland (1890)

To avoid a direct clash with the Ndebele regiments, the Company decided to occupy **Mashonaland**, which Lobengula claimed as tributary land, rather than Matabeleland.

- The **Pioneer Column** was organised by the contractor **Frank Johnson**, guided by the hunter **Frederick Selous** and commanded by Lieutenant-Colonel **Edward Pennefather**. It had about 200 pioneers and about 500 Company police, with African workers (numbers given in sources vary slightly).
- Each pioneer was promised a **farm of about 3,000 acres** and **15 gold claims**.
- The column left the Macloutsie area in Bechuanaland in June 1890, crossed into the territory near the Tuli River, and followed a route to the east of Matabeleland, building forts at **Tuli, Victoria and Charter** on the way.
- It reached the site of **Fort Salisbury** and raised the flag on **12 September 1890**.

:::example{title="Explaining why Lobengula did not attack the Pioneer Column"}
1. **Military reality:** he knew the Company had machine guns and modern rifles, and that war would bring British troops.
2. **Diplomacy:** he still hoped that protests to the Queen would undo the concession.
3. **The route:** the column avoided Matabeleland and entered Mashonaland, so it did not attack the heart of his kingdom.
4. **Internal pressure:** many young warriors wanted to fight, but the king held them back, believing war would destroy the nation. His caution delayed conflict until 1893.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the Moffat Treaty and the Rudd Concession"}
The Moffat Treaty (February 1888) put Lobengula in the British sphere of influence. The Rudd Concession (October 1888) gave mining rights to Rhodes's agents.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the Rudd Concession gave land"}
The written concession gave rights to metals and minerals, not land. The Company later used the Lippert Concession and conquest to claim land.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the Pioneer Column fought its way to Salisbury"}
The column avoided Matabeleland and was not attacked. The conquest of the Ndebele came in 1893.
:::
