## Introduction

In 1890 the Pioneer Column of the British South Africa Company (BSAC) occupied Mashonaland, but the Ndebele state under King Lobengula remained independent, with its capital at Bulawayo. Just three years later the Company invaded Matabeleland, and within a few months Bulawayo had been burnt and Lobengula had fled. The war of 1893-94 destroyed the Ndebele kingdom as an independent state and opened the whole of the country to settler rule.

This topic links what you know about the Ndebele state (its army, regiments and raiding) and the occupation of 1890 (the Rudd Concession and the Royal Charter) with the risings of 1896-97. Examiners ask you to **state the causes**, **describe the course** and **analyse the results** of the war.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="British South Africa Company (BSAC)"}
The company formed by Cecil Rhodes that received a Royal Charter in 1889 to occupy and govern territory north of the Limpopo. It ruled the country until 1923.
:::

:::definition{term="Victoria Agreement"}
The promises Leander Starr Jameson made to settler volunteers in August 1893: a farm, gold claims and a share of captured cattle for every man who fought against the Ndebele.
:::

:::definition{term="Native reserve"}
An area set aside by the colonial government for African occupation. After 1894 the Gwaai and Shangani reserves were created for the Ndebele.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- July 1893: the Victoria incident near Fort Victoria.
- October-November 1893: the battles of Shangani and Bembesi.
- 4 November 1893: Company forces enter the burnt royal town of Bulawayo.
- 4 December 1893: the Shangani Patrol under Major Allan Wilson is wiped out.
- Early 1894: Lobengula dies in the north of his kingdom.
:::

## Causes of the war

Historians agree that the Victoria incident was only the **trigger**. The deeper causes lay in the Company's position after 1890.

### The Company's economic problems

The Company had promised its shareholders a "second Rand", a goldfield as rich as the Witwatersrand. Mashonaland's gold deposits proved small and costly to work, so the Company's shares lost value and settlers grew discontented. Many settlers and Company officials believed that richer gold lay in Matabeleland. Conquering the Ndebele looked like a way to rescue the Company's finances.

### Settler demands for land, cattle and labour

Settlers wanted Matabeleland's grazing land and the large Ndebele herds of cattle. They also complained that Ndebele raids into Mashonaland frightened Shona workers away from farms and mines, so the labour supply was unreliable.

### Lobengula's authority over the Shona

The Ndebele state collected tribute from some Shona communities and sometimes raided those that refused. Lobengula regarded these people as under his authority; the Company regarded Mashonaland as its own territory. No agreed border separated the two, so conflict was likely.

### The Victoria incident (July 1893)

In July 1893 an Ndebele force entered the Fort Victoria district to punish Shona communities that had cut and taken telegraph wire and cattle. Settlers and their workers were frightened and work stopped. Jameson met the Ndebele commanders, ordered them to leave, and Company troopers under Captain Lendy then pursued and fired on the retreating Ndebele, killing a number of them. Jameson used the crisis to persuade the High Commissioner and Rhodes that war was necessary.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why the Victoria incident led to war"}
1. **What happened:** an Ndebele raid near Fort Victoria disrupted settler work and Company troopers fired on the Ndebele.
2. **Why it mattered to Jameson:** he could present the raid as a threat to settler lives and to the labour supply.
3. **Link to deeper aims:** war offered land, cattle and possible gold in Matabeleland, which the Company needed.
4. **Result:** Jameson signed the Victoria Agreement in August 1893 and prepared columns to invade. The incident was the excuse; the economic motives were the underlying cause.
:::

## The course of the war

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| July 1893 | Victoria incident near Fort Victoria |
| August 1893 | Victoria Agreement promises land, gold claims and cattle to volunteers |
| October 1893 | Columns from Fort Salisbury and Fort Victoria advance into Matabeleland |
| Late October 1893 | Battle of Shangani: Ndebele attacks on the laager beaten back by machine-gun fire |
| 1 November 1893 | Battle of Bembesi: heavy Ndebele losses |
| 4 November 1893 | Company forces enter Bulawayo, already burnt on Lobengula's orders |
| 4 December 1893 | The Shangani Patrol is wiped out near the Shangani River |
| Early 1894 | Death of Lobengula in the north |

Two columns, from Fort Salisbury and Fort Victoria, joined and marched towards Bulawayo, while a smaller force advanced from Bechuanaland in the south-west. The invaders were few compared with the Ndebele regiments, but they had **Maxim machine guns**, horses and wagons. At night they formed a *laager* (a ring of wagons).

At the **Shangani** and at **Bembesi** the Ndebele regiments made brave frontal attacks on the laagers. Machine-gun and rifle fire cut them down at long range, and the Ndebele lost very heavily. Lobengula ordered his capital burnt and fled north towards the Zambezi. Company troops entered the ruins of Bulawayo on 4 November 1893.

Jameson sent a patrol after the king. On 4 December 1893 a small party under Major Allan Wilson crossed the Shangani River ahead of the main force; the river rose behind them and Ndebele regiments surrounded and killed them all. Settler writers later made the **Shangani Patrol** a heroic legend, but it was a small episode in a war the Company had already won. Lobengula died early in 1894; the exact cause is not known for certain.

:::example{title="Analysing a cause of defeat: weapons or tactics?"}
Some accounts explain the Ndebele defeat almost entirely by the Maxim gun. A stronger answer weighs several factors. The machine gun did make frontal attacks on a laager extremely costly. But Ndebele commanders also had to fight in open country close to the invaders' defended positions, the regiments had little experience of fighting an enemy armed in this way, and the kingdom was divided over whether to fight at all. Lobengula had tried diplomacy until the last moment. The best answers show how technology, tactics and politics combined.
:::

## Results of the war

### For the Ndebele

- **Loss of independence:** the Ndebele state was destroyed; the king was dead and the royal town was a settler town.
- **Loss of land:** settlers pegged farms around Bulawayo under the Victoria Agreement. A Land Commission in 1894 created the **Gwaai and Shangani reserves**, which were far from markets, dry and affected by tsetse fly, and most Ndebele did not want to move there.
- **Loss of cattle:** the Company and settlers seized most of the royal and many private herds. Cattle were the basis of Ndebele wealth, marriage and status.
- **Forced labour and tax:** men were made to work for settlers and the Company, and taxes were introduced.
- **Humiliation:** some Ndebele were recruited into the Native Police and used against their own people, which caused deep resentment.

These grievances were major causes of the rising of 1896.

### For the Company and the settlers

- The Company gained control of Matabeleland and joined it to Mashonaland as one territory under its administration.
- Settlers gained land, cattle and mining claims; Bulawayo grew as a settler town.
- The expected rich goldfield was still not found, so the Company's financial problems were not solved.
- The British government accepted Company rule over Matabeleland (by an Order in Council in 1894), strengthening Rhodes's position in southern Africa.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Treating the Victoria incident as the whole cause"}
The raid near Fort Victoria was the trigger. Explain the underlying causes too: the Company's need for gold and money, settler demands for land and cattle, and the clash over authority in Mashonaland.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing 1893 with 1896"}
The Anglo-Ndebele War was 1893-94 and was started by the Company. The First Chimurenga/Umvukela began in March 1896 and was started by the Ndebele and Shona against Company rule.
:::

:::mistake{title="Narrating the Shangani Patrol at length"}
The Shangani Patrol is a detail of the course of the war. Do not spend half an answer on it; the battles of Shangani and Bembesi and the fall of Bulawayo decided the war.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="State, describe and explain on Paper 1"}
In a Paper 1 structured question, a **state** part needs short points (five causes or five results in five lines). A **describe** part needs the sequence of events with places and dates. An **explain** part needs a reason and a development: "The Company wanted war because Mashonaland's gold was disappointing, so its shares were falling and it hoped to find gold in Matabeleland."
:::

:::exam-tip{title="How far was the Victoria incident responsible?"}
For a "How far" question, explain the role of the incident first, then explain the other causes, and end with a judgement: for example, that the incident gave Jameson an excuse but the Company's economic needs explain why it chose war.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Victoria incident of July 1893 was the trigger; the Company's financial problems, hopes of gold, and settler demand for land, cattle and labour were the deeper causes.
- Jameson's Victoria Agreement promised volunteers farms, gold claims and a share of captured cattle.
- Maxim guns and laagers defeated the Ndebele at Shangani and Bembesi; Bulawayo fell on 4 November 1893 and Lobengula fled and died early in 1894.
- The Shangani Patrol was wiped out on 4 December 1893, a small episode later turned into a settler legend.
- The Ndebele lost their independence, land, cattle and freedom of labour; these grievances led to the rising of 1896.
:::
