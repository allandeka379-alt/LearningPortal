## Introduction

The First World War (28 July 1914 to 11 November 1918) was fought between the **Allies** (above all France, Britain and its empire, Russia, Italy from 1915 and the USA from 1917) and the **Central Powers** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire and Bulgaria). It was called the Great War because nothing on its scale had been seen before: millions of soldiers were killed, four empires collapsed, and the map of Europe, the Middle East and Africa was redrawn.

Africa was a battlefield too. British, French, Belgian and South African forces attacked every German colony, and hundreds of thousands of Africans served as soldiers and, above all, as carriers. Men from Southern Rhodesia, black and white, fought in German South West Africa and German East Africa.

You should already know the causes of the war (alliances, the arms race, imperial rivalry and the assassination at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Stalemate"}
A situation in which neither side can win. On the Western Front, trench warfare produced a stalemate from late 1914 until 1918.
:::

:::definition{term="War of attrition"}
A strategy of wearing the enemy down by steady losses of men and resources until it cannot continue.
:::

:::definition{term="Unrestricted submarine warfare"}
Sinking any ship, including neutral merchant ships, in a war zone without warning. Germany's use of it helped bring the USA into the war.
:::

:::definition{term="Armistice"}
An agreement to stop fighting while peace terms are worked out. The armistice with Germany took effect at 11 a.m. on 11 November 1918.
:::

:::definition{term="Carrier"}
An African porter who carried supplies, ammunition and the wounded for the armies, especially in East Africa where there were few roads or railways.
:::

### The Western Front

Germany's **Schlieffen Plan** aimed to defeat France quickly by attacking through Belgium, then turn east against Russia. It failed: Belgian resistance, the British Expeditionary Force and the French counter-attack at the **Marne** (September 1914) stopped the Germans. Both sides dug in, and by the end of 1914 a line of trenches ran from the Channel coast to Switzerland.

Attacks against machine guns, barbed wire and artillery cost huge numbers of lives for little ground. At **Verdun** (1916) Germany tried to "bleed France white". The British-led attack on the **Somme** began on 1 July 1916, when the British army suffered about 57,000 casualties, nearly 20,000 of them killed, on the first day alone. New weapons, including poison gas (1915) and tanks (first used in September 1916), did not break the stalemate quickly.

### The Eastern Front

The Russian armies advanced into East Prussia but were defeated at **Tannenberg** (August 1914). The war in the east was more mobile than in the west, but Russia suffered enormous losses and shortages. After the revolutions of 1917 the Bolsheviks made peace with Germany at **Brest-Litovsk** (March 1918), giving up vast territories.

### The war at sea

Britain's navy blockaded Germany, cutting its imports of food and raw materials. The only major battle between the two fleets, **Jutland** (1916), was indecisive, but the German surface fleet stayed mostly in port afterwards. Germany relied on **U-boats** (submarines). The sinking of the liner *Lusitania* in May 1915, with American passengers aboard, angered the USA. In 1917 Britain's adoption of the **convoy** system greatly reduced shipping losses.

### Gallipoli

In April 1915 British, French, Australian and New Zealand forces landed on the Gallipoli peninsula, aiming to knock the Ottoman Empire out of the war and open a sea route to Russia. Ottoman forces held the high ground, and the Allies withdrew in January 1916 after heavy losses.

### The war in Africa

- **Togoland** fell to British and French forces in August 1914, and **Kamerun** by early 1916.
- **German South West Africa** surrendered to South African forces in July 1915.
- In **German East Africa** the German commander **Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck** led a long guerrilla campaign with mostly African troops (askari). He surrendered in Northern Rhodesia only on 25 November 1918, two weeks after the armistice.
- The campaign depended on African carriers. Historians estimate that tens of thousands of carriers, and probably many more, died, mainly from disease, hunger and exhaustion.
- From Southern Rhodesia, white volunteers served in South West Africa, East Africa and Europe, and the **Rhodesia Native Regiment**, formed in 1916, fought in East Africa.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why did the USA enter the war in 1917?"}
1. **Trade and loans:** American business had lent and sold far more to the Allies than to Germany, so an Allied defeat threatened American interests.
2. **Unrestricted submarine warfare:** in February 1917 Germany resumed sinking all ships around Britain, including American ones.
3. **The Zimmermann Telegram:** a German message, intercepted by the British and published in March 1917, proposed an alliance with Mexico against the USA.
4. **Result:** on 6 April 1917 Congress declared war. American troops and supplies helped tip the balance in 1918.
:::

## The end of the war

In March 1918 Germany launched its **Spring Offensive**, using troops freed from the east, and advanced close to Paris. But it ran out of men and supplies. From August the Allies, with fresh American troops and many tanks, drove the Germans back in the **Hundred Days**. Bulgaria, the Ottoman Empire and Austria-Hungary collapsed. In Germany sailors mutinied, the Kaiser abdicated on 9 November, and the new government signed the **armistice**, which took effect at 11 a.m. on 11 November 1918.

## Results of the war

**Human cost:** historians estimate that about 9-10 million soldiers were killed and over 20 million wounded; millions of civilians also died. The influenza pandemic of 1918-1919, spread partly by troop movements, killed tens of millions more around the world, including many in southern Africa.

**Political results:**

- Four empires fell: the German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian and Ottoman.
- New states appeared, including Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia and the Baltic states.
- The Bolsheviks took power in Russia.
- The League of Nations was created, and German colonies and Ottoman Arab lands became **mandates**.
- Bitterness over the peace helped extremist movements later grow in Germany and Italy.

**Economic results:** Britain and France owed huge debts, especially to the USA, which became the world's leading creditor; northern France and Belgium were devastated; inflation and unemployment followed in many countries.

**Social results:** a "lost generation" of young men; many women worked in new jobs, and women in Britain over 30 won the vote in 1918; veterans' experiences changed attitudes to war.

:::example{title="Weighing an interpretation: what did the war mean for Africa?"}
**Change:** African soldiers and carriers saw that Europeans could be defeated and that colonial powers depended on African labour; some returning men became more politically aware; German colonies changed hands as mandates.
**Continuity:** colonial rule continued and, in some areas, labour demands increased; Africans were not consulted at Paris.
**Judgement:** historians generally see the war as an early stage in the growth of African political awareness, but its effects were much smaller than those of the Second World War.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the war outside Europe"}
ZIMSEC expects the war in Africa, Gallipoli and the war at sea as well as the Western Front.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the USA was in the war from the start"}
The USA joined in April 1917, nearly three years after the war began.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the armistice with the peace treaty"}
The armistice stopped the fighting on 11 November 1918; the Treaty of Versailles was signed on 28 June 1919.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="ZIMSEC Paper 2: describe the events"}
Organise by front (Western, Eastern, sea, Gallipoli, Africa), with a date and an outcome for each, rather than writing a long narrative.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Examine the consequences"}
Group results as political, economic and social, and include at least one point about Africa. For a "to what extent" part, weigh short-term against long-term results.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The war (28 July 1914-11 November 1918) set the Allies against the Central Powers.
- The Schlieffen Plan failed at the Marne, leading to trench stalemate; Verdun and the Somme cost huge losses.
- Russia was defeated in the east and left the war at Brest-Litovsk (1918); Gallipoli failed; the British blockade and U-boats shaped the war at sea.
- Africans fought and carried supplies in campaigns in every German colony; Lettow-Vorbeck surrendered only on 25 November 1918.
- The USA entered in 1917; the war ended with the armistice and led to the fall of four empires, debt, mandates and the League.
:::
