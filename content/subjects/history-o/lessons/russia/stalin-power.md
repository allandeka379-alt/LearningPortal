## Introduction

When Lenin died in January 1924, most observers expected **Leon Trotsky**, the brilliant organiser of the October Revolution and creator of the Red Army, to succeed him. Instead, by 1928 the leader of the Soviet Union was **Joseph Stalin**, a party official many colleagues had regarded as dull and unimportant. Over the next decade Stalin built one of the most complete dictatorships in history, in which millions of people were arrested and hundreds of thousands executed.

How one man gained such power inside a party that claimed to rule on behalf of the workers is a question that matters for anyone studying how power is held and abused. ZIMSEC studies Stalin's domestic policies (Paper 2, topic 14), and Cambridge Depth study C, key question 3. You should know about Bolshevik rule and the NEP. Numbers of victims in this lesson are historians' estimates.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="General Secretary"}
The head of the Communist Party's central organisation. Stalin held this post from 1922, which gave him power over appointments to party jobs.
:::

:::definition{term="Purges"}
The removal of people from the party, the army and other institutions, from the mid-1930s usually by arrest, imprisonment or execution.
:::

:::definition{term="Show trial"}
A public trial staged for propaganda, in which the defendants, often after torture or threats, confessed to invented crimes.
:::

:::definition{term="Gulag"}
The system of forced labour camps in the Soviet Union, run by the secret police.
:::

:::definition{term="Cult of personality"}
The use of propaganda to present a leader as a wise, all-knowing hero to be loved and obeyed.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **1922:** Stalin becomes General Secretary.
- **January 1924:** Lenin dies.
- **1927:** Trotsky expelled from the party; exiled in 1928 and deported in 1929.
- **1928-29:** Stalin defeats Bukharin and the "Right".
- **1 December 1934:** Kirov murdered.
- **1936-38:** the show trials and the Great Terror.
:::

## The struggle for power, 1924-28

### The contenders

| Leader | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| **Trotsky** | Organiser of the October Revolution; created the Red Army; brilliant speaker and writer | Joined the Bolsheviks only in 1917; seen as arrogant; feared as a possible military dictator; favoured "permanent revolution" |
| **Stalin** | General Secretary; controlled appointments; seen as a moderate; "Socialism in One Country" | Lenin's Testament criticised him; seen as a dull administrator |
| **Zinoviev and Kamenev** | Senior Old Bolsheviks; led the party in Leningrad and Moscow | Had opposed the October rising in 1917 |
| **Bukharin** | Popular theorist; leading supporter of the NEP | Lacked a strong power base |

### Lenin's Testament

In notes dictated in 1922-23, known as his **Testament**, Lenin criticised all the leading Bolsheviks. In a final addition he said that Stalin was too rude and suggested removing him as General Secretary. After Lenin's death the party leaders decided not to publish it, partly because it also criticised Zinoviev, Kamenev and Trotsky. Stalin survived.

### How Stalin outmanoeuvred his rivals

1. **1923-25:** Stalin allied with **Zinoviev and Kamenev** against Trotsky. Trotsky lost his post as Commissar for War in 1925. Stalin's policy of **"Socialism in One Country"**, building socialism in the USSR first, was more attractive to most party members than Trotsky's call for **"permanent revolution"** spreading across the world.
2. **1925-27:** Stalin turned on Zinoviev and Kamenev, now allied with Bukharin. When they joined Trotsky in a "United Opposition" in 1926, Stalin used the party's ban on factions against them. In **1927** Trotsky, Zinoviev and Kamenev were expelled from the party. Trotsky was exiled to Central Asia in 1928 and deported from the USSR in 1929.
3. **1928-29:** Stalin abandoned the NEP and turned against **Bukharin** and the "Right", who wanted to keep it. Bukharin was removed from the Politburo in 1929. Stalin was now the leader.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why Stalin, not Trotsky, became leader"}
1. **Control of the party machine:** as General Secretary Stalin appointed local party officials, who then chose delegates to party congresses that voted on policy.
2. **The new members:** hundreds of thousands of new members joined in 1924; many owed their positions to Stalin's organisation.
3. **Political skill:** he allied with each group in turn, then turned on it, while presenting himself as the loyal follower of Lenin.
4. **Trotsky's weaknesses:** he seemed arrogant, did not build alliances, missed Lenin's funeral, and failed to use the Testament against Stalin.
5. **Result:** by 1928 Stalin controlled the party's votes, and his opponents had defeated themselves one by one.
:::

## Stalin's dictatorship

### The Purges and the Terror

In December 1934 **Sergei Kirov**, the popular party leader in Leningrad, was murdered. Historians still debate whether Stalin was involved; what is clear is that he used the murder as a reason to hunt for "enemies". The secret police, now part of the **NKVD** (the People's Commissariat for Internal Affairs), arrested growing numbers of party members.

- **Show trials:** in 1936 Zinoviev and Kamenev confessed to plotting with Trotsky and were shot. In 1937 and 1938 further trials followed; Bukharin was executed in 1938. Almost all of Lenin's original leadership was killed.
- **The army:** in 1937 Marshal **Tukhachevsky** and other senior commanders were shot. Three of the five marshals and a large share of senior officers were removed, weakening the Red Army just before war.
- **The Great Terror of 1937-38:** the purges spread to ordinary people: managers, engineers, teachers, writers, national minorities and people denounced by neighbours. Using Soviet archives, historians estimate that in 1937-38 alone about 1.5 million people were arrested and about 680,000 executed.
- **The Gulag:** millions passed through the labour camps in the 1930s, where prisoners worked on canals, mines and forestry in harsh conditions, and many died. By the late 1930s well over a million people were held in the camps at any one time.

Why did Stalin launch the Purges? Historians suggest several reasons: to remove anyone who might challenge him, especially Old Bolsheviks who remembered Lenin's warnings; to find scapegoats for economic failures; to create fear so that no one would resist; and, some argue, because of his own suspicious personality.

### Propaganda and culture

- **The cult of personality:** Stalin's portraits, statues and quotations were everywhere. He was presented as Lenin's closest comrade, the "father of the peoples". Towns were named after him, such as Stalingrad.
- **Rewriting history:** an official history of the party (1938) exaggerated Stalin's role in 1917; photographs were altered to remove purged leaders.
- **Socialist realism:** from 1934 writers, artists and composers had to produce optimistic works glorifying socialism, workers and Stalin. Many who did not were silenced or arrested.
- **Religion:** churches, mosques and synagogues were closed, and many clergy were arrested.
- **Education** spread literacy but taught loyalty to Stalin. The 1936 "Stalin Constitution" promised rights on paper that did not exist in practice.

## How complete was Stalin's control by 1941?

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: total control?"}
**Very complete:** all rivals were dead or in exile; the party, army and secret police had been purged and were filled with people who owed everything to Stalin; propaganda shaped what people read, saw and heard; terror made open opposition almost impossible.
**Limits:** the Terror created chaos and weakened the economy and the army; officials sometimes ignored or bent instructions; peasants resisted quietly by working slowly or hiding grain; and many people kept private beliefs, including religious faith.
**Judgement:** by 1941 no one could challenge Stalin's political power, but control over people's minds and the economy was less complete than propaganda claimed.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Lenin chose Stalin as his successor"}
Lenin named no successor, and his Testament suggested removing Stalin as General Secretary.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the Purges targeted only political rivals"}
They began with the party elite but spread to the army, managers, minorities and millions of ordinary people.
:::

:::mistake{title="Presenting estimates as exact figures"}
Numbers of victims are historians' estimates based on archives; say "historians estimate" and give approximate figures.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (b): 'Explain why Stalin, and not Trotsky, emerged as Lenin's successor'"}
Balance Stalin's strengths (the General Secretary's power, political skill, popular policies) with Trotsky's weaknesses. Develop each reason with an example.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Cambridge Paper 4: 'Describe the methods Stalin used to control the Soviet Union'"}
Organise by method: terror (purges, show trials, NKVD, Gulag), propaganda (cult, rewriting history), and control of culture and religion. Use dates and named examples.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Stalin used his post as General Secretary to control the party and allied with each rival group in turn.
- Trotsky was expelled in 1927 and deported in 1929; Bukharin and the Right were defeated in 1928-29.
- After Kirov's murder (1934) the show trials and the Great Terror of 1937-38 destroyed the Old Bolsheviks, much of the army's leadership and huge numbers of ordinary people.
- The Gulag, the NKVD, propaganda, the cult of personality and socialist realism maintained control.
- By 1941 Stalin's political power was unchallenged, though control of society was never total.
:::
