## Introduction

Seizing Petrograd in November 1917 was the easy part. The Bolsheviks then had to rule a vast country at war with Germany, with most of the population against them or indifferent, and with enemies gathering on every side. Within four years they had made a humiliating peace, fought and won a brutal civil war, crushed a rising by the sailors who had once been their strongest supporters, and reversed their own economic policy.

Many movements that take power by force face the same challenge of turning a seizure of power into a working state, and must decide how far to use compromise and how far coercion. Lenin used both.

ZIMSEC studies Bolshevik rule, the Civil War and the NEP (Paper 2, topic 14), and Cambridge Depth study C, key question 2. You should know how the Bolsheviks seized power.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Cheka"}
The Bolshevik secret police set up in December 1917 to fight "counter-revolution". It became the main instrument of the Red Terror.
:::

:::definition{term="Reds and Whites"}
The two main sides in the Civil War: the Bolsheviks and their Red Army, and the varied anti-Bolshevik forces known as the Whites.
:::

:::definition{term="War Communism"}
The Bolshevik economic policy of 1918-21: state control of industry, forced requisitioning of grain from peasants, rationing and a ban on private trade.
:::

:::definition{term="New Economic Policy (NEP)"}
The policy introduced in 1921 that ended grain requisitioning, allowed peasants to sell their surplus and permitted small private businesses, while the state kept control of heavy industry, banking and foreign trade.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
- **November 1917:** Decrees on Peace and Land.
- **December 1917:** the Cheka set up.
- **January 1918:** the Constituent Assembly dissolved after one day.
- **3 March 1918:** Treaty of Brest-Litovsk.
- **1918-21:** the Civil War and War Communism.
- **March 1921:** the Kronstadt Rising; the NEP announced.
- **21 January 1924:** Lenin dies.
:::

## Consolidating power, 1917-18

### Early decrees

Lenin's government, the Council of People's Commissars, issued decrees at once:

- **Decree on Peace:** a call for an immediate end to the war.
- **Decree on Land:** landlords' estates were handed to peasant committees, approving the land seizures already happening.
- **Workers' control** of factories; banks nationalised; an eight-hour day.
- Opposition newspapers were closed, and the **Cheka**, led by Felix Dzerzhinsky, was set up in December 1917.

### The Constituent Assembly

Elections to the long-promised **Constituent Assembly** were held in November 1917. The Socialist Revolutionaries, with strong peasant support, won about 40% of the vote; the Bolsheviks won about a quarter. When the Assembly met in **January 1918** and refused to accept Bolshevik rule, Red Guards closed it the next day. Lenin argued that the soviets were a higher form of democracy. From then on the Bolsheviks ruled as a one-party dictatorship.

### Brest-Litovsk

Russia's army had collapsed, and Lenin believed the revolution could survive only with peace. After long negotiations, and a new German advance, the Bolsheviks signed the **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk** on **3 March 1918**. Russia gave up Poland, Finland, the Baltic lands and Ukraine, losing much of its population, farmland, industry and coal. Many Russians, including some Bolsheviks, saw it as a betrayal. The government moved the capital from Petrograd to **Moscow**. (The treaty was cancelled when Germany was defeated in November 1918.)

## The Civil War, 1918-21

From the spring of 1918 the Bolsheviks faced enemies on every side:

- **The Whites:** former Tsarist officers such as Admiral **Kolchak** in Siberia, General **Denikin** in the south and General **Yudenich** near Petrograd, together with liberals and others who opposed the Bolsheviks.
- **The Czech Legion,** former prisoners of war who rebelled in May 1918 and seized much of the Trans-Siberian Railway.
- **Foreign intervention:** Britain, France, the USA, Japan and others sent troops, at first to keep Russia in the war against Germany and protect supplies, and then to support the Whites.
- **Other groups:** Socialist Revolutionaries, peasant armies (the "Greens") and national independence movements.

In July 1918, as Czech and White forces approached Yekaterinburg, the Bolsheviks shot **Nicholas II and his family**. After an attempt on Lenin's life in August 1918, the Cheka began the **Red Terror**: thousands of hostages and suspected opponents were executed, and camps were set up. The Whites also carried out terror and killings, including massacres of Jews.

By the end of 1920 the main White armies had been defeated; the last were evacuated from Crimea in November 1920.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why the Reds won the Civil War"}
1. **Geography:** the Reds held the central core of Russia, with Moscow, Petrograd, most of the industry and the railway network, so they could move troops quickly from one front to another.
2. **Leadership and organisation:** Trotsky, Commissar for War, built the **Red Army** of several million by conscription, using former Tsarist officers watched by political commissars, and enforced harsh discipline.
3. **Unity:** the Reds had one leadership and one aim, while the Whites were divided in aims and spread around the edges of Russia, unable to co-ordinate their attacks.
4. **Support:** many peasants disliked the Reds' grain seizures but feared that a White victory would bring back the landlords.
5. **Limited foreign help:** foreign troops were few and soon withdrawn, while the Reds used foreign intervention to present themselves as defenders of Russia.
6. **Result:** War Communism and terror supplied and controlled the Red Army, and the Whites were defeated one by one.
:::

## War Communism

To feed the Red Army and the cities, the Bolsheviks introduced **War Communism**:

- **Grain requisitioning:** armed squads seized grain from peasants, often leaving them too little to live on.
- Industry was **nationalised** and run by the state; workers faced strict discipline.
- **Private trade was banned**; food was rationed, with the best rations for workers and soldiers.

It helped win the war but ruined the economy. Peasants grew less, knowing that surplus would be taken; industrial production collapsed; and people fled the starving cities. In **1921 famine** struck the Volga region and other areas: historians estimate that about five million people died, despite foreign relief. Peasant revolts broke out, notably in the Tambov region.

## Kronstadt and the NEP

In **March 1921** sailors at the **Kronstadt** naval base near Petrograd, who had been heroes of 1917, rebelled. They demanded freely elected soviets, freedom of speech, and an end to requisitioning. The Red Army crushed the rising across the frozen sea, and many sailors were killed or executed.

At the Tenth Party Congress the same month, Lenin announced the **New Economic Policy**:

- Grain requisitioning was replaced by a **tax**; peasants could **sell their surplus** for profit.
- **Small businesses** and private trade were allowed again; traders known as **Nepmen** flourished.
- The state kept the **"commanding heights"**: heavy industry, banking, transport and foreign trade.
- A new, stable currency was introduced.

Lenin admitted that the NEP was a temporary retreat to rescue the economy. At the same time the Congress **banned factions** within the party, so political control was tightened as economic control was relaxed.

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: how far did the NEP succeed?"}
**Success:** food production and industry recovered quickly; by about 1926 output had returned to roughly pre-war levels; famine ended; peasants and traders were calmer, and the regime survived.
**Limits:** heavy industry recovered more slowly than farming and small trade; prices of manufactured goods rose faster than farm prices in 1923, the "scissors crisis"; and many Bolsheviks hated the NEP as a return to capitalism, creating a split that shaped the struggle after Lenin's death.
**Judgement:** as an emergency measure to save the economy and the regime, the NEP worked; as a long-term route to a socialist, industrial Russia, many Bolsheviks believed it had failed.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying War Communism was the cause of victory and of nothing else"}
War Communism supplied the Red Army, but it also caused famine and revolts. Explain both sides.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the NEP was full capitalism"}
Under the NEP the state kept the commanding heights, and the Communist Party kept total political control. It was a mixed economy.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up Brest-Litovsk and the Constituent Assembly"}
Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) was the peace treaty with Germany. The Constituent Assembly (January 1918) was the elected assembly the Bolsheviks closed.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Bolsheviks gave land to the peasants, set up the Cheka and closed the Constituent Assembly in January 1918.
- The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) ended the war with Germany at a huge cost in land.
- The Reds won the Civil War through central position, Trotsky's Red Army, unity and the Whites' divisions.
- War Communism supplied the army but caused famine, revolts and the Kronstadt Rising of March 1921.
- The NEP (1921) allowed private trade and small business; the economy recovered, but many communists saw it as a retreat.
:::
