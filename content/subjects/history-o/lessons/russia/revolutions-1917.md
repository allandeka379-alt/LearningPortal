## Introduction

In 1917 Russia had two revolutions in eight months. In the first, crowds in the capital and a mutiny of soldiers brought down Tsar Nicholas II. In the second, a small, disciplined party, the **Bolsheviks** led by **Lenin**, seized power and began building the world's first communist state. Its ideas and its rivalry with the West shaped the Cold War and the politics of many newly independent African countries.

This lesson is studied by ZIMSEC (Paper 2, topic 14: the February and October revolutions and the role of Lenin) and by Cambridge (Depth study C, key questions 1 and 2). You should know how the Tsar ruled and how he survived the 1905 Revolution.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Provisional Government"}
The temporary government formed from members of the Duma after the Tsar's abdication in 1917. It was meant to rule until an elected Constituent Assembly decided how Russia should be governed.
:::

:::definition{term="Petrograd Soviet"}
The council of workers' and soldiers' representatives in Petrograd (the new name of St Petersburg from 1914). In 1917 it shared power with the Provisional Government.
:::

:::definition{term="Dual power"}
The situation from March to November 1917 in which the Provisional Government and the Petrograd Soviet both claimed authority, and the soldiers and workers often obeyed the Soviet first.
:::

:::definition{term="Bolsheviks"}
The Marxist party led by Lenin, which seized power in November 1917 and later renamed itself the Communist Party.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Two calendars, two names"}
Russia used the Julian (old-style) calendar until February 1918; it was 13 days behind the Western (new-style) calendar. So the revolution that began on 23 February (old style) began on 8 March (new style), and the Bolshevik seizure of power on 25 October (old style) took place on 7 November (new style).
- ZIMSEC and most Russian sources say the **February** and **October** Revolutions.
- Cambridge uses new-style dates: the **March** and **November** Revolutions.
They are the same events. This lesson gives new-style dates first, old-style in brackets.
:::

## The impact of the First World War

Russia entered the war in August 1914 with a wave of patriotism, but the war exposed every weakness of the Tsarist state.

![Timeline of Russia from 1915 to 1917: the Tsar takes command, Rasputin is murdered, the February (March) Revolution brings abdication, Lenin returns in April, and the Bolsheviks take power in the October (November) Revolution on 7 November 1917.](media:diagrams/russia-1917-timeline.png "Figure: timeline of Russia, 1915-1917")

- **Military defeat:** the army was defeated at **Tannenberg** in 1914 and lost huge areas in 1915. It was short of rifles, shells and boots, and by early 1917 millions of soldiers had been killed, wounded or captured.
- **The Tsar took command:** in September 1915 Nicholas II made himself Commander-in-Chief. From then on he was personally blamed for defeats.
- **Government at home:** the Tsarina, Alexandra, who was German-born, ran the government in Petrograd and was influenced by **Rasputin**. Ministers were changed again and again. Rasputin was murdered by nobles in December 1916, but the damage to the monarchy's reputation was done.
- **Economic collapse:** the railways could not carry both soldiers and food, so cities ran short of bread and fuel. Prices rose far faster than wages.

## The February (March) Revolution

| Date (new style) | Old style | Event |
|---|---|---|
| 8 March 1917 | 23 February | International Women's Day; women queueing for bread join striking workers |
| 10 March | 25 February | General strike in Petrograd |
| 12 March | 27 February | Soldiers mutiny and join the crowds; Petrograd Soviet formed; Duma committee set up |
| 15 March | 2 March | Nicholas II abdicates; the Provisional Government is formed |

The revolution was largely **spontaneous**: no party planned it, and most Bolshevik leaders were in exile or prison. When the Tsar ordered troops to restore order, many soldiers in the Petrograd garrison, often peasants in uniform, refused to fire on the crowds and joined them. The army's generals and Duma leaders told Nicholas that only his abdication could save the war effort. He abdicated for himself and his son; his brother Michael refused the throne the next day. Three hundred years of Romanov rule were over.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why the February (March) Revolution succeeded"}
1. **Long-term discontent:** poverty, autocracy and the broken promises of 1905.
2. **The war:** defeats, shortages and the Tsar's personal responsibility for the army.
3. **The trigger:** bread shortages brought women and workers onto the streets in March 1917.
4. **The decisive moment:** the soldiers of the Petrograd garrison refused to fire and joined the crowds.
5. **Result:** without the army, the Tsar had no way to restore order, and his generals advised him to abdicate.
:::

## The Provisional Government

The Provisional Government, first led by Prince Lvov and from July 1917 by **Alexander Kerensky**, granted freedom of speech, released political prisoners and promised elections. But it faced huge problems:

- **Dual power:** the Petrograd Soviet's **Order No. 1** told soldiers to obey their officers and the government only if the Soviet agreed. The government had authority but little power.
- **The war:** it kept fighting to honour Russia's alliances. Kerensky's **summer offensive** of 1917 collapsed, and soldiers deserted in large numbers.
- **Land:** peasants began seizing landlords' estates, but the government said land reform must wait for the Constituent Assembly.
- **Delay:** elections to the Constituent Assembly were postponed until November.
- **Economic chaos:** shortages and inflation grew worse.

## Lenin and the Bolsheviks

**Lenin** returned from exile in Switzerland in April 1917; the German government allowed him to travel through Germany, hoping he would take Russia out of the war. In his **April Theses** he demanded that the Bolsheviks give no support to the Provisional Government, that the war be ended and that power pass to the soviets, summed up in the slogan **"All power to the Soviets"**. The Bolsheviks' simple slogan, **"Peace, Land and Bread"**, matched what soldiers, peasants and workers wanted.

- **The July Days (July 1917):** armed demonstrations in Petrograd against the government failed. The Bolsheviks were blamed, Lenin fled to Finland and Trotsky was arrested.
- **The Kornilov affair (September 1917, late August old style):** the army's commander, General **Kornilov**, appeared to be moving troops on Petrograd to crush the Soviet. Kerensky turned to the Soviet for help and armed the workers, including the Bolshevik **Red Guards**. Kornilov's advance collapsed, and the Bolsheviks were seen as the defenders of the revolution.
- By September the Bolsheviks had **majorities in the Petrograd and Moscow Soviets**, and **Trotsky** became chairman of the Petrograd Soviet.

## The October (November) Revolution

Lenin, returning secretly, persuaded the Bolshevik Central Committee to vote for an armed rising. **Trotsky** organised it through the Soviet's **Military Revolutionary Committee**. On **6-7 November** (24-25 October), Red Guards and soldiers took over bridges, railway stations, the telephone exchange and other key points in Petrograd with little resistance. During the night of 7-8 November they captured the **Winter Palace**, where ministers of the Provisional Government were arrested; Kerensky had already left the city.

The Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets, meeting in Petrograd, was told that power had passed to the soviets. Moderate socialists walked out in protest, and a new government, the **Council of People's Commissars**, was formed with Lenin at its head.

## Why did the Bolsheviks succeed?

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: a popular revolution or a coup?"}
**A popular revolution:** the Bolsheviks won majorities in the soviets of the main cities; their promises of peace and land matched what millions wanted; and few people were willing to defend the Provisional Government.
**A coup:** the seizure of power was planned and carried out by a small armed force on the orders of one party, timed before the Congress of Soviets could decide; and in the Constituent Assembly elections soon afterwards the Bolsheviks won only about a quarter of the votes.
**Judgement:** historians disagree. Many argue that it was a coup carried out at a moment of real popular support for "soviet power", which the Bolsheviks then took for themselves.
:::

Key reasons historians give for Bolshevik success:

- **The weakness of the Provisional Government:** the war, delay over land, dual power and the Kornilov affair.
- **Lenin's leadership:** clear slogans and his insistence on seizing power.
- **Trotsky's organisation** of the Military Revolutionary Committee and the Red Guards.
- **Party discipline** compared with the divided Mensheviks and Socialist Revolutionaries.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the Bolsheviks overthrew the Tsar"}
The Tsar was overthrown in the February (March) Revolution, which the Bolsheviks did not lead. They overthrew the Provisional Government in October (November).
:::

:::mistake{title="Muddling the two calendars"}
February and March 1917 are the same revolution; so are October and November. Use the board's names, and say which calendar you are using.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Kerensky led the Provisional Government from March"}
Prince Lvov led it first; Kerensky became Prime Minister in July 1917.
:::

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The First World War brought defeat, shortages and blame on the Tsar, who took command in 1915.
- In the February (March) Revolution of 1917 strikes and a mutiny in Petrograd forced Nicholas II to abdicate.
- The Provisional Government shared power with the Petrograd Soviet and failed by continuing the war and delaying land reform and elections.
- Lenin's slogans, the Kornilov affair and Trotsky's organisation strengthened the Bolsheviks.
- In the October (November) Revolution the Bolsheviks seized Petrograd and formed a government led by Lenin.
:::
