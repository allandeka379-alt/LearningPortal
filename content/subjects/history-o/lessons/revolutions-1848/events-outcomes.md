## Introduction

In March 1848 the old rulers of Europe seemed beaten. By the end of 1849 almost all of them were back in control. In France a republic survived for a few years but chose a Bonaparte as president; in the Austrian Empire the army crushed Vienna, Prague and finally Hungary. Yet 1848 was not simply a failure. Peasants in the Austrian lands were freed from labour services for good, and nationalists learned hard lessons about power that shaped the unification of Italy and Germany.

Independence movements in twentieth-century Africa faced a similar test: once the colonial power was shaken, could the nationalists stay united, and who controlled the army? Keep these questions in mind.

You should already know the causes of the 1848 revolutions (liberalism, nationalism, the Metternich system and the economic crisis). This lesson covers Cambridge 0470 Option A, key question 1, focus points on the events, failure and results of 1848.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Second Republic"}
The French republic proclaimed in February 1848, which lasted until Louis Napoleon made himself Emperor Napoleon III in December 1852.
:::

:::definition{term="National Workshops"}
Public works set up in Paris in 1848 to give jobs to the unemployed. Their closure in June 1848 caused the June Days.
:::

:::definition{term="Universal male suffrage"}
The right of every adult man to vote, regardless of wealth. France introduced it in March 1848.
:::

:::definition{term="Robot"}
The unpaid labour service that peasants in the Austrian Empire owed to their landlords. It was abolished in 1848.
:::

:::keyfact{title="People to know"}
- **Louis Napoleon Bonaparte:** nephew of Napoleon I, elected President of France in December 1848.
- **General Cavaignac:** crushed the June Days in Paris.
- **Lajos Kossuth:** leader of the Hungarian national movement.
- **Windischgrätz and Radetzky:** Austrian generals who crushed the revolutions in Prague, Vienna and northern Italy.
- **Schwarzenberg:** Austrian chief minister from November 1848.
- **Franz Joseph:** became Emperor of Austria, aged 18, in December 1848.
:::

## France: from republic to Bonaparte

After Louis Philippe's fall on 24 February 1848, a **provisional government** took over. It included the poet Lamartine and the socialist Louis Blanc. It introduced **universal male suffrage**, ended censorship and set up the **National Workshops** to employ the jobless of Paris.

The new voters were mostly peasants who distrusted the radicals of Paris, and the elections of April 1848 returned a moderate and conservative majority. The workshops were expensive and seemed to the new assembly a dangerous concentration of armed workers. When the government closed them in June, the workers of eastern Paris rose. In the **June Days** (23-26 June 1848) General **Cavaignac** used the army to crush the rising. Several thousand people were killed, and thousands more were arrested, many later deported. The middle class and the workers who had made the February Revolution together were now enemies.

A new constitution created a president elected by all men. In the election of **10 December 1848**, **Louis Napoleon Bonaparte** won about three-quarters of the votes. Peasants voted for the famous name; property owners saw him as a guarantee of order. In December 1851 he seized full power in a coup, and in December 1852 he became **Emperor Napoleon III**.

## The Austrian Empire

### Vienna and the fall of Metternich

The rising in Vienna on **13 March 1848** forced Metternich to resign. Emperor Ferdinand promised a constitution. After further unrest in May the imperial family left the capital for Innsbruck. An elected assembly met in July and in September 1848 passed a law **abolishing the robot** and other feudal dues. Once peasants had their land free of labour services, most lost interest in the revolution.

### Prague and the Czechs

Czech nationalists, led by the historian Palacký, wanted self-government for Bohemia within a reformed Austrian Empire, not unity with Germany. A Slav Congress met in Prague in June 1848. When street fighting broke out, General **Windischgrätz** bombarded the city and crushed the rising in mid-June. This was the first major victory for the army.

### Italy, Vienna again and the new emperor

In northern Italy, Field Marshal **Radetzky** defeated King Charles Albert of Piedmont at Custoza in July 1848 (taught with Italian unification). In October a new rising in Vienna was crushed by Windischgrätz's troops at the end of the month. **Schwarzenberg** became chief minister, and in December Ferdinand abdicated in favour of his nephew **Franz Joseph**, who was not bound by Ferdinand's promises. The assembly was dissolved in March 1849, and by the end of 1851 the emperor had abolished constitutional government altogether.

### Hungary and Kossuth

In spring 1848 the Hungarian Diet passed the **April Laws**, giving Hungary its own government responsible to its own parliament, ending serfdom and allowing a free press. The movement was led by **Lajos Kossuth**. But Hungary's own minorities (Croats, Serbs, Romanians and Slovaks) feared rule by Magyar speakers. In September 1848 Jelačić, the governor of Croatia, invaded Hungary on behalf of the emperor, and war followed.

The Hungarians fought well and declared independence in April 1849. Franz Joseph then asked Tsar **Nicholas I** of Russia for help. A large Russian army invaded, and in **August 1849** the main Hungarian army surrendered at Világos. Hungarian generals and the former prime minister Batthyány were executed in October 1849, and Kossuth fled into exile.

:::example{title="A cause explained step by step: why the Hungarian revolution failed"}
1. **Divided population:** Croats, Serbs and Romanians in Hungary did not want Magyar rule and fought for the emperor.
2. **Austria recovered:** by the end of 1848 the army had retaken Prague and Vienna and could turn on Hungary.
3. **Russian intervention:** Nicholas I feared that revolution would spread to Russian Poland, so he sent a large army.
4. **Result:** facing two empires at once, the Hungarians surrendered in August 1849.
:::

## Why most of the revolutions failed

- **Divisions among the revolutionaries.** Liberals wanted constitutions and order; workers wanted jobs and social reform. After the June Days, the middle class in France and elsewhere sided with the forces of order.
- **National rivalries.** Germans, Czechs, Magyars, Croats and Italians wanted different things, and rulers could use one nationality against another.
- **The armies stayed loyal.** The revolutions won in March because rulers lost their nerve, not because armies were defeated. Generals such as Windischgrätz and Radetzky stayed loyal and later retook the cities.
- **Peasants dropped out.** Once labour services were abolished, peasants had what they wanted.
- **Foreign intervention.** Russia crushed Hungary, and French troops ended the Roman Republic in 1849.
- **Concessions withdrawn.** Rulers promised reforms in March, waited, then took them back once they had recovered.

:::example{title="An interpretation weighed: did the revolutions change anything?"}
**Little changed:** by 1851 absolute rule was restored in Austria, Louis Napoleon had made himself dictator in France, and Italy and Germany were still divided.
**Much changed:** the robot was never restored, so millions of Austrian peasants were freed; universal male suffrage, restricted by the French assembly in 1850, was restored by Louis Napoleon in 1851 and lasted; Piedmont and Prussia kept constitutions; and nationalists learned that unity would need armies and diplomacy, not only speeches.
**Judgement:** the revolutions failed in their main aims, but they ended feudal obligations in central Europe and changed the methods of nationalists, which helps explain the unifications of the 1860s.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the two Napoleons"}
Louis Napoleon, elected president in 1848 and emperor as Napoleon III from 1852, was the nephew of Napoleon I. Napoleon I died in 1821.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Austria defeated Hungary alone"}
Austria needed Russian help. The Hungarian army surrendered in August 1849 after a large Russian army invaded.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the revolutions achieved nothing"}
Examiners reward balance: the end of the robot, universal male suffrage in France and the constitutions in Piedmont and Prussia were lasting changes.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 1 (b): 'Explain why the revolutions of 1848 failed'"}
Choose two or three reasons and support each with a named example: the June Days (class divisions), Jelačić (national rivalry), Windischgrätz (the army), Russia in Hungary (intervention).
:::

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- France became a republic with universal male suffrage, but the June Days split workers and the middle class, and Louis Napoleon was elected president in December 1848.
- In the Austrian Empire, Metternich fell, the robot was abolished, and the army then crushed Prague, Vienna and northern Italy.
- Hungary under Kossuth fought for independence until Russian intervention forced surrender in August 1849.
- The revolutions failed because of class and national divisions, loyal armies, satisfied peasants and foreign intervention.
- Lasting changes included the end of labour services in Austria, universal male suffrage in France and new methods for nationalists.
:::
