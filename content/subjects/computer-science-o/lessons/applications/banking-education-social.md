## Introduction

Chipo's mother runs a vegetable stall at Mbare Musika in Harare. Customers pay her with mobile money from their phones; at the end of the week she transfers the takings to her bank account and pays school fees online. Chipo's school sends her progress report through its administration system, and she revises using video lessons on a learning platform. In the evening she chats with her cousin in Bulawayo on a social network. Every one of these activities depends on computer systems.

ZIMSEC Form 1 (topic 6.2, Application of Computer Science) asks you to describe how computers are applied in banking, education, social networks and research and development. Paper 2 questions usually describe a situation and ask how a system works, or ask for its benefits and drawbacks. Answers earn marks when they are specific: name the system, say what it does and who benefits.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Automated teller machine (ATM)"}
A self-service banking terminal that lets a customer withdraw cash, check a balance, deposit money or pay bills using a bank card and a PIN.
:::

:::definition{term="Electronic funds transfer (EFT)"}
Moving money from one bank account to another electronically, without cash or cheques changing hands; used for salaries, bill payments and transfers between banks.
:::

:::definition{term="Mobile money"}
A service that lets people store, send and receive money and pay for goods using a mobile phone account, often without a traditional bank account.
:::

:::definition{term="E-learning"}
Learning delivered using electronic technology, such as online courses, video lessons, interactive quizzes and virtual classrooms.
:::

:::definition{term="Social network"}
An online service where users create profiles, connect with other people and share messages, photos, videos and links.
:::

## Banking

| System | How it works | Benefits | Drawbacks |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| ATM | Card is read (chip or magnetic stripe); PIN entered and checked by the bank's computer; balance checked; cash dispensed and account updated | Cash available 24 hours a day; no queue at the counter | Card skimming and shoulder-surfing; machines run out of cash; withdrawal limits |
| EFT | The bank's computer debits one account and credits another, sending the details over a secure network | Fast, no cash to carry or lose; salaries paid on time; record kept automatically | Needs network access; errors in account numbers send money to the wrong person |
| Internet banking | Customer logs in to the bank's secure website to view statements, pay bills and transfer money | Bank from anywhere at any time; saves travel; up-to-date statements | Phishing and hacking risks; needs internet access and digital skills |
| Mobile banking and mobile money | Customer uses an app or a short code menu on a phone, authorised by a PIN | Works on basic phones; reaches people without bank accounts; quick payments to traders and for bills | Lost phone or shared PIN puts money at risk; network outages stop payments; transaction charges |

Banks also use computers behind the scenes: processing cheques with magnetic ink character recognition, calculating interest, detecting unusual transactions that may be fraud, and keeping customer records.

## Education

- **E-learning:** video lessons, online quizzes that are marked automatically, virtual classrooms and digital libraries let learners study at their own pace and revisit difficult topics.
- **School administration systems:** store learner records, attendance, timetables, fees, marks and reports; produce reports for parents and statistics for the head.
- **Computer-assisted learning (CAL):** software that teaches, tests and gives instant feedback, such as typing tutors and simulation software for science experiments.
- **Communication:** school websites, email and messaging groups keep parents and learners informed.

**Benefits:** learners can study anywhere and at any time; instant feedback; resources can be shared with many schools; less paperwork for teachers; records are easy to search and analyse. **Drawbacks:** devices and internet data are expensive, so rural learners may be left behind (the digital divide); less face-to-face contact; distractions online; teachers need training; data must be protected.

## Social networks

Social networks are used to keep in touch with family and friends, share news, advertise small businesses, organise community groups and share learning resources.

**Benefits:** cheap, instant communication over any distance; businesses reach customers cheaply; information spreads quickly in emergencies. **Drawbacks:** cyberbullying; false information spreads quickly; personal data and privacy can be misused; addiction and time-wasting; scams and identity theft.

## Research and development

Researchers use computers to collect and analyse very large amounts of data, run **simulations** and **models** (for example, testing a new bridge design or predicting the spread of a disease), search online journals, share results with researchers in other countries, and control laboratory equipment that logs readings automatically.

**Benefits:** complex calculations are done quickly and accurately; experiments that would be dangerous or expensive can be simulated first; results can be shared worldwide. **Drawbacks:** expensive powerful computers and software; a model is only as good as the data and assumptions it uses.

:::example
**How an ATM withdrawal works.** Tatenda withdraws US\$40 from an ATM in Gweru.

1. He inserts his card; the card reader reads the account details from the chip.
2. He types his PIN on the keypad. The PIN is checked; if it is wrong three times, the card is retained.
3. He selects "withdraw cash" and enters 40.
4. The ATM sends a request to the bank's computer, which checks that his balance and daily limit allow the withdrawal.
5. If the request is approved, the account is debited, the cash is dispensed and a receipt can be printed. If not, a message explains why.
:::

:::example
**Evaluating e-learning for a rural school.** A secondary school near Binga is offered tablets loaded with e-learning lessons.

1. **Benefits:** learners can replay video lessons when a teacher is absent; quizzes give instant feedback; the lessons can be stored on the tablets, so they work without an internet connection.
2. **Drawbacks:** the tablets need charging, which is difficult without reliable electricity; teachers need training; tablets can be damaged or stolen; learners may be distracted.
3. **Conclusion:** e-learning can support, but not replace, teachers; solar charging and offline content make it practical in this setting.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Vague benefits"}
"Banking is easier" earns nothing. Say what is easier and why: "customers can pay bills at any time without travelling to a branch".
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the ATM stores the customer's balance"}
The balance is held on the **bank's computer**. The ATM sends a request and receives approval.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the drawbacks"}
Paper 2 often asks for **both** benefits and drawbacks. Give at least one security or cost drawback for every system.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"**Describe** how the system works" means a sequence of steps with the hardware named: card reader, keypad, network, bank's computer, cash dispenser.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked about mobile money, remember why it matters in Zimbabwe: it works on basic phones and reaches people far from bank branches.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Banking uses ATMs, EFT, internet banking, mobile banking and mobile money: convenient, fast and available all day, but exposed to fraud and network failure.
- Education uses e-learning, CAL and school administration systems: flexible learning and less paperwork, but costly devices and the digital divide.
- Social networks give cheap, instant communication but bring cyberbullying, false information and privacy risks.
- Research uses computers for data analysis, simulation, modelling and sharing results.
:::
