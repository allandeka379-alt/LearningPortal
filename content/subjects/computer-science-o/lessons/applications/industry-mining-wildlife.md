## Introduction

An engineering firm in Bulawayo designs a new water pump on screen, tests it in a simulation, and sends the design straight to a machine that cuts the metal parts. At a gold mine near Kadoma, computers control the crushers, pumps and ventilation fans deep underground, and sound an alarm if gas levels rise. In Hwange National Park, rangers watch a map on which every collared elephant appears as a moving dot, while cameras at waterholes send images of anyone entering at night.

ZIMSEC Form 3 (topic 6.2) asks how computers are applied in computer-aided manufacturing, mining and wildlife management, as well as intelligent systems (covered in the artificial intelligence lesson). Manufacturing, mining and wildlife are important to Zimbabwe's economy, so exam scenarios are often set in a factory, a mine or a national park.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Computer-aided design (CAD)"}
Using computer software to create, edit, analyse and test precise drawings and 3D models of products, buildings or parts.
:::

:::definition{term="Computer-aided manufacturing (CAM)"}
Using computers to control the machines that make products, for example computer numerical control (CNC) lathes, cutters, 3D printers and robot arms, working directly from a CAD design.
:::

:::definition{term="Computer numerical control (CNC)"}
Control of a machine tool by a computer that follows a program of exact co-ordinates and movements, so parts are cut, drilled or shaped precisely.
:::

:::definition{term="Process control"}
The use of computers, sensors and actuators to monitor and control an industrial process continuously, keeping conditions such as temperature, pressure, flow or gas levels within set limits.
:::

:::definition{term="Tracking collar"}
A collar fitted to an animal that contains a GPS receiver and a transmitter, sending the animal's position to researchers or rangers at regular intervals.
:::

## Manufacturing: CAD and CAM

In **CAD**, designers draw parts accurately to scale, view them in 3D from any angle, change them quickly, and test them on screen (for example how strong a beam is, or whether parts fit together) before anything is made. Libraries of standard parts save time, and designs can be emailed to clients and factories.

In **CAM**, the CAD file is converted into instructions for machines: CNC machines cut and drill, 3D printers build parts layer by layer, and robots weld, paint and assemble. Computers also manage stock, schedule production and check quality, for example with cameras that reject faulty items on a production line. When design and manufacture are linked directly it is called **CAD/CAM**.

**Benefits:** high accuracy and consistency; designs can be changed and re-tested quickly; less waste of materials; machines work continuously and safely with dangerous tools; faster production. **Drawbacks:** expensive software and machines; staff must be retrained; some manual jobs are lost; a software error can produce many faulty parts.

## Mining

| Use | How computers are used |
|:--|:--|
| Exploration | Geological survey data, satellite images and expert systems help predict where ore deposits are; 3D models of the ore body are built |
| Process control | Sensors monitor crushers, mills, conveyor belts, pumps and chemical processing tanks; computers adjust speeds, flow and chemical doses to keep each stage within limits |
| Safety monitoring | Gas sensors (methane, carbon monoxide), temperature and airflow sensors underground; computers control ventilation fans and raise alarms; workers' positions are tracked |
| Automated and remote equipment | Remote-controlled or driverless drilling rigs, loaders and haul trucks; robots inspect dangerous tunnels |
| Management | Records of output, stock, equipment maintenance and payroll; models to plan which areas to mine next |

**Benefits:** fewer people in dangerous places; continuous, accurate control of processing, so more mineral is recovered and less is wasted; quicker warnings of gas or flooding. **Drawbacks:** high cost of equipment and skilled technicians; harsh conditions (dust, water, vibration) damage sensors; failures can halt production; fewer jobs for unskilled workers.

## Wildlife management

- **Tracking collars:** GPS collars on elephants, lions, rhinos and wild dogs send positions via satellite or mobile networks. Researchers study migration routes and home ranges; rangers receive alerts if an animal enters a farm or village (reducing human-wildlife conflict) or stops moving (possible poaching or injury).
- **Anti-poaching surveillance:** camera traps with motion sensors photograph animals and people; thermal cameras and drones patrol at night; acoustic sensors detect gunshots; images and alerts are sent to a control room.
- **Park records:** databases hold animal counts, sightings, births and deaths, ranger patrol routes, incidents, visitor bookings and permits.
- **Analysis and modelling:** GIS maps of water points, vegetation and animal movements help decide where to drill boreholes or place patrols; models predict population changes.

**Benefits:** animals can be followed day and night over huge areas; poachers can be detected and caught sooner; decisions are based on accurate data. **Drawbacks:** collars, drones and cameras are expensive; batteries must be replaced or recharged in remote places; equipment can be damaged by animals or stolen; position data must be kept secret, because poachers could use it to find animals.

:::example
**CAD/CAM in a furniture factory.** A furniture maker in Harare receives an order for 200 identical school desks.

1. The designer draws the desk in CAD software, views it in 3D and checks that the parts fit together.
2. A strength test in the software shows a weak joint; the designer thickens it and re-tests, all on screen.
3. The CAD file is sent to a CNC cutter, which cuts all 200 sets of parts from boards with the same exact dimensions, arranging them to waste as little wood as possible.
4. Benefits: every desk is identical; mistakes are found before any wood is cut; production is fast. Drawback: the CNC machine and training were expensive, and fewer hand-cutting staff are needed.
:::

:::example
**Process control in an ore-processing plant.** At a mine near Shurugwi, crushed ore is mixed with water in a tank before chemicals are added.

1. Sensors measure the level in the tank, the flow of water and the pH of the mixture.
2. Readings are converted to digital and sent to the control computer, which compares each with the stored limits.
3. If the pH is too low, the computer signals a pump (actuator) to add more lime; if the level is too high, it slows the conveyor feeding ore into the tank.
4. Readings are displayed in the control room and logged; alarms sound if any value cannot be corrected.
5. The cycle repeats continuously, day and night.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up CAD and CAM"}
CAD is **design** on a computer; CAM is **manufacturing** controlled by a computer. They are often linked, but they are different stages.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying collars 'protect' animals"}
A collar only reports the animal's position. Protection comes from rangers acting on the information, so describe the whole system.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For process control, use the control cycle: sensor → (ADC) → computer compares with stored limits → actuator → repeat, naming sensors and actuators that fit the scenario.
:::

:::exam-tip
In wildlife questions, a strong drawback is that **location data must be kept secure** from poachers, as well as cost and battery life.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- CAD designs and tests products on screen; CAM uses CNC machines, 3D printers and robots to make them; CAD/CAM links the two.
- In mining, computers support exploration, process control of ore processing, safety monitoring (gas, airflow), remote equipment and management records.
- Wildlife management uses GPS tracking collars, camera traps, drones and acoustic sensors against poaching, park databases and GIS.
- Benefits: accuracy, safety, continuous monitoring, better decisions; drawbacks: cost, maintenance in harsh conditions, job losses, data security.
:::
