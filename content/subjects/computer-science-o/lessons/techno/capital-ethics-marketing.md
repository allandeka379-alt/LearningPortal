## Introduction

Tendai and Rudo, two school leavers in Mutare, notice that small shops in their area lose customers because they cannot accept mobile payments or advertise online. They start a small business that sets up simple websites, social media pages and payment links for local traders. They are **technopreneurs**: entrepreneurs who build a business around technology.

Their business has little money or equipment, so its real value lies in what the partners know, the way they organise their work and the people they know. They must also decide how to treat customers fairly and how to attract them. ZIMSEC Form 1 asks you to describe human, organisational and social capital, explain the attributes of business ethics (Unhu/Ubuntu/Vumunhu) and identify the elements of marketing and business strategies, including e-commerce.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Technopreneurship"}
Starting and running a business that uses technology, especially ICT, to create new products, services or ways of working, and taking the risks involved to make a profit.
:::

:::definition{term="Intellectual capital"}
The intangible (non-physical) value of a business that comes from its knowledge, skills, systems and relationships, rather than from its money, buildings or equipment.
:::

:::definition{term="Business ethics"}
The moral principles that guide how a business behaves towards its customers, employees, competitors, suppliers, the community and the environment.
:::

:::definition{term="Marketing"}
The activities a business uses to identify what customers need and to promote, price and deliver products or services that satisfy those needs profitably.
:::

:::definition{term="E-commerce"}
Buying and selling goods and services over the internet, including online ordering and electronic payment.
:::

## Intellectual capital

| Form | What it is | Examples in an ICT business |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Human capital | The knowledge, skills, experience, creativity and qualifications of the people in the business | A programmer's coding skills; a designer's creativity; staff training |
| Organisational (structural) capital | Knowledge kept in the business itself, which stays when staff go home or leave | Software code, databases, documented procedures, patents, trademarks, brand, company culture |
| Social (relational) capital | The value of the business's relationships and networks | Loyal customers, links with suppliers, partners and investors, reputation and goodwill, mentors |

Human capital belongs to the people: when a skilled worker leaves, their knowledge leaves too. Organisational capital turns that knowledge into something the business keeps, for example by documenting code and procedures. Social capital brings referrals, partnerships and trust.

:::example{title="Identifying intellectual capital"}
Nyasha runs a small app-development company in Harare.

- Her two developers can build Android apps and know payment interfaces: **human capital**.
- The company owns a library of reusable code, a project-management process and a registered trademark for its name: **organisational capital**.
- A local bank recommends the company to its business customers, and past clients return for new apps: **social capital**.
:::

## Business ethics and Unhu/Ubuntu/Vumunhu

Unhu/Ubuntu/Vumunhu teaches that "I am because we are": a business exists within a community and should treat people with respect, honesty and care. Attributes of ethical business behaviour include:

| Attribute | In practice for an ICT business |
|:--|:--|
| Honesty and integrity | Truthful adverts; no hidden charges; delivering what was promised |
| Respect | Polite service; respecting customers' privacy and data |
| Fairness | Fair prices and fair wages; no exploiting staff or customers |
| Accountability and responsibility | Admitting and fixing mistakes; honouring guarantees |
| Legality | Paying taxes; using licensed software; obeying data protection laws |
| Care for community and environment | Recycling e-waste safely; supporting local skills and schools |
| Transparency | Clear terms and conditions; openness about how data is used |

Ethical behaviour builds **trust and reputation**, which bring loyal customers (social capital). Unethical behaviour, such as selling customer data or installing pirated software, can lead to lost customers, legal action and the end of the business.

## Marketing and business strategies

The elements of marketing are often summarised as the **marketing mix (the 4 Ps)**:

| Element | Question | ICT example |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Product | What are we selling, and what makes it different? | A school fees app that works offline |
| Price | How much will we charge? | A low monthly subscription; a free basic version with paid extras |
| Place | How will customers get it? | App store download; online shop; local agents |
| Promotion | How will customers find out about it? | Social media adverts, demonstrations at schools, word of mouth, a website |

Other business strategies include:

- **Market research**: finding out what customers want before building (see the lesson on finance and market research).
- **Targeting a niche**: serving one group very well, such as small farmers or schools.
- **Differentiation**: offering something competitors do not, such as local-language support.
- **Low-cost strategy**: keeping costs low to offer lower prices.
- **Customer service and relationships**: after-sales support and loyalty rewards.
- **Partnerships**: working with banks, mobile networks or schools.

:::example{title="A marketing strategy for a small web business"}
Tendai and Rudo's business in Mutare (from the introduction) plans its marketing.

- **Product:** a ready-made shop web page with a mobile payment link, set up in one day, with free changes for the first month. This differentiates them from larger firms that take weeks.
- **Price:** a low set-up fee and a small monthly charge, so that market traders can afford it.
- **Place:** they visit traders at their stalls and work from their phones and a laptop; no office is needed.
- **Promotion:** before-and-after examples on social media, a free page for the first three traders in each market in exchange for recommendations, and printed cards.
- **Strategy:** a **niche** (small traders in Mutare), good **customer service** and **word of mouth**, which builds their social capital.
- **Ethics:** clear prices with no hidden costs, and traders' customer data is never shared.
:::

## E-commerce

E-commerce lets even a very small business reach customers far beyond its town.

| Advantages for the business | Disadvantages and risks |
|:--|:--|
| Open 24 hours a day; reaches customers nationally and internationally | Needs a reliable internet connection and electricity |
| Lower costs: no need for a large physical shop | Online fraud, hacking and fake payment messages |
| Electronic payments (mobile money, cards) are recorded automatically | Customers cannot touch or try products; returns and delivery must be organised |
| Sales data can be analysed to target customers | Strong competition; the website must be secure and kept up to date |

Types include **business-to-consumer (B2C)**, such as an online electronics shop; **business-to-business (B2B)**, such as a wholesaler selling stock to shops online; and **consumer-to-consumer (C2C)**, such as an online marketplace for second-hand goods.

## The ICT business plan

A business plan is a written document that describes the business and how it will succeed. It is used to plan and to persuade lenders or investors. Its usual sections are: an executive summary; a description of the business and its products; market research and analysis of competitors; a marketing plan (the 4 Ps); an operations plan (equipment, premises, suppliers); a management and staffing plan; and a financial plan (start-up costs, expected sales, profit and funding needed).

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling money intellectual capital"}
Cash, buildings and computers are **physical (financial) capital**. Intellectual capital is knowledge, systems and relationships.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up human and organisational capital"}
Skills held **by a person** are human capital. Knowledge **written down or built into the business** (code, procedures, patents) is organisational capital.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "describe human, organisational and social capital", define each **and** give an example from the scenario.
:::

:::exam-tip
When explaining an ethical attribute, say what the business should do **and** why it matters (trust, reputation, legal consequences).
:::

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
Technopreneurship is examined by ZIMSEC only. Cambridge 0478 has no business topics; e-commerce appears there only through topics such as digital currency and online security.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Technopreneurship is building a business around technology.
- Intellectual capital: human (people's skills and knowledge), organisational (code, procedures, patents, brand) and social (relationships, reputation).
- Business ethics guided by Unhu/Ubuntu/Vumunhu: honesty, respect, fairness, accountability, legality, care for the community and transparency.
- The marketing mix: product, price, place and promotion; strategies include niche targeting, differentiation, low cost, service and partnerships.
- E-commerce (B2C, B2B, C2C) widens reach and cuts costs but brings fraud, delivery and connectivity challenges.
- A business plan sets out the business, market, marketing, operations, people and finances.
:::
