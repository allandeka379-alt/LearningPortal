## Introduction

Chipo has built a popular app in Gweru that helps minibus passengers find the next kombi on their route. It earns money from adverts, stores thousands of users' phone numbers and locations, and has a bright logo that people now recognise. A larger company launches an app with almost the same name and a similar logo, and a stranger asks to "buy her code". Meanwhile the tax authority writes to ask why her income has not been declared.

Running a technology business is not only about technology: it must obey the law, and it must protect its most valuable assets, which are usually ideas, code, names and designs. ZIMSEC Form 3 asks you to identify the laws that govern technopreneurship and to describe intellectual property rights such as patents and copyright. Questions often ask which right protects a given asset, or which law a business has broken.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Intellectual property (IP)"}
Creations of the mind, such as inventions, software, written and artistic works, brand names, logos and designs, that can be owned and protected by law.
:::

:::definition{term="Intellectual property rights (IPR)"}
The legal rights that give the owner of intellectual property the exclusive right to use it and to stop others copying or using it without permission, for a period of time.
:::

:::definition{term="Patent"}
A registered right, granted by the state, that gives the inventor of a new, inventive and useful invention the exclusive right to make, use and sell it for a limited period (usually 20 years), in return for publishing how it works.
:::

:::definition{term="Trademark"}
A registered sign (a name, logo, slogan, symbol or combination) that distinguishes one business's goods or services from those of others.
:::

:::definition{term="Industrial design"}
The registered right in the appearance of a product: its shape, pattern, lines or decoration, but not how it works.
:::

## Laws and policies affecting a technology business

The laws below are given in outline; you do not need section numbers.

| Area | What the business must do |
|:--|:--|
| Business registration | Register the business name, or register as a company with the Registrar of Companies, so that it can trade legally, open a bank account and sign contracts |
| Tax | Register with the Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (ZIMRA), keep records, declare income and pay income tax, and charge and pay value added tax (VAT) when required |
| Consumer protection | Describe goods and services honestly, give clear prices and terms, honour guarantees and handle complaints fairly |
| Cyber and data protection | Under the **Cyber and Data Protection Act [Chapter 12:07]**: process personal data lawfully with consent, keep it secure, and do not commit cybercrimes such as unauthorised access or spreading malware |
| Copyright | Under the **Copyright and Neighbouring Rights Act [Chapter 26:05]**: use only licensed software, images and music, and protect your own works |
| Employment | Written contracts, at least the agreed minimum wages, safe working conditions and fair treatment of staff |
| Telecommunications and licensing | Some services (for example internet service provision) need a licence from the regulator, POTRAZ |
| Local authority rules | Trading licences and permits for premises from the city or town council |

**Government policies** can also help technopreneurs: funding schemes for young entrepreneurs, tax incentives in special economic zones, support for innovation hubs, and policies that encourage the use of local ICT products.

## Intellectual property rights

| Right | What it protects | Registration? | Example for an ICT business |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Patent | A new, inventive, useful **invention** (how something works) | Must be applied for and granted | A new type of low-power sensor circuit |
| Copyright | Original **works**: software code, text, music, images, films | Automatic once created | The app's source code, its manual, its graphics |
| Trademark | **Brand identity**: names, logos, slogans | Registered (unregistered marks have weaker protection) | The app's name and logo |
| Industrial design | The **appearance** of a product | Must be registered | The distinctive shape of a charging dock |
| Trade secret | Confidential business information that gives an advantage | Not registered; protected by keeping it secret and by contracts | The algorithm that ranks search results |

In Zimbabwe, patents, trademarks and industrial designs are registered with the **Zimbabwe Intellectual Property Office (ZIPO)**. The **African Regional Intellectual Property Organization (ARIPO)**, which has its headquarters in Harare, offers a route to apply for protection in several member countries.

**Why IPR matters to technopreneurs:**

- the owner can stop competitors copying the product, so the investment in research pays off;
- IP can be **licensed** or **sold** for income (royalties);
- registered IP is an asset that attracts investors and can support loans;
- a protected brand builds customer trust.

:::example{title="Which right protects it?"}
Chipo's kombi app:

1. The source code of the app: **copyright** (automatic).
2. The app's name and its green bus logo: **trademark** (she should register it, which helps her act against the copycat app).
3. A new method she invents for a small device that counts passengers boarding: possibly a **patent**, if it is new and inventive. She must apply **before** making it public.
4. The unique shape of a passenger-counting device: **industrial design**.
5. Her secret formula for predicting arrival times: a **trade secret**, protected by keeping it confidential and by non-disclosure agreements with staff.
:::

## How to protect an innovation

1. **Keep it confidential** until protected: do not publish or demonstrate an invention before applying for a patent, because public disclosure can destroy its novelty.
2. Use **non-disclosure agreements (NDAs)** with partners, investors and staff.
3. **Search** existing patents and trademarks to make sure the idea or name is new and not someone else's.
4. **Register** the patent, trademark or industrial design with the IP office, paying the fees and renewing on time.
5. Mark works and brands with © or ® (® only once registered).
6. Use **licences** that state how others may use your software.
7. **Monitor** the market and take action (letters, legal action) against infringers.

:::worked-solution[Advising a technopreneur]
Rudo has designed a solar-powered water-level sensor with a new circuit and a distinctive drop-shaped casing, and has written the firmware. She wants to show it at a trade fair next month. Advise her.

1. **Firmware** is protected by **copyright** automatically; she should keep copies with dates as proof of authorship.
2. The **circuit**, if new and inventive, could be **patented**. She should apply **before** the trade fair, because showing it publicly first could prevent a patent.
3. The **drop-shaped casing** can be protected by registering an **industrial design**.
4. The product name and logo should be registered as a **trademark**.
5. Before discussing details with manufacturers, she should ask them to sign a **non-disclosure agreement**.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying software ideas are protected by copyright"}
Copyright protects the **code as written**, not the underlying idea. Someone could write different code that does the same job.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing trademark and copyright"}
A logo used as a brand is protected by a **trademark**. Copyright protects creative works such as the program, text and images.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting that patents must be applied for"}
Unlike copyright, a patent does not exist automatically; it must be applied for and granted, and the invention must be new.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Technology businesses must register, pay tax (ZIMRA), protect consumers, obey the Cyber and Data Protection Act and the Copyright Act, follow employment law and obtain licences where needed.
- Intellectual property rights give owners exclusive use of their creations for a period.
- Patents protect inventions (applied for, about 20 years); copyright protects code and works (automatic); trademarks protect names and logos; industrial designs protect appearance; trade secrets are kept confidential.
- Protect an innovation by keeping it secret, using NDAs, searching, registering with ZIPO, using licences and monitoring for copying.
:::
