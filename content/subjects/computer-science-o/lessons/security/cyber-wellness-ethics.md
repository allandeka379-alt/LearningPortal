## Introduction

Rudo, a Form 1 learner in Masvingo, joins her class group chat on her phone. Within a week she has seen a funny video, a rumour about a classmate, a link offering "free airtime" and a message from a stranger who says he is also fifteen. Each of these needs a decision: share it, ignore it, report it or block it. **Cyber-wellness** is about making those decisions well, so that time online is safe, positive and respectful.

ZIMSEC Form 1 asks you to outline cyber culture and its impact, describe safe and unsafe websites and suggest the effects of online content and behaviour. Paper 2 questions often give a short scenario (a chat message, a suspicious website, a bullying incident) and ask you to identify the risk, explain its effect and recommend what the person should do.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Cyber-wellness"}
The positive well-being of a person who uses the internet: behaving responsibly and safely online, protecting oneself and respecting others.
:::

:::definition{term="Cyber culture"}
The shared habits, language, values and ways of behaving that develop among people who communicate and spend time online.
:::

:::definition{term="Cyber-bullying"}
Using digital technology (messages, social media, games, email) deliberately and repeatedly to threaten, embarrass, exclude or hurt another person.
:::

:::definition{term="Computer ethics"}
The moral principles that guide how people should behave when they use computers and the internet, for example honesty, respect for others' privacy and respect for others' work.
:::

:::definition{term="Digital footprint"}
The permanent trail of data a person leaves online, including posts, photos, comments, searches and the sites they visit.
:::

## Cyber culture and its impact

Cyber culture includes the use of short forms and emojis, sharing memes and videos, online gaming communities, "going viral", influencers and the habit of being always connected. Its impact can be positive or negative.

| Positive impact | Negative impact |
|:--|:--|
| Fast communication with family and friends anywhere | Addiction and too much screen time, affecting sleep and schoolwork |
| Learning resources, online classes and research | Spread of false information and rumours |
| Communities that share hobbies and support each other | Cyber-bullying and online harassment |
| Opportunities for online business and creativity | Loss of privacy; a permanent digital footprint |
| Preserving and sharing local languages and culture | Less face-to-face interaction; exposure to harmful content |

## Safe and unsafe websites

Before entering any personal details, check whether a website can be trusted.

**Characteristics of a safe website**

- The address begins with `https://` and the browser shows a padlock, so data sent is encrypted.
- The domain name is spelt correctly and belongs to the real organisation.
- It gives contact details, a physical address and a privacy policy.
- It is professionally written, without spelling mistakes, and is kept up to date.
- It does not demand unnecessary personal details or show endless pop-ups.

**Warning signs of an unsafe website**

- Only `http://`, or a browser warning that the certificate is not valid.
- A misspelt or strange address that imitates a real one (for example an extra letter in a bank's name).
- Offers that are too good to be true: free airtime, prizes you never entered for, cheap phones.
- Urgent demands ("your account will be closed in one hour").
- Pop-ups that download files automatically or ask you to install a "required" program.

:::example{title="Is this website safe?"}
Farai receives a link: `http://ecocash-free-bonus.win/claim`. The page asks for his phone number and PIN to "release a bonus of 500 dollars".

- It uses **http**, not https, so anything typed is sent unencrypted.
- The domain is not the official domain of the mobile money provider; it only uses the brand name to look genuine.
- The offer is too good to be true and **no genuine service ever asks for a PIN**.

Conclusion: the site is unsafe (a phishing site). Farai should close it, not enter any details, and report the message.
:::

## Handling online content and online relationships

**Online content.** Not everything online is true or suitable. A responsible user:

- checks information against reliable sources before believing or sharing it;
- does not forward rumours, private photos or hurtful content;
- avoids or reports violent, adult or extremist material;
- thinks before posting, because a post can be copied, screenshotted and kept forever.

**Online relationships.** People online may not be who they say they are. A responsible user:

- never shares a home address, school, passwords, PIN or private photos with someone met online;
- uses privacy settings so only trusted people see posts;
- never meets an online "friend" alone; tells a parent, guardian or teacher first;
- blocks and reports anyone who makes them uncomfortable.

## Cyber-bullying

Cyber-bullying includes sending threatening messages, spreading rumours, posting embarrassing photos, creating fake profiles of someone and deliberately excluding a person from a group chat. It is different from bullying in person because it can happen at any time of day, reach a large audience quickly and be anonymous.

**Effects on the victim:** fear, anxiety, depression, poor concentration and schoolwork, withdrawal from friends, and in serious cases self-harm.

**What to do:** do not reply or retaliate; keep evidence (screenshots); block the bully; report to the platform and tell a trusted adult or teacher. Bystanders should not share the content and should support the victim.

## Computer ethics and Unhu/Ubuntu/Vumunhu

Unhu/Ubuntu/Vumunhu is the value that "a person is a person through other people": treat others with respect, honesty, kindness and responsibility. These values apply online just as they do in the community.

| Unhu/Ubuntu value | What it means online |
|:--|:--|
| Respect | Polite language, no insults, respect other people's privacy and opinions |
| Honesty | No impersonation, no false information, no cheating or plagiarism |
| Responsibility | Think before posting; report harmful content; protect your own accounts |
| Care for others | Support victims of bullying; do not share embarrassing content |
| Integrity | Do not access other people's accounts or files without permission |

Unethical activities include hacking, spreading false information, using someone else's account, copying work, invading privacy and cyber-bullying.

:::example{title="Effects of unethical online activity"}
A learner creates a fake social media account using a teacher's name and posts insulting messages.

- **On the individual (the teacher):** damage to reputation, stress, possible loss of trust from parents.
- **On the learner:** suspension or expulsion; possible criminal charges under Zimbabwe's cyber laws; a permanent record.
- **On society:** loss of trust in online information; a culture in which people are afraid to use social media.

Under Unhu/Ubuntu values the learner has failed to show respect and honesty towards another member of the community.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking a padlock means the content is true"}
HTTPS only shows that data is **encrypted** in transit. A dishonest site can also have a padlock; check the domain, contact details and the offer too.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying deleted posts disappear"}
Once posted, content can be screenshotted, shared and archived. Deleting it does not remove every copy; it remains part of the digital footprint.
:::

:::mistake{title="Replying to the bully"}
The advice is: do not reply, keep evidence, block and report. Replying usually makes bullying worse.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "describe the characteristics of a safe website" give a **feature and why it matters**: "the address starts with https, so data entered is encrypted".
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked for **effects**, say who is affected (the individual, the family, the school, society) and give a specific consequence for each.
:::

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
Cyber-wellness and Unhu/Ubuntu/Vumunhu ethics are examined by ZIMSEC only. Cambridge 0478 covers related threats such as phishing under cyber security.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Cyber-wellness means being safe, responsible and respectful online.
- Cyber culture brings fast communication and learning, but also addiction, false information, bullying and loss of privacy.
- Safe sites use https, a correct domain, contact details and a privacy policy; unsafe sites have strange addresses, too-good-to-be-true offers and urgent demands.
- Cyber-bullying victims should not reply, keep evidence, block, report and tell a trusted adult.
- Unhu/Ubuntu values (respect, honesty, responsibility, care) guide ethical computer use; unethical activity harms individuals, offenders and society.
:::
