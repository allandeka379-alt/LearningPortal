## Introduction

Thandeka, a young musician in Bulawayo, records a song on her laptop and uploads it to a streaming site. Two weeks later she hears it in an advert for a phone shop that never asked her permission or paid her. Meanwhile, at a school in Mutare, a learner hands in a history project copied word for word from a website, and a small business installs one paid copy of an accounting package on all ten of its computers.

All three situations are about **using other people's work without permission**. ZIMSEC Form 1 asks you to explain the importance of copyright and describe the consequences of plagiarism and piracy. Paper 2 questions often present a scenario like these and ask you to name the offence, explain why it is wrong and suggest how it could be prevented.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Copyright"}
A legal right that gives the creator of an original work (writing, music, film, artwork, photographs, software) control over who may copy, distribute, perform or adapt it, for a limited period.
:::

:::definition{term="Plagiarism"}
Presenting another person's words, ideas or work as your own without acknowledging the source.
:::

:::definition{term="Software piracy"}
Copying, distributing, selling or using software without the permission (licence) of the copyright holder.
:::

:::definition{term="Software licence"}
A legal agreement that states how a piece of software may be used, for example how many computers it may be installed on and whether it may be copied or changed.
:::

## Copyright and why it matters

Copyright protects the **expression** of an idea (the actual text, recording, code or image), not the idea itself. It exists automatically as soon as an original work is created and fixed in some form; the creator does not have to register it. The symbol © with a name and year is often added as a reminder.

In Zimbabwe, copyright is governed by the **Copyright and Neighbouring Rights Act [Chapter 26:05]**. In outline, the Act:

- protects literary, musical and artistic works, sound recordings, broadcasts and computer programs;
- gives the owner the exclusive right to reproduce, publish, perform, broadcast and adapt the work;
- protects the rights of performers and producers ("neighbouring rights");
- makes copying or selling works without permission an offence, with fines or imprisonment;
- allows limited "fair dealing", such as quoting a short extract for study, criticism or news reporting, with acknowledgement.

**Why copyright is important**

- Creators **earn income** from their work, which rewards the time and money they invested.
- It **encourages creativity and innovation**, because people know their work cannot simply be taken.
- It protects the creator's **reputation**, since others cannot change the work and present it under the creator's name.
- It supports the **creative and software industries**, jobs and taxes in the economy.

## Plagiarism

Plagiarism includes copying text from a website or book into an assignment without quotation marks and a reference, paraphrasing someone's ideas without acknowledging them, submitting a friend's work, or buying an essay. It is easy online because text can be copied and pasted in seconds.

**Consequences of plagiarism**

- The work gets zero marks, or the candidate is disqualified from an examination.
- Disciplinary action: suspension or expulsion from school or university.
- Loss of reputation and trust; in professional life, loss of a job or legal action for copyright infringement.
- The learner does not actually learn the skills the task was set to develop.

**Avoiding plagiarism:** write in your own words, use quotation marks for exact words, and **acknowledge (reference)** every source: author, title, website address and date accessed.

**Anti-plagiarism software** compares a submitted document with a large database of web pages, books and earlier submissions. It highlights matching passages and gives a similarity percentage, so a teacher can see which parts were copied and from where.

## Software piracy

Examples of piracy include installing one licensed copy on many computers, sharing a product key, downloading "cracked" software, and selling copied discs or files.

**Consequences of piracy**

| Who is affected | Consequence |
|:--|:--|
| Software developers | Loss of income; less money for new versions and support; jobs lost |
| The user or business | Fines or prosecution; pirated copies often contain malware; no updates, security patches or technical support |
| The country | Lower tax revenue; investors avoid markets with high piracy; the local software industry is discouraged |

**How licences and technology help.** A licence states exactly what the buyer may do. Common types:

| Licence type | What it allows |
|:--|:--|
| Single-user licence | Installation on one computer (or for one user) only |
| Multi-user / site licence | Installation on a stated number of computers, or on every computer at one site, for a single fee |
| Freeware | Free to use, but the copyright holder keeps the copyright, so the code may not be changed |
| Shareware | Free to try for a limited period or with limited features; payment needed to keep using it |
| Open source | The source code is available and may be studied, changed and shared, under the licence's conditions |

Developers also use **product keys**, **online activation** (the key is checked against the developer's server) and **digital rights management (DRM)** to make illegal copying harder.

:::example{title="Naming the offence"}
1. A learner submits a project copied from a website: **plagiarism**. Consequence: zero marks and disciplinary action. Prevention: anti-plagiarism software, teaching referencing.
2. A shop in Harare sells copied films on flash drives: **copyright infringement (piracy)**. Consequence: the shop owner can be fined or jailed under the Copyright Act; film makers lose income.
3. A company installs a single-user copy of a design package on 15 computers: **software piracy**. Prevention: buy a site licence or 15 licences; use online activation.
:::

:::example{title="Choosing a licence for a school"}
Masvingo Hills High School (fictional) needs a word processor for its 40-computer laboratory. Buying 40 single-user licences is expensive and hard to manage. A **site licence** covers all 40 computers legally for one fee and makes updates easier. Alternatively, the school could use an **open-source** word processor, which is legal to install on every computer free of charge.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking copyright needs registration"}
Copyright exists automatically once an original work is created. Patents and trademarks are the rights that must be registered.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling freeware copyright-free"}
Freeware is free to use, but the creator still owns the copyright. You may not change it or sell it as your own.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing plagiarism and piracy"}
Plagiarism is passing off someone's work **as your own**. Piracy is **copying or using** protected material (often software) without permission, even if you do not claim you made it.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Explain the importance of copyright" needs reasons linked to the creator **and** to society: income, incentive to create, protection of reputation, growth of creative industries.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked how piracy can be prevented, give a technical measure (product key, online activation, DRM) **and** a legal or organisational one (licences, laws, education).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Copyright protects original works, including software; in Zimbabwe it is governed by the Copyright and Neighbouring Rights Act [Chapter 26:05].
- Copyright matters because it gives creators income, encourages creativity and protects reputation.
- Plagiarism is presenting another's work as your own; it leads to zero marks, disqualification and loss of trust. Acknowledge sources.
- Software piracy is copying or using software without a licence; it costs developers income and exposes users to fines and malware.
- Licences (single-user, site, freeware, shareware, open source), product keys, activation and anti-plagiarism software help protect work.
:::
