## Introduction

Thandeka's mother has opened a small bakery in Bulawayo. She wants a fast internet connection at the shop, an email address with the bakery's name, a website where customers can order cakes, and a page on social media. She cannot connect to the internet on her own: she needs an **internet service provider** (ISP). Once connected, she can use the internet's services to sell, learn and keep in touch with customers.

ZIMSEC asks you to use internet services such as email, e-commerce, e-learning and social media (Form 1), and to explain the services offered by internet service providers (Form 2). Paper 2 questions ask you to list ISP services, or describe benefits and risks of a service for a person or business.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Internet service provider (ISP)"}
A company that provides individuals and organisations with access to the internet, usually for a fee, together with related services such as email, web hosting and domain names.
:::

:::definition{term="Web hosting"}
Storing an organisation's website on a web server that is permanently connected to the internet, so that anyone can view it at any time.
:::

:::definition{term="Domain name"}
The human-readable name of a website, such as `bakery.co.zw`, which the domain name system translates into the IP address of the server.
:::

:::definition{term="E-commerce"}
Buying and selling goods and services over the internet, including online payment.
:::

## What does an ISP provide?

1. **Internet access**: the connection itself, by fibre, ADSL over a telephone line, fixed wireless (such as WiMAX or a microwave link), satellite, or mobile data. Packages differ in speed (bandwidth) and in how much data may be used.
2. **Equipment**: usually a router or modem to connect the customer's network to the ISP's network.
3. **An IP address**: the ISP gives the customer's router a public IP address, usually dynamic; a static address costs extra and suits a business running its own server.
4. **Email accounts**: mailboxes on the ISP's mail servers, often with the customer's own domain name.
5. **Web hosting**: space on the ISP's web servers for the customer's website.
6. **Domain name registration**: registering a name such as `bakery.co.zw` on the customer's behalf.
7. **Security services**: spam filtering, virus scanning of email, firewalls and, for families or schools, filtering of unsuitable content.
8. **Technical support**: help when the connection fails, often by phone or online chat.

In Zimbabwe, ISPs must be licensed by **POTRAZ**, the Postal and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority, which regulates telecommunications.

:::example
**Choosing an ISP package.** The bakery compares two packages.

- Package 1: a cheaper mobile data bundle with a limited amount of data each month.
- Package 2: a fibre connection with a higher bandwidth, a business email account, hosting for a small website and help registering the bakery's domain name.

The bakery takes online orders, so it needs a reliable connection and a website that is always available. Package 2 suits it better: fibre is faster and more reliable, and hosting and email with its own domain name make the business look professional. Package 1 would be enough for a household that only browses and uses social media.
:::

## Internet services: benefits and risks

### Email

Messages, with attachments, sent to one or many people almost instantly, at very low cost.

- Benefits: fast, cheap, a written record, can be sent to many people at once, readable anywhere.
- Risks: spam, **phishing** emails that try to steal passwords, attachments carrying malware, messages sent to the wrong person.

### E-commerce

Online shops, mobile money payments, online banking, and buying airtime or electricity tokens.

- Benefits for customers: shop at any time from anywhere, compare prices easily, goods delivered.
- Benefits for businesses: reach customers across the country and abroad, lower costs than a physical shop, open 24 hours.
- Risks: online fraud and fake shops, card or account details stolen, goods not delivered or not as described, the business must pay for delivery and a secure website.

### E-learning

Online courses, video lessons, virtual classrooms and learning platforms.

- Benefits: learn at your own pace and at any time, reach learners in remote areas, revisit lessons, materials updated easily.
- Risks and drawbacks: needs a device and affordable data, less face-to-face contact with teachers, self-discipline required, distractions online.

### Social media

Sharing posts, photos and videos with friends or followers; also used by businesses to advertise.

- Benefits: keep in touch with family far away, share news quickly, cheap advertising for small businesses, support groups and communities.
- Risks: cyberbullying, fake news, oversharing personal information, contact with strangers, addiction and wasted time, damage to reputation from careless posts.

:::example
**A balanced judgement.** Tatenda, a Form 2 learner in Masvingo, uses an e-learning platform for mathematics. He can replay a video on simultaneous equations as often as he needs, at night when the house is quiet, which a classroom lesson does not allow. On the other hand, he cannot ask the teacher a quick question, and when his data runs out his studying stops. The best use is a mix: e-learning for revision and practice, with class time for questions.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the ISP with the internet"}
The ISP **connects** you to the internet and offers services; it does not own the internet, which is a worldwide network of networks run by many organisations.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing benefits only"}
Questions on internet services almost always want both sides. Give at least one benefit and one risk, each tied to the person or business in the question.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying web hosting means designing the website"}
Hosting is **storing** the site on a server connected to the internet. Designing the pages is a separate job.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "explain the services offered by an ISP", name each service and say what it does for the customer: "web hosting: stores the bakery's website on a server that is always online, so customers can order at any time".
:::

:::exam-tip
In "discuss" questions, structure your answer as benefit, risk, then a short conclusion for the person in the scenario.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- An ISP provides internet access plus equipment, IP addresses, email, web hosting, domain names, security services and support; ISPs in Zimbabwe are licensed by POTRAZ.
- Email: fast and cheap, but spam, phishing and malware attachments are risks.
- E-commerce: shopping and selling at any time, but fraud and stolen payment details are risks.
- E-learning: flexible study anywhere, but needs a device and data and gives less face-to-face support.
- Social media: keeps people connected and helps businesses advertise, but brings cyberbullying, fake news and privacy risks.
:::
