## Introduction

On a Friday evening in Bulawayo, a youth theatre group performs a play about a family torn apart by migration. The audience laughs, then goes quiet, then argues about the ending on the way home. The next morning, a girls' netball team in Masvingo travels to a provincial tournament, and in Murehwa a group of dancers rehearses *Jerusarema/Mbende* for a festival. On Sunday, sculptors at a craft market in Victoria Falls sell stone carvings to tourists.

All of these are part of **sport, arts and culture**. They entertain, but they also employ thousands of people, carry heritage from one generation to the next and give Zimbabweans a shared sense of pride. In this lesson you study dance and drama as entertainment, leisure and employment, meet some of the country's well-known traditional dances, and explain why sport, arts and culture matter. You should already know about indigenous entertainment, studied earlier.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Dance"}
Rhythmic movement of the body, usually to music, performed for enjoyment, ceremony, communication or performance.
:::

:::definition{term="Drama"}
The acting out of a story by performers for an audience, on stage, in the open air, on radio, television or film.
:::

:::definition{term="Leisure"}
Free time spent on activities a person enjoys, such as sport, reading, music or watching performances.
:::

:::definition{term="The arts"}
Creative activities such as music, dance, drama, visual art (painting, sculpture), crafts, film and literature.
:::

:::definition{term="Intangible cultural heritage"}
Living traditions such as dances, songs, rituals, skills and knowledge that a community passes on, as opposed to buildings and objects.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Jerusarema/Mbende"}
The *Jerusarema/Mbende* dance of the Murehwa and Mutoko areas was recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage: it was proclaimed in 2005 and later placed on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
:::

## Traditional dances (in outline)

Every community in Zimbabwe has its own dances, performed at ceremonies, celebrations and, today, at festivals and competitions.

| Dance | Community and area | Features (in outline) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Jerusarema/Mbende* | Zezuru Shona of Murehwa and Mutoko | Fast, energetic movements by men and women to drums, hand clapping and whistles; once linked to fertility and courtship; recognised by UNESCO |
| *Isitshikitsha* | Ndebele | A dance of the royal court and of ceremonies, with stamping and singing in unison |
| *Muchongoyo* | Ndau and Shangani of the south-east (Chipinge, Chimanimani) | Men dance with powerful stamping steps while women sing and drum |
| *Mhande* | Karanga of Masvingo Province | Associated with rain-making ceremonies and other rituals |

Learn the name, the community and one feature of each. Do not claim more detail than you are sure of, and remember that each community has many other dances.

## Dance and drama as entertainment, leisure and employment

### Entertainment and leisure

Dances at weddings, harvest festivals and independence celebrations bring people together and give joy. Drama entertains audiences in theatres, schools, churches and community halls and on radio and television. For performers and audiences alike, dance and drama are a healthy and creative use of leisure time.

### Drama as education

Drama can carry messages. Theatre groups and school clubs perform plays about health, child marriage, gender-based violence, the environment and good citizenship. An audience that sees a problem acted out is more likely to discuss it. This is sometimes called **drama for development**.

### Employment

Sport, arts and culture create jobs and incomes:

- **Performers**: dancers, actors, musicians, comedians.
- **Creative workers**: sculptors, painters, basket-makers, potters, writers, film-makers, designers.
- **Sport**: professional players, coaches, referees, groundsmen, physiotherapists, sports journalists.
- **Support jobs**: event organisers, sound and lighting technicians, ticket sellers, transport and catering at events.

Zimbabwean stone sculpture is known worldwide, and baskets woven by Tonga women in Binga, among other crafts, are sold to tourists and exported.

:::example{title="A dance group becomes a business"}
A youth dance group in Chipinge performs *Muchongoyo* at school competitions.

1. A lodge invites the group to perform for tourists every weekend and pays a fee.
2. The group registers as a cooperative, opens a bank account and buys drums and costumes.
3. Members earn income, younger dancers learn from older ones, and visitors learn about the culture of the south-east.

Dance here is entertainment, **employment**, **heritage** and **tourism** at the same time.
:::

## The importance of sport, arts and culture

| Importance | Explanation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entertainment and relaxation | Relieves stress and brings enjoyment | Watching a league football match or a play |
| Socialisation | Teaches discipline, teamwork, fair play and respect for rules | A school netball team learning to win and lose well |
| Identity | Carries a community's language, history and values | Performing *Isitshikitsha* or *Mhande* at a festival |
| Unity | Brings together people of different groups, regions and faiths | Supporters of all backgrounds cheering a national team |
| Health | Exercise improves fitness and reduces some health risks | Running, dancing, football |
| Employment and income | Creates jobs for performers, artists and support workers | Sculptors selling at a craft market |
| Tourism | Attracts visitors and foreign currency | Dance performances and curio markets at Victoria Falls |
| National pride and image | Success abroad presents the country positively | A national team qualifying for an international tournament |
| Education and awareness | Spreads messages about health and social issues | A play about the dangers of child marriage |

Government and other bodies support these sectors. For example, the **National Arts Council of Zimbabwe** promotes and coordinates the arts, and the **Sports and Recreation Commission** oversees sport. Festivals, school competitions and community clubs give young people a chance to take part.

:::example{title="Answering an 'explain the importance' question"}
Question: *Explain two ways in which sport promotes national unity.*

1. **Point:** supporting a national team. **Explanation:** fans from every province and background cheer the same team, so they share a common identity as Zimbabweans.
2. **Point:** school and club competitions across provinces. **Explanation:** young people travel, meet and make friends with people from other regions and language groups.

Each point is named and then developed with *so* or *because*.
:::

## Challenges

Many artists and athletes struggle with low and irregular pay, a lack of facilities and equipment, the cost of travel, piracy of music and films, and the loss of talented people to other countries. Traditional dances face competition from modern music and media. These challenges help explain why the sectors have not created as many jobs as they could.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the dances and their communities"}
Link each dance to its community: *Jerusarema/Mbende* (Murehwa and Mutoko), *Isitshikitsha* (Ndebele), *Muchongoyo* (Ndau and Shangani, south-east), *Mhande* (Karanga, Masvingo).
:::

:::mistake{title="Seeing the arts only as entertainment"}
Questions on importance want employment, identity, unity, tourism, education and national pride as well.
:::

:::mistake{title="Naming without explaining"}
"Tourism" earns little on its own. Say how: performances and crafts attract visitors who spend money, creating income and jobs.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Dance and drama provide entertainment, leisure, education and employment.
- Traditional dances include *Jerusarema/Mbende* (recognised by UNESCO), *Isitshikitsha*, *Muchongoyo* and *Mhande*.
- Sport, arts and culture create jobs for performers, artists, athletes and support workers.
- They promote socialisation, identity, unity, health, tourism and national pride.
- Low pay, poor facilities, piracy and migration of talent are challenges.
:::
