## Introduction

At a funeral in a village near Masvingo, nobody sends out invitations. Neighbours arrive with mealie-meal, firewood and buckets of water. Young men dig the grave, women cook for the mourners, and elders sit with the bereaved family through the night. When the family thanks them, an old man answers that this is simply what people do for one another.

That answer sums up **Unhu** (Shona), **Ubuntu** (Ndebele) and **Vumunhu**, the same idea named in other Zimbabwean languages. It is the belief that a person becomes fully human through good relationships with other people. The Heritage Studies syllabus places it at the centre of the whole course: the norms, values, ceremonies and responsibilities you study later are all ways of living out Unhu.

You already know the idea from home. Greeting elders properly, sharing food with a visitor and helping a classmate who is struggling are all Unhu in action. This lesson gives you the words to describe it, explain it and judge its importance in an examination.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Unhu / Ubuntu / Vumunhu"}
A philosophy and way of life in which a person's humanity is shown and completed through respectful, caring and responsible relationships with other people. The names differ by language; the idea is shared.
:::

:::definition{term="Attribute"}
A quality or characteristic. The attributes of Unhu are the qualities a person with Unhu shows, such as respect, honesty and compassion.
:::

:::definition{term="Communalism"}
A way of life that puts the good of the family and community alongside, and often ahead of, the wishes of the individual.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Two proverbs that carry the idea"}
In Shona, *munhu munhu nekuda kwevanhu*; in Ndebele, *umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu*. Both mean that a person is a person because of other people.
:::

## The attributes of Unhu

The syllabus lists a set of attributes. Learn them with an example of each, because examiners reward an attribute that is explained, not just named.

| Attribute | What it means | Everyday example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Respect | Treating others, especially elders, as valuable | Greeting elders first, kneeling or clapping when receiving something |
| Integrity | Being the same person in private as in public | Returning extra change a shopkeeper gave by mistake |
| Honesty | Telling the truth and not cheating | Owning up to breaking a neighbour's window |
| Humility | Not boasting or looking down on others | A top pupil helping slower classmates without mocking them |
| Tolerance | Accepting people whose views, faith or background differ | Welcoming a new pupil who speaks another language |
| Oneness (unity) | Seeing yourself as part of a group | A village repairing a borehole together |
| Compassion | Feeling for those who suffer and acting to help | Visiting and helping a sick neighbour |
| Hospitality | Welcoming visitors and strangers | Offering water and food to a traveller |
| Sharing | Letting others benefit from what you have | Sharing a harvest with a widow's household |

Many of these attributes overlap. Hospitality is a form of sharing; tolerance grows out of respect. In an answer, choose attributes that fit the question and explain each one with an example.

## Showing Unhu, and lacking it

Unhu is judged by behaviour. A person who greets, helps, shares and tells the truth is said to *have* Unhu; a person who is rude, greedy, dishonest or cruel is said to *lack* it. In Shona people say of such a person *haana hunhu*, "he or she has no Unhu".

:::example{title="Two pupils, one bus fare"}
Nyasha and Tatenda find a purse with US\$15 and a bus ticket on the road outside their school in Gweru.

1. Tatenda suggests keeping the money: nobody saw them.
2. Nyasha points out that the owner may be a parent who needs the ticket to travel home, and that their families would be ashamed if they kept it.
3. They hand the purse to the head teacher, who finds the owner, an elderly woman, that afternoon.

Nyasha shows **honesty** and **integrity** (doing right when nobody is watching) and **compassion** (thinking of the owner's need). Her reasoning also shows the communal side of Unhu: her actions reflect on her family, not just on herself.
:::

:::example{title="A wedding in Bulawayo"}
Sipho's family is hosting a wedding. Cousins arrive from Gwanda and Lupane, and a neighbour who is not related brings chairs and helps cook.

1. Sipho's mother makes sure every guest is fed, including people she does not know: **hospitality**.
2. Elders are served first and greeted by name: **respect**.
3. The neighbour's help is repaid when she hosts her own ceremony: **sharing** and **oneness**, which build lasting ties between households.

The event is a family celebration, but it strengthens the whole community.
:::

## Why Unhu matters

Examiners often ask you to *explain the importance* of Unhu. Organise your answer by level.

### For the individual

Unhu shapes good character. A young person who is honest and respectful earns trust, finds it easier to get help, work and friends, and is less likely to fall into crime or harmful behaviour.

### For the family

Respect between generations keeps families together. Children who respect parents listen to advice; parents who show compassion raise confident children. Disputes are settled through discussion rather than violence.

### For the community

Sharing and oneness are a safety net. In a drought, a funeral or an illness, a community with Unhu supports the household in need. Communal work such as the work party (*nhimbe/ilima*) depends on it.

### For the nation

Tolerance and respect make it possible for people of different languages, faiths, regions and political views to live peacefully together. Honesty and integrity in public life reduce corruption. Unhu is therefore linked to national unity, good governance and development.

:::keyfact{title="Unhu in national life"}
One of the stated aims of the Heritage Studies syllabus is to instil and sustain Unhu/Ubuntu/Vumunhu, and the value runs through the whole course, from the family to citizenship.
:::

### Unhu today

Some people argue that Unhu is weakening because of urban life, individualism and the media; others point out that Unhu still shows in burial societies, church groups, school clubs and online fundraising for sick neighbours. Both views can be supported, and a strong answer gives both before reaching a judgement.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing attributes without explaining them"}
Writing "respect, honesty, humility" earns little. Say what the attribute means and give an example of it in action.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating Unhu as belonging to one group"}
Unhu, Ubuntu and Vumunhu are names in different languages for a value shared across Zimbabwe's communities. Do not describe it as the custom of one ethnic group.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing Unhu with obedience"}
Unhu is not simply doing what elders say. It includes honesty, compassion and justice, so a person with Unhu will politely refuse to do something wrong.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying Unhu is only for the past"}
Unhu applies in town and in the village, at school, at work and online. Give modern examples as well as traditional ones.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**Define** (Paper 2 Section A): give the "being human through others" idea and mention that it is shown through relationships. One accurate sentence earns the mark.
:::

:::exam-tip
**State** or **identify** attributes: one word each is enough (respect, honesty, humility, tolerance, compassion, hospitality). **Describe** or **explain**: say what the attribute looks like and why it matters, with an example.
:::

:::exam-tip
In a **discuss** or **how far** question (Paper 2 Section B), such as whether Unhu is still alive today, give evidence on both sides and finish with a judgement that answers the question.
:::

:::exam-tip
Paper 1 multiple-choice items often describe a person's behaviour and ask which attribute it shows. Read the scenario carefully: helping a stranger is compassion or hospitality, not integrity.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Unhu (Shona), Ubuntu (Ndebele) and Vumunhu name one idea: a person becomes fully human through relationships with others.
- Its attributes include respect, integrity, honesty, humility, tolerance, oneness, compassion, hospitality and sharing.
- Unhu is judged by behaviour: greeting, helping, sharing and telling the truth show it; greed, rudeness and dishonesty show its absence.
- It matters for the individual (character), the family (harmony), the community (mutual support) and the nation (unity, tolerance and honest public life).
- Unhu applies today at home, school, work and online, though people debate whether it is weakening.
:::
