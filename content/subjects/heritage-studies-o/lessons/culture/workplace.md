## Introduction

Tendai has just started work as a clerk at a fictional seed company in Chinhoyi. On his first morning his supervisor explains the rules: work starts at 07:30, the reception desk is never left empty, customer records are not discussed outside the office, and protective boots are worn in the warehouse. None of this was in his school timetable, yet much of it feels familiar.

The norms and values you learnt at home and at school (respect, honesty, punctuality, hard work) follow you into the world of work. At the workplace they take particular forms and carry higher stakes: a dishonest employee can cost a company money, a careless one can cause an accident, and a corrupt official can deny a whole community its services. This lesson looks at those workplace norms and values, why they matter, what threatens them and how they can be upheld.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Workplace"}
Any place where people do paid or organised work: an office, factory, farm, mine, shop, school, clinic or market stall.
:::

:::definition{term="Work ethic"}
A set of values centred on the importance of work: diligence, reliability, punctuality and pride in doing a job well.
:::

:::definition{term="Accountability"}
Being answerable for your decisions and actions, and accepting responsibility when things go wrong.
:::

:::definition{term="Confidentiality"}
Keeping private information (about customers, patients, pupils or the organisation) secret and using it only for the purpose of the job.
:::

:::definition{term="Corruption"}
The abuse of entrusted power or position for private gain, for example demanding a bribe for a service that should be free.
:::

:::keyfact{title="A constitutional body"}
The Zimbabwe Anti-Corruption Commission is one of the independent commissions set up by the 2013 Constitution. Its work includes investigating and exposing corruption in both public and private organisations.
:::

## Norms and values at work

| Norm or value | What it looks like | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Punctuality | Arriving on time, meeting deadlines | Customers are served and work flows on schedule |
| Responsibility | Doing your tasks fully without being chased | Colleagues can rely on you |
| Accountability | Owning up to errors, reporting honestly | Problems are fixed early; trust grows |
| Integrity and honesty | No theft, no false claims, no bribes | Protects the organisation's money and reputation |
| Respect | Polite treatment of colleagues and customers, whatever their rank, sex or background | A fair, peaceful workplace |
| Confidentiality | Not sharing patients' or customers' details | Protects people's privacy and the law |
| Teamwork | Sharing tasks and information | More gets done; skills are shared |
| Dress code | Uniform or neat dress, protective clothing where needed | Professional image; safety |
| Safety | Following rules on machines, chemicals and protective clothing | Prevents injury and death |

Many of these are the attributes of Unhu applied to work. Integrity and honesty are the same values you learnt at home; teamwork is oneness at work; respect for customers is a form of hospitality.

:::example{title="Confidentiality at a clinic"}
Nyasha is a receptionist at a clinic in Marondera. A neighbour asks her why a mutual friend visited the clinic.

1. **Norm:** patient information is confidential.
2. **Value behind it:** respect for privacy and trust.
3. **Nyasha's response:** she politely says she cannot discuss any patient.
4. **Why it matters:** if patients feared their information would be shared, some would stay away from the clinic, and their health would suffer.
:::

## Threats to workplace norms and values

| Threat | Example | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corruption and bribery | An official demands money to process a licence | Services go to those who pay, not those entitled; public trust collapses |
| Absenteeism and late-coming | Workers often absent without reason | Work piles up, customers are turned away |
| Theft and fraud | Stealing stock, false expense claims | Losses, higher prices, job cuts |
| Harassment, including sexual harassment | Unwanted comments or demands, bullying by a supervisor | Fear, poor performance, people leaving jobs; it is unlawful |
| Poor work ethic | Doing the minimum, using work time for private business | Low productivity, poor service |
| Nepotism and favouritism | Jobs given to relatives rather than the best candidate | Unfairness; less competent staff |
| Substance abuse | Arriving at work drunk | Accidents, poor decisions |

Some threats come from outside the worker: very low pay, delayed salaries or poor working conditions can push people into moonlighting (doing a second job) or petty corruption. Recognising these causes does not excuse wrongdoing, but it helps in finding solutions.

## Upholding workplace norms and values

- **Clear codes of conduct** and induction for new workers, so everyone knows the rules.
- **Leadership by example:** managers who are punctual and honest set the standard.
- **Fair rewards:** recognition, promotion on merit and fair pay reduce temptation.
- **Accountability systems:** audits, receipts, clocking-in, and safe ways to report wrongdoing (whistle-blowing).
- **Discipline procedures** that are fair and consistent, through workers' committees and the law.
- **Training** in safety, customer care and ethics.
- **Anti-corruption bodies and the courts**, which investigate and punish serious cases.

:::example{title="A council office tackles late-coming"}
A fictional town council in Gweru finds that its rates office opens late most mornings, angering residents.

1. **Problem:** a threat to punctuality and responsibility.
2. **Causes found:** some staff travel far; supervisors themselves arrive late.
3. **Actions:** supervisors start arriving first; a sign-in register is introduced; staff with transport problems are moved to a later shift with the office still opening on time.
4. **Result:** the office opens at 08:00 each day, residents' complaints fall, and staff who are always on time are recognised at the end of the month.

The council combined example, accountability and fair treatment rather than relying only on punishment.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing norms without saying why they matter"}
"Punctuality, teamwork, honesty" scores only identification marks. Explain each: punctuality ensures customers are served on time.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating harassment as a minor matter"}
Harassment, including sexual harassment, is a serious threat to workplace values and is unlawful. Describe it factually and name ways it is reported and dealt with.
:::

:::mistake{title="Blaming only individual workers"}
Strong answers recognise that poor pay, weak management and lack of accountability can also undermine workplace norms.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**Identify** workplace norms (Paper 2 Section A): one or two words each are enough: punctuality, confidentiality, teamwork.
:::

:::exam-tip
**Analyse threats**: name the threat, give an example and state its effect on the organisation or the public. The three-column pattern of the threats table is a good model.
:::

:::exam-tip
**Suggest ways of upholding** norms: give practical measures (a code of conduct, audits, rewards, training) and explain how each works.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Workplace norms and values include punctuality, responsibility, accountability, integrity, respect, confidentiality, teamwork, dress codes and safety.
- They protect customers, the public, the organisation and the workers themselves.
- Threats include corruption, absenteeism, theft, harassment, poor work ethic, nepotism and substance abuse; poor pay and weak management can add to them.
- They are upheld by codes of conduct, leadership by example, fair rewards, accountability systems, fair discipline and training, backed by the law.
- Workplace values are the values of Unhu applied to working life.
:::
