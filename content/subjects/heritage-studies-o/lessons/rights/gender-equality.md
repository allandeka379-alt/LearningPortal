## Introduction

At a prize-giving day in Masvingo, the top science prize goes to Chipo and the best cook in the home economics class is Tatenda. A few parents look surprised. Their surprise shows how strongly people still expect boys and girls to be good at different things. Yet the school has simply rewarded the best pupils.

**Gender equality** is about making sure that being a man or a woman, a boy or a girl, does not limit a person's rights, opportunities or dignity. It is written into the Constitution of Zimbabwe and into international agreements the country has signed, but achieving it in daily life is an ongoing task. In this lesson you learn the key terms, the main laws and conventions, and the progress and barriers. You should already know about the roles of men and women in marriage and about vulnerable groups. Gender-based violence is studied in the next lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Sex"}
The biological differences between males and females, which a person is born with.
:::

:::definition{term="Gender"}
The roles, behaviours and expectations that a society considers suitable for men and women. Gender is learned through socialisation and can change over time.
:::

:::definition{term="Gender roles"}
The tasks and responsibilities a society expects of men and of women, such as "men herd cattle" or "women cook".
:::

:::definition{term="Gender stereotype"}
A fixed, over-simplified belief about what men or women are like or can do, for example "girls are not good at mathematics".
:::

:::definition{term="Gender discrimination"}
Treating a person unfairly because of their sex or gender, such as paying a woman less for the same work.
:::

:::definition{term="Gender equality"}
Women and men have the same rights, opportunities and dignity, and are treated equally by the law and society.
:::

:::definition{term="Gender equity"}
Fairness in the way women and men are treated, which may mean giving extra support to the disadvantaged group so that both can reach equal outcomes.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Equity leads to equality"}
Equality is the goal (the same rights and opportunities). Equity is the means (fair measures, sometimes special support, to reach that goal). Reserving seats for women in Parliament is a measure of equity aimed at equality of representation.
:::

## Sex and gender compared

| Sex | Gender |
| --- | --- |
| Biological | Social and cultural |
| Present at birth | Learned through socialisation |
| Largely the same in every society | Differs between societies and over time |
| Example: only women can give birth | Example: the belief that only women should cook |

## Gender equality in the Constitution

The Constitution of Zimbabwe (2013) contains several provisions on gender:

| Provision | What it says (paraphrased) |
| --- | --- |
| Founding values (section 3) | Gender equality is among the values and principles on which Zimbabwe is founded |
| Section 17 (national objective: gender balance) | The state must promote full gender balance, work for equal representation of women and men in government institutions at every level, and promote women's access to resources, including land, on an equal basis with men |
| Section 56 (equality and non-discrimination) | Everyone is equal before the law; women and men have the right to equal treatment and equal opportunities in political, economic, cultural and social life; no one may be unfairly discriminated against on grounds such as sex or gender |
| Section 80 (rights of women) | Women have full and equal dignity with men; laws, customs and practices that infringe women's rights are void to that extent |
| Reserved seats | Seats in the National Assembly are reserved for women, elected by proportional representation |
| Zimbabwe Gender Commission (Chapter 12) | An independent commission that monitors gender equality, investigates complaints of gender discrimination and recommends action |

The **Legal Age of Majority Act (1982)** made women legal adults at 18, and the **Domestic Violence Act (2007)** protects people, most often women and children, from abuse at home.

## International conventions (in outline)

| Convention | Year | Main purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW) | 1979 | Requires states to end discrimination against women in law, politics, education, work, health and family life |
| Maputo Protocol (Protocol to the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights on the Rights of Women in Africa) | 2003 | Protects African women's rights, including to dignity, education, health, property and protection from harmful practices |
| SADC Protocol on Gender and Development | 2008 | Commits SADC member states to gender equality in governance, education, the economy and protection from gender-based violence |

Countries that join these agreements promise to change their laws and policies and report on progress.

:::example{title="Equity or equality?"}
A fictional agricultural college in Chinhoyi notices that few girls apply for its engineering course.

1. It keeps the same entry standards for everyone: **equality** of rules.
2. It offers a short bridging course in technical drawing to girls who never had the subject at school, and runs open days for girls: **equity** measures.
3. Within a few years the course has many more female students who meet the same standards.

Equity measures removed a barrier so that equality of opportunity became real.
:::

## Progress towards gender equality

- **Education**: girls and boys attend primary and secondary school in large numbers, and more women qualify as doctors, engineers, lawyers and teachers.
- **Law**: women are legal adults, can own property and land, inherit and sign contracts, and are protected by laws against domestic violence and child marriage.
- **Representation**: reserved seats and other measures have increased the number of women in Parliament and in public bodies.
- **Work and business**: many women run businesses, lead organisations and work in all sectors.
- **Awareness**: schools, the media, churches and organisations teach about gender equality.

## Barriers to gender equality

- **Cultural attitudes and stereotypes**: beliefs that men should lead and women should obey, or that some jobs are only for men.
- **Gender-based violence**, which harms women's health, confidence and participation.
- **Poverty**: poor families may keep girls at home to work, or marry them off early, which is illegal.
- **Unpaid care work**: women carry most of the cooking, caring and water-fetching, leaving less time for paid work, study or leadership.
- **Unequal access** to land, credit and senior positions in practice, even where the law is equal.
- **Weak enforcement** of good laws, and lack of awareness of rights.

Views differ on how much progress has been made. Some people point to the strong laws and the many women in professions; others point out that attitudes and daily practice lag behind the law. Equality also concerns men and boys: for example, stereotypes can discourage boys from caring roles or from seeking help.

:::example{title="Spotting a stereotype"}
A careers poster at a fictional school shows a boy as a pilot and a girl as a nurse.

1. **Stereotype:** it suggests that flying is for men and nursing for women.
2. **Effect:** pupils may not consider careers they would be good at.
3. **Better practice:** show both boys and girls in a range of careers.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing equity and equality"}
Equality is the same rights and opportunities. Equity is fair treatment, sometimes with extra support, to reach equality.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing sex and gender"}
Sex is biological; gender is social and learned. "Women cook" is a gender role, not a fact of sex.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating gender equality as only about women"}
It concerns everyone. Stereotypes can limit boys and men too.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Sex is biological; gender is social and learned; stereotypes and discrimination limit people because of their sex.
- Gender equality is equal rights and opportunities; gender equity is fair treatment, sometimes with extra support, to reach equality.
- The Constitution promotes gender balance (section 17), equality and non-discrimination (section 56) and women's rights (section 80), and set up the Zimbabwe Gender Commission.
- CEDAW (1979), the Maputo Protocol (2003) and the SADC Protocol on Gender and Development (2008) commit Zimbabwe to gender equality.
- There has been progress in education, law and representation, but attitudes, violence, poverty and unpaid care work remain barriers.
:::
