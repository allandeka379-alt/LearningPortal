## Introduction

One Saturday in a suburb of Chinhoyi, a burst water pipe floods the road. Some residents walk past and complain. Others phone the council, put stones around the hole so that children do not fall in, and organise a group to clear the drain. A month later, the same residents attend a ward meeting to ask the council about the budget for road repairs.

The second group is practising **active citizenship**: they accept that a country belongs to its people and that rights come with responsibilities. Being a citizen is not only about having a passport or an identity card; it is about what you do for your community and nation. In this lesson you learn the roles and responsibilities of citizens, how they apply at community and national level, and why they matter. You should already know what citizenship is and the difference between rights and responsibilities.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Citizen"}
A person who is legally a member of a country, with the rights and duties that membership brings. In Zimbabwe citizenship is acquired by birth, descent or registration.
:::

:::definition{term="Civic responsibility"}
A duty that a citizen owes to the community and the nation, such as obeying the law, paying taxes, voting and protecting public property.
:::

:::definition{term="Active citizenship"}
Taking part willingly in the life of the community and nation: voting, volunteering, attending meetings, raising concerns through proper channels and helping others.
:::

:::definition{term="Sovereignty"}
A country's independence and its right to govern itself without control by another country.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Duties in the Constitution"}
The citizenship chapter of the Constitution (Chapter 3) lists duties of every citizen, including loyalty to Zimbabwe, observing the Constitution and respecting its ideals and institutions, respecting the national flag and anthem, and defending Zimbabwe and its sovereignty to the best of one's ability.
:::

## Roles and responsibilities of citizens

| Responsibility | What it involves | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defending sovereignty | Being loyal to the country; supporting the defence forces; never helping anyone to harm the nation | Protects independence and peace |
| Upholding national values and the Constitution | Respecting the founding values (such as equality, the rule of law and human dignity), the national flag and anthem | Keeps the nation united and governed by agreed rules |
| Obeying the law | Following laws and by-laws, respecting the police and courts, reporting crime | Keeps order and protects everyone's rights |
| Paying taxes | Paying income tax, VAT, rates and fees | Funds schools, hospitals, roads and public services |
| Voting | Registering and voting in elections from age 18 | Lets citizens choose leaders and hold them accountable |
| Protecting heritage | Respecting monuments, shrines and cultural sites; passing on language and customs | Preserves national identity for future generations |
| Protecting the environment | Not littering, not starting veld fires, conserving water, planting trees | Keeps the country healthy and productive |
| Respecting others' rights | Tolerating different views, faiths and groups; avoiding violence | Builds peace and unity |

## Civic responsibilities at community and national level

| Area | At community level | At national level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Security | Neighbourhood watch; reporting crime; keeping children safe | Loyalty; supporting the police and defence forces; avoiding acts that endanger peace |
| Education | Supporting the local school through school development committees, building classrooms, encouraging children to attend | Paying taxes that fund education; valuing learning |
| Health | Keeping homes and water sources clean; supporting immunisation campaigns; caring for the sick | Supporting public health campaigns; following health rules in emergencies |
| Shelter | Helping to rebuild a neighbour's house after a storm; following building by-laws | Supporting housing programmes; paying rates and rents |
| Community projects | Joining clean-ups, repairing roads and boreholes, community gardens | Taking part in national days of service, disaster relief and tree planting |

:::example{title="A ward meeting in Marondera"}
Residents learn that the council plans to raise rates.

1. Chipo, Tafadzwa and others **attend the ward meeting**: active participation.
2. They **ask questions** about how the money will be used and request a public report: holding leaders accountable.
3. They agree to **pay their rates** once the plan is explained, and form a committee to monitor road repairs.
4. They raise complaints peacefully through the councillor, not by vandalising council property.

The residents combine rights (to information and to be heard) with responsibilities (paying rates, taking part peacefully).
:::

## Rights and responsibilities go together

The Declaration of Rights (Chapter 4 of the Constitution) gives citizens rights such as freedom of expression, freedom of assembly, the right to vote and the right to education. Each right is easier to enjoy when citizens also carry out matching responsibilities.

- The **right to vote** is matched by the responsibility to register, vote and accept a fair result peacefully.
- **Freedom of expression** is matched by the responsibility not to spread hate, lies or insults that harm others.
- The **right to a clean and safe environment** is matched by the responsibility not to litter, pollute or start veld fires.
- The **right to education** is matched by the responsibility of parents to send children to school and of pupils to learn.

Section 86 of the Constitution also allows some rights to be limited by law, for example to protect public safety or the rights of others. This shows that rights are exercised within a community, not in isolation.

## Why active citizenship matters

- **Development**: taxes, community projects and volunteers build schools, clinics and roads faster than government alone could.
- **Democracy and accountability**: citizens who vote and attend meetings choose leaders and check how public money is spent.
- **Peace and unity**: tolerance, respect for the law and peaceful participation reduce conflict.
- **Protection of rights**: when citizens respect others' rights, everyone's rights are safer.
- **Heritage and environment**: active citizens look after what will be passed on to the next generation.

## Why some citizens do not meet their responsibilities

Some people do not vote, avoid taxes, litter, vandalise public property or ignore community meetings. Reasons can include lack of awareness, poverty, distrust of institutions, fear or apathy (lack of interest), and migration away from home areas. Some argue that citizens must do their part regardless; others point out that institutions must also earn trust by being fair and accountable. A good answer considers both.

:::example{title="Reading a scenario"}
In a fictional village near Masvingo, residents stop contributing to the borehole maintenance fund because they say the committee never reports how the money is spent. The pump breaks.

1. **Responsibility not met:** contributing to a community project.
2. **Underlying cause:** a lack of trust because there was no accountability.
3. **Solution:** the committee publishes accounts at a village meeting; residents agree to contribute again and elect two new members to check the books.

The case shows that responsibilities work best when leaders are also accountable.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing rights with responsibilities"}
Voting is both a right and a civic responsibility. Paying taxes is a responsibility. Freedom of expression is a right that must be used responsibly.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing without explaining"}
"Paying taxes" earns a mark for identification; the explanation is what taxes do: fund schools, clinics and roads.
:::

:::mistake{title="Taking sides in politics"}
Civic responsibility means taking part, respecting others and obeying the law. It does not mean supporting any particular party. Keep answers non-partisan.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**State** or **list** responsibilities (Paper 2 Section A): a short phrase each, such as "paying taxes" or "voting in elections".
:::

:::exam-tip
**Explain** the importance of a responsibility: say what it achieves for the community or nation.
:::

:::exam-tip
**How far** do citizens meet their civic responsibilities? (Paper 2 Section B): give evidence of citizens meeting them, evidence of failures, the reasons, and a supported judgement.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Citizens' roles include defending sovereignty, upholding national values and the Constitution, obeying the law, paying taxes, voting and protecting heritage and the environment.
- The Constitution's citizenship chapter lists duties of citizens such as loyalty and respect for the Constitution, flag and anthem.
- Civic responsibilities apply at community and national level in security, education, health, shelter and community projects.
- Active citizenship supports development, democracy, peace, rights and heritage.
- Some citizens fail in their responsibilities because of lack of awareness, poverty, distrust or apathy.
:::
