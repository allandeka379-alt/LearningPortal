## Introduction

In a village near Gwanda, 74-year-old Gogo Ncube looks after three grandchildren whose parents died. Her knees are weak, the borehole is two kilometres away and her small vegetable garden is all the income she has. Down the road, Themba, who uses a wheelchair, cannot get into the clinic because there is no ramp. In town, a widow has been told by her late husband's relatives to leave the house she shared with him for twenty years.

Each of these people belongs to a **vulnerable group**: people who are more likely than others to be harmed, excluded or left without support. The Constitution of Zimbabwe gives special attention to them, and Heritage Studies asks you to know their rights, the challenges they face and how families, communities and the government can help. You should already know the meaning of a right and a responsibility and the rights of children.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Vulnerable group"}
A group of people who, because of age, disability, sex, health, poverty or circumstances, are more likely than others to suffer harm, discrimination or neglect and may need extra protection.
:::

:::definition{term="Disability"}
A long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairment that, together with barriers in society, can prevent a person from taking part fully and equally.
:::

:::definition{term="Discrimination"}
Treating a person unfairly because of who they are, for example because of their sex, age or disability.
:::

:::definition{term="Social welfare"}
Help given by the state, and by organisations, to people in need, such as food aid, school fees assistance or cash payments.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Sections to know (Declaration of Rights, Chapter 4)"}
Section 80: rights of women. Section 81: rights of children. Section 82: rights of the elderly. Section 83: rights of persons with disabilities. Section 84: rights of veterans of the liberation struggle.
:::

## Rights in the Constitution (in outline)

| Group | Section | Main rights (paraphrased) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Women | 80 | Full and equal dignity with men, including equal opportunities in political, economic and social life; equal rights with men over the custody and guardianship of children; laws, customs and practices that infringe women's rights are void to that extent |
| Children | 81 | Equal treatment and to be heard; a name and birth certificate; family care; education, health care, nutrition and shelter; protection from exploitation, child labour and abuse; best interests paramount |
| The elderly | 82 | People over seventy have the right to reasonable care and assistance from their families and the state, to health care and medical assistance from the state, and to financial support through social security and welfare |
| Persons with disabilities | 83 | The state must take measures, within its resources, to help them become self-reliant, live with their families and take part in social and recreational life, be protected from exploitation and abuse, get medical and other treatment, and receive special facilities for education and state-funded education where they need it |
| Veterans of the liberation struggle | 84 | Due recognition for their contribution to the liberation of Zimbabwe, and suitable welfare such as pensions and access to basic health care |

Other provisions help too. **Sign language** is one of the sixteen officially recognised languages (section 6), and many rights are limited to what the state can do "within the limits of the resources available".

## Responsibilities of vulnerable groups

Members of vulnerable groups are citizens like everyone else. As far as they are able, they share the same responsibilities: obeying the law, respecting others, taking part in community life and voting if they are adults. Some groups also make special contributions:

- **The elderly** pass on wisdom, history, language and customs; they counsel the young and often care for grandchildren.
- **Persons with disabilities** take part in work, education, sport and community life, and advocate for their own needs.
- **War veterans** preserve the history of the liberation struggle and promote peace and national unity.
- **Women** contribute as citizens, workers, leaders and parents, with the same duties as men.

## Challenges faced by vulnerable groups

| Group | Common challenges |
| --- | --- |
| The elderly | Poverty and lack of income; ill health and cost of care; caring for orphaned grandchildren; neglect when younger relatives migrate; abuse, including accusations of witchcraft |
| Persons with disabilities | Buildings and transport without ramps; lack of sign language interpreters, Braille materials and special schools; stigma and discrimination; unemployment; abuse |
| Women | Gender-based violence; property grabbing after a husband's death; unequal access to land, credit and jobs; harmful practices |
| Children and orphans | Lack of fees, food and care; child-headed households; child labour; abuse and exploitation; missing birth certificates |
| War veterans | Ageing and ill health; some struggle with income and the effects of war |

:::example{title="Property grabbing: a widow's rights"}
After Tendai's father dies in Mutare, an uncle tells Tendai's mother to leave the house and hand over the car.

1. **Rights involved:** as a surviving spouse she has rights to the estate, and section 80 says customs that infringe women's rights are void to that extent.
2. **What she can do:** report the death and the estate to the Master of the High Court, which supervises deceased estates, and seek legal help, for example from a legal aid organisation.
3. **Community role:** the family's elders and the aunt (*vatete/ubabakazi*) can remind the uncle that property grabbing is unjust and unlawful.

The example applies constitutional rights to a common problem.
:::

## How vulnerable groups are supported

### Government

- **Department of Social Welfare**: public assistance, food aid and cash transfers for very poor households, and child protection.
- **Basic Education Assistance Module (BEAM)**: helps pay school fees for vulnerable children.
- **Special schools and resource units** for learners with disabilities; recognition of sign language.
- **Pensions** for retired workers and war veterans; health care in public hospitals and clinics.
- **Laws** that protect against discrimination, domestic violence and property grabbing.

### Communities

- **Churches, mosques and other faith groups** visit the sick, feed the hungry and support orphans.
- **Non-governmental organisations** such as the Jairos Jiri Association (for people with disabilities) and HelpAge Zimbabwe (for older people) provide services and advocacy.
- **Traditional support**: the chief's field or granary (*zunde ramambo*) was used to feed orphans, widows and others in need, and some communities have revived it.
- **Neighbours and burial societies** share food, labour and money in times of need.

### Individuals

- Helping an elderly neighbour fetch water or collect medicine.
- Including a classmate with a disability in games and group work.
- Speaking up against bullying, discrimination or abuse.
- Volunteering at a home for the elderly or a children's home.

:::example{title="A school makes room"}
A fictional secondary school in Gweru enrols Nokuthula, who is deaf.

1. The school arranges for a teacher trained in sign language and seats her near the front.
2. Classmates learn basic sign language greetings during a club activity.
3. Nokuthula joins the athletics team and takes part in school events.

The **state** (training teachers), the **community** (the school) and **individuals** (classmates) each play a part in upholding her rights under section 83.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the section numbers"}
Women 80, children 81, elderly 82, persons with disabilities 83, war veterans 84. If you are not sure, describe the right without the number.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing vulnerable people only as helpless"}
Vulnerable groups have rights and responsibilities and make real contributions. Show this in your answers.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing support without explaining it"}
"The government helps" earns little. Say how: BEAM pays school fees, so orphans stay in school.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**Identify** vulnerable groups and **state** their rights (Paper 2 Section A): one group or one right per point.
:::

:::exam-tip
**Explain** how a group is supported: name the support (government, community, individual) and its effect.
:::

:::exam-tip
**Suggest** or **assess** (Paper 2 Section B), for example how far vulnerable groups are protected: weigh the law and support against the challenges that remain, and conclude.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Vulnerable groups include the elderly, persons with disabilities, women, children, orphans and war veterans.
- Sections 80 to 84 of the Constitution set out rights for women, children, the elderly, persons with disabilities and war veterans.
- Members of vulnerable groups share citizens' responsibilities and make special contributions.
- Challenges include poverty, discrimination, inaccessible buildings, abuse, property grabbing and neglect.
- Government (social welfare, BEAM, special schools), communities (faith groups, NGOs, *zunde ramambo*) and individuals all help.
:::
