## Introduction

Land is the most important inheritance of most Zimbabwean families. It feeds them, gives them a home, carries the graves of their ancestors and links them to their clan. Yet in many parts of the country you can see land that is losing its value: deep gullies cutting through fields, hillsides stripped of grass, red soil washing into rivers after a storm, and dams that are half full of sand. This loss of land quality is called **land degradation**.

Land degradation is a Form 3 topic, but it builds on what you already know about deforestation and the environment. It matters because once good topsoil is lost it takes a very long time to form again, and a family or a nation that loses its soil loses its ability to feed itself. In this lesson you will learn what land degradation is, what causes it, what it does and how it can be reduced. Who owns land, and how land was redistributed after 1980, is studied separately; here we focus on the condition of the land itself.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Land degradation"}
A decline in the quality and productivity of land, so that it can support fewer plants, animals and people than before.
:::

:::definition{term="Soil erosion"}
The removal of topsoil by water or wind faster than new soil forms.
:::

:::definition{term="Siltation"}
The filling of rivers and dams with soil and sand washed from the land, so that they hold less water.
:::

:::definition{term="Desertification"}
The process by which land in dry areas becomes more and more like a desert, mainly through human activity and drought.
:::

:::definition{term="Overgrazing"}
Keeping more livestock on an area than the grass can support, so that the grass cover is destroyed.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Facts to learn"}
- Topsoil forms very slowly; once lost to erosion it cannot be replaced in a human lifetime.
- The Environmental Management Agency (EMA) runs campaigns against veld fires and stream-bank cultivation and requires land disturbed by mining to be rehabilitated.
- National Tree Planting Day (first Saturday of December) encourages afforestation.
:::

## Causes of land degradation

| Cause | How it degrades land |
|---|---|
| Deforestation | Removes the trees whose roots bind soil and whose leaves protect it from heavy rain |
| Overgrazing | Too many cattle and goats eat grass to the roots; bare soil is trampled and washed away |
| Poor farming methods | Ploughing up and down slopes, growing the same crop year after year without manure, cultivating river banks and wetlands |
| Veld fires | Burn grass and young trees, leaving soil bare when the first heavy rains come; kill soil organisms |
| Illegal gold panning | Pits and trenches are dug and left open; riverbeds and banks are torn up; mercury pollutes soil and water |
| Sand and clay extraction | Uncontrolled digging for building sand and brick clay leaves pits and gullies |
| Urban expansion | Houses, roads and paving cover farmland and wetlands; rainwater runs off fast and cuts gullies |

### Why some areas suffer more

Land degradation is often worst where many people and animals depend on small areas of land, where rainfall is low and unreliable, and where soils are sandy and easily washed away. During colonial rule, laws such as the Land Apportionment Act (1930) crowded many African families onto poorer land, which made overgrazing and erosion worse in many communal areas. Today, poverty can push families to cultivate steep slopes and river banks because they have no other land.

## Effects of land degradation

| Effect | What it means for people |
|---|---|
| Soil erosion and gullies | Fields shrink, paths and roads are cut, homes on slopes are threatened |
| Loss of soil fertility | Lower crop yields, more money spent on fertiliser, more hunger |
| Siltation of rivers and dams | Dams hold less water for irrigation, towns and livestock; rivers flood more easily and dry up sooner |
| Loss of grazing | Cattle grow thin, fewer calves are born, families lose wealth and draught power |
| Desertification | Dry areas become unable to support farming at all; people may have to move |
| Loss of biodiversity | Wild plants, medicinal herbs and animals disappear |

:::example{title="From bare slope to silted dam"}
A fictional village near Masvingo keeps many goats on a small hillside.

1. **Cause:** goats eat the grass and shrubs to the roots, and dry-season fires burn what is left.
2. **First effect:** when the rains come, water runs off the bare slope, carrying topsoil and cutting small channels.
3. **Second effect:** the channels grow into gullies; fields at the foot of the hill are covered in sand.
4. **Wider effect:** soil washes into the village dam, which fills with silt and dries up two months earlier than it used to.

One cause leads to a chain of effects that reaches people who never set foot on the hillside.
:::

## Measures to reduce land degradation

| Measure | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Afforestation and reforestation | Planting trees binds soil, breaks the force of rain and wind, and provides fuel and fruit |
| Contour ridges and terraces | Ridges built across a slope slow running water so that it sinks in instead of carrying soil away |
| Rotational grazing and destocking | Moving livestock between paddocks and keeping fewer animals lets grass recover |
| Conservation farming | Minimum tillage, mulching and planting basins keep soil covered and moist |
| Crop rotation and manure | Changing crops each season and adding manure or compost keep soil fertile |
| Filling gullies and protecting river banks | Stones, brush and grass stop gullies growing; uncultivated strips of vegetation protect banks |
| Fire guards and fire awareness | Cleared strips and campaigns reduce the spread of veld fires |
| Laws and enforcement | EMA and councils act against illegal panning, stream-bank cultivation and fires; miners must rehabilitate land |

:::example{title="A family plan for a degraded field"}
Tendai's family in a fictional ward in Chivi has a sloping field with small gullies.

1. **Contour ridges:** they build ridges across the slope with help from an agricultural extension officer.
2. **Planting basins and mulch:** they dig basins, add manure and cover the soil with crop stalks instead of burning them.
3. **Gully repair:** they pack stones and brush into the gullies and plant vetiver grass along the edges.
4. **Result over three seasons:** less soil is lost, the soil holds more water and yields improve even in a dry year.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling soil erosion a cause"}
Soil erosion is an *effect* (and a form) of land degradation. The causes are human activities such as overgrazing and deforestation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing siltation with pollution"}
Siltation is the filling of rivers and dams with soil; pollution is contamination with harmful substances. Both can happen at once, but they are different.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving measures without linking them to causes"}
A good answer shows how a measure tackles a cause: "rotational grazing lets grass recover, so the soil stays covered".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Land degradation is a decline in the quality and productivity of land.
- Causes include deforestation, overgrazing, poor farming methods, veld fires, illegal gold panning, sand extraction and urban expansion.
- Effects include soil erosion, gullies, loss of fertility, siltation of rivers and dams, desertification and hunger.
- Measures include afforestation, contour ridges, rotational grazing, conservation farming, gully repair, fire guards and enforcing laws.
- Effective measures tackle the cause, not just the symptom, and need communities to take part.
:::
