## Introduction

Your parents or grandparents may remember 18 April 1980: crowds at Rufaro Stadium in Harare, the new flag rising as the old one came down, and celebrations in every town and village. After ninety years of colonial rule, Zimbabwe was independent and every adult could vote.

What did independence change? In this Form 4 lesson you learn the main **gains of independence**: the policy of reconciliation, the rapid expansion of education and health care, new rights for women, political and economic changes, and the Unity Accord of 1987. The challenges the country has faced since 1980 are covered in the next lesson; a good answer about independent Zimbabwe weighs both. You should already know the causes and results of the liberation struggle.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Independence"}
The condition of a country that governs itself, free from control by another country.
:::

:::definition{term="Reconciliation"}
Bringing together former enemies so that they can live and work together in peace. At independence the new government announced a policy of national reconciliation between black and white Zimbabweans and between those who had fought on different sides.
:::

:::definition{term="Majority rule"}
A system in which the government is chosen by the votes of all adult citizens, so it reflects the wishes of the majority.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and laws to learn"}
Independence **18 April 1980**. Legal Age of Majority Act **1982**. Unity Accord **22 December 1987**. Zimbabwe joined the **UN** and the **OAU** in 1980 and was a founder member of **SADCC** (1980). Unity Day is commemorated on **22 December**.
:::

## Political gains

- **Majority rule and universal adult suffrage**: every citizen aged 18 and over could vote, regardless of race, income or education.
- **A sovereign state** with its own flag, anthem, coat of arms and national holidays.
- **Reconciliation**: the new government invited former opponents to stay and help build the country; white farmers, business people and civil servants continued to work.
- **International recognition**: Zimbabwe joined the United Nations, the Organisation of African Unity and the Commonwealth, and became a founder member of SADCC. Harare hosted the summit of the Non-Aligned Movement in 1986.
- **Removal of racial laws**: discriminatory laws on land, housing, schooling and public places were repealed over time.

## Social gains

| Area | Gains after 1980 (outline) |
|---|---|
| Education | Primary education was made free from 1980; many new primary and secondary schools were built, especially in rural areas; enrolment rose sharply; teacher training was expanded; adult literacy campaigns were run |
| Health | Free health care for people on low incomes; many new rural clinics and health centres; village health workers; immunisation programmes for children |
| Women's rights | The Legal Age of Majority Act (1982) made women and men legal adults at 18, so women could enter contracts, own property and marry without a guardian's consent; equal pay and maternity leave were promoted |
| Workers | Minimum wages were introduced; labour laws protected workers' rights |
| Infrastructure | Rural roads, boreholes, dams and electricity were extended to many communal areas |
| Culture | Indigenous languages, history and culture were given a larger place in schools and national life |

:::example{title="How one law changed a woman's life"}
Before 1982, a woman in Zimbabwe was, under customary law, often treated as a legal minor all her life, under the guardianship of her father or husband.

1. **The problem:** Chipo, aged 25, wants to open a bank account and buy a stand in Gweru. She needs a male guardian's consent.
2. **The law:** the Legal Age of Majority Act (1982) makes every person a legal adult at 18.
3. **The change:** Chipo can now sign contracts, own property and marry in her own right.
4. **Significance:** the Act was an important step towards gender equality, later strengthened by the 2013 Constitution.
:::

## Economic gains in outline

- The end of international sanctions on Rhodesia allowed trade with the world.
- An international donor conference (ZIMCORD) in 1981 raised money for reconstruction and development.
- The government expanded marketing and support for **communal farmers**, whose production of crops such as maize and cotton grew in the 1980s.
- More Africans entered the civil service, business and the professions.
- **Land redistribution** began on a willing-buyer willing-seller basis (see the lesson on land ownership).

## National unity: the Unity Accord

In the early 1980s, tension between the two main parties, ZANU-PF and PF-ZAPU, led to violence in Matabeleland and parts of the Midlands (studied in the lesson on challenges). After long talks, the leaders of the two parties signed the **Unity Accord** on **22 December 1987**. The parties merged as ZANU-PF, the violence ended, and in 1988 an amnesty was announced. **22 December** is commemorated as **Unity Day**.

:::example{title="Assessing a gain"}
Question: *How far was education the greatest gain of independence?*

1. **For:** free primary education and new schools gave millions of children opportunities their parents never had, and literacy rose to among the highest in Africa.
2. **Against:** majority rule and the vote were the foundation of every other gain; the Unity Accord brought peace.
3. **Balance:** education changed families most directly, but it depended on political independence.
4. **Judgement:** a supported conclusion, e.g. "Education was the most widely felt gain, but majority rule made it possible."
:::

## Different views

Most people agree that the 1980s brought real gains in education, health and rights. People differ on how far these gains have been kept since then, especially in the economic difficulties after 1990 and 2000 (see the lesson on challenges). In an exam, show that you know both the gains and the limits.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing reconciliation with the Unity Accord"}
Reconciliation (1980) was a policy towards former enemies, especially between black and white. The Unity Accord (22 December 1987) united two political parties, ZANU-PF and PF-ZAPU.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing slogans instead of evidence"}
"Independence brought everything" earns nothing. Name the gain and give evidence: "Primary education became free from 1980, and many new schools were built in rural areas."
:::

:::mistake{title="Inventing statistics"}
Do not make up numbers of schools or clinics. "Enrolment rose sharply" is enough.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State* or *identify* (Paper 2 Section A): name a gain. *Describe*: say what changed. *Explain*: give the gain and why it mattered. For a Section B essay on gains and challenges, write a balanced answer: gains, challenges, then a supported judgement.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1"}
Multiple-choice items often test the dates of independence and the Unity Accord, and what the Legal Age of Majority Act did.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Zimbabwe became independent on 18 April 1980, with majority rule and votes for every adult.
- The policy of reconciliation invited former enemies to build the nation together.
- Education and health care expanded rapidly; primary education became free from 1980.
- The Legal Age of Majority Act (1982) made women legal adults at 18.
- The Unity Accord of 22 December 1987 united ZANU-PF and PF-ZAPU and ended the violence of the 1980s.
:::
