## Introduction

Every young Zimbabwean has heard adults talk about hard times: queues for fuel or cash, prices that changed every day, a relative who left to work in South Africa or the United Kingdom, a drought that ruined the harvest, a cyclone that destroyed homes in Chimanimani. Independence brought real gains, but the country has also faced serious challenges.

In this Form 4 lesson you study the main challenges since 1980: natural disasters, economic problems, sanctions or restrictive measures, disease, brain drain and the painful conflict of the 1980s. These are subjects on which people hold different views. Your task in an exam is to describe the facts accurately, explain causes and effects, and present different views fairly. You should already know the gains of independence.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Economic structural adjustment"}
A programme of economic reforms, usually agreed with international lenders, that cuts government spending, removes price controls and opens the economy to trade. Zimbabwe's Economic Structural Adjustment Programme (ESAP) began in 1991.
:::

:::definition{term="Hyperinflation"}
Extremely rapid rise in prices, so that money loses its value very quickly.
:::

:::definition{term="Brain drain"}
The emigration of skilled and educated people, such as doctors, nurses, teachers and engineers, to work in other countries.
:::

:::definition{term="Sanctions or restrictive measures"}
Penalties imposed by one country or group of countries on another, for example limits on trade, loans or travel. Whether the measures imposed on Zimbabwe from the early 2000s should be called *sanctions* or *targeted restrictive measures* is itself a matter of debate.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
ESAP from **1991**. Severe drought **1991-92**. Unity Accord **22 December 1987**, ending the disturbances of the 1980s. Cyclone Idai **March 2019**. First COVID-19 case in Zimbabwe **March 2020**.
:::

## Natural disasters

| Disaster | Examples | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| Drought | Severe droughts in the early 1980s and in 1991-92, and several since | Crop failure, hunger, death of cattle, food imports and food aid, water shortages in towns |
| Cyclones and floods | Cyclone Idai, March 2019, which struck Chimanimani and Chipinge especially hard | Loss of life, destroyed homes, roads and bridges, people displaced |
| Pests and animal diseases | Outbreaks such as foot-and-mouth disease | Losses to farmers and to exports of beef |

The government responded with drought relief, grain imports through the Grain Marketing Board, and help from partners such as the World Food Programme.

## Economic difficulties

**Structural adjustment (ESAP, from 1991).** The government borrowed from international lenders and agreed to reduce its budget deficit, remove price controls, cut civil service jobs and open the economy to imports. Supporters hoped this would bring investment and growth. In practice, many workers lost their jobs as firms closed in the face of imports, prices of basic goods rose, and fees were charged for some school and health services. Coming at the same time as the severe drought of 1991-92, ESAP caused great hardship.

**Hyperinflation (late 2000s).** In the 2000s the economy shrank sharply. Prices rose so fast that shops changed prices more than once a day and savings lost their value. In 2009 the Zimbabwe dollar was abandoned and people used foreign currencies such as the US dollar and the South African rand.

**Currency changes.** Since 2016 the country has introduced several local currencies and currency arrangements, including bond notes and, in 2024, the ZiG. Frequent changes have made planning and saving difficult for families and businesses.

Other economic challenges include unemployment, the growth of the informal sector, shortages of fuel, power and foreign currency, and debt.

:::example{title="Why hyperinflation hurts savers"}
Thandeka saves US\$100 worth of local currency in a bank in January. Prices then double every month.

1. After one month, her savings buy only half of what they bought in January.
2. After three months, they buy about one-eighth.
3. People therefore spend money as soon as they get it and avoid saving.
4. **Conclusion:** hyperinflation destroys savings and pensions, and makes people lose trust in the currency.
:::

## Sanctions or restrictive measures

From the early 2000s, after the fast-track land reform and disputes over elections, the United States, the European Union and some other countries imposed measures on Zimbabwe. These included travel bans and asset freezes on named individuals and companies, an arms embargo, and limits on certain loans and aid.

| View | Main arguments |
|---|---|
| The government and its supporters | They are **sanctions** that have damaged the whole economy by cutting off credit and investment and discouraging trade; they punish ordinary people; SADC and the AU have called for their removal |
| Western governments that imposed them | They are **targeted restrictive measures** aimed at specific individuals and companies, not at ordinary people; humanitarian aid continued; they were linked to concerns about human rights and governance |
| Many economists and observers | The measures were one factor among several; economic policy, falling farm output, debt and corruption also played a part |

Students should present these views fairly and reach their own judgement.

## Disease

**HIV and AIDS** affected many families from the 1980s, especially young adults, leaving many orphans and placing heavy pressure on health services. Prevention campaigns, testing and antiretroviral treatment have since helped many people living with HIV to live long and healthy lives. Other challenges include **cholera** outbreaks (a major one in 2008-09), malaria and tuberculosis, and **COVID-19**, which reached Zimbabwe in March 2020 and led to lockdowns, school closures and loss of income.

## Brain drain

Many skilled people, including doctors, nurses, teachers and engineers, have left to work in South Africa, Botswana, the United Kingdom and elsewhere, attracted by higher pay and better conditions. Hospitals and schools lose experienced staff, although the money sent home by people working abroad (remittances) supports many families.

## The disturbances in Matabeleland and the Midlands (Gukurahundi)

In the early 1980s, tension between ZANU-PF and PF-ZAPU and the activity of armed dissidents led the government to deploy army units, including the Fifth Brigade, in Matabeleland and parts of the Midlands from 1983. Many thousands of civilians were killed, and many more were beaten, detained or displaced; armed dissidents also killed civilians. The period is known as **Gukurahundi**. The violence ended with the **Unity Accord** of 22 December 1987 and the amnesty of 1988.

This remains a painful memory for many families and communities. The 2013 Constitution set up the **National Peace and Reconciliation Commission**, which is meant to help the nation deal with past conflicts, and there have been calls for truth-telling, healing, reburials and the issuing of documents such as birth and death certificates to affected families.

:::example{title="Writing sensitively about a painful subject"}
A Section B question asks about challenges since 1980.

1. **State the facts plainly:** army units, including the Fifth Brigade, were deployed in Matabeleland and the Midlands from 1983; many thousands of civilians died.
2. **Avoid blame of living people or slogans;** describe events and institutions.
3. **Acknowledge the victims and the continuing need for healing.**
4. **Link to the outcome:** the Unity Accord (1987) ended the violence; the NPRC was created under the 2013 Constitution.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Presenting one view on sanctions as fact"}
Say who holds each view: "The government argues…; Western governments say…". Then give your judgement.
:::

:::mistake{title="Inventing numbers"}
Do not invent inflation figures or death tolls. "Many thousands", "rose sharply" and "very rapid" are acceptable.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing challenges without effects"}
"Drought" earns one mark at most. "Drought in 1991-92 caused crop failure, so the country had to import maize and many families relied on food aid" is developed.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Balanced essays"}
For Paper 2 Section B (*discuss*, *assess*, *how far*), weigh gains against challenges, or one cause against others (e.g. sanctions versus policy and drought), and finish with a supported judgement.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1"}
Expect items on what ESAP was, when Cyclone Idai struck, the meaning of brain drain and the date of the Unity Accord.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Natural disasters include droughts (e.g. 1991-92) and Cyclone Idai (March 2019).
- ESAP (from 1991) brought job losses and higher prices; hyperinflation in the late 2000s led to the use of foreign currencies from 2009, followed by several currency changes.
- Measures imposed from the early 2000s are called sanctions by some and targeted restrictive measures by others; their effects are debated.
- HIV and AIDS, cholera and COVID-19, and the brain drain have strained services.
- The disturbances in Matabeleland and the Midlands (Gukurahundi) in the 1980s cost many thousands of lives and ended with the Unity Accord of 1987.
:::
