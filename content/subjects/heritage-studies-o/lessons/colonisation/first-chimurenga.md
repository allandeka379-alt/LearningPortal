## Introduction

The word **Chimurenga** (Shona) and **Umvukela** (Ndebele) are used in Zimbabwe for the struggles against colonial rule. The liberation war of the 1960s and 1970s is called the **Second** Chimurenga because its fighters saw themselves as continuing a **first** one: the risings of 1896-97, when the Ndebele and many Shona communities took up arms against the British South Africa Company.

Only six years after the Pioneer Column arrived, and less than three years after the Ndebele state was destroyed, people across much of the country rose against the Company. They were defeated, but the memory of the risings, and of leaders such as Mbuya Nehanda and Sekuru Kaguvi, inspired later generations. In this Form 3 lesson you learn the causes, the main events, the role of spirit mediums, why the risings failed and what they achieved. You should already know about the occupation of 1890 and the Anglo-Ndebele War.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Chimurenga / Umvukela"}
Shona and Ndebele words for uprising or struggle, used for the risings of 1896-97 (the First Chimurenga) and the liberation war (the Second Chimurenga).
:::

:::definition{term="Hut tax"}
A tax charged by the Company on every hut, introduced in the 1890s; it forced Africans to work for wages or sell cattle and crops to pay.
:::

:::definition{term="Forced labour"}
Work that people were made to do for settlers, mines or the Company, often for little or no pay, under threat of punishment.
:::

:::definition{term="Rinderpest"}
A deadly cattle disease that swept through southern Africa in 1896, killing very large numbers of cattle.
:::

:::definition{term="Spirit medium"}
A person through whom an ancestral spirit is believed to speak (svikiro in Shona); mediums of powerful spirits such as Nehanda and Kaguvi held great authority.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
Rising in **Matabeleland**: from **March 1896**. Rising in **Mashonaland**: from **June 1896**. Fighting in Matabeleland largely ended after **indabas** (meetings) between Cecil Rhodes and Ndebele leaders in the Matobo Hills in **1896**. Mashonaland resistance continued into **1897**. **Nehanda and Kaguvi** were executed in **April 1898**.
:::

## Causes of the risings

| Cause | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Loss of land | Settlers took the best land; Africans were moved to reserves or became tenants on settler farms |
| Loss of cattle | After 1893 the Company seized most Ndebele cattle; in Mashonaland cattle were also taken |
| Forced labour | Africans were made to work on farms, mines and roads, often with harsh treatment and little pay |
| Hut tax | Introduced in the 1890s and collected forcefully, sometimes by seizing property |
| Brutal police and officials | The Company's African police and some native commissioners beat people and abused their authority |
| Loss of authority | Kings, chiefs and izinduna lost their power; Company officials ignored traditional leaders |
| Natural disasters | **Rinderpest** killed cattle in 1896; **drought** and **locusts** damaged crops; the Company shot cattle to stop the disease spreading; many people blamed the newcomers |
| The Jameson Raid | Late in 1895 Jameson led many Company police into the Transvaal in a failed raid; this left the country with few police and showed that the Company could be defeated |
| Ndebele desire to restore their state | Many Ndebele hoped to restore the kingdom destroyed in 1893 |

## The course of the risings (outline)

![Timeline of the First Chimurenga: rising in Matabeleland from March 1896, in Mashonaland from June 1896, Matabeleland fighting largely ended after the 1896 indabas in the Matobo Hills, Mashonaland resistance continued into 1897 and Nehanda and Kaguvi were executed in April 1898.](media:diagrams/first-chimurenga-timeline.png "Figure: key dates of the First Chimurenga, 1896 to 1898 (not to scale)")

### Matabeleland (from March 1896)

In late **March 1896** Ndebele fighters attacked settlers, Company police and farms in many parts of Matabeleland. Settlers withdrew to laagers in Bulawayo and other centres. Fighters gathered in the **Matobo Hills**, using the hills' caves and boulders as strongholds. The Company brought in reinforcements from South Africa and British troops. Fighting was fierce, but neither side could win quickly. **Cecil Rhodes** then went into the Matobo Hills to hold **indabas** with Ndebele leaders. Promises of better treatment and land, and the return of some authority to izinduna, ended most of the fighting in Matabeleland in 1896.

### Mashonaland (from June 1896)

In **June 1896** many Shona communities rose, surprising settlers who had believed the Shona would not fight. Attacks took place around Mazowe, Hartley (now Chegutu), Goromonzi and elsewhere. Leaders included chiefs such as **Mashayamombe** and **Makoni**, and the spirit mediums **Kaguvi** and **Nehanda**. Communities fought from fortified hills and **caves**. Company forces used dynamite against caves, burnt crops and villages and starved strongholds. Resistance continued well into **1897**. Mashayamombe was killed and Makoni executed in 1897, and in late 1897 Kaguvi and Nehanda were captured. They were tried and executed in **April 1898**.

## The role of spirit mediums

- **Inspiration:** mediums told people that the ancestors were angry at the newcomers and the disasters they had brought, and called on them to fight.
- **Organisation:** mediums such as **Kaguvi** and **Nehanda** gave instructions and encouraged chiefs to join.
- **Link between areas:** **Mkwati**, associated with the Mwari shrines in the Matobo Hills, is believed to have linked the Ndebele and Shona risings.
- **Morale:** mediums gave fighters courage through rituals and medicine.
- **Legacy:** their example inspired the spirit mediums who worked with guerrillas in the Second Chimurenga.

:::example{title="The coordination debate"}
Did the spirit mediums coordinate the risings across the country?

1. **One view:** the historian Terence Ranger (1967) argued that the Mwari shrines and spirit mediums, including Mkwati, Kaguvi and Nehanda, linked and coordinated the Ndebele and Shona risings.
2. **Another view:** later historians such as Julian Cobbing and David Beach argued that the risings were more local, led mainly by chiefs, with mediums playing an important but less central role.
3. **What both accept:** mediums inspired and encouraged many communities, and the risings were widespread.
4. **How to answer:** present both views fairly and reach a supported judgement; do not state either as certain fact.
:::

## Reasons for defeat

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Superior weapons | Maxim guns, rifles, artillery and dynamite against mostly older guns and spears |
| Reinforcements | Troops and volunteers came from South Africa and Britain |
| Lack of unified command | Communities often fought separately; there was no single leader or plan for the whole country |
| Siege and starvation tactics | Company forces burnt crops and starved people in caves and hills |
| Divisions | Some communities stayed neutral or helped the Company; old rivalries remained |
| Natural disasters | Drought, locusts and rinderpest reduced food for fighters and their families |

## Results

- **Defeat and loss of life:** many Africans were killed, and many settlers also died; crops, villages and cattle were destroyed, causing hunger.
- **Execution of leaders:** Nehanda, Kaguvi and others were executed; chiefs lost land and power.
- **Changes in Company rule:** the British government increased its supervision of the Company; a more organised **Native Department** of native commissioners was set up; some promises were made to the Ndebele leaders after the indabas.
- **More land taken:** settlers continued to occupy land, and reserves were extended.
- **Legacy of resistance:** the risings became a symbol of resistance. The nationalists of the 1960s and 1970s called their war the **Second Chimurenga**, drawing inspiration from 1896-97.

:::example{title="Assessing the results"}
Were the risings a total failure?

1. **Short term:** yes, militarily: the Company won, leaders were executed and many people died.
2. **Medium term:** the Company changed some methods of rule and Britain watched it more closely.
3. **Long term:** the memory of resistance inspired the liberation struggle.
4. **Judgement:** a military defeat, but a lasting source of national pride and inspiration.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Starting dates"}
The Ndebele rose first, in **March** 1896; most Shona communities rose from **June** 1896.
:::

:::mistake{title="Stating coordination as fact"}
Whether the risings were coordinated is a historians' debate. Give both views.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing causes without linking them to grievances"}
Explain each cause: "Hut tax forced people to work for settlers (1), causing anger at Company rule (1)."
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Causes: loss of land and cattle, forced labour, hut tax, brutal police, loss of authority, rinderpest, drought, locusts and the Jameson Raid.
- The Ndebele rose in March 1896 and fought from the Matobo Hills until Rhodes's indabas; the Shona rose in June 1896 and resisted into 1897.
- Spirit mediums such as Nehanda, Kaguvi and Mkwati inspired and encouraged the risings; how far they coordinated them is debated.
- The risings failed because of superior weapons, reinforcements, divided command, starvation tactics and natural disasters.
- Results: defeat, executions (April 1898), changes in Company rule, more land loss, and a lasting legacy for the Second Chimurenga.
:::
