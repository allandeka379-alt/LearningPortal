## Introduction

In Bulawayo, the site of Lobengula's royal town is still remembered and visited. In 1893 the king ordered that his capital be burnt rather than let it fall into the hands of the British South Africa Company. Within months, the Ndebele state that Mzilikazi had founded half a century earlier had lost its king, its capital and much of its land and cattle.

Three years after the Pioneer Column reached Fort Salisbury, the Company went to war against the Ndebele. In this Form 3 lesson you learn why the war happened, how it was fought, and what it meant for the Ndebele people and for the Company. You should already know about the Rudd Concession, the Royal Charter and the occupation of Mashonaland in 1890.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Anglo-Ndebele War"}
The war of 1893-94 between the British South Africa Company, with its volunteers and allies, and the Ndebele state of Lobengula, which ended with the destruction of the Ndebele state.
:::

:::definition{term="Victoria incident"}
The events of July 1893 near Fort Victoria (now Masvingo), when an Ndebele force sent to punish Shona communities clashed with Company forces; the Company used it as an excuse for war.
:::

:::definition{term="Victoria Agreement"}
The promise made by the Company in 1893 to volunteers that they would receive land, gold claims and a share of captured cattle in Matabeleland in return for fighting.
:::

:::definition{term="Reserve"}
An area of land set aside by the colonial authorities for African occupation, usually poorer land far from towns and railways.
:::

:::definition{term="Maxim gun"}
An early machine gun that could fire many rounds a minute, which gave the Company forces a great advantage in battle.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and places"}
Victoria incident: **July 1893**. Battle of **Shangani**: late **October 1893**. Battle of **Bembesi**: **1 November 1893**. Company forces entered **Bulawayo** in early **November 1893**. **Shangani Patrol** destroyed: **December 1893**. Lobengula died in early **1894**.
:::

## Causes of the war

| Cause | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Disappointment in Mashonaland | The Company had hoped to find a "second Rand" of gold in Mashonaland, but the gold was less than expected; settlers and shareholders looked to Matabeleland |
| Desire for land and cattle | Settlers wanted the good grazing land and the large herds of the Ndebele |
| The Ndebele state as an obstacle | The Company saw an independent, armed Ndebele state as a threat to its control and to investment |
| Border disputes | The boundary between Ndebele and Company areas was not agreed; both claimed authority over Shona communities |
| Tribute and raids | The Ndebele expected tribute from some Shona communities; the Company now claimed to protect them, so Ndebele raids were treated as attacks on the Company |
| The Victoria incident | In July 1893 an Ndebele force sent to punish Shona communities near Fort Victoria was fired on by Company forces; Jameson used this as an excuse for war |
| The Victoria Agreement | Volunteers were promised land, gold claims and loot cattle, giving them a personal interest in war |

Many historians argue that the Company, especially its administrator **Leander Starr Jameson** and Cecil Rhodes, had already decided that war was needed, and that the Victoria incident provided the **excuse** rather than the real cause.

:::example{title="Cause or excuse?"}
A learner is asked whether the Victoria incident caused the war.

1. **What happened:** an Ndebele force entered the Fort Victoria area to punish Shona communities; Company forces fired on it.
2. **Long-term causes:** gold disappointment, desire for land and cattle, the Company's wish to remove an independent state.
3. **Evidence of planning:** volunteers were recruited with promises of land and cattle soon after the incident.
4. **Judgement:** the incident was the **trigger** (immediate cause), but the deeper causes made war likely anyway.
:::

## The course of the war (outline)

1. **The advance.** In October 1893 Company columns, made up of volunteers, Company police and some African allies, advanced from Fort Victoria and Fort Salisbury towards Bulawayo. A British force also moved up from the south.
2. **Shangani (late October 1893).** Ndebele regiments attacked the Company laager near the Shangani River. They fought bravely, but **Maxim guns** inflicted very heavy losses.
3. **Bembesi (1 November 1893).** Another attack by Ndebele regiments was again broken by machine-gun fire.
4. **The fall of Bulawayo.** Lobengula ordered his capital to be burnt and moved north. Company forces entered the ruins in early November 1893.
5. **Lobengula's flight.** The king moved towards the Zambezi. A patrol under **Major Allan Wilson** followed him across the Shangani River.
6. **The Shangani Patrol (December 1893).** Ndebele soldiers surrounded and killed all the members of Wilson's patrol. Settlers later made this a heroic legend; to the Ndebele, it was a victory in defence of their king.
7. **Lobengula's death.** Lobengula died in early 1894, in the north of the country; the exact circumstances are not certain. With no king, Ndebele leaders came to terms with the Company.

| Factor | Company forces | Ndebele forces |
|---|---|---|
| Weapons | Maxim guns, rifles, artillery | Spears, shields and some rifles (many soldiers not trained in their use) |
| Tactics | Defensive laagers (wagon circles) with machine guns | Massed attacks across open ground |
| Leadership | Unified command under the Company | Strong regiments, but the king's withdrawal reduced central command |

## Effects of the war

### On the Ndebele

- **Loss of the state:** the Ndebele kingdom was destroyed and the king was dead.
- **Loss of land:** the best land around Bulawayo was given to settlers; the 1894 Land Commission created the **Gwaai and Shangani reserves**, which were dry and far from markets.
- **Loss of cattle:** the Company and volunteers seized large numbers of Ndebele cattle, the main form of wealth.
- **Forced labour and tax:** Africans were forced to work for settlers and mines, and taxes were introduced.
- **Loss of authority:** izinduna lost their power, and the Company's African police, some of them recruited from former Ndebele subjects, often treated people harshly.
- **Anger and resentment**, which were among the causes of the **Umvukela/Chimurenga of 1896**.

### On the Company

- It gained control of **Matabeleland** as well as Mashonaland and opened it to settlers, prospectors and investment.
- Bulawayo was rebuilt as a settler town.
- Volunteers received land and gold claims, as promised.
- However, the war was costly, and the harsh treatment of the Ndebele led directly to the rising of 1896.

:::example{title="Reading a source about the reserves"}
A fictional official's note of 1895 says: "The natives have been given land in the Gwaai district, which is large enough for them."

1. **What it claims:** the reserve is adequate.
2. **What we know:** the Gwaai and Shangani reserves were dry, far from markets and lacked good water.
3. **Why the writer may say this:** officials wanted to justify giving the best land to settlers.
4. **Conclusion:** the note is useful evidence of the official view but not reliable about conditions.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling the Victoria incident the only cause"}
It was the trigger. Explain the deeper causes too: gold, land, cattle and the Company's wish to remove the Ndebele state.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the 1893 war and the 1896 rising"}
The Anglo-Ndebele War (1893-94) destroyed the Ndebele state. The Umvukela/Chimurenga (1896-97) was a rising against Company rule after the war.
:::

:::mistake{title="Glorifying either side"}
Describe the war factually: the Ndebele fought bravely, the Company had superior weapons, and both sides suffered losses.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Company wanted Matabeleland's land, cattle and possible gold, and saw the Ndebele state as an obstacle.
- The Victoria incident (July 1893) was the trigger; the Victoria Agreement promised volunteers land, gold claims and cattle.
- Maxim guns broke Ndebele attacks at Shangani and Bembesi; Bulawayo was burnt and occupied in November 1893.
- Wilson's Shangani Patrol was destroyed in December 1893; Lobengula died in early 1894.
- The Ndebele lost their state, land, cattle and freedom; the Gwaai and Shangani reserves were created; resentment led to the 1896 rising.
:::
