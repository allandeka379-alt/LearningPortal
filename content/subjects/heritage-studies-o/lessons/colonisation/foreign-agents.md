## Introduction

Many towns and schools in Zimbabwe carry names from the nineteenth century: Inyati Mission, Hope Fountain, Livingstone (across the Zambezi in Zambia), Selous. These names mark the arrival of foreigners who came to the lands of the Ndebele and Shona long before the Pioneer Column. Some came to preach, some to explore, some to trade or hunt, and some to obtain mining rights.

Historians call these groups the **forerunners** or **agents of colonisation**, because their activities, whatever their intentions, helped to prepare the way for colonial rule. In this Form 2 lesson you identify these groups and examine the role each played. You should already know about the Ndebele state under Mzilikazi and Lobengula, because many of these visitors came to the Ndebele king's court.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Missionary"}
A person sent by a church to spread its religion, often also setting up schools, clinics and mission stations.
:::

:::definition{term="Explorer"}
A person who travels to places unknown to their own people in order to describe them, often writing reports and drawing maps.
:::

:::definition{term="Concession"}
A written grant of rights, for example to mine minerals or use land, given by a ruler to a person or company, usually in return for payment.
:::

:::definition{term="Concession seeker"}
A person who tried to obtain a concession from an African ruler, often on behalf of a company or a powerful backer.
:::

:::definition{term="Forerunners of colonialism"}
The foreigners (missionaries, explorers, traders, hunters, concession seekers and adventurers) whose activities prepared the way for colonial rule.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and names to learn"}
**1859**: the London Missionary Society (LMS) opened **Inyati** mission, the first permanent mission station in Zimbabwe, with the permission of Mzilikazi. **1855**: David Livingstone reached the waterfall he named the **Victoria Falls** (Mosi-oa-Tunya). Hunters such as **Frederick Selous** and traders visited Lobengula's court; Selous later guided the Pioneer Column (1890).
:::

## The groups and their activities

| Group | Examples | Main activities |
|---|---|---|
| Missionaries | London Missionary Society at Inyati (1859) and Hope Fountain; Robert Moffat and his son John Smith Moffat; Jesuits later in the century | Preaching, setting up mission stations, schools and clinics; translating; advising and writing letters for the king |
| Explorers | David Livingstone; the German geologist Karl Mauch | Travelling, mapping, writing books and reports about the land, rivers, minerals and peoples |
| Traders | Many European traders at Bulawayo | Exchanging guns, cloth, beads, and other goods for ivory and cattle |
| Hunters | Frederick Selous; Henry Hartley | Hunting elephants for ivory and other game; learning routes and the land |
| Concession seekers | Charles Rudd, Rochfort Maguire and Francis Thompson (for Cecil Rhodes); agents of other companies | Seeking mining and land rights from Lobengula |
| Adventurers | Individuals seeking quick wealth or excitement | Prospecting, trading and later joining the occupation |

## The role of each group in colonisation

### Missionaries

Missionaries came mainly to spread Christianity. At first they made very few converts among the Ndebele. However, their work helped colonisation in several ways:

- They sent **reports** home describing the land, the people and the resources, which raised interest in Britain.
- Some described African societies negatively in their writings, which helped to justify colonial rule in European eyes.
- Their teaching sometimes **weakened** loyalty to traditional beliefs and leaders among converts.
- They acted as **interpreters, advisers and witnesses** for the king. John Smith Moffat helped to arrange the **Moffat Treaty** (1888), and the missionary Charles Helm interpreted and witnessed the **Rudd Concession** (1888).

Missionaries also introduced schools, literacy and Western medicine, which many Zimbabweans later used. Historians therefore see their role as mixed.

### Explorers

Explorers such as **David Livingstone** wrote popular books about central Africa. **Karl Mauch** reported gold in the region in the 1860s and visited Great Zimbabwe in 1871. Their reports and maps:

- **publicised** the land and its minerals, encouraging prospectors and investors;
- showed **routes** into the interior;
- encouraged the idea that Europe should bring "civilisation" and trade to Africa.

### Traders and hunters

Traders and hunters spent long periods at Lobengula's court and in the countryside. They:

- gathered **knowledge** of routes, water, the strength of the Ndebele army and the politics of the state;
- **depleted** elephant herds for ivory;
- brought **guns** into the region and created demand for European goods;
- later guided or joined the colonisers (Selous guided the Pioneer Column).

### Concession seekers and adventurers

Concession seekers played the most **direct** role. They competed for written rights from Lobengula. The most important was the **Rudd Concession** of 30 October 1888, obtained for Cecil Rhodes, which the British government later used to justify granting the Royal Charter to the British South Africa Company. Adventurers flocked in once gold was rumoured, adding pressure on the Ndebele state.

:::example{title="How a report becomes a reason to colonise"}
A fictional explorer's report of 1870 says: "Rich gold reefs, good grazing and a healthy climate on the high plateau."

1. **Readers in Europe:** investors and prospectors learn of possible wealth.
2. **Action:** prospectors and concession seekers travel to Bulawayo.
3. **Pressure:** the king faces many requests for mining rights.
4. **Result:** a concession is signed that opens the way to occupation.
5. **Lesson:** even without intending it, a writer could help to bring colonisation.
:::

:::example{title="Mixed effects: a mission station"}
A fictional mission station near Inyati opens in the 1860s.

1. **Positive for many later Zimbabweans:** a school teaches reading and writing; a clinic treats the sick.
2. **Negative for the state:** missionaries' letters describe the Ndebele unfavourably; converts are encouraged to abandon some customs.
3. **Role in colonisation:** missionaries serve as interpreters for concession seekers.
4. **Judgement:** the mission's role was mixed, and historians weigh both sides.
:::

| Group | Direct role (obtaining land or rights) | Indirect role (information, influence) |
|---|---|---|
| Missionaries | Interpreters and witnesses to treaties | Reports, changing beliefs, education |
| Explorers | Few | Maps, books, publicity about minerals |
| Traders and hunters | Guiding the occupation | Knowledge of routes and the Ndebele army; guns |
| Concession seekers | Obtained the Rudd Concession and others | Pressure on the king |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying missionaries came to colonise"}
Most missionaries came to spread Christianity. Their role in colonisation was often **indirect**. Explain how their activities helped colonisation, rather than claiming it was their only aim.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the Moffats"}
Robert Moffat was a famous LMS missionary and a friend of Mzilikazi. His son **John Smith Moffat** arranged the Moffat Treaty of 1888.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing the groups only"}
*Examine the role* questions need explanation. For each group say what they did **and how it helped colonisation**.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Identify and examine"}
For *identify the foreign groups* (Paper 2 Section A (b)), list missionaries, explorers, traders, hunters, concession seekers and adventurers. For *examine the role each played* (Section B (a) or (b)), take each group, give an example and explain its contribution to colonisation.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Which group mattered most?"}
A *how far* question might ask whether missionaries were the most important agents. Weigh them against concession seekers, whose role was most direct, and reach a judgement.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Missionaries, explorers, traders, hunters, concession seekers and adventurers came to Zimbabwe in the nineteenth century.
- The LMS opened Inyati mission in 1859; Livingstone reached the Victoria Falls in 1855; Mauch reported gold in the 1860s.
- Missionaries reported on the land, influenced beliefs and acted as interpreters and witnesses to treaties.
- Explorers, traders and hunters spread information about routes and resources; Selous guided the Pioneer Column.
- Concession seekers played the most direct role, obtaining the Rudd Concession (30 October 1888).
:::
