## Introduction

When Tafadzwa registered for her Form 4 examinations, the school asked for a copy of her birth certificate. When her older brother turned 16, he went with their mother to the Registrar General's office to apply for his national identity card. When their aunt took a job in another country, she first had to get a passport.

Each of these documents answers the same question: **who is this person?** In daily life we know who we are through our name, family, totem, language and faith. The State also needs proof of who we are, so it records each citizen in official documents. In this lesson you identify the forms of personal identity, describe the national identification documents and explain why they are important. The rules about who is a citizen are studied in the Constitution topic, and totems have their own lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Personal identity"}
The features that make a person a unique individual and show who they are and where they belong, such as name, family, totem, language, religion, nationality and identity documents.
:::

:::definition{term="National identification documents"}
Official documents issued by the State that prove who a person is and record their citizenship or nationality: the birth certificate, the national identity card and the passport.
:::

:::definition{term="Registrar General"}
The government department (under the Ministry of Home Affairs) that registers births and deaths and issues birth certificates, national identity cards and passports.
:::

:::definition{term="Birth registration"}
The official recording of a child's birth with the State, after which a birth certificate is issued.
:::

## Forms of personal identity

| Form of identity | What it tells others | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name and surname | Who the person is and which family they belong to; many names carry meaning | Nyasha Moyo |
| Family and clan | Parents, relatives, ancestry, home area | Belonging to an extended family in Masvingo Province |
| Totem (mutupo / isibongo) | Clan membership and ancestry | Shumba (lion), Nzou / Ndlovu (elephant) |
| Language | Community and culture | Speaking Tonga at home |
| Religion | Faith and the values a person follows | Christian, Muslim, indigenous religion |
| Nationality | The country a person belongs to as a citizen | Zimbabwean |
| Physical features | Appearance, photograph, fingerprints | A photograph on an ID card |
| Documents | Official proof of identity and citizenship | Birth certificate, ID card, passport |

Most forms of identity are learned in the family and community. Documents are different: they are **official proof**, issued by the State.

## The national identification documents

### The birth certificate

The birth certificate is the **first** official identity document. It is issued when a birth is registered with the Registrar General. Birth registration should be done soon after birth. Parents usually need the clinic or hospital record of the birth and their own identity documents.

It records, in outline: the child's full name, sex, date and place of birth, the names and particulars of the parents, and a registration number.

### The national identity card

The national identity (ID) card is issued from the age of **16**. The applicant usually needs a birth certificate and, in many cases, a parent or relative to confirm their identity.

It shows, in outline: a unique ID number, the holder's full name, date of birth, place of birth, village or district of origin, date of issue and a photograph of the holder.

### The passport

The passport is a travel document. It proves identity and nationality outside the country and is needed to cross most international borders. Applicants usually need a birth certificate and a national ID (or, for children, their parents' documents).

It shows, in outline: the holder's name, nationality, sex, date and place of birth, a photograph, the holder's signature, the passport number and the dates of issue and expiry.

| Document | When obtained | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Birth certificate | Soon after birth | Proves birth details and parents; needed for school, examinations and the ID card |
| National ID card | From age 16 | Proves identity inside Zimbabwe; needed to vote, work, open a bank account, register a phone line |
| Passport | When needed for travel | Proves identity and nationality abroad; needed to cross international borders |

:::example{title="One document leads to the next"}
Thandeka was born at a clinic in Lupane.

1. **Birth**: her parents take the clinic's birth record and their own IDs to the Registrar General's office, and Thandeka is issued a birth certificate.
2. **School**: the birth certificate is used to enrol her and, later, to register her for public examinations.
3. **Age 16**: she uses her birth certificate to apply for her national ID card.
4. **Age 19**: she uses her birth certificate and ID to apply for a passport to attend a youth conference in a neighbouring country.

Each document depends on the one before, which is why birth registration is so important.
:::

## Importance of national identification documents

1. **Proof of identity and citizenship**: they show who a person is and that they belong to Zimbabwe.
2. **Education**: a birth certificate is needed to enrol at school and to register for public examinations.
3. **Employment**: employers need proof of identity and age.
4. **Voting**: a citizen aged 18 or over needs identity documents to register as a voter and to vote.
5. **Travel**: a passport is needed to cross international borders.
6. **Services**: opening a bank account, registering a mobile phone line, getting a driver's licence, registering a marriage, receiving social assistance and inheriting property all require identity documents.
7. **Protection of children**: birth registration proves a child's age, which helps protect them from child marriage and child labour.
8. **Planning**: registration of births and deaths helps the government count the population and plan schools, clinics and other services.

:::example{title="The cost of having no birth certificate"}
Tatenda's parents died before registering his birth, and he lives with his grandmother in a village near Mutare.

1. **Problem at school**: when he reaches Form 4, he cannot be registered for examinations without a birth certificate.
2. **Future problem**: without a birth certificate he cannot get an ID card, so later he would struggle to find formal work, open a bank account or vote.
3. **Solution**: his grandmother, with help from the village head and the school, approaches the Registrar General's office to arrange a late registration, bringing witnesses and any records available.

The case shows why every child's birth should be registered as early as possible.
:::

### Looking after your documents

Keep documents safe and dry, never lend them to others, and make photocopies for everyday use. If a document is lost or stolen, report it to the police and apply to the Registrar General for a replacement. Using someone else's identity document, or a false one, is a crime.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking the ID card is the first document"}
The birth certificate is the first official identity document. The ID card comes at 16.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing identity with documents"}
Personal identity is much wider than documents: name, family, totem, language and religion are also forms of identity.
:::

:::mistake{title="Vague importance points"}
"IDs are important" earns nothing. Say what they allow: "A national ID is needed to register as a voter, so without it a citizen cannot vote."
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="List and describe contents"}
For *list the documents and describe the contents of each*, name all three and give two or three items each: "Passport: name, nationality, photograph, passport number, expiry date."
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Explain importance"}
For *explain*, link a document to a right or service: "A birth certificate proves a child's age (1), which protects the child from child marriage and child labour (1)."
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Personal identity includes name, family, totem, language, religion, nationality, physical features and documents.
- The national identification documents are the birth certificate, the national ID card (from 16) and the passport, issued by the Registrar General.
- Each records key details: names, dates and places of birth, parents or origin, numbers, and (for the ID and passport) a photograph.
- Documents prove identity and citizenship and are needed for school, examinations, work, voting, travel and many services.
- Birth registration protects children and helps the State plan services.
:::
