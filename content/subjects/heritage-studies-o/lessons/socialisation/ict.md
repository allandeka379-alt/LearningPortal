## Introduction

Nyasha's grandmother grew up learning from elders by the fire. Nyasha learns from a phone as well. She revises with videos, chats with her cousin in Johannesburg, follows musicians online and belongs to three group chats. Her grandmother worries that the phone teaches her to ignore elders; Nyasha points out that it also helped her find a bursary and join an online debate.

Both of them have a point. **Information and Communication Technologies (ICT)** are now one of the most powerful agents of socialisation, especially for young people in towns and, increasingly, in rural areas too. In this Form 4 lesson you study how ICT socialises people, its positive and negative effects, and how to use it safely. You should already know the role of the mass media, which is a separate lesson; here the focus is on devices, the internet and how people behave online.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Information and Communication Technologies (ICT)"}
Technologies used to create, store, process and exchange information, such as mobile phones, computers, tablets, the internet, email, messaging apps and social networking platforms.
:::

:::definition{term="Social networking"}
Using online platforms to create a profile, share content and interact with friends, family and strangers.
:::

:::definition{term="Cyberbullying"}
Using phones or the internet to threaten, insult, embarrass, exclude or spread rumours about someone, often repeatedly.
:::

:::definition{term="Digital footprint"}
The permanent record of everything a person posts, shares, likes or searches online, which others may find later.
:::

:::definition{term="Online safety"}
Protecting yourself and others online: keeping personal information private, using strong passwords, avoiding strangers and harmful content, and reporting abuse.
:::

## How ICT socialises young people

ICT combines many agents in one device. Through a phone a young person can talk to family, meet peers, read the news, watch entertainment, worship with a congregation and attend a lesson. This makes ICT a very strong influence on values, behaviour and identity.

| ICT tool | How it socialises |
| --- | --- |
| Mobile phones and messaging apps | Keep families and friends in touch; group chats set norms of language and behaviour |
| Social networking platforms | Shape identity, fashion and opinions; provide role models and peer approval through likes and comments |
| The internet and search engines | Give access to information on almost any topic, true or false |
| Online learning platforms and educational videos | Teach school subjects and new skills; allow distance learning |
| Online shopping and mobile money | Teach new ways of buying, selling and running small businesses |
| Online games | Teach teamwork and strategy, but can encourage violence or long hours of play |

## Positive effects of ICT on socialisation

- **Access to information and learning**: learners can revise, research and attend online classes; during school closures, for example during the COVID-19 pandemic, many learners continued to learn online or through radio and phone lessons.
- **Communication with family**: families separated by work in other towns or countries keep in touch by voice and video calls, so relationships and family values are maintained.
- **Enterprise and skills**: young people learn digital skills, sell goods online and use mobile money, which prepares them for work.
- **Promoting heritage**: people share music, folk tales, recipes, proverbs and history in indigenous languages, reaching young people who live far from their home areas.
- **Participation**: young people can join discussions about community issues and organise volunteer projects.

## Negative effects of ICT on socialisation

- **Cyberbullying**: hurtful messages, embarrassing photos and exclusion from groups can cause deep distress.
- **Addiction and time-wasting**: hours on the phone reduce study, sleep, exercise and family time.
- **Exposure to harmful content**: violent, sexual, hateful or extremist material, and content promoting drugs or gambling.
- **False information**: rumours and scams spread rapidly through groups.
- **Loss of indigenous values**: some young people copy online trends that clash with unhu/ubuntu, such as disrespect for elders, or spend less time learning from family.
- **Privacy and safety risks**: sharing personal details, location or photos can expose people to fraud, stalking or grooming by strangers.
- **Digital footprint**: a careless post can harm a person's reputation, schooling or job prospects years later.

:::example{title="A family uses ICT well"}
Themba's parents work in Bulawayo while he attends school in Lupane and lives with his grandparents.

1. **Communication**: every Sunday the family has a video call, so his parents still guide his behaviour and studies.
2. **Heritage**: his grandfather records voice notes of family history and totem praises, which Themba shares with his cousins.
3. **Learning**: Themba uses revision videos to prepare for examinations.
4. **Rules**: his grandparents ask him to switch the phone off during meals and after 21:00.

ICT here strengthens family bonds and heritage, because it is used with clear rules.
:::

:::example{title="Cyberbullying in a group chat"}
In a fictional Form 3 group chat in Kariba, some members start posting jokes about Chipo's clothes and then remove her from the group.

1. **Effect**: Chipo feels humiliated and stops attending school for a few days.
2. **Bystanders**: classmates who laughed or kept quiet allowed the bullying to continue.
3. **Response**: Chipo keeps screenshots and tells her class teacher; the school deals with the bullies under its code of conduct and runs a lesson on online respect.
4. **Lesson**: online behaviour has real consequences. Bullying online is still bullying.
:::

## Using ICT safely and responsibly

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Keep passwords secret and use privacy settings | Share your address, school, location or ID number with strangers |
| Think before you post: would you be happy for your family to see it? | Post or forward embarrassing images or messages about others |
| Check information before sharing it | Agree to meet an online stranger in person |
| Report cyberbullying and abuse to a trusted adult, the platform or the police | Reply to bullies or threats with abuse |
| Set limits on screen time and keep phone-free times | Use the phone during lessons, meals or late at night |
| Respect others online as you would face to face | Download from unknown links or give out codes sent to your phone |

Sharing private images of a person or threatening someone online can be a criminal offence, and anyone who is being harmed online should tell a trusted adult and, where necessary, the police.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Seeing ICT as wholly good or wholly bad"}
Examiners expect both positive and negative effects. The effect depends on how ICT is used.
:::

:::mistake{title="Repeating media points without the ICT angle"}
For ICT, refer to devices, the internet and online behaviour: cyberbullying, digital footprints, online learning, mobile money, privacy.
:::

:::mistake{title="Vague negative effects"}
"Phones are bad" earns nothing. Explain: "Long hours on social media reduce study time, so results may fall."
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Describe the role of ICT"}
In Paper 2 Section A, name an ICT tool and say what it teaches or does: "Video calls (1) let parents working abroad keep guiding their children (1)."
:::

:::exam-tip{title="How far does ICT threaten indigenous values?"}
For a Section B essay, argue both ways: ICT spreads foreign trends and reduces time with elders, but it also records and shares heritage in indigenous languages. Conclude that the threat depends on how families and communities use it.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- ICT (phones, computers, the internet, social networking, messaging, online learning) is a powerful agent of socialisation that combines many agents in one device.
- Positive effects: information and learning, family communication, enterprise skills, sharing heritage, participation.
- Negative effects: cyberbullying, addiction, harmful content, false information, loss of indigenous values, privacy risks, a permanent digital footprint.
- Safe use means protecting personal information, thinking before posting, checking information, reporting abuse and limiting screen time.
:::
