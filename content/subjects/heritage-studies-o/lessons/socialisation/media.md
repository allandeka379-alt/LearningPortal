## Introduction

Before school, Tendai's family listens to the morning news on the radio. On the bus he reads the sports page of a newspaper someone left behind. In the evening his sister watches a drama series, and before bed his cousin forwards a video that "everybody is sharing". By the end of the day Tendai has received more messages from the media than from his teachers.

The **media** are an agent of secondary socialisation: they shape what people know, what they admire, what they buy and even how they speak. Because the media reach so many people at once, learning to judge them is an important life skill. This lesson covers the types of media, their role and effects, and how to use media content responsibly. Phones, the internet and online behaviour are studied more closely in the lesson on ICT.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Media"}
The channels used to communicate information, ideas and entertainment to many people at once, such as newspapers, radio, television, film and social media.
:::

:::definition{term="Mass media"}
Media that reach a very large audience at the same time, such as national radio and television stations and widely read newspapers.
:::

:::definition{term="Stereotype"}
A fixed, oversimplified idea about a group of people, for example that only men can be engineers or that all young people are lazy.
:::

:::definition{term="False information"}
Content that is untrue. **Misinformation** is shared by people who believe it; **disinformation** is spread on purpose to deceive.
:::

:::definition{term="Media literacy"}
The ability to find, understand, question and judge media messages, and to create and share content responsibly.
:::

## Types of media

| Type | Examples | Main features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Print media | Newspapers, magazines, books, newsletters, posters | Can be kept and re-read; need reading skills; slower to update |
| Electronic (broadcast) media | Radio, television, film | Reach people who cannot read; radio reaches remote areas; sound and pictures attract attention |
| Social and digital media | Social networking sites, messaging groups, video-sharing sites, online news | Fast; anyone can create content; two-way (users comment and share); hard to check |

Radio is especially important in Zimbabwe because it reaches rural areas in many languages and needs no electricity if a battery or wind-up set is used. Community radio stations broadcast in local languages, which helps to keep those languages alive.

## The role of the media in socialisation

The media socialise people in several ways:

1. **Informing**: news tells people what is happening at home and abroad, including weather warnings, health campaigns and government announcements.
2. **Educating**: school radio lessons, documentaries and farming programmes teach knowledge and skills.
3. **Entertaining**: music, drama, sport and comedy, which also carry values and models of behaviour.
4. **Providing role models**: musicians, athletes and characters in dramas are copied by young people in dress, speech and attitudes.
5. **Spreading values and culture**: programmes can promote national values, languages and heritage, or bring in foreign values and fashions.
6. **Advertising**: adverts create wants and influence what people buy, eat and wear.
7. **Shaping opinions**: the way stories are reported affects how people see events, groups and leaders.

:::example{title="A health message on the radio"}
During a cholera outbreak, a radio station plays a short drama in Shona, Ndebele and English about washing hands and treating drinking water.

1. **Informing**: listeners learn that there is an outbreak and where to get help.
2. **Educating**: they learn how cholera spreads and how to prevent it.
3. **Changing behaviour**: families start boiling or treating drinking water.

This shows the media acting as a positive agent of socialisation, reaching many people quickly in their own languages.
:::

## Advantages and disadvantages of the media as a socialising agent

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| Reach many people quickly, including remote areas | Can spread false information and rumours quickly |
| Educate (school broadcasts, documentaries, health campaigns) | Can expose children to violence, explicit content or bad language |
| Promote national languages, music and heritage | Can promote foreign values and weaken respect for indigenous culture |
| Provide positive role models and entertainment | Can create harmful stereotypes (about gender, ethnic groups, disability) |
| Help people take part in national debates | Advertising encourages wasteful spending and unhealthy products |
| Give warnings (droughts, storms, disease outbreaks) | Long viewing can replace family time, study and physical activity |

## Using media content responsibly

Appropriate content suits the viewer's age, is truthful and respects other people. Inappropriate content includes violent, sexual or hateful material, content that mocks a group of people, and false information.

Responsible users of the media:

- **check the source**: who made this, and are they reliable? Is it from a known news organisation or an anonymous forward?
- **check the date**: old stories are often shared as if they were new;
- **compare with other sources**: if a big event is true, several reliable sources will report it;
- **notice the purpose**: is the content trying to inform, to sell something or to make people angry?
- **choose age-appropriate content** and follow age ratings on films and games;
- **think before sharing**: if in doubt, do not forward;
- **balance media use** with study, family time, sleep and exercise.

:::example{title="Checking a shocking message"}
Rudo receives a message on a group chat claiming that all schools will close tomorrow because of a storm. It has no source.

1. **Source**: the message does not say who wrote it. Rudo notes that it is anonymous.
2. **Check**: she looks for an announcement from the Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education or her school, and listens to the radio news. Neither mentions closures.
3. **Date**: a search shows a similar message circulated two years earlier.
4. **Decision**: she does not forward it and tells the group that there is no official announcement.

Rudo has stopped false information from spreading, which is part of responsible media use.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the media are only bad for young people"}
The media inform, educate and entertain. Balanced answers give both advantages and disadvantages.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the media with ICT"}
The media are channels of mass communication (newspapers, radio, television, social media). ICT is the wider technology (devices, the internet, computers) and is a separate concept.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing media without explaining their effect"}
"Television" earns little on its own. Explain: "Television provides role models whose dress and speech young people copy."
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Identify and describe"}
For Paper 2 Section A, *identify types of media* wants examples from the three groups: print, electronic and social media. *Describe their role* wants a function with an example.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Advantages and disadvantages"}
In an essay, pair the points: the media spread information quickly, but they also spread false information quickly. Finish with a judgement that depends on responsible use.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The media are channels that communicate to many people: print, electronic (radio, television, film) and social media.
- They socialise by informing, educating, entertaining, providing role models, spreading values and advertising.
- Advantages include fast information, education and promoting heritage; disadvantages include false information, harmful content, stereotypes and foreign values.
- Responsible use means checking the source, the date and other reports, choosing age-appropriate content and thinking before sharing.
:::
