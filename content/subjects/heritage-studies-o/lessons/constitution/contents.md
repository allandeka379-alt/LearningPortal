## Introduction

A Form 3 class in Bulawayo is debating whether learners should be allowed to answer exam questions in Ndebele. One pupil says, "It is our right. The Constitution recognises our language." Another asks, "Which part of the Constitution says that?" The teacher smiles and hands out a small printed copy of the Constitution of Zimbabwe. "Let us find out together," she says.

The Constitution can look like a long and difficult document, but it is organised in a logical way, and once you know its main parts you can find almost anything in it. In this lesson you will learn how the 2013 Constitution is structured, what its founding values and national objectives are, which features make it distinctive, and why it matters to every citizen, including you. You should already know what a constitution is and how the 2013 Constitution was made through outreach and a referendum.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Founding values and principles"}
The basic beliefs on which the nation and its government are built, listed in section 3 of the Constitution, such as the supremacy of the Constitution, the rule of law and gender equality.
:::

:::definition{term="Supremacy of the Constitution"}
The principle (section 2) that the Constitution is the highest law: any law, practice, custom or conduct inconsistent with it is invalid to the extent of the inconsistency.
:::

:::definition{term="Rule of law"}
The principle that everyone, including leaders and the state, is subject to the law and that laws are applied fairly and equally.
:::

:::definition{term="Devolution"}
The transfer of some powers and responsibilities from the national government to provincial and metropolitan councils and local authorities.
:::

:::definition{term="National objectives"}
Goals that guide the state in making laws and policies (Chapter 2), such as national unity, good governance, food security, and the welfare of children, youth, the elderly and persons with disabilities.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Facts to learn"}
- The Constitution has **18 chapters**.
- **Section 1:** Zimbabwe is a unitary, democratic and sovereign republic. **Section 2:** supremacy of the Constitution. **Section 3:** founding values and principles. **Section 4:** national flag, anthem, public seal and coat of arms. **Section 6:** sixteen officially recognised languages.
- **Chapter 4** is the Declaration of Rights.
- The Constitution has been amended since 2013 (in 2017 and 2021); amendments must follow a special procedure.
:::

## The structure of the Constitution

| Chapter | Title (in short) | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Founding provisions | The republic, supremacy, founding values, national symbols, tiers of government, languages |
| 2 | National objectives | Goals guiding the state: unity, peace, good governance, development, empowerment, care for vulnerable groups, culture |
| 3 | Citizenship | Citizenship by birth, descent and registration |
| 4 | Declaration of Rights | Fundamental human rights and freedoms, their enforcement and limits |
| 5 | The Executive | The President, Vice-Presidents, Cabinet |
| 6 | The Legislature | Parliament: the Senate and the National Assembly |
| 7 | Elections | Electoral system, voting, conduct of elections |
| 8 | The Judiciary and the courts | Independence of the courts; the Constitutional Court, Supreme Court, High Court and others |
| 9 | Public administration and leadership | Principles for public officers: honesty, accountability, efficiency |
| 10 | Civil service | The Civil Service Commission and the civil service |
| 11 | Security services | Defence forces, police, intelligence, prisons and correctional service |
| 12 | Independent commissions supporting democracy | Electoral, Human Rights, Gender, Media, and Peace and Reconciliation commissions |
| 13 | Institutions to combat corruption and crime | The anti-corruption commission and the national prosecuting authority |
| 14 | Provincial and local government | Provincial and metropolitan councils, urban and rural local authorities, devolution |
| 15 | Traditional leaders | Chiefs, headmen and village heads; their roles and limits |
| 16 | Agricultural land | Land reform and the ownership of agricultural land |
| 17 | Finance | Taxes, public money, the budget, the Auditor-General |
| 18 | General and supplementary provisions | Interpretation, amendment and transitional matters |

You do not need to memorise every chapter number, but you should know the main ones: Chapter 2 (national objectives), Chapter 3 (citizenship), Chapter 4 (rights), Chapters 5 to 8 (the arms of government and elections), Chapter 12 (independent commissions) and Chapter 14 (provincial and local government).

## Founding values and principles

Section 3 lists the values on which Zimbabwe is founded. They include:

- the **supremacy of the Constitution** and the **rule of law**;
- **fundamental human rights and freedoms**;
- recognition of the **inherent dignity and worth** of every human being, and the **equality** of all human beings;
- **gender equality**;
- **good governance**;
- recognition of and respect for the **liberation struggle**.

Section 3 also sets out principles of good governance, such as a multi-party democratic political system, free, fair and regular elections, transparency, justice, accountability, the separation of powers, and the devolution of governmental power.

## Key features and their significance

| Key feature | Where | Why it is significant |
|---|---|---|
| Supremacy of the Constitution | Section 2 | No law, custom or official is above it, which protects citizens from abuse of power |
| Declaration of Rights | Chapter 4 | Rights and freedoms are protected and can be enforced in court |
| Separation of powers | Chapters 5, 6 and 8 | Executive, Legislature and Judiciary check one another, so no single body has all power |
| Presidential term limit | Chapter 5 | A President may serve at most two five-year terms, which supports peaceful change of leadership |
| Three tiers of government | Section 5 and Chapter 14 | National government, provincial and metropolitan councils, and local authorities; devolution brings decisions closer to the people |
| Sixteen official languages | Section 6 | Recognises the country's diversity and the right to use one's language |
| Independent commissions | Chapters 12 and 13 | Bodies outside the Executive that support democracy, monitor rights and fight corruption |
| Traditional leaders | Chapter 15 | Recognises chiefs and their role in custom and community, while requiring them to be non-partisan |
| Agricultural land | Chapter 16 | Gives constitutional status to land reform and rules on land ownership |
| Rights to education and health care | Chapter 4 | Places duties on the state to provide basic services, as resources allow |

:::example{title="Finding the answer in the Constitution"}
The Bulawayo class returns to its debate about language.

1. **Locate:** section 6 lists sixteen officially recognised languages, including Ndebele, Shona, English and sign language.
2. **Read the duty:** the state must ensure that the languages are treated equitably and must promote and advance their use.
3. **Apply:** the Constitution supports the use and development of Ndebele, though how exams are set is decided by laws and policies made under it.
4. **Lesson:** knowing the structure lets citizens check their rights for themselves.
:::

## Why the Constitution is important

- It is the **supreme law**, so it protects citizens from unlawful actions by the state or anyone else.
- It **protects rights** and gives citizens a way to defend them.
- It **limits and shares power** through the separation of powers, term limits, elections and independent commissions.
- It **unites the nation** by setting out shared values and recognising diversity.
- It **guides government** through national objectives on unity, development and care for vulnerable groups.
- It **brings government closer to the people** through devolution.

:::example{title="A feature in daily life"}
Themba, a market trader in Mutare, wants the council to repair the market roof.

1. **Tier of government:** markets are a local authority matter (Chapter 14).
2. **Principle:** accountability and transparency (section 3) mean councillors must explain their decisions.
3. **Action:** Themba raises the issue with his ward councillor and at a council meeting.
4. **Lesson:** the Constitution shapes everyday services, not only national politics.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing founding values with rights"}
Founding values (section 3) are principles the nation is built on; rights (Chapter 4) belong to individuals and can be enforced in court.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing features without significance"}
"Sixteen languages" alone earns one mark. Explain: "it recognises diversity and allows people to use their own language".
:::

:::mistake{title="Wrong number of chapters or languages"}
The Constitution has 18 chapters and recognises 16 languages. Do not swap the numbers.
:::

:::

:::

:::exam-tip

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The 2013 Constitution has 18 chapters, from founding provisions to general provisions.
- Section 2 makes it supreme; section 3 lists founding values such as the rule of law, human rights, equality, gender equality, good governance and respect for the liberation struggle.
- Chapter 2 sets national objectives; Chapter 4 is the Declaration of Rights.
- Key features include separation of powers, presidential term limits, three tiers of government with devolution, sixteen official languages, independent commissions, and provisions on traditional leaders and land.
- It matters because it protects rights, limits and shares power, unites the nation and guides government.
:::
