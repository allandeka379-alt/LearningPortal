## Introduction

Many school leavers in Zimbabwe will not find a formal job straight away. Some will wait; others will create work for themselves and for others. A learner who keeps chickens during the holidays, a group of friends who record and sell their own music, a young woman who designs and sews clothes, a potter who sells to tourists at Great Zimbabwe: all of them are using **enterprise skills**.

In this Form 4 lesson you learn what enterprise is, the skills it needs, how **self-reliance projects** and **creative industries** create employment, and how enterprise helps the economy grow. You should already know the factors of production, especially entrepreneurship, and the informal sector.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Enterprise"}
The willingness and ability to start and run a project or business, seeing opportunities and taking risks to make a profit or meet a need. The word is also used for a business itself.
:::

:::definition{term="Enterprise skills"}
The abilities a person needs to identify opportunities and turn them into successful projects, such as creativity, risk-taking, planning, financial literacy and marketing.
:::

:::definition{term="Self-reliance project"}
A project through which a person, school or community produces goods or services to meet its own needs and earn income, instead of depending on others.
:::

:::definition{term="Creative industries"}
Industries based on individual creativity, skill and talent, such as music, art, film, theatre, dance, poetry, fashion and crafts, that produce goods, performances and services for sale.
:::

:::definition{term="Financial literacy"}
The knowledge and skills needed to manage money well: budgeting, saving, recording income and spending, pricing and understanding loans and interest.
:::

## The main enterprise skills

| Skill | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Creativity and innovation | Thinking of new products, services or better ways of doing things | Designing school bags from recycled material |
| Risk-taking | Being willing to invest time and money without being sure of success, after weighing the risks | Using savings to buy the first batch of chicks |
| Planning | Setting goals, deciding the steps, resources and time needed, and writing a simple business plan | Planning broilers to be ready before the holidays |
| Financial literacy | Budgeting, keeping records, calculating costs, pricing and saving profits | Recording every dollar spent on feed and every bird sold |
| Marketing | Finding out what customers want and telling them about the product | Selling eggs to neighbours and a local shop; advertising on social media |
| Communication and negotiation | Explaining ideas, persuading customers and suppliers, agreeing prices | Agreeing a lower price for bulk feed |
| Problem-solving and decision-making | Finding solutions when things go wrong | Changing supplier when deliveries are late |
| Teamwork and leadership | Working with and leading others | Sharing tasks in a school club |
| Perseverance | Continuing after setbacks | Starting again after losing birds to disease |

:::example{title="Rudo's sewing project: enterprise skills at work"}
Rudo, a Form 4 learner in Masvingo, notices that many parents buy school uniforms from far away.

1. **Creativity:** she plans to sew uniforms locally and offer alterations.
2. **Planning and financial literacy:** she lists her costs (a second-hand sewing machine, cloth, thread, transport) and works out a price that covers costs and leaves a profit.
3. **Risk-taking:** she borrows part of the money from a savings club, knowing she must repay it.
4. **Marketing:** she shows samples at a school meeting and offers a small discount for early orders.
5. **Results:** she earns money for her fees, employs her cousin at busy times and keeps records that show her profit.
:::

## Self-reliance projects

Self-reliance projects help individuals, schools and communities to meet their own needs and earn income. Common examples include:

- **Agricultural projects:** gardens, poultry, rabbits, goats, fish ponds, beekeeping, fruit trees.
- **Production projects:** sewing, knitting, soap and candle making, peanut butter, baking, carpentry, welding, brick-making.
- **Service projects:** hairdressing, phone repairs, photography, catering.

Many schools run such projects as part of the curriculum. They teach practical skills, raise funds for the school and show learners that they can create work rather than only look for it.

## Creative industries and employment

Zimbabwe has a rich cultural heritage and many talented artists. The creative industries turn talent into livelihoods.

| Creative industry | How it creates employment and income |
|---|---|
| Music | Musicians, producers, sound engineers, promoters and sellers earn from performances, recordings and online platforms |
| Visual art and sculpture | Painters and stone sculptors sell locally and abroad; galleries and exhibitions employ staff |
| Sport | Players, coaches, referees, groundsmen, sports journalists and kit makers earn incomes |
| Dance | Traditional and contemporary dance groups perform at festivals, ceremonies and for tourists |
| Pottery, basketry and weaving | Crafters sell to households and tourists; designs carry cultural heritage |
| Poetry and writing | Poets and writers perform, publish and teach |
| Theatre and film | Actors, directors, camera operators, costume designers and editors work on plays, films and online content |
| Fashion and design | Designers and tailors make clothing using local fabrics and patterns |

Creative work is also protected by **copyright** law, which gives creators rights over their work so that others cannot copy and sell it without permission. Knowing these rights helps artists earn from what they create.

:::example{title="A community dance group becomes an enterprise"}
A traditional dance group in a fictional village near Hwange performs at weddings and ceremonies.

1. **Opportunity:** tourists visiting the national park want to see local culture.
2. **Plan:** the group agrees with a lodge to perform twice a week for a fee, and makes and sells drums and beaded crafts after each show.
3. **Skills used:** negotiation (agreeing the fee), marketing (posters and social media), financial literacy (sharing income fairly and saving for costumes), teamwork.
4. **Employment created:** dancers, drummers, crafters and a member who manages bookings.
5. **Wider benefit:** income stays in the community, and young people learn and value traditional dances.
:::

## How enterprise contributes to economic growth

- **Employment:** new businesses employ the owner and often others, reducing unemployment.
- **Incomes and living standards:** families earn money for food, fees and health care.
- **Production and import substitution:** local products replace some imports, saving foreign currency.
- **Exports and tourism:** crafts, art and music earn foreign currency.
- **Taxes:** businesses that grow and register pay taxes that fund public services.
- **Innovation:** new ideas and technologies improve productivity.
- **Use of local resources:** clay, stone, grass, wood and agricultural produce are turned into products of higher value.
- **Heritage:** creative industries preserve and share culture.

Enterprise also faces difficulties, such as lack of capital, small markets, copying of creative work and the risk of failure. Support such as training, access to finance, workspaces and protection of creators' rights helps more enterprises succeed.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Equating enterprise only with big business"}
Enterprise includes small self-reliance projects such as a garden, a poultry run or a dance group. Size does not matter; skills and initiative do.
:::

:::mistake{title="Naming skills without examples"}
"Marketing" alone earns less than "marketing: Rudo shows samples at a school meeting to attract buyers".
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring risk"}
Enterprise involves risk. Good answers show how planning and financial literacy reduce it.
:::

## Summary

- Self-reliance projects help people and schools meet their needs and earn income.
- Creative industries such as music, art, sport, dance, crafts, theatre and film create many jobs.
- Enterprise contributes to growth through jobs, incomes, local production, exports, taxes and innovation.
