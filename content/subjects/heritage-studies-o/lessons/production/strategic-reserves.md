## Introduction

A careful family does not eat all its harvest at once. It keeps some grain in the granary for the hungry months before the next harvest, and some seed for planting. If the rains fail, that store may be the difference between eating and going hungry.

A nation does the same on a much larger scale. The government keeps **strategic reserves** of essential goods, especially grain and fuel, so that the country can cope with droughts, floods, supply interruptions and sudden price rises. In this Form 3 lesson you learn what national strategic reserves are, which ones Zimbabwe keeps, what they are for, why they matter and why they are difficult to maintain. You should already know about food storage at household level and about the importance of food security.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="National strategic reserve"}
A stock of an essential commodity, such as grain or fuel, held by or for the state so that the country can meet its needs in an emergency or shortage.
:::

:::definition{term="Strategic grain reserve"}
A national stock of grain, mainly maize, kept to protect the country from food shortages.
:::

:::definition{term="Food security"}
A situation in which all people at all times have enough safe and nutritious food.
:::

:::definition{term="Buffer stock"}
A stock that is added to when supply is high and released when supply is low, to keep supply and prices steady.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Institutions to know"}
The **Grain Marketing Board (GMB)** buys, stores and sells grain and holds the **strategic grain reserve**. Fuel reserves were formerly managed through the **National Oil Company of Zimbabwe (NOCZIM)**, which was later restructured into separate state companies.
:::

## Examples of strategic reserves in Zimbabwe

| Reserve | Who holds it | What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic grain reserve | The Grain Marketing Board, in depots and silos across the country | Mainly maize; also other grains such as wheat and small grains |
| Fuel reserves | State oil bodies (formerly NOCZIM) and storage depots | Petrol and diesel |
| Other stocks | Government and its agencies | For example, seed and fertiliser held for input schemes; some countries also hold medicines and other essentials |

### How the strategic grain reserve works

1. **Buying:** in a good season, GMB buys grain from farmers at its depots across the country.
2. **Storing:** grain is dried, treated against pests and stored in silos and warehouses.
3. **Rotating:** older stocks are sold, for example to millers, and replaced with new grain so that the reserve does not spoil.
4. **Releasing:** when harvests fail or prices rise sharply, grain is released to millers, to areas in need and for drought relief.

:::example{title="A drought year"}
Rains in a fictional season fail across Masvingo and Matabeleland South, and many families harvest little.

1. **The problem:** household granaries are empty months before the next harvest, and maize prices in local markets rise.
2. **Use of the reserve:** grain from the strategic reserve is released to millers and to food-relief programmes in the affected districts.
3. **Effect:** families have food, and the extra supply helps to stop prices from rising further.
4. **Afterwards:** in the next good season, GMB buys grain from farmers to rebuild the reserve.
:::

## Functions of national strategic reserves

| Function | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Food security | Ensures there is grain to feed people when harvests fail |
| Fuel security | Keeps vehicles, farms, factories and emergency services running if imports are interrupted |
| Price stability | Releasing stocks when supply is short stops prices from rising too steeply; buying when there is plenty supports farmers' prices |
| Emergency relief | Supplies food after disasters such as droughts, floods and cyclones (for example after Cyclone Idai in March 2019) |
| Bridging gaps in supply | Covers the period between harvests or delays in imports |
| National security and sovereignty | Reduces dependence on imports and emergency food aid |
| A market for farmers | Gives farmers a buyer for their grain, encouraging production |

## Significance of strategic reserves

Strategic reserves matter because Zimbabwe's farming depends mainly on rain, and droughts are common. Many households, especially in drier regions, grow too little to last the whole year. Fuel is imported through neighbouring countries, so supply can be interrupted by problems along the route or shortages of foreign currency. Without reserves, a single bad season or supply disruption could cause hunger, sharp price rises and even unrest.

Reserves also link the national level to the household level. A family granary protects one household; the national reserve protects the whole country when many granaries are empty at once.

:::example{title="Fuel supply interrupted"}
A fictional problem delays fuel imports for three weeks.

1. **Without reserves:** queues form, buses stop running, farmers cannot plough, and prices rise.
2. **With reserves:** stored fuel keeps ambulances, transport and essential industries going while imports resume.
3. **Lesson:** fuel reserves protect the whole economy from short interruptions, but they must be large enough and well managed.
:::

## Reserves at every level

Strategic reserves are not only a national matter. They work at three levels, and each supports the others.

- **Household level:** families keep grain in granaries, pots or bags, dry vegetables and save seed. This protects them through the hungry months and allows them to plant again without buying seed.
- **Community level:** in some communities, families have long contributed grain or labour to a store kept under the authority of the chief (known in Shona as Zunde raMambo), from which needy households could be helped in a bad year. Today, community gardens, irrigation schemes and savings groups play a similar role.
- **National level:** the strategic grain reserve and fuel stocks protect the whole country when many households are affected at once.

When households and communities are prepared, the national reserve does not have to carry the whole burden. This is why advice on household food storage, drought-tolerant crops and saving is part of national food-security planning.

## Challenges of keeping strategic reserves

| Challenge | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Cost and funding | Buying, storing and transporting grain or fuel needs large amounts of money and foreign currency |
| Storage losses | Pests, damp and poor facilities can spoil grain; fuel tanks and silos need maintenance |
| Repeated droughts | Several poor seasons in a row can empty the reserve before it is rebuilt |
| Late payment of farmers | If farmers are paid late, some sell to other buyers, and the reserve is harder to fill |
| Side marketing | Farmers who received inputs may sell their grain elsewhere instead of to GMB |
| Transport and roads | Moving grain to remote areas is costly and slow |
| Price setting | If the buying price is too low, farmers will not sell; if too high, costs to government rise |
| Accountability | Poor record-keeping, theft or corruption can reduce stocks; audits are needed |

### Possible solutions

- Paying farmers promptly and at fair prices so they sell to the reserve.
- Improving storage: repairing silos, using hermetic bags and fumigation, and rotating stocks.
- Encouraging drought-tolerant small grains and irrigation, so harvests are more reliable.
- Partnerships with private storage companies and regional cooperation through SADC.
- Regular audits and published records to improve transparency.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking the reserve is only for drought relief"}
Reserves also stabilise prices, supply millers between harvests, give farmers a market and cover interruptions in imports.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the institutions"}
The GMB holds the grain reserve. Fuel reserves were associated with NOCZIM. Do not invent names or figures for other bodies.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A national strategic reserve is a state-held stock of essential goods for emergencies and shortages.
- Zimbabwe's main reserve is the strategic grain reserve held by the Grain Marketing Board; fuel reserves were formerly managed through NOCZIM.
- Reserves provide food and fuel security, stabilise prices and supply emergency relief.
- They matter because farming depends on rain and fuel is imported.
- Challenges include cost, storage losses, repeated droughts, late payment of farmers and accountability.
:::
