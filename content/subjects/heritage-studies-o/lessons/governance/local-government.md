## Introduction

When the tap runs dry, the refuse is not collected or the street lights go out, people in Zimbabwe do not usually call the President or a minister. They call the **council**. Local authorities provide many of the services that affect daily life most directly: water, sewage, refuse collection, roads, clinics, markets and housing stands. In rural areas, chiefs, headmen and village heads also play an important part in local governance.

In this Form 4 lesson you learn how government is organised below the national level, what devolution means, how councils are run and funded, what they do, and how citizens can take part. You should already know the three arms of central government.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Local government"}
The level of government that runs services and makes by-laws for a particular town, city or rural district, through elected councils.
:::

:::definition{term="Local authority"}
A council responsible for an area: an **urban council** (city, municipality, town council or local board) or a **rural district council (RDC)**.
:::

:::definition{term="Devolution"}
The transfer of powers and responsibilities from central government to provincial, metropolitan and local authorities, so that decisions are made closer to the people.
:::

:::definition{term="By-law"}
A rule made by a local authority that applies only in its area, for example on refuse disposal, street trading or keeping animals.
:::

:::definition{term="Rates"}
A charge that property owners pay to the council, based on the value of their property, to help fund local services.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Facts to learn"}
**Chapter 14** of the Constitution deals with provincial and local government and devolution; **Chapter 15** with traditional leaders. Zimbabwe has **ten provinces**: eight provinces plus the metropolitan provinces of **Harare** and **Bulawayo**. Councillors are elected for **wards** at the harmonised elections every five years.
:::

## Tiers of government

| Tier | Bodies | Main responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| National (central) | President and Cabinet, Parliament, the courts | National laws, defence, foreign affairs, the national budget, national policies |
| Provincial and metropolitan | Provincial and metropolitan councils | Planning and coordinating social and economic development in the province |
| Local | Urban councils and rural district councils | Local services, by-laws, local development |

The Constitution sets out the principle of **devolution**: powers and resources should be given to provincial and local tiers where appropriate, so that communities manage their own affairs and development is shared fairly across the country. It also requires that a share of national revenue be allocated to provinces and local authorities each year. Supporters of devolution argue that it brings decisions closer to the people; others point out that it depends on adequate funds and skilled staff.

## How councils are organised

| Urban council | Rural district council |
|---|---|
| Covers a city, town or municipality | Covers a rural district, including communal and resettlement areas |
| Councillors elected by wards | Councillors elected by wards |
| Led by a **mayor** (or chairperson of a town council or local board), chosen by the councillors | Led by a **chairperson**, chosen by the councillors |
| Run day to day by the **town clerk** and council officials (engineers, health officers, treasurer) | Run day to day by a **chief executive officer** and council officials |
| Committees, e.g. health, housing, finance | Committees, plus ward and village development committees |

**Councillors** are elected representatives who make policy, pass the budget and by-laws, and represent their wards. **Council officials** are employed staff who carry out the council's decisions. The **Ministry of Local Government** supervises local authorities on behalf of central government.

## Functions of local authorities

- Supplying clean **water** and removing **sewage**.
- Collecting and disposing of **refuse**.
- Building and maintaining local **roads**, bridges, street lights and storm drains.
- Running **clinics**, and in some areas schools, libraries and community halls.
- Allocating **housing stands** and approving building plans.
- Running **markets**, bus termini, parks, cemeteries and sports facilities.
- **Licensing** businesses and controlling street trading.
- **Fire and ambulance** services in towns.
- Making and enforcing **by-laws**.
- Planning local development and protecting the local environment.

:::example{title="A council explains a by-law"}
A fictional town council in Marondera passes a by-law banning the dumping of refuse on open land, with a fine for offenders.

1. **Problem:** illegal dumps attract flies and rats and block drains, raising the risk of cholera.
2. **Process:** the draft by-law is published and residents comment at ward meetings before councillors vote.
3. **Council's duty:** it must collect refuse regularly so that residents have a lawful alternative.
4. **Citizens' duty:** use the bins and collection points and report illegal dumping.
5. **Lesson:** a by-law works only when the council provides the service and residents cooperate.
:::

## Sources of income

| Source | Example |
|---|---|
| Rates | Charges on residential, commercial and industrial property |
| Service charges | Water bills, refuse and sewer charges |
| Licences and fees | Shop, vendor and liquor licences; market stall fees; building-plan fees |
| Rents and sales | Rents for council houses; sale of stands |
| Grants from central government | Devolution funds and other transfers |
| Partners and loans | Donor-funded projects; loans for major works |

## Traditional leaders in local governance

In communal areas, **chiefs**, **headmen** and **village heads** (sabhuku in Shona) play an important role. Chapter 15 of the Constitution recognises traditional leaders. Their functions include:

- allocating communal land and settling land disputes;
- resolving family and community disputes under customary law;
- promoting culture, traditions and ceremonies;
- mobilising communities for development projects;
- working with the rural district council and with **ward development committees (WADCOs)** and **village development committees (VIDCOs)**.

The Constitution requires traditional leaders to be non-partisan: they must not act as members of a political party.

:::example{title="A community plans a borehole"}
Villagers in a ward near Gwanda need a new borehole.

1. **Village level:** the village head calls a meeting; the VIDCO lists the borehole as its top priority.
2. **Ward level:** the WADCO, chaired by the ward councillor, combines village plans into a ward plan.
3. **District level:** the RDC includes the borehole in its budget and seeks a partner to help fund it.
4. **Community role:** villagers provide labour and form a water-point committee to maintain the pump.
5. **Lesson:** local governance links traditional leaders, elected councillors and residents.
:::

## How citizens take part

Citizens can vote for councillors, attend ward and budget consultation meetings, pay rates and bills on time, report faults, join residents' associations, serve on development committees and obey by-laws.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing councillors and council officials"}
Councillors are **elected** and make decisions; officials such as the town clerk are **employed** and carry them out.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing by-laws with Acts"}
Acts of Parliament apply to the whole country; by-laws apply only in a council's area.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Government has national, provincial and metropolitan, and local tiers; Chapter 14 provides for devolution.
- Local authorities are urban councils and rural district councils, led by elected councillors and run by officials.
- Councils provide water, sewage, refuse, roads, clinics, stands and markets, and make by-laws.
- Income comes from rates, service charges, licences, fees, rents and government grants.
- Traditional leaders (Chapter 15) allocate communal land, settle disputes and work with RDCs, WADCOs and VIDCOs.
:::
