## Introduction

Think of how decisions are made in your own school. Perhaps the prefects are chosen by the head alone; perhaps pupils vote for them. Perhaps the school development committee holds a meeting where parents discuss the fees, or perhaps a letter simply arrives announcing them. These are small examples of different **systems of governance**: different ways of deciding who holds power, how decisions are made and who can question them.

In this Form 4 lesson you learn the difference between *governance* and *government*, the main systems of governance found in the world and in Zimbabwe's history, and the characteristics of democracy and autocracy. The lesson describes systems, not particular countries: in an exam you are asked to explain characteristics, not to label present-day states.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Government"}
The group of people and institutions that have the authority to make and carry out decisions for a country, such as the Executive, Parliament and the courts.
:::

:::definition{term="Governance"}
The way power is used to manage a country's affairs: how decisions are made, how leaders are chosen, how they are held accountable and how citizens take part.
:::

:::definition{term="Democracy"}
A system of governance in which power belongs to the people, who take part in decisions either directly or through representatives they elect in free and fair elections.
:::

:::definition{term="Autocracy"}
A system of governance in which one person (or a small group) holds unlimited power and is not accountable to the people.
:::

:::definition{term="Rule of law"}
The principle that everyone, including leaders, is subject to the law, and that laws are applied fairly and equally by independent courts.
:::

:::keyfact{title="The Constitution and democracy"}
Section 1 of the Constitution describes Zimbabwe as a **unitary, democratic and sovereign republic**, and section 3 lists founding values and principles, including the **supremacy of the Constitution**, the **rule of law** and **good governance**.
:::

## Systems of governance

| System | Who holds power | How leaders are chosen | Examples of features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct democracy | All citizens | Citizens decide issues themselves | Meetings or referendums where everyone votes on a decision |
| Representative democracy | The people, through elected representatives | Regular free and fair elections | Parliament, councils, opposition parties |
| Autocracy | One person or a small group | Not chosen by the people, or chosen in elections that are not free | Power is not checked; little accountability |
| Dictatorship | One ruler (a dictator), often backed by the army | Often seizes power, for example by a coup | Opponents silenced; rights restricted |
| Monarchy | A king or queen | By birth (hereditary) | *Absolute* monarchy: the monarch rules; *constitutional* monarchy: the monarch is head of state but elected leaders govern |
| Indigenous systems | Kings, chiefs and councils of elders | By custom, usually from a ruling family | Decisions after discussion at the dare/inkundla; advisers could check a ruler |

**Direct democracy** works best in small groups. A **referendum** is a modern example: in Zimbabwe all voters were asked to approve the new Constitution in the referendum of 16 March 2013. Most countries use **representative democracy**, because millions of people cannot meet to discuss every law.

**Indigenous systems** in pre-colonial Zimbabwe were not simply autocratic. A mambo or inkosi was advised by councils of elders and relatives; matters were discussed openly at the **dare** (Shona) or **inkundla** (Ndebele) court; and spirit mediums and the ruler's advisers could warn a ruler who abused power.

## Characteristics of democracy

| Characteristic | What it means |
|---|---|
| Free and fair elections | Regular elections in which all adult citizens can vote secretly and choose between candidates and parties |
| Rule of law | Everyone, including leaders, obeys the law; courts apply it equally |
| Separation of powers | The Executive, the Legislature and the Judiciary have separate roles and check each other |
| Protection of rights | Freedoms of speech, assembly, association and the press are protected |
| Accountability and transparency | Leaders explain and answer for their decisions; information is available |
| Multiparty politics | Citizens may form and join political parties; there is an opposition |
| Citizen participation | Citizens vote, petition, attend meetings and comment on laws |
| Independent institutions | Courts, the electoral commission and the media can act without interference |

### How citizens take part in a democracy

Democracy depends on citizens as well as institutions. In Zimbabwe, citizens aged 18 and over can register and vote in harmonised elections held every five years, choosing the President, members of the National Assembly and councillors. Between elections they can attend public hearings when Parliament considers a bill, comment on council budgets, sign petitions, join residents' associations, political parties or community organisations, and contact their representatives about local problems. A democracy is strongest when many citizens take part, stay informed and respect the rights of people who disagree with them.

## Characteristics of autocracy

- Power is **concentrated** in one person or a small group.
- There are **no free elections**, or elections are not fair.
- **Weak checks and balances**: Parliament and courts follow the ruler's wishes.
- **Restricted rights**: criticism is punished; the media is controlled.
- **Little accountability**: the ruler does not have to explain decisions to the people.
- Decisions can be made **quickly**, which supporters of strong rule sometimes see as an advantage, but mistakes are hard to correct.

:::example{title="Comparing two fictional town councils"}
The fictional town of Mkoba Valley has two possible ways of deciding where to build a new market.

1. **Democratic way:** councillors elected by residents hold a public meeting; residents comment; the council votes; the decision is published; residents can vote the councillors out at the next election if unhappy.
2. **Autocratic way:** one official decides alone and announces the site; nobody can question the decision.
3. **Speed:** the autocratic way is faster.
4. **Quality and acceptance:** the democratic way uses local knowledge and residents accept the result, because they took part.
5. **Conclusion:** democracy is slower but more accountable and more likely to produce decisions people support.
:::

:::example{title="Recognising a characteristic in a scenario"}
Scenario: in a fictional country, a court rules that a minister must pay a fine for breaking a traffic law, just like any other driver.

1. **Characteristic shown:** the rule of law: nobody is above the law.
2. **Supporting characteristic:** an independent judiciary, which could rule against a minister.
3. **Why it matters:** citizens trust that laws are applied equally.
:::

## Advantages and disadvantages

| | Democracy | Autocracy |
|---|---|---|
| Advantages | Protects rights; leaders can be changed peacefully; decisions reflect many views; mistakes can be corrected | Quick decisions; can act firmly in a crisis |
| Disadvantages | Slow decision-making; elections are costly; majority may ignore minorities if rights are not protected | Abuse of power; no peaceful way to change leaders; rights restricted; corruption is hard to expose |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing government with governance"}
Government is the institutions and people; governance is the way power is used and shared.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying democracy means only elections"}
Elections are one characteristic. Without the rule of law, rights and accountability, elections alone do not make a democracy.
:::

:::mistake{title="Labelling real countries"}
Answer with characteristics and fictional or historical examples, not by calling present-day countries democracies or dictatorships.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*Define* (Paper 2 Section A): one accurate sentence. *Distinguish between democracy and autocracy*: compare them point by point (who holds power, elections, rights, accountability). For a Section B essay such as *"Democracy is better than autocracy." Discuss*, give advantages and disadvantages of each and reach a supported judgement.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1"}
Expect items that describe a feature in a scenario and ask which system or characteristic it shows.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Government is the institutions that rule; governance is how power is used and shared.
- Systems include direct and representative democracy, autocracy, dictatorship, monarchy and indigenous systems.
- Democracy: free and fair elections, rule of law, separation of powers, rights, accountability and participation.
- Autocracy: concentrated power, no free elections, weak checks, restricted rights, little accountability.
- Section 1 of the Constitution declares Zimbabwe a democratic republic; section 3 lists the rule of law and good governance among its founding values.
:::
