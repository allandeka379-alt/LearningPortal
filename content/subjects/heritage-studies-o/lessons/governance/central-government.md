## Introduction

On the evening news you may see the President opening a new road, a parliamentary committee questioning a minister about water shortages, and a judge delivering a ruling in the High Court. These three scenes show the three **arms of government** at work. Each has a different job, and the Constitution makes sure that no single arm holds all the power.

In this Form 4 lesson you learn how central government is organised under the **2013 Constitution of Zimbabwe**: the Executive (Chapter 5), the Legislature (Chapter 6) and the Judiciary (Chapter 8), what each does, and how they check one another. The ranking of the courts is studied in the lesson on laws and courts. You should already know the difference between democracy and autocracy.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Executive"}
The arm of government that carries out laws and runs the country day to day: the President, the Vice-Presidents, the Cabinet and the ministries and civil service.
:::

:::definition{term="Legislature"}
The arm of government that makes laws: Parliament, made up of the Senate and the National Assembly.
:::

:::definition{term="Judiciary"}
The arm of government that interprets the law and settles disputes: the judges and magistrates of the courts.
:::

:::definition{term="Separation of powers"}
The principle that the powers of government are divided among three separate arms so that no single person or body holds all power.
:::

:::definition{term="Checks and balances"}
The ways in which each arm of government can limit the actions of the others, so that power is not abused.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Chapters to learn"}
Chapter 5: **the Executive**. Chapter 6: **the Legislature** (Senate and National Assembly). Chapter 8: **the Judiciary** (section 162 lists the courts). Elections are held every **five years**; the National Assembly has **210 constituency seats** plus seats reserved for women.
:::

## The three arms at a glance

| Arm | Who | Main functions |
|---|---|---|
| Executive | The President (head of state and government, and Commander-in-Chief of the Defence Forces), Vice-Presidents, Cabinet ministers, the civil service | Carries out laws and policies; runs ministries and services; prepares the national budget; conducts foreign relations; proposes bills |
| Legislature | Parliament: the Senate and the National Assembly | Makes laws; approves the budget and taxes; oversees the Executive; represents the people |
| Judiciary | Judges of the Constitutional Court, Supreme Court, High Court and other courts, and magistrates; headed by the Chief Justice | Interprets laws; settles disputes; tries criminal cases; protects rights; decides whether laws and actions follow the Constitution |

## The Executive

The **President** is elected by voters in the harmonised elections every five years. A candidate must win **more than half** of the votes cast; otherwise a run-off election is held between the two leading candidates. Under the 2013 Constitution a person may serve **no more than two terms** as President. The President is helped by **Vice-Presidents** and appoints **ministers**, who together form the **Cabinet**. Each minister heads a ministry, such as the Ministry of Health and Child Care or the Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education, which is run day to day by civil servants.

Functions of the Executive include:

- carrying out the laws passed by Parliament;
- making and implementing government policy;
- preparing the national budget for Parliament to approve;
- defending the country and keeping order;
- representing Zimbabwe abroad and signing international agreements;
- assenting to (signing) bills so that they become Acts of Parliament.

## The Legislature

**Parliament** has two houses:

- the **National Assembly**, with 210 members elected in constituencies plus women elected by proportional representation; it is led by the **Speaker**;
- the **Senate**, which includes senators elected by proportional representation in each province, chiefs, and representatives of persons with disabilities; it is led by the **President of the Senate**.

Parliament has three main functions:

1. **Law-making.** A proposed law (a **bill**) is debated and passed by both houses and then signed by the President to become an **Act**.
2. **Oversight.** Members question ministers, and **portfolio committees** examine the work and spending of ministries.
3. **Representation.** Members speak for the people of their constituencies and provinces, and hold public hearings on bills.

:::example{title="How a bill becomes a law"}
A fictional *Community Libraries Bill* is proposed by the Minister of Primary and Secondary Education.

1. **First reading:** the bill is published and introduced in the National Assembly.
2. **Committee stage and public hearings:** a portfolio committee holds hearings in Gweru, Lupane and Mutare, where citizens give their views.
3. **Debate and vote** in the National Assembly, then in the Senate; amendments may be made.
4. **Assent:** the bill goes to the President, who signs it.
5. **Result:** it becomes an Act of Parliament, which the Executive must carry out and the courts will interpret.
:::

## The Judiciary

The courts settle disputes between people, try people accused of crimes, and decide whether laws and government actions are consistent with the Constitution. The **Constitutional Court** is the highest court on constitutional matters, and the **Chief Justice** heads the Judiciary. The Constitution requires the courts to be **independent**: judges must decide cases according to the law, without interference from the Executive, Parliament, political parties or anyone else. The **Judicial Service Commission** plays a part in the appointment of judges and in the running of the courts.

## Separation of powers and checks and balances

| Arm | How it can check the others |
|---|---|
| Executive on Legislature | The President may refer a bill back to Parliament with reservations instead of signing it |
| Legislature on Executive | Approves or rejects the budget and taxes; questions ministers; portfolio committees investigate; can remove a President through the procedure set out in the Constitution |
| Judiciary on Executive and Legislature | Can declare a law or government action unconstitutional and invalid |
| Executive and Legislature on Judiciary | Take part, with the Judicial Service Commission, in the process of appointing judges; Parliament makes laws the courts must apply |

:::example{title="Checks and balances in action"}
Scenario: a fictional ministry issues an order banning all public meetings in a district without any reason being given. A residents' association goes to court.

1. **Judiciary:** the court examines the order against the Constitution's protection of freedom of assembly.
2. **Ruling:** the court sets the order aside as unconstitutional.
3. **Legislature:** a portfolio committee calls the minister to explain the order.
4. **Lesson:** two arms checked the third, protecting citizens' rights.
:::

## Why government is important

Government keeps law and order, defends the country, makes laws, provides services such as education, health and roads, manages the economy, protects rights and represents the nation abroad. Without it, there would be no agreed rules and no way to settle disputes peacefully.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the functions of the arms"}
Parliament **makes** laws; the Executive **carries them out**; the courts **interpret** them. Ministers belong to the Executive even when they sit in Parliament.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling the Cabinet the Parliament"}
The Cabinet is the President, the Vice-Presidents and the ministers. Parliament is the Senate and the National Assembly.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying separation of powers means the arms never interact"}
The arms work together but limit one another; that is what checks and balances mean.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Central government has three arms: the Executive (Chapter 5), the Legislature (Chapter 6) and the Judiciary (Chapter 8).
- The President, Vice-Presidents and Cabinet carry out laws and run the country; the President must win more than half of the votes cast.
- Parliament (Senate and National Assembly) makes laws, oversees the Executive and represents the people.
- The courts, headed by the Chief Justice, interpret the law and must be independent.
- Separation of powers and checks and balances prevent any one arm from abusing power.
:::
