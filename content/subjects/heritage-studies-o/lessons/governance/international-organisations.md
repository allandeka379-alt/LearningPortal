## Introduction

You may have seen the blue UN logo on a vaccination card, a school feeding sack, a water tank or a sign at Great Zimbabwe. These are reminders that Zimbabwe is part of a worldwide family of nations. Through international organisations, countries cooperate on peace, health, education, food security and development.

In this Form 4 lesson you learn about the **United Nations (UN)**, its main organs and the agencies most active in Zimbabwe, and the **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)**. You also learn what Zimbabwe gains from membership and what it must do in return. The Commonwealth is described in outline. You should already know about the regional organisations SADC, COMESA and the African Union.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="International organisation"}
An organisation whose members are states from many parts of the world, formed to cooperate on shared aims.
:::

:::definition{term="Specialised agency"}
A UN body that works in one field, such as health (WHO), children (UNICEF), education and culture (UNESCO) or food (WFP).
:::

:::definition{term="Veto"}
The power of any one of the five permanent members of the UN Security Council to block a decision of the Council.
:::

:::definition{term="Non-alignment"}
A policy of not joining either side in a conflict between powerful blocs, especially the Cold War between the USA and the Soviet Union.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates to learn"}
UN founded **24 October 1945** (UN Day); Zimbabwe a member since **1980**. Non-Aligned Movement founded **1961**; Harare hosted the NAM summit in **1986**. Zimbabwe left the Commonwealth in **2003**.
:::

## The United Nations

The UN was founded on **24 October 1945**, after the Second World War, by states determined to prevent another world war. Zimbabwe joined soon after independence in **1980**.

**Aims of the UN:** to maintain international peace and security; to develop friendly relations among nations based on equal rights and self-determination; to cooperate in solving economic, social, cultural and humanitarian problems and to promote human rights; and to be a centre where nations coordinate their actions.

**Main organs**

| Organ | Membership and role |
|---|---|
| General Assembly | All member states, one vote each; discusses world issues, approves the budget |
| Security Council | 15 members: 5 permanent members with a veto (China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States) and 10 elected for two years; responsible for peace and security |
| Secretariat | Led by the Secretary-General; runs the UN's daily work |
| International Court of Justice | Sits in The Hague; settles legal disputes between states |
| Economic and Social Council | Coordinates economic and social work and the agencies |

**Agencies active in Zimbabwe**

| Agency | Field | Examples of work in Zimbabwe |
|---|---|---|
| UNICEF (United Nations Children's Fund) | Children | Immunisation, child nutrition, education, water and sanitation, child protection |
| UNESCO (UN Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization) | Education, science and culture | World Heritage listing of sites such as Great Zimbabwe and Mana Pools; education programmes |
| WHO (World Health Organization) | Health | Disease control (malaria, TB, cholera, COVID-19), health advice and standards |
| WFP (World Food Programme) | Food | Food aid in droughts and disasters, such as after Cyclone Idai; school feeding |

The UN also helped the struggle for independence by applying **sanctions to Rhodesia** after UDI and by supporting the right of colonial peoples to self-determination.

:::example{title="The UN at work after a disaster"}
After a fictional flood in a district near Kariba:

1. **WFP** delivers maize meal, beans and oil to displaced families.
2. **UNICEF** provides water-purification tablets and helps set up temporary classrooms.
3. **WHO** supports the Ministry of Health and Child Care in preventing cholera.
4. **Government** coordinates the response through its own departments and the district council.
5. **Lesson:** agencies support, but do not replace, the national government.
:::

## The Non-Aligned Movement

The **Non-Aligned Movement** was founded in **1961** in Belgrade by countries that did not want to join either the Western bloc led by the USA or the Eastern bloc led by the Soviet Union during the Cold War. Its members were mainly newly independent countries of Africa, Asia and Latin America.

**Aims:** to stay independent of the rival power blocs; to oppose colonialism, apartheid and racism; to promote world peace and disarmament; to defend the sovereignty of member states; and to promote cooperation and fairer economic relations between developing and developed countries.

Zimbabwe joined at independence. In **1986**, Harare hosted the NAM summit, bringing leaders from many developing countries to Zimbabwe and raising the country's international profile.

### What NAM meant for southern Africa

For the liberation movements of southern Africa, NAM was an important platform. Its members repeatedly condemned colonial rule and apartheid, supported sanctions against Rhodesia and South Africa, and recognised the right of peoples to self-determination. After 1980, Zimbabwe used its membership to speak for the Frontline States and against apartheid. Hosting the 1986 summit allowed Zimbabwe to draw the world's attention to the situation in South Africa and Namibia. Today NAM members continue to discuss issues such as development, debt, trade and climate change from the point of view of developing countries.

## The Commonwealth in outline

Zimbabwe joined the Commonwealth, an association of mainly former British territories, at independence in 1980. Zimbabwe withdrew from the Commonwealth in 2003.

## Benefits and obligations

| Benefits to Zimbabwe | Obligations of Zimbabwe |
|---|---|
| A voice and vote in world affairs (General Assembly) | Pay assessed contributions to the UN budget |
| Humanitarian help in droughts, floods and epidemics | Respect the UN Charter: settle disputes peacefully, respect other states' sovereignty |
| Health, education and child-welfare programmes | Implement conventions it has ratified, such as the Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989), and report on progress |
| World Heritage status for sites, bringing recognition and tourism | Protect listed sites and the environment |
| Opportunities for Zimbabweans to serve in UN missions and agencies | Contribute to peacekeeping when able |
| Solidarity with other developing countries through NAM | Take part in meetings and support agreed positions |

## Challenges

- The **veto** lets one permanent member block action, and many African states argue that Africa should have permanent seats on the Security Council.
- Agencies depend on voluntary funding, which can fall when donors cut budgets.
- Since the end of the Cold War, the role of NAM has been debated, although it still gives developing countries a shared voice.

:::example{title="Evaluating benefits"}
Question: *How far has Zimbabwe benefited from UN membership?*

1. **For:** emergency food aid, child immunisation, disease control, World Heritage recognition, a voice in world affairs.
2. **Limits:** programmes depend on donor funding; decisions in the Security Council are shaped by the permanent members.
3. **Judgement:** benefits in health, food security and heritage are clear, though Zimbabwe's influence over major decisions is limited.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up UN agencies"}
UNICEF: children. UNESCO: education, science and culture (World Heritage). WHO: health. WFP: food.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the UN and the AU"}
The UN is worldwide, founded in 1945; the AU is continental, launched in 2002 to replace the OAU (1963).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The UN was founded on 24 October 1945 to keep peace and promote cooperation; Zimbabwe joined in 1980.
- Its main organs are the General Assembly, Security Council, Secretariat, International Court of Justice and Economic and Social Council.
- UNICEF, UNESCO, WHO and WFP support children, heritage and education, health and food security in Zimbabwe.
- NAM (1961) united countries that rejected the Cold War blocs; Harare hosted its summit in 1986.
- Zimbabwe joined the Commonwealth in 1980 and withdrew in 2003; membership of international bodies brings benefits and obligations.
:::
