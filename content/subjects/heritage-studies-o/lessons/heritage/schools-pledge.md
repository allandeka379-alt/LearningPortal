## Introduction

At a primary school in Chinhoyi, the school day begins at assembly. The flag is raised, the pupils sing the national anthem, and then they recite the National Schools Pledge together. A Form 1 pupil who has just moved to a secondary school in Bindura hears the same words there. Across the country, from Hwange to Mutare, pupils say the same promise each school week.

A **pledge** is a solemn promise. The National Schools Pledge was introduced in Zimbabwe's schools in **2016** as a short statement of the values the nation hopes its young people will hold. In this lesson you will learn its main ideas (in your own words, because the exam asks you to explain it, not to copy it), how it connects to the national flag and anthem, why it is significant, and the different views people expressed when it was introduced. You should already know the meanings of the national flag and the main ideas of the national anthem.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Pledge"}
A solemn promise or commitment, often made publicly, for example to be loyal or to behave in a certain way.
:::

:::definition{term="National Schools Pledge"}
A short promise recited by learners in Zimbabwean schools since 2016, expressing respect for God, the flag, the nation's heroes, its natural resources and cultures, and a commitment to honesty and hard work.
:::

:::definition{term="Unity in diversity"}
The idea that people of different languages, cultures, faiths and backgrounds can live together as one nation while keeping their differences.
:::

:::definition{term="Values"}
Beliefs about what is good, right and important, which guide how people behave, such as honesty, respect and hard work.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Facts to learn"}
- The pledge was introduced in schools by the Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education in **2016**.
- It is recited by learners, usually at assembly, together with the raising of the flag and the singing of the national anthem.
- You should be able to recite it and explain its meaning; in written answers, paraphrase its ideas.
:::

## The main ideas of the pledge

The pledge is short, but each part carries a value. The table gives each idea in paraphrase with the value it promotes.

| Main idea (paraphrased) | Value or meaning |
|---|---|
| It acknowledges Almighty God, in whose hands the nation's future is placed | Faith and humility |
| The learner salutes the national flag | Respect for national symbols and patriotism |
| Zimbabweans are united in their diversity by a shared desire for freedom, justice and equality | Unity in diversity, justice and equality |
| It honours the men and women who gave their lives in the liberation struggles (Chimurenga/Umvukela) | Remembrance and respect for heroes |
| Zimbabweans are proud inheritors of the country's natural resources | Pride in and care for natural heritage |
| Zimbabweans are proud of and take part in their traditions and cultures | Cultural identity and pride |
| The learner commits to honesty and to the dignity of hard work | Integrity and a strong work ethic |

Notice that almost every idea links to another part of the Heritage Studies course: national symbols, the liberation struggle, natural resources, culture and identity, and citizenship.

## The pledge, the flag and the anthem

The pledge, the flag and the anthem are used together at school assemblies and they share many of the same messages.

| Theme | National flag | National anthem | National Schools Pledge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heroes and sacrifice | Red: the blood shed in the struggle | Praises those who fought for freedom | Honours those who died in the liberation struggles |
| Natural resources | Green: land and agriculture; yellow: minerals | Praises the beauty and riches of the land | Pride in inheriting the country's resources |
| Peace and unity | White triangle: peace | Calls on the nation to stand together | Unity in diversity; freedom, justice and equality |
| Hope and the future | Red star: hopes and aspirations | Asks for blessing on the land | Places the nation's future in God's hands; commits to hard work |

:::example{title="Linking the pledge to the flag"}
A Form 4 class is asked to relate one line of the pledge to the flag.

1. **Pledge idea:** pride in inheriting the country's natural resources.
2. **Flag link:** the green stripe stands for land and agriculture and the yellow stripe for mineral wealth.
3. **Explanation:** both teach that the land and minerals are national heritage, which citizens must value and protect for future generations.

A strong answer names the pledge idea, names the flag part and explains the shared message.
:::

## Why the pledge is significant

Supporters of the pledge give several reasons for its importance:

- **Patriotism.** Saying it regularly builds love of and loyalty to the country from an early age.
- **Values.** It reminds learners of honesty, hard work, justice and equality, which are needed in a good citizen.
- **Unity.** All learners, whatever their language or background, say the same promise, which reinforces unity in diversity.
- **Remembrance.** It keeps the memory of those who died in the liberation struggles alive among young people.
- **Heritage.** It encourages pride in and care for natural resources and cultures.
- **Identity.** It gives learners a shared sense of what it means to be Zimbabwean.

## The debate when it was introduced

When the pledge was introduced in 2016 there was public debate. Supporters said it would build patriotism, discipline and good values in young people. Others raised concerns. Some parents and religious groups felt that some parts conflicted with their beliefs, for example about saluting a flag or about a statement of faith being required of every learner; some people said the pledge had been introduced without enough consultation; and some questioned whether a recited promise changes behaviour. Heritage Studies does not ask you to take a side: you should be able to explain the main views fairly and recognise that, in a democracy, citizens may disagree about such matters while still respecting one another and the law.

:::example{title="Explaining a value in action"}
Farai, a Form 2 pupil in Gweru, finds a wallet with money in it near the school gate.

1. **Pledge value:** honesty.
2. **Action:** he hands the wallet to the duty teacher so that it can be returned to its owner.
3. **Link:** the pledge's commitment to honesty becomes real when learners act on it, not only when they recite it.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the pledge with the anthem"}
The anthem is sung; the pledge is recited. The pledge was introduced in 2016; the anthem is much older.
:::

:::mistake{title="Copying out the pledge instead of explaining it"}
An *explain* question wants the meaning of an idea and why it matters, not the words recited at assembly.
:::

:::mistake{title="Presenting one side of the debate as fact"}
Say "some people argued … while others …". Do not present your own view as the only correct one.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**State** or **identify** (Paper 1, Paper 2 Section A): give one idea of the pledge in your own words, e.g. "a commitment to honesty and hard work".
:::

:::exam-tip
**Relate** the pledge to the flag and anthem (Paper 2 Section A): pair a pledge idea with a flag part or anthem theme and explain the shared message, as in the example above.
:::

:::exam-tip
**Discuss** its significance (Paper 2 Section B): give the reasons supporters value it, the concerns some people raised, and a balanced conclusion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The National Schools Pledge is a promise recited by learners, introduced in 2016.
- Its ideas: acknowledging God, saluting the flag, unity in diversity, honouring heroes, pride in natural resources and cultures, honesty and hard work.
- It shares the messages of the flag and the anthem: heroes, resources, peace and unity, and hope.
- Supporters say it builds patriotism, values, unity and identity.
- Its introduction was debated; good answers explain the different views fairly.
:::
