## Introduction

Every Monday morning at a secondary school in Masvingo, the pupils line up in front of the flagpole. Two prefects raise the national flag, everyone stands still, and the school sings the national anthem. Later that day, a Form 1 pupil notices the same bird that sits on the flag printed on a coin, on her birth certificate and on the sign outside the district office. Why does one bird appear in so many places?

The answer is that Zimbabwe, like every nation, uses **national symbols** to show who it is. A symbol is something simple that stands for something much bigger: the flag stands for the whole country, its history and its hopes. In this lesson you will learn what national heritage and national symbols are, what each of Zimbabwe's main symbols looks like and what it is commonly taught to mean, and why citizens are expected to treat them with respect. You should already know that Zimbabwe became independent on 18 April 1980 and that the Great Zimbabwe monument is part of the country's history.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Heritage"}
Everything a people inherit from past generations and pass on to future ones: land and natural resources, buildings and monuments, language, beliefs, customs, knowledge and values.
:::

:::definition{term="National heritage"}
The heritage that belongs to the whole nation rather than to one family or community, such as the national flag, national monuments and the country's natural resources.
:::

:::definition{term="National symbol"}
An object, design, song or emblem chosen to represent a country and its people, for example a flag, a coat of arms or a national anthem.
:::

:::definition{term="Patriotism"}
Love of, loyalty to and pride in one's country, shown by respecting its laws, people, heritage and symbols.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Facts to learn"}
- **Section 4** of the Constitution of Zimbabwe (2013) names the national flag, the national anthem, the public seal and the coat of arms as the national symbols, and says further details are set by an Act of Parliament.
- The national flag was adopted on **18 April 1980**, Independence Day.
- The current national anthem, known in Shona as *Simudzai Mureza weZimbabwe* and in Ndebele as *Kalibusiswe Ilizwe leZimbabwe* ("Blessed be the land of Zimbabwe"), replaced the first anthem used at independence.
- The **flame lily** is Zimbabwe's national flower.
:::

## The national flag

The flag has seven horizontal stripes: green, yellow, red, black, red, yellow and green. On the side nearest the pole is a white triangle edged in black. Inside the triangle is a red five-pointed star, and on the star sits a yellow Zimbabwe Bird. The meanings below are the ones commonly taught in Zimbabwean schools.

| Part of the flag | Meaning commonly taught |
|---|---|
| Green | Vegetation, the land and agriculture |
| Yellow | Mineral wealth |
| Red | The blood shed during the liberation struggle |
| Black | The heritage, race and pride of the majority of the people |
| White triangle | Peace |
| Red star | The nation's hopes and aspirations for the future |
| Zimbabwe Bird | National identity and the history of the country |

Notice that the meanings link the flag to the rest of the Heritage Studies course: the land and minerals (natural resources), the liberation struggle (national history) and peace and unity (citizenship).

## The other national symbols

| Symbol | What it looks like or is | What it represents |
|---|---|---|
| Coat of arms | A shield showing a representation of the Great Zimbabwe walls and wavy blue and white lines, held by two kudu; above it the red star and Zimbabwe Bird; below it a mound with wheat, cotton and maize, a crossed hoe and rifle, and the motto "Unity, Freedom, Work" | The country's history, water and land, farming, the move from war to peace, and the values the nation works by |
| National anthem | A song with words in Shona, Ndebele and English versions, sung on national occasions and at schools | Praise for the land, its heroes and its people, and a call to unity and hard work |
| Zimbabwe Bird | A bird carved in soapstone; several such carvings were found at Great Zimbabwe | The link between the modern nation and the pre-colonial state of Great Zimbabwe |
| Public seal | The official seal of the state | Proof that an official state document is genuine |
| Flame lily | A red and yellow wild flower | The beauty of the country's natural heritage |

The soapstone birds from Great Zimbabwe are among the most treasured objects in the country. During the colonial era several of them were taken out of the country; some have since been returned, and their return was celebrated as the recovery of national heritage.

The first national anthem used at independence was *Ishe Komborera Africa* ("God bless Africa"), a hymn sung across southern Africa. It was later replaced by an anthem written in Zimbabwe for Zimbabwe. You should know the anthem's title and main ideas, and be able to sing it, but in an exam you describe its message rather than copy out the verses.

:::example{title="Reading a symbol: the hoe and the rifle"}
A Form 2 class is asked why the coat of arms shows a hoe crossed with a rifle.

1. **Describe:** the hoe is a farming tool; the rifle is a weapon.
2. **Link to history:** independence was won through an armed struggle, and the country lives mainly from the land.
3. **Explain the message:** the nation turned from war to peaceful work, but it must still be ready to defend its independence.

A good answer always moves from *what the symbol shows* to *what it stands for* and *why that matters*.
:::

## Why national symbols matter

National symbols do important work for a country.

- **Identity.** They show the world, and Zimbabweans themselves, who the nation is. The flag at the Olympic Games or at an embassy abroad says "Zimbabwe" without a word being spoken.
- **Unity.** People of every language, faith, region and background share the same flag and anthem. In a country with sixteen official languages, shared symbols help people see themselves as one nation.
- **Patriotism and pride.** Standing for the anthem or raising the flag reminds citizens of what the country has achieved and of their duty to it.
- **Remembrance.** The red of the flag and the national shrines remind citizens of those who died for independence.
- **Authority.** The public seal and the coat of arms on official documents show that a document comes from the state and can be trusted.
- **Education.** Symbols teach young people the history and values of the nation in a simple form they remember.

## Using national symbols correctly

Because symbols stand for the nation, how people treat them shows respect or disrespect for the country. Good practice includes:

- standing still and silent, with hats removed, when the national anthem is played or the flag is raised or lowered;
- keeping the flag clean and in good repair, and replacing faded or torn flags;
- never letting the flag touch the ground and never using it as a cloth, a costume or an advertisement without permission;
- flying the flag at **half-mast** as a sign of national mourning when the government directs;
- using the coat of arms and the public seal only for official purposes.

:::example{title="A council's flag problem"}
A fictional town council in Gweru notices that the flag outside its offices is faded and torn at one edge.

1. **Problem:** a damaged flag shows a lack of care for a national symbol.
2. **Action:** the council replaces it with a new flag and lowers the old one respectfully instead of throwing it away.
3. **Lesson for citizens:** respect for symbols is shown in everyday actions, not only on national holidays.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the colours and their meanings"}
Yellow stands for mineral wealth, not the sun; red stands for the blood shed in the liberation struggle, not danger. Learn the table as pairs.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing symbols without explaining them"}
"The flag, the anthem and the coat of arms" earns little in an *explain* question. Say what each represents and how it builds identity or unity.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the pledge with the anthem"}
The national anthem is a song; the National Schools Pledge is a promise said by pupils. They are related but different.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
**State** or **identify** (Paper 1 and Paper 2 Section A): give the exact part and meaning, e.g. "the red star: hopes and aspirations of the nation".
:::

:::exam-tip
**Explain the significance** of national symbols (Paper 2 Section A): make a point (identity, unity, patriotism, remembrance, authority) and develop it with an example, e.g. "the flag is raised at international events, so the world recognises Zimbabwe".
:::

:::exam-tip
In a Section B essay on heritage, use the symbols as evidence of how the nation remembers its history, then weigh how far citizens actually respect them.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Heritage is what a people inherit and pass on; national symbols represent the whole nation.
- Section 4 of the Constitution names the national flag, the national anthem, the public seal and the coat of arms.
- The flag: green (agriculture), yellow (minerals), red (blood shed in the struggle), black (the majority), white triangle (peace), red star (hopes), Zimbabwe Bird (identity and history).
- The Zimbabwe Bird comes from the soapstone carvings found at Great Zimbabwe; the flame lily is the national flower.
- Symbols build identity, unity, patriotism and remembrance, so they must be used correctly and respectfully.
:::
