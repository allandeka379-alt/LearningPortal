## Introduction

Rudo Chikwanha runs a fictional printing company in Bulawayo. This year she needs to show her accounts to three different people: the bank, which will decide on a loan; the tax authority, which will assess her tax; and her new business partner, who is deciding whether to invest. Each wants something different from the same set of figures. This lesson looks at **who uses accounting information and why**, the **basic rules (concepts)** that make accounts reliable, the **annual report** that a company publishes, and how managers use financial statements to **develop strategy**.

It is tested in ZIMSEC Business Studies (Form 6) and Business Enterprise Skills (Form 5), and in Cambridge **A Level** Paper 3 and Paper 4 (topic 10.4). The accounting concepts are named in Business Enterprise Skills; Cambridge expects you to use the ideas rather than list them. You should know the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
| ZIMSEC | Cambridge |
| --- | --- |
| Users of accounting information; accounting concepts (double entry, accruals, money measurement, prudence, realisation, business entity); the importance of accurate records (BES) | Users of financial information; the annual report and its usefulness to the business and other stakeholders; use of financial statements in developing strategies |

The concepts are examined by name in BES. Cambridge tests the **application**: for example, why a business must not record sales before they are made.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Annual report"}
A document published each year by a company that contains its financial statements and other information about its performance, for owners and other stakeholders.
:::

:::definition{term="Accruals concept"}
Revenue and expenses are recorded in the period in which they are earned or incurred, not when cash is received or paid.
:::

:::definition{term="Prudence concept"}
Profits and assets must not be overstated, so losses are recorded as soon as they are likely, but gains only when they are realised.
:::

:::definition{term="Business entity concept"}
The business is treated as separate from its owner, so private transactions are not recorded in the business accounts.
:::

## Users of accounting information and what they need

| User | What they want to know | Typical information used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owners and shareholders | Is the business profitable? What return and dividend can they expect? | Profit for the year, dividends, earnings per share, ROCE |
| Managers | Are costs controlled? Are targets met? What should be done next? | Margins, expenses, budgets, cash position |
| Banks and lenders | Can interest and loans be repaid? Is there security? | Profit from operations, gearing, current ratio, assets |
| Suppliers (trade creditors) | Will they be paid on time? | Liquidity ratios, payables days |
| Employees and trade unions | Is the business secure? Can it afford a pay rise? | Profit, cash, trends |
| Government and tax authority | Is the tax calculated correctly? | Profit before tax, sales |
| Customers | Will the business survive to give service and guarantees? | Profit, liquidity |
| Competitors and investors | How strong is the rival? Is it worth investing? | Margins, growth, investor ratios |

**Accurate records matter** because decisions, loans, tax and legal duties depend on them. Poor records lead to wrong prices, missed bad debts, fraud, penalties from the tax authority and the loss of lender confidence.

## Accounting concepts

- **Double entry:** every transaction has two sides, which keeps the accounts in balance.
- **Business entity:** the business is separate from the owner, so the owner's private spending is **drawings**, not a business expense.
- **Money measurement:** only items that can be measured in money are recorded; staff morale or customer loyalty are left out.
- **Accruals:** match revenue with the costs of earning it in the same period. Rent paid in advance is partly an asset (prepayment); an unpaid electricity bill is a liability (accrual).
- **Realisation:** revenue is recorded when goods are delivered or the service is given, not when an order is placed or cash is paid.
- **Prudence:** do not overstate profit or assets; inventory is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value.

## The annual report

A company's annual report normally includes the **statement of profit or loss**, the **statement of financial position**, a **statement of cash flows**, **notes** (accounting policies, details of items), the **directors' or chairman's report** (performance and plans) and an **auditor's report** (an independent opinion that the accounts give a true and fair view). Large companies also report on governance, social and environmental matters.

**Usefulness.** The report gives owners, lenders and employees an independent, standard picture; it allows comparison over time and with other firms. **Limitations:** it is historic and sometimes late; directors write the narrative and may present events favourably; accounting policies differ; non-financial matters are limited. Different stakeholders have different needs, so no single report suits all.

## Using financial statements in strategy

Managers use the statements to decide what to do next: **pricing** (margins show room to cut or need to raise prices), **cost control** (which expenses are growing faster than revenue), **expansion** (is there cash and borrowing capacity?), **financing** (debt or shares, from gearing and ROCE), **dividends** (cover and retained profit), and **closing or selling units** that earn poor returns. The statements also help to set targets and to judge whether a strategy has worked afterwards.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: case analysis, users and their needs]
**Context.** Rudo's printing company (a fictional business) has revenue of US\$240,000, profit for the year of US\$40,000 and a bank overdraft that has been rising. The bank, the new partner and the tax authority each ask to see the accounts.

**Point.** The same accounts give different answers to different users.

**Application.** The bank will study profit from operations and the overdraft: US\$40,000 of profit against a rising overdraft suggests that cash is being absorbed by inventory or receivables. The new partner will look at the return on capital and the growth in profit. The tax authority will check that the profit has been calculated correctly and that expenses are business ones.

**Analysis.** The bank is concerned with safety, so liquidity and gearing matter most; the partner is concerned with return, so ROCE and growth matter most; the tax authority with accuracy and honesty. If the records are inaccurate, all three would take wrong decisions, and Rudo might be fined.

**Judgement.** Rudo should keep complete records so that she can supply different information to each user, and she should not rely on profit alone: the bank in particular will want a cash flow forecast. Her accounts also say nothing about her skilled staff or customer loyalty, which the partner would want to hear about separately.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: applying the concepts to correct profit]
**Context.** The draft profit for the year of Rudo's company is US\$40,000. The accountant finds four items: (1) the company paid US\$18,000 on 1 October for 12 months' rent and charged all of it to the year, which ends on 31 December; (2) an electricity bill of US\$1,200 for December has not yet arrived; (3) inventory that cost US\$9,000 can be sold for only US\$7,800 after costs; (4) a sale of US\$6,000 (cost US\$3,900) was included in revenue, but the goods were delivered in January.

| Adjustment | Effect on profit (US\$) |
| --- | ---: |
| Draft profit for the year | 40,000 |
| Rent: only 3 of the 12 months paid belong to the year (accruals) | 13,500 |
| Electricity for December not yet billed (accruals) | (1,200) |
| Inventory cost 9,000, NRV 7,800 (prudence) | (1,200) |
| Sale of 6,000 delivered in January (realisation); cost 3,900 also removed | (2,100) |
| **Corrected profit for the year** | 49,000 |

1. **Accruals.** Only 3 months of rent (US\$4,500) belongs to the year; US\$13,500 is a prepayment (a current asset), which raises profit by US\$13,500. The December electricity is an expense of the year and lowers profit by US\$1,200.
2. **Prudence.** The inventory is written down to US\$7,800, so profit falls by US\$1,200.
3. **Realisation.** The sale belongs to next year, so revenue falls by US\$6,000 and cost of sales by US\$3,900; profit falls by US\$2,100.
4. **Judgement.** The corrected profit is US\$49,000, US\$9,000 higher than the draft. The correction shows that the concepts change profit in both directions, and that the bank, the partner and the tax authority would each have reached the wrong conclusion from the draft. The inventory estimate of net realisable value remains a judgement.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing users without their needs"}
Naming "bank, employees, government" earns little. For each user say what they want to know, which figure they use and why.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating the concepts as definitions only"}
Questions ask you to apply them. Say which concept applies, what the correct treatment is and what happens to profit.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the annual report is always reliable"}
The report is historic, uses estimates and policies that differ between firms, and includes narrative written by directors. State both usefulness and limitations.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Business Studies Papers 1 and 2 and Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2 ask you to *state*, *outline* and *explain* users, concepts and the importance of accurate records; Cambridge Papers 3 and 4 ask you to *explain*, *analyse*, *assess* and *evaluate* the usefulness of the annual report and financial statements for strategy. Link each user to a specific figure, apply each concept to the case, and weigh usefulness against limitations before reaching a judgement.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Different users (owners, managers, lenders, suppliers, employees, government, customers, competitors) need different information from the accounts.
- Accurate records are needed for decisions, loans, tax and legal duties.
- The main concepts are double entry, business entity, money measurement, accruals, realisation and prudence.
- An annual report contains financial statements, notes, a directors' report and an auditor's report; it is useful but historic and limited.
- Managers use the statements to guide pricing, cost control, expansion, financing and dividend decisions.
- A good answer applies each idea to the case and ends with a judgement.
:::
