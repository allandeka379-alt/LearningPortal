## Introduction

Chipo runs a fictional bakery in Harare and has bought a new automatic dough mixer. Her six bakers are skilled with the old mixer but have never used a machine with settings and a control panel. If she does nothing, the mixer will be used badly and may break. She can ask the supplier's technician to train them on the premises, send them to a course in another city, or let an experienced baker show the others while production continues. Each route costs differently in money and in lost output.

Training and development are tested in ZIMSEC Business Studies and Business Enterprise Skills (Form 6) and in Cambridge AS Level Papers 1 and 2. Questions ask you to compare methods for a stated job and to judge whether the cost of training is worthwhile. You should already know how HRM plans for workforce needs and how recruitment brings in new staff.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Training"}
Teaching employees the specific skills and knowledge needed to do their present job well.
:::

:::definition{term="Development"}
The broader and longer-term growth of an employee's abilities, through experience, education and responsibility, to prepare for future and more senior roles.
:::

:::definition{term="Induction"}
The introduction of a new employee to the business, its rules, safety procedures, colleagues and the job itself.
:::

:::definition{term="Intrapreneurship"}
The use of entrepreneurial skills, such as initiative, risk taking and idea generation, by employees within an existing business.
:::

:::definition{term="Multi-skilling"}
Training employees to do several different jobs so that they can be moved between tasks as needed.
:::

## Types of training

| | On-the-job | Off-the-job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Learning at the workplace while working, for example by watching or being coached by an experienced worker | Learning away from the job, for example at a college, workshop or training centre, or from an outside trainer |
| Advantages | Cheaper; trainee produces something; relevant to the actual job; no travel | Wider knowledge and new ideas; trained specialists; trainee away from workplace pressure; recognised qualifications |
| Disadvantages | Trainer's time is lost; the trainee may learn bad habits; mistakes may damage equipment or customers | Costly (fees, travel); output is lost while the trainee is away; skills may not match the job |

**Induction** is the first stage: introduction to the workplace and its rules, safety training and the job description. A good induction reduces early mistakes and the chance of the new employee leaving.

**Training and development compared.** Training answers *what do you need to be able to do now?* Development answers *what could you become?* Development includes project work, rotation between jobs, mentoring, study towards higher qualifications and promotion. Training can be finished; development continues through a career.

## Impact of training and development

- **Productivity and quality** improve because workers make fewer errors and use equipment better.
- **Motivation and retention:** staff feel valued and see a future (Herzberg would include advancement and growth as motivators), so turnover falls.
- **Flexibility:** multi-skilled workers can cover absence and move to where demand is.
- **Intrapreneurship:** development of initiative and decision making encourages employees to suggest and lead new ideas.
- **Costs:** fees, trainers, lost output, and the risk of **poaching**, when a rival hires staff whom the business has trained and pays them more. This makes some firms reluctant to train, and encourages them to link training to agreements to stay or to build a culture that keeps staff.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: comparing the cost of two training methods]
Chipo must train six bakers on the new mixer. On-the-job training by the supplier's technician costs the bakery, for each baker, US\$60 of the head baker's time and US\$90 of lost output. Off-the-job training at a centre costs, for each baker, a fee of US\$250 and US\$140 of lost output.

| Method | Cost per baker (US\$) | Number of bakers | Total cost (US\$) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| On-the-job (60 + 90) | 150 | 6 | 900 |
| Off-the-job (250 + 140) | 390 | 6 | 2,340 |
| Extra cost of off-the-job | | | 1,440 |

1. On-the-job: 60 + 90 = 150 per baker; 6 × 150 = US\$900.
2. Off-the-job: 250 + 140 = 390 per baker; 6 × 390 = US\$2,340.
3. The off-the-job course costs US\$1,440 more.
4. Judgement: for a single machine used only in this bakery, on-the-job training is more relevant and cheaper. Off-the-job training would be worth the extra cost only if it gives qualifications that bakers can use for promotion, or if the supplier recommends it for safety.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: does training pay for itself?]
Chipo expects that, once trained, each baker will make better use of the mixer, raising the bakery's contribution by US\$65 a month per baker.

1. Monthly gain from six trained bakers = 6 × 65 = US\$390.
2. Payback of on-the-job training = 900 ÷ 390 = 2.3 months.
3. Payback of off-the-job training = 2,340 ÷ 390 = 6.0 months.
4. Both pay back within a year, so training is worthwhile if the gain lasts, though the on-the-job method recovers its cost sooner. The estimates of the gain are uncertain, and the risk that a baker leaves for a rival after training should be considered.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: case analysis of development]
**Context.** A fictional lodge near Victoria Falls rotates its receptionists through the restaurant, booking office and tour desk, and gives the best a small project: to improve the booking process.

**Point.** The lodge is developing staff for multi-skilling and intrapreneurship.

**Application.** A receptionist who has worked on all three desks can cover absences, and the project encourages ideas that the manager had not thought of.

**Analysis.** Multi-skilled staff give flexibility in busy periods and reduce the number of temporary workers needed. Staff given responsibility tend to feel valued, which improves retention. The costs are the time taken while staff learn, and the risk that a rival hires them after they have developed.

**Judgement.** The programme is likely to be worthwhile for a lodge with seasonal peaks, provided the lodge links progression and reward to the new skills so that staff have a reason to stay.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using training and development as if they were the same"}
Training is for a present job and can be completed. Development prepares for future roles and is longer-term. Use the terms precisely.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the cost of lost output"}
The cost of training includes the output lost while the trainee is learning, as well as fees and trainers' time.
:::

:::mistake{title="Assuming training is always worthwhile"}
Evaluate: weigh the cost and the risk of staff leaving against the benefits in productivity, quality and retention.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (state or outline types of training; evaluate a method), Paper 2 (essays on training and staff development) and Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2; Cambridge Paper 1 (define, explain) and Paper 2 (assess the impact of training in a data response).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/outline*: name the type and a feature. *Describe/explain*: say how the method works and apply it to the job in the case. *Analyse/examine*: link training to productivity, quality, motivation and cost. *Evaluate/assess/discuss*: weigh costs against benefits, consider the risk of poaching, and justify a choice of method.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Induction introduces new staff; on-the-job training is cheaper and relevant; off-the-job training gives wider knowledge but costs more.
- Training is for the present job; development prepares for future roles and continues through a career.
- Benefits include productivity, quality, motivation, retention, flexibility and intrapreneurship.
- Costs include fees, trainers' time, lost output and the risk that trained staff are poached.
- A payback calculation shows how quickly training recovers its cost.
- Evaluate the method against the job, the cost and the expected gain.
:::
