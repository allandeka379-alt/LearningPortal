## Introduction

Nyasha manages a fictional textile factory in Gweru. Orders have halved since a cheaper imported fabric arrived, and she must cut the wage bill. At the same time one machine operator has arrived late twenty times this term and has twice damaged equipment. These are two very different problems. In the first, jobs disappear although the workers have done nothing wrong; in the second, one worker's conduct is the issue. The law, the procedure and the effect on morale are different in each case, and an owner who mixes them up risks a costly dispute.

Redundancy and dismissal are tested in ZIMSEC Business Studies and Business Enterprise Skills (Form 6) and in Cambridge AS Level Papers 1 and 2. You should already understand the aims of HRM, recruitment and contracts from the earlier lessons.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Redundancy"}
The ending of a worker's job because the job is no longer needed, not because of anything the worker has done.
:::

:::definition{term="Dismissal"}
The ending of a worker's employment by the employer because of the worker's conduct or capability, or another reason connected with the worker.
:::

:::definition{term="Unfair dismissal"}
A dismissal without a valid reason, or one carried out without following the correct procedure or in breach of the contract or the law.
:::

:::definition{term="Voluntary redundancy"}
Redundancy that workers accept by choice, usually in return for a payment, when the employer asks for volunteers.
:::

## Redundancy

**Causes:** falling demand, new technology that replaces labour, closure of a plant, a merger with duplicated posts, or restructuring to cut costs.

**Types.** *Voluntary* redundancy lets workers choose, and the employer keeps its best staff only if the volunteers are not the most skilled. *Compulsory (involuntary)* redundancy means the employer selects workers, using fair criteria such as skills, performance record and length of service, not personal dislike.

**Procedure (outline).** The employer reviews other ways of saving costs, consults workers or their representatives, gives notice, explains how people were chosen, and pays any redundancy payment set by the law or the contract. Following the procedure reduces disputes and protects the reputation of the business.

**Ways of reducing the need for redundancy:** natural wastage (not replacing leavers), a freeze on recruitment, retraining and redeployment, shorter hours or job sharing, early retirement, and cutting other costs first.

## Dismissal and discipline

Discipline is how the employer deals with poor conduct or performance. A typical procedure rises in steps:

1. **Informal talk** with the supervisor, to explain the problem and agree to improve.
2. **Verbal warning**, recorded.
3. **Written warning**, stating what must change and by when.
4. **Final written warning**.
5. **Dismissal**, after a hearing at which the worker can state his or her case, usually with the right to be accompanied, and the right to appeal.

**Fair reasons** usually include serious misconduct (theft, violence), persistent poor conduct after warnings, lack of the capability or qualification needed for the job after support, or a genuine redundancy. **Unfair reasons** include joining a union, pregnancy, race or sex, making a complaint, or personal dislike by a manager. Some very serious misconduct (summary dismissal) can justify dismissal without warnings, but the employer must still be able to prove it.

| | Redundancy | Dismissal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reason | Job no longer needed | Worker's conduct or capability |
| Is the worker at fault? | No | Usually yes |
| Replacement hired? | No (the post disappears) | Often yes |
| Payment | Redundancy pay may be due | Usually only notice pay or pay due |

## Effects on the business and on morale

- **Costs:** redundancy payments, legal and administrative costs, and, if the process is unfair, compensation and reinstatement. Savings come later through lower wages.
- **Morale and trust:** remaining staff may fear for their own jobs ("survivor syndrome"), becoming less motivated, and the best workers may leave first because they find jobs easily.
- **Reputation:** an employer who treats workers unfairly finds it harder to recruit and may face industrial action.
- **Output:** fewer workers may mean overwork, errors and falling quality unless the work is reorganised.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: calculating a redundancy payment]
Assume that Nyasha's factory has a policy of paying two weeks' pay for each full year of service, with no upper limit (this is a policy assumption for the example, not a statement of the law). Three machine operators are to be made redundant. Each is paid US\$85 a week.

| Operator | Full years of service | Weeks' pay due | Payment (US\$) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Rudo | 4 | 8 | 680 |
| Farai | 9 | 18 | 1,530 |
| Sipho | 13 | 26 | 2,210 |
| **Total** | | | **4,420** |

1. Weeks' pay due = 2 × years: 8, 18 and 26.
2. Payment = weeks × US\$85: 8 × 85 = 680; 18 × 85 = 1,530; 26 × 85 = 2,210.
3. Total = 680 + 1,530 + 2,210 = US\$4,420.
4. The weekly wage saved is 3 × 85 = US\$255, so the payment is recovered in 4,420 ÷ 255 = 17.3 weeks, about four months. After that the saving is a gain, provided the workers are not replaced and output holds up.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis of a dismissal]
**Context.** The machine operator at Nyasha's factory has arrived late twenty times this term and twice damaged equipment. His supervisor has spoken to him informally but nothing is on record. Nyasha wants to dismiss him tomorrow.

**Point.** Dismissal may be justified, but not tomorrow and not without following the procedure.

**Application.** The lateness and damage are conduct issues. Without warnings on record she cannot show that he was told to improve, and without a hearing he cannot state his case.

**Analysis.** A dismissal carried out now would risk being found unfair, which could mean compensation or reinstatement and a poor reputation among other workers. A recorded verbal and written warning, a hearing and a right of appeal would protect the firm, and might also correct his behaviour at lower cost than recruiting a replacement.

**Judgement.** Nyasha should start the written warning procedure with a clear improvement plan. If conduct does not improve, dismissal will then be fair, and she will have the records to show it.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling a redundancy a dismissal for misconduct"}
Redundancy removes the job; the worker is not at fault. Dismissal is linked to the worker's conduct or capability. Use the terms precisely.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the cost of unfair procedure"}
A good evaluation says that skipping warnings or a hearing can lead to compensation, reinstatement and damage to reputation, so following the procedure is a saving, not an expense.
:::

:::mistake{title="Overlooking the morale of those who stay"}
The effects of redundancy continue after the leavers go. Staff who remain may be less motivated, and the best workers may leave.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (distinguish redundancy from dismissal; state steps of discipline) and Paper 2 (essays on labour relations and HRM); Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2; Cambridge Paper 1 (define, explain) and Paper 2 (analyse and assess the effects of redundancy in a data response).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*Distinguish/state*: give a clear definition of each term. *Explain/describe*: give the cause and effect for the business in the case. *Analyse/examine*: follow the chain from job losses to costs, morale and output. *Evaluate/assess/discuss*: weigh savings against costs and morale, consider alternatives, and justify a recommendation.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Redundancy ends a job that is no longer needed; dismissal ends employment because of the worker's conduct or capability.
- Redundancy can be voluntary or compulsory, and can be reduced by natural wastage, retraining, shorter hours and recruitment freezes.
- A disciplinary procedure usually moves from informal talk through warnings to a hearing, dismissal and appeal.
- Fair dismissal needs a valid reason and a correct procedure; unfair dismissal can lead to compensation or reinstatement.
- Redundancy saves wages but costs payments, trust and morale; the best workers may leave first.
- Follow labour legislation; refer to it generally in answers.
:::
