## Introduction

Chipo manages the dispatch team of a fictional logistics company in Lusaka. Over six months the team's delivery errors rose, three of her eight drivers were frequently absent and customers began to complain. Chipo's director asked: "How do you know who is performing well, why is performance falling and what will you do about it?" Without measurement, a manager can only guess. With measurement, she can find the cause, agree targets with each driver, and review progress. This lesson explains how businesses measure and improve the performance of employees and how **management by objectives (MBO)** links individual targets to business objectives.

This lesson applies to ZIMSEC Business Studies (Form 6), Business Enterprise Skills (Form 6) and Cambridge **A Level** (Papers 3 and 4). Performance appraisal, job evaluation and remuneration are ZIMSEC detail. You should already know the motivation theories and the management styles.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Employee performance"}
How well an employee carries out their job, judged against agreed standards of output, quality, attendance and behaviour.
:::

:::definition{term="Performance appraisal"}
A formal, regular assessment of an employee's performance by a manager, usually followed by feedback and agreed targets.
:::

:::definition{term="Management by objectives (MBO)"}
A method of management in which managers and employees agree specific objectives for each individual that support the objectives of the business, and performance is then reviewed against those objectives.
:::

:::definition{term="Job evaluation"}
A systematic way of comparing the worth of different jobs in a business, in order to decide pay grades. (ZIMSEC detail)
:::

:::definition{term="Remuneration"}
All forms of payment and benefits that an employee receives in return for work, such as wages, salary, bonuses and benefits. (ZIMSEC detail)
:::

## Measuring employee performance

| Measure | What it shows | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Quantity produced or sold per employee or per hour | 40 deliveries a week per driver |
| Quality | Errors, rejects, complaints, returns | 3% of deliveries damaged |
| Absenteeism | Attendance and commitment | Days absent as a percentage of days available |
| Labour turnover | Employees leaving as a percentage of the workforce | 25% of drivers left this year |
| Appraisal results | A manager's overall rating against targets | "Meets expectations" |
| Customer feedback | Customer satisfaction | Compliments and complaints |

No single measure is enough: a driver may deliver many parcels (high output) but damage many (low quality). Measures should be fair, based on things the employee controls, and compared with a target or last year's result.

**Formulas used in this lesson**

:::keyfact{title="Formulas"}
- Labour productivity = output / number of employees (or per hour).
- Absenteeism rate (%) = days lost through absence / (employees x working days) x 100.
- Labour turnover (%) = employees leaving in the year / average number of employees x 100.
:::

## Causes and consequences of poor performance

| Causes | Consequences |
| --- | --- |
| Lack of skills or training | Low output and mistakes |
| Poor motivation (low pay, no recognition, poor supervision) | High absenteeism and labour turnover |
| Unclear targets or poor communication | Waste of materials and time |
| Poor working conditions or faulty equipment | Dissatisfied customers and lost sales |
| Personal problems or health | Lower profit and damaged reputation |
| Poor job design or overload | Low morale and conflict among staff |

## Strategies for improving performance

| Strategy | How it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Training and coaching | Raises skills and confidence |
| Motivation: recognition, enriched jobs, fair pay | Increases effort (see motivation theories) |
| Clear targets and regular feedback | Employees know what is expected |
| Job redesign | Matches the job to ability; reduces overload |
| Better equipment and conditions | Removes obstacles to performance |
| Discipline | Used when employees break rules or ignore warnings; a last resort |
| Appraisal and development plans | Identifies strengths and needs |

The manager should first find the **cause** of poor performance: training will not help if the problem is low morale.

## ZIMSEC detail: appraisal, job evaluation and remuneration

**Performance appraisal.** Usually yearly or twice a year. The manager and employee discuss results against targets, strengths and weaknesses, and agree training needs and goals. It is fair when criteria are clear and appraisers are trained; it is unfair when the appraiser is biased or the criteria are vague. Appraisal results can be used to decide promotion, training and pay.

**Job evaluation.** The business compares the worth of different jobs (not the people in them) using factors such as skill, responsibility, effort and conditions. Common methods are **ranking** jobs in order, **grading** jobs into pay grades and awarding **points** for each factor. The aim is a fair pay structure in which jobs of similar worth are paid similarly.

**Remuneration.** Pay may be a wage (usually weekly, often hourly) or a salary (usually monthly), plus bonuses, commission and benefits such as transport or medical aid. A good remuneration system is fair, competitive and linked to performance.

## Management by objectives (MBO)

MBO was popularised by Peter Drucker. It works as follows:

1. The business sets **overall objectives** (for example, raise on-time delivery to 95%).
2. Managers and employees **agree individual objectives** that support the overall objectives (for example, "each driver achieves 95% on-time delivery").
3. Objectives are **SMART**: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.
4. Employees are given the **authority and resources** to reach them.
5. Progress is **reviewed regularly**, and results are discussed.
6. Achievement leads to **recognition and reward**, and objectives are reset.

| Benefits | Limitations |
| --- | --- |
| Links individual effort to business objectives | Time-consuming to set and review for every employee |
| Employees take part, so motivation and commitment rise | Objectives may be set too low (to be sure of reaching them) or too high |
| Gives clear targets and a basis for appraisal | Focus on measurable targets may ignore quality, teamwork and other aspects |
| Develops managers and delegation | May fail if conditions change and objectives are not revised |
| Easier to identify poor performance | Can lead to conflict or stress when targets are missed |

MBO suits businesses with skilled, motivated staff and results that can be measured (for example sales or deliveries). It works less well where results are hard to measure, where jobs depend on teamwork, or where conditions change quickly.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: measuring performance with figures]
**Context.** Chipo's dispatch team has 8 drivers. In April the team worked 22 days. The figures are shown below.

| Item | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Deliveries made in April | 1,320 |
| Deliveries with errors | 66 |
| Days lost through absence in April | 22 |
| Drivers who left in the year | 2 |
| Average number of drivers in the year | 8 |

| Measure | Working | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Productivity (deliveries per driver) | 1,320 / 8 | 165 |
| Error rate | 66 / 1,320 x 100 | 5.0% |
| Absenteeism rate | 22 / (8 x 22) x 100 | 12.5% |
| Labour turnover | 2 / 8 x 100 | 25.0% |

1. An error rate of 5.0% and absenteeism of 12.5% are high; the team's output hides quality and attendance problems.
2. A labour turnover of 25.0% means one in four drivers left, adding recruitment and training cost.
3. Judgement: Chipo should find the cause (for example, long hours or poor vehicles), not blame the drivers. The figures also need comparison with last year's results and with other teams.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: applying MBO to the dispatch team]
**Context.** The company's objective is to raise on-time delivery from 85% to 95% within six months. Chipo agrees objectives with each driver.

| Step | Application |
| --- | --- |
| Overall objective | 95% on-time delivery within six months |
| Individual objective | Each driver: 95% on-time delivery by 30 September; error rate below 2% |
| Resources and authority | Drivers may choose their route order; the company repairs the two oldest vehicles |
| Review | Monthly meeting to compare results with the objective |
| Reward | Recognition and a bonus for drivers who reach the objective |

**Point.** MBO links each driver's work to the company's objective.

**Analysis.** Drivers who help set the objective feel committed. The authority to choose routes and the repair of old vehicles remove obstacles. Monthly reviews let Chipo spot problems early.

**Judgement.** MBO is suitable because delivery performance is measurable. The risk is that drivers rush and cause errors, so the quality target is included. The objective must be revised if conditions change, such as fuel shortages.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Judging performance by one measure only"}
High output with high errors is not good performance. Use output, quality and attendance together.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating all poor performance as laziness"}
Poor performance may be due to lack of training, poor equipment, unclear targets or personal problems. Find the cause before choosing a strategy.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing MBO as targets imposed from above"}
In MBO the objectives are agreed between manager and employee, and are linked to the objectives of the business. Targets imposed without discussion are not MBO.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (state measures; calculate absenteeism or turnover) and Paper 2 (data response and essays on MBO); Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2; Cambridge Paper 3 (calculations such as labour turnover; short cases) and Paper 4 (essays: evaluate MBO).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/identify*: name a measure or a stage of MBO. *Calculate*: show the formula and working. *Explain/describe*: say what the measure or cause means for the business. *Analyse/examine*: trace the effect of poor performance or of MBO. *Evaluate/discuss/justify*: weigh MBO against other approaches for this business and give a reasoned judgement.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Employee performance is measured by output, quality, absenteeism, labour turnover, appraisal results and customer feedback; one measure alone can mislead.
- Poor performance has many causes (training, motivation, targets, conditions); find the cause before choosing a strategy.
- Strategies include training, motivation, clear targets and feedback, job redesign, better equipment and, last, discipline.
- ZIMSEC detail: appraisal assesses employees against targets, job evaluation compares the worth of jobs, and remuneration is the total pay and benefits.
- MBO: agree objectives linked to the business objectives, give authority and resources, review and reward.
- MBO motivates through involvement but is time-consuming and may neglect aspects that are hard to measure.
:::
