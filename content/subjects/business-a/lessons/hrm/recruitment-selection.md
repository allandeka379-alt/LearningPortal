## Introduction

Tatenda owns a fictional garage in Mutare. His best mechanic has just resigned and a rush of orders is coming. He can promote his workshop assistant, advertise in the local paper, ask a trade college, or use an employment agency. Each route costs a different amount, brings a different pool of candidates, and carries a different risk of a bad appointment. Choosing and appointing staff is one of the most expensive decisions a business makes, because a wrong choice affects output, morale and cost for years.

Recruitment and selection are tested in ZIMSEC Business Studies and Business Enterprise Skills (Form 6) and in Cambridge AS Level Papers 1 and 2. Questions ask you to explain a method, compare internal and external recruitment for a stated post, or judge which selection method is most useful. You should know what HRM does and how a workforce plan identifies the gap in staff numbers (the previous lesson).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Recruitment"}
The process of finding and attracting suitable candidates to apply for a vacancy.
:::

:::definition{term="Selection"}
The process of choosing, from the candidates who have applied, the person who is best suited to the post.
:::

:::definition{term="Job description"}
A document that sets out the title of a job, its main duties and responsibilities, to whom the post holder reports, and the place and conditions of work.
:::

:::definition{term="Person specification"}
A document that describes the qualifications, skills, experience and personal qualities needed by the person who will do the job.
:::

:::definition{term="Employment contract"}
A legally binding agreement between employer and employee that sets out the terms of the job, such as pay, hours, duties and notice.
:::

## The recruitment process

1. **Identify the vacancy** and decide whether the job is still needed in its present form (a workforce plan may show this).
2. **Write the job description and person specification.** The job description says what the job is; the person specification says who is wanted.
3. **Attract candidates** by advertising internally or externally.
4. **Shortlist** the applicants by comparing their applications with the person specification.
5. **Select** by interview and other tests, take up references, and offer the post.
6. **Issue a contract** and begin induction.

**ZIMSEC detail: a job advertisement** states the job title, the employer, the main duties, the essential qualifications, the pay or how to find out, how to apply and the closing date. It is worded to attract suitable people without promising more than the job offers.

## Internal and external recruitment

| | Internal (existing staff) | External (from outside) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advantages | Cheaper and faster; the person knows the business; rewards loyalty and motivates others; lower risk of a bad choice | Wider choice of candidates; new skills and fresh ideas; may avoid jealousy among staff |
| Disadvantages | Small choice; creates a new vacancy to fill; may cause resentment among those not chosen; few new ideas | Advertising and selection are costly and slow; candidates are less well known; the new person needs induction |

**Methods of attracting external candidates.** Job advertisements (newspapers, notice boards, trade magazines), employment agencies (which find and sometimes screen candidates for a fee), online recruitment (websites and social media, with a wide, cheap reach), and word of mouth (cheap, but may limit variety and may not reach good candidates).

:::worked-solution[Example 1: comparing the cost of two recruitment routes]
Tatenda wants to fill a post with a first-year salary of US\$9,600. In-house recruitment would cost US\$180 for an advertisement and four interviews at US\$45 of manager time each. An agency charges 15% of the first-year salary.

| Route | Cost items | Cost (US\$) |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Advertise and interview in-house | Advertisement 180 + 4 interviews at 45 | 360 |
| Use an agency | 15% of 9,600 | 1,440 |
| Extra cost of the agency | 1,440 − 360 | 1,080 |

1. In-house: 180 + (4 × 45) = 180 + 180 = US\$360.
2. Agency: 9,600 × 0.15 = US\$1,440.
3. The agency costs US\$1,080 more, but it saves Tatenda's own time in a busy period and may bring candidates he would not reach.
4. Judgement: advertising in-house is cheaper and acceptable if Tatenda has the time; the agency is worthwhile only if the post is hard to fill or a poor appointment would be very costly.
:::

## Selection methods

| Method | What it shows | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application form / curriculum vitae (CV) | Qualifications and experience; used to shortlist | May be exaggerated; shows little about personality |
| Interview | Communication, attitude and fit; allows questions | Time-consuming; interviewers may be biased |
| References | Previous employer's view of reliability | Usually favourable; may be out of date |
| Tests (skill, aptitude, personality) | Direct evidence of ability for the job | Cost and time; may not reflect real work |
| Assessment centre | Several tests, group tasks and interviews over a day or more | Expensive; best for senior or important posts |

The usefulness of a method depends on the post. For a driver, a driving test is more useful than an interview; for a sales manager, an interview and a role-play are more useful. Using several methods gives a more reliable picture than any one, but costs more.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis of a selection decision]
**Context.** A fictional private school near Gweru must appoint a bursar to manage fees and budgets. Two candidates remain: Chipo, an accountant with five years' experience who interviews well but has not worked in a school, and Farai, the school's deputy bursar for three years, whose references are excellent but who has no formal qualification.

**Point.** Both candidates have strengths; the choice depends on which matters most to the school.

**Application.** The bursar's job needs accounting skill (the person specification), trust in handling fees and understanding of the school.

**Analysis.** Chipo meets the skill requirement and could bring fresh ideas, but she needs induction and costs the school more to recruit. Farai knows the school and is a low-risk internal appointment who would motivate other staff, but he lacks the qualification. A skills test and the references of both would add evidence.

**Judgement.** The school should give both a practical test in budgeting. If Farai passes, appoint him and fund his qualification; otherwise appoint Chipo. This uses evidence beyond the interview, reducing the risk of a costly mistake.
:::

## The employment contract

A contract usually states: the names of employer and employee, job title and main duties, place of work, pay and how often it is paid, hours of work, holidays, the notice each side must give, any probation period, and the disciplinary and grievance procedures.

**Why it matters.** It makes terms clear so that disputes are fewer; it protects the employee from unfair treatment and the employer from claims about what was promised; and it gives evidence if a dispute reaches a tribunal. Contracts must follow the labour legislation that applies in the country (do not quote particular laws unless certain). Changing a contract needs the agreement of both sides.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing job description and person specification"}
The job description is about the *job* (duties, reporting lines, conditions). The person specification is about the *person* (qualifications, skills, qualities). Many answers swap them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating recruitment and selection as one stage"}
Recruitment attracts applicants; selection chooses among them. Marks are awarded for distinguishing the stages.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing methods without judging usefulness"}
"Interview" earns little on its own. Say what it shows for this post and what its limits are.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (state, outline; write or analyse a job advertisement) and Paper 2 (essays on recruitment and selection); Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2; Cambridge Paper 1 (define, explain a method) and Paper 2 (assess internal against external recruitment in a data response).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/outline*: name the method or the content of a document. *Explain/describe*: say how it works and apply it to the post. *Examine/analyse*: link the method to cost, speed or quality of appointment. *Evaluate/assess/discuss/justify*: compare two methods or routes for the post in the case, say which is best and why, and note a condition.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Recruitment attracts candidates; selection chooses between them; the job description describes the job and the person specification the person.
- Internal recruitment is cheaper and faster with lower risk; external recruitment gives a wider choice and fresh ideas.
- External methods include advertisements, agencies, online recruitment and word of mouth; selection methods include CVs, interviews, references, tests and assessment centres.
- The best selection method depends on the post; several methods together are more reliable.
- A contract of employment sets out the terms of the job and protects both sides.
- ZIMSEC also asks about determining the demand for staff and writing a job advertisement.
:::
