## Introduction

When a fictional bus company in Harare lost its main contract, the managing director, Chipo, did not hide in her office. She called a meeting of all 60 drivers and mechanics, explained what had happened, set out a plan to win two new routes and asked each team what it could do. Within a month the staff were working extra shifts without being told. Meanwhile, in the same depot, the head mechanic, Kudakwashe, has no formal title above the others but the younger mechanics follow his advice and copy his habits. Both are leaders, but in different ways. Leadership determines whether people follow a plan willingly or only because they are told to.

This lesson is examined in ZIMSEC Business Studies (Form 5), Business Enterprise Skills (Form 5) and Cambridge **A Level** (Papers 3 and 4). Formal and informal leaders are ZIMSEC detail. You should know the functions of management and the main management styles.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Leadership"}
The ability to influence, inspire and guide other people to work willingly towards a shared goal.
:::

:::definition{term="Formal leader"}
A person who holds authority because of a position in the organisation, such as a director, manager or supervisor.
:::

:::definition{term="Informal leader"}
A person who has influence over colleagues because of respect, skills or personality, without holding a formal position of authority. (ZIMSEC detail)
:::

## The purpose of leadership

Leadership gives a business **direction** (a vision of where it is going), builds **commitment** (people want to contribute), and helps the business to **cope with change**. In a crisis or period of change, staff look to leaders for guidance. Good leadership aligns the work of many people towards the objectives of the business.

## Leadership roles in a business

| Role | What leadership looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Directors | Set the vision, values and strategy; appoint senior managers; are accountable to owners |
| Managers | Lead departments; turn strategy into plans; develop staff |
| Supervisors | Lead teams at the point of work; allocate tasks; give feedback and solve everyday problems |
| Worker representatives | Speak for employees; negotiate with management; influence attitudes of colleagues |

Leaders are found at every level. A supervisor who is respected on the shop floor can have more influence on day-to-day output than a distant director.

## Qualities of a good leader

| Quality | Why it matters in business |
| --- | --- |
| Vision | Gives staff a direction and a reason for effort |
| Communication | Explains goals and listens, so that staff understand and feel heard |
| Integrity and fairness | Builds trust; staff follow leaders they respect |
| Decisiveness | Makes timely decisions even with incomplete information |
| Empathy | Understands the needs of staff and customers |
| Confidence and resilience | Keeps the team steady in setbacks |
| Technical or business knowledge | Earns credibility and makes better decisions |
| Willingness to delegate | Develops others and frees time for strategy |

No list of qualities guarantees success. Different situations need different qualities: a crisis needs decisiveness, a creative team needs openness. The qualities can also be learned and developed.

## Leadership compared with management

| | Leadership | Management |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | Direction, vision, change and inspiring people | Planning, organising, controlling and efficiency |
| Source of influence | Trust, respect and personal influence; may not need a formal position | Formal authority from the position held |
| Question asked | "Where should we go and why?" | "How do we get there, with what resources?" |
| People | Followers choose to follow | Subordinates are expected to follow |

A manager without leadership skills may achieve routine tasks but fail to inspire. A leader without management skills may inspire but lack the organisation to deliver. Effective businesses need both, and the same person is often expected to do both.

## Formal and informal leaders (ZIMSEC detail)

Formal leaders hold authority from their position and can use rewards and sanctions. Informal leaders emerge within groups and have influence because colleagues respect them. Informal leaders can **help** a business, for example by passing on good practice and supporting change, or **harm** it by resisting change. A sensible manager identifies informal leaders and involves them in decisions.

## How leadership affects performance

Good leadership raises motivation, commitment and teamwork, which improves productivity, quality and customer service, and reduces absence and staff turnover. Weak leadership causes confusion, conflict and loss of good employees. However, leadership is only one factor: market conditions, resources and the quality of staff also matter, so performance cannot be credited or blamed on the leader alone.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: leader or manager?]
**Context.** A fictional school production unit near Gweru makes uniforms. Three staff are described.

| Person | What they do | Leader, manager or both? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The unit head, Mr Dube | Draws up the production schedule, allocates machines and checks quality against standards | Mainly management: planning, organising and controlling |
| The senior tailor, Mrs Moyo | Has no title but is followed by younger tailors, who copy her methods and take her advice | Informal leader: influence from respect and skill |
| The bursar, Tendai | Explains to staff why the unit must double output for the new term, listens to concerns and sets out a plan | Leadership as well as management: vision, communication and organisation |

**Judgement.** The unit needs all three. Mr Dube's schedule is needed for efficiency, but staff will only work harder if they believe in the goal, which Tendai provides. The unit head should work with Mrs Moyo, who can encourage younger tailors to accept new methods.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis, the effect of a leader on performance]
**Context.** Two fictional branches of a hardware chain in Mutare sell the same products at the same prices. Branch A's manager, Rudo, explains targets, asks for suggestions and thanks staff. Branch B's manager gives orders on a noticeboard and is rarely seen. Staff turnover is 5% a year in Branch A and 30% in Branch B.

**Point.** Leadership differences help to explain the gap in staff turnover.

**Application.** Rudo communicates the purpose of targets and listens to suggestions, qualities of a good leader. Staff in Branch A feel valued. The manager of Branch B shows little communication, empathy or presence.

**Analysis.** High turnover in Branch B increases recruitment and training costs and loses experienced staff who know the customers, which may reduce sales and service quality.

**Judgement.** Leadership probably explains some of the difference, but not all: location, pay and the local labour market may also differ. The chain should compare more evidence, such as exit interviews, before blaming the leader.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying that leader and manager mean the same thing"}
A manager has formal authority and a duty to plan, organise and control. A leader inspires and influences people, and may not hold a formal position. The same person can be both, but the roles differ.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing qualities without applying them"}
"A good leader is honest" earns little. Say why the quality matters for this business, for example how honesty builds trust among staff during a redundancy.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating informal leaders as always harmful"}
Informal leaders can support a change if they are consulted. They are a risk only if they oppose it and are ignored.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (state qualities; compare leader and manager; short cases) and Paper 2 (essays); Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2; Cambridge Paper 3 (short case questions) and Paper 4 (essays that assess the importance of leadership).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/identify*: name qualities or roles. *Explain/describe*: say why a quality matters in the business. *Analyse/examine*: trace the effect of leadership on motivation and performance. *Assess/evaluate/discuss/justify*: weigh the importance of leadership against other factors and reach a reasoned judgement for this business.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Leadership is the ability to influence and guide others to work willingly towards shared goals; it gives direction, commitment and a way to cope with change.
- Leadership roles exist at every level: directors, managers, supervisors and worker representatives.
- Qualities include vision, communication, integrity, decisiveness, empathy, resilience and knowledge, and the qualities needed depend on the situation.
- Management is about planning, organising and controlling; leadership is about direction and influence; businesses need both.
- ZIMSEC detail: informal leaders have influence through respect and can support or resist change.
- Leadership affects performance through motivation and commitment but is only one factor.
:::
