## Introduction

Tatenda runs a tuck-shop supply business from Chinhoyi. Every year before schools open in January she needs US\$12,000 to buy extra stock, which she repays by March. This year she also wants US\$40,000 for a delivery truck that will last eight years. The same owner, two different needs: a bank overdraft may suit the first, but a three-month overdraft would be a poor choice for the truck. Choosing a source of finance is a decision, and the best source depends on the purpose, the time period, the cost, the risk, the form of business and how much control the owner is willing to give up.

This lesson draws together the internal and external sources. It is tested in ZIMSEC Business Studies and Business Enterprise Skills (Form 5) and Cambridge AS Level, usually as a case-study question: *recommend a source of finance for this business*. You should already know the sources and their features.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
| ZIMSEC | Cambridge |
| --- | --- |
| Drivers of the choice of finance; suitability of sources to the type of business | Factors influencing the choice of finance; appropriateness of each source for a stated purpose |

Both boards examine the same judgement. Whatever the term, the answer should link a source to a stated business, purpose and period, and justify the choice.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Gearing (capital structure)"}
The proportion of a business's long-term finance that is borrowed. A highly geared business has a large share of debt, and so must pay interest whether or not it makes a profit.
:::

:::definition{term="Security (collateral)"}
An asset that a borrower offers to a lender, who may take it if the loan is not repaid. A lender is more willing to lend, and charges less interest, when security is offered.
:::

## Ownership and the sources available

| Form of business | Typical sources available | Sources not available |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sole trader | Owner's savings, retained profit, bank loan or overdraft, hire purchase, leasing, trade credit, micro-finance, grants, crowd funding | Shares |
| Partnership | The above, plus new partners and their capital | Shares |
| Private limited company | The above, plus shares sold to chosen investors, debentures, venture capital | Shares to the general public |
| Public limited company | The above, plus shares sold to the public (on a stock exchange) | None of the usual sources |
| Co-operative or social enterprise | Members' funds, grants, loans, crowd funding | Shares for outside profit-seekers |

A new business without trading history or assets often finds that banks will not lend, so it relies on the owners' savings, micro-finance, grants or a business angel or venture capitalist. An established business with property can use a mortgage or a loan secured on its assets.

## Factors influencing the choice

1. **Purpose and period.** Match short-term needs (stock, wages) with short-term sources (overdraft, trade credit) and long-term needs (premises, machinery) with long-term sources (loan, mortgage, shares, retained profit).
2. **Cost.** Compare interest, fees and total repayments; equity has a cost in dividends and a share of profit.
3. **Flexibility.** Can the business borrow only when it needs to? An overdraft is flexible; a loan is fixed.
4. **Control.** Shares and new partners dilute control; loans do not.
5. **Existing debt and risk.** A business that already has a lot of debt (high gearing) should avoid more, because it must pay interest even in a poor year.
6. **Security available.** Lenders want assets as security.
7. **Speed and availability.** Some sources take months to arrange (a share issue), others days (an overdraft).
8. **Size and form of business.** A sole trader cannot sell shares; a small business may be unable to attract investors.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: overdraft or loan for a seasonal need?]
Tatenda needs US\$12,000 for stock for 3 months. An overdraft costs 18% a year on the amount used, and she can use it for only the 3 months. A bank loan costs 10% a year but must be taken for a full year. (Both rates are assumptions.)

| Option | Working | Interest cost (US\$) |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Overdraft for 3 months | 12,000 × 18% × 3 ÷ 12 | 540 |
| Bank loan for 12 months | 12,000 × 10% | 1,200 |

1. The overdraft rate is higher, but she pays it only for three months: 12,000 × 0.18 × 0.25 = US\$540.
2. The loan must be taken for a year, so the interest is 12,000 × 0.10 = US\$1,200, even though she needs the money for only three months. (She could repay early only if the bank allows it without a charge.)
3. The overdraft is cheaper in total and more flexible for a short, seasonal need. For a need lasting a year or more the loan's lower rate would be cheaper.
4. The decision therefore depends on the period, not only on the interest rate.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: scoring the options]
A scoring table helps compare sources against the factors, but scores are judgements. Tatenda gives each source a score from 1 (poor) to 5 (best) on each factor and weights the factors by importance (weights add up to 100).

| Criterion | Weight | Overdraft | Bank loan | Share issue |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cost | 30 | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| Control kept | 25 | 5 | 5 | 2 |
| Flexibility | 20 | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| Speed of arranging | 15 | 5 | 3 | 1 |
| Low risk | 10 | 2 | 3 | 5 |
| **Weighted total** | 100 | 410 | 360 | 265 |

1. Overdraft: 30 × 3 + 25 × 5 + 20 × 5 + 15 × 5 + 10 × 2 = 90 + 125 + 100 + 75 + 20 = 410.
2. Bank loan: 30 × 4 + 25 × 5 + 20 × 2 + 15 × 3 + 10 × 3 = 120 + 125 + 40 + 45 + 30 = 360.
3. Share issue: 30 × 3 + 25 × 2 + 20 × 3 + 15 × 1 + 10 × 5 = 90 + 50 + 60 + 15 + 50 = 265.
4. The overdraft scores highest for this seasonal need. But the scores and weights are subjective: if Tatenda gave much more weight to low risk, the order could change, and none of the figures show whether the bank will agree to the overdraft.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: case analysis, which source for the truck?]
**Context.** Tatenda needs US\$40,000 for a truck that will last eight years. She has an overdraft already used for stock, owns no property and has made a modest profit for three years. A bank offers an eight-year loan secured on the truck. A cousin offers to join as a partner with US\$40,000 and a share of profit.

**Point.** A long-term loan matches the eight-year life of the truck.

**Application.** The loan is secured on the truck, so the bank is willing to lend even though she owns no property; the repayments can be spread over the truck's life. The partner would bring no interest cost, but he would share profit and decision-making for good.

**Analysis.** The loan adds a fixed cost, and her existing overdraft means her debt is already significant, so repayments could strain cash in a poor year. The partner reduces this risk but costs her control and a share of all future profit, which may exceed the interest over the truck's life.

**Judgement.** Tatenda should choose the loan if her profit comfortably covers the repayments, because she keeps control and the cost ends after eight years. If profit is very uncertain, she might negotiate a smaller loan combined with part of the cousin's money.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Recommending a source without linking it to the purpose and period"}
An overdraft is not suitable for buying a building. Always say how the source matches the length and size of the need.
:::

:::mistake{title="Judging only on cost"}
The cheapest source may be inflexible, slow to arrange or unavailable to the business. Weigh cost with control, risk and flexibility.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring the form of business"}
A sole trader cannot issue shares, and a private company cannot sell shares to the general public.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving a list without a decision"}
A recommendation needs a reason and a condition, for example 'a loan, provided profits are stable'.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Papers 1 and 2 and Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2 (recommend and justify a source for a business); Cambridge Paper 1 (a short explanation of why a source suits a business), Paper 2 (data response with a final recommendation) and the AS essay.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/identify*: name a suitable source. *Explain*: link it to the purpose, period or cost. *Analyse*: show how the source affects cash flow, control and risk. *Evaluate/justify/advise/recommend*: compare at least two sources for the stated purpose, weigh them against the factors, give a decision and say what it depends on.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The form of ownership limits the sources: only companies issue shares; partnerships can add partners.
- Match the source to the purpose and period: short-term needs with short-term sources, long-term needs with long-term sources.
- Compare cost, flexibility, control, existing debt, risk, security and speed.
- A source that is cheapest per year may be dearer in total if it must be held longer than needed.
- A good recommendation names a source, links it to the case and states a condition or risk.
:::
