## Introduction

Walk through almost any town in Zimbabwe and you will see enterprise at work: a woman selling airtime and cold drinks from a table at a bus stop, a young man in Chipinge keeping bees on a hillside, a family in Marondera growing oyster mushrooms in a darkened shed. None of them started with a large factory. Each noticed something that local people wanted and found a low-cost way to supply it.

This lesson is for ZIMSEC Business Enterprise Skills Form 6 only. It is examined in Paper 1 (structured parts and case studies, where you may be asked to appraise a venture or identify its risks) and Paper 2 (essays such as *discuss the constraints on enterprises*). You should know the qualities of an enterpriser from the entrepreneurs lesson and the idea of costs and revenue.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Enterprise venture"}
An income-earning activity started by an enterpriser to meet a local need or want, such as bee farming, mushroom farming, vending or software services.
:::

:::definition{term="Opportunity"}
A gap between what people want and what is currently supplied that an enterpriser could profitably fill.
:::

:::definition{term="Constraint"}
A limit, outside the enterpriser's control or required by rules, that restricts what a venture may do or how well it can do it.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Board note"}

:::

## Ways of enterprising and example ventures

| Venture | Typical start-up needs | Main income | Typical risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bee farming | Hives, protective clothing, a site with flowering plants | Honey, wax | Colonies abscond or die; theft |
| Mushroom farming | A shed that can be kept dark and humid, spawn, substrate | Fresh and dried mushrooms | Contamination spoils a crop; fast deterioration |
| Vending (airtime, snacks, vegetables) | A table or stall, stock, a licence | Margin on resale | Weather, competition, council action |
| Software and digital services | A computer, internet, skills | Fees for apps, websites, repairs | Rapid change, poor payment, unreliable power |
| Small-scale processing (peanut butter, juice) | Equipment, hygiene standards | Value added on raw produce | Quality failure, supply of raw materials |

## Finding and judging opportunities

An enterpriser finds opportunities by **observing the local environment**: what do residents complain they cannot buy; what materials are plentiful and cheap; what skills does the community have; what do other places already do that has not reached this one?

Before committing money, the opportunity is judged by asking:

1. Is there **demand**, and will customers pay a price that covers costs?
2. Can the enterpriser obtain the **resources** (land, skills, finance, inputs)?
3. How does it compare with the **competition**?
4. Is it **legal and acceptable** to the community?
5. What is the **likely return** compared with the risk and with other uses of the money?

## Risks and how to reduce them

| Risk | Example | Ways to reduce it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production | Disease kills a crop; a machine breaks | Training, hygiene, maintenance, insurance if available |
| Market | Customers buy less or rivals cut prices | Research first; sell to several customers |
| Financial | Costs exceed income; customers pay late | Budget, cash control, start small, credit limits |
| Natural | Drought, flood, fire | Choose the site carefully, diversify, store safely |
| Theft and fraud | Stock or cash stolen | Security, record keeping, separate duties |

Risk cannot be removed; it can only be reduced and accepted knowingly. Reducing it usually costs money or time, so the enterpriser must weigh the cost of protection against the loss it prevents.

## Constraints on enterprises

- **Political**: policies on land, taxes and trade that change with government; unstable conditions reduce confidence.
- **Legal**: licences, registration, health and safety rules, labour laws and contracts.
- **Social**: community attitudes, customs and religious beliefs; availability of skills; crime.
- **Ecological**: limits on water, soil and forest use; pollution; the need to protect the environment.
- **Economic**: inflation, interest rates, falling incomes, high costs of electricity and transport, access to markets.

Constraints are not always negative: a hygiene rule protects customers and improves quality, though it adds cost.

## Ethics in running a venture

Ethical behaviour means being honest with customers (correct weights and measures, truthful descriptions), fair to workers, careful of the environment and open in dealing with suppliers and the authorities. It matters because customers return to businesses they trust, because dishonest behaviour can lead to penalties and loss of licence, and because the community supports enterprises that act responsibly.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: appraising a mushroom venture]
A fictional mushroom project in Marondera sells fresh mushrooms at US\$3.00 a kilogram. Variable costs (spawn, substrate, packaging) are US\$1.40 a kilogram and fixed costs (shed rent, labour, water and power) are US\$400 a month. The grower expects to sell 400 kg a month.

1. Contribution per kg = 3.00 − 1.40 = US\$1.60.
2. Break-even output = 400 ÷ 1.60 = 250 kg a month.

| Output (kg) | Revenue (US\$) | Total cost (US\$) | Profit / (loss) (US\$) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 0 | 400 | (400) |
| 125 | 375 | 575 | (200) |
| 250 | 750 | 750 | 0 |
| 400 | 1,200 | 960 | 240 |

3. At 400 kg, profit = 400 × 1.60 − 400 = US\$240 a month. The margin of safety is 400 − 250 = 150 kg, which is 37.5% of expected sales.
4. Risk test: if contamination cuts output by 20% to 320 kg, profit = 320 × 1.60 − 400 = US\$112, so the venture still makes a profit. The risk is acceptable, but a second bad month would use up most of the profit, so hygiene is the key risk to reduce.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis, constraints and ethics]
**Context.** Chipo, a fictional vendor in Gweru, sells fresh milk in plastic bottles from a cool box at a bus stop. The council says that vendors must be registered and that milk must be pasteurised. Competitors sell cheaper milk without pasteurising.

**Point.** Legal and economic constraints affect Chipo's venture, and ethics are involved.

**Application.** Registering and pasteurising raise her costs (a licence fee, a pasteuriser, fuel). Her unregistered rivals charge less.

**Analysis.** Her higher costs mean she must charge more or accept a smaller margin. But safe milk protects her customers from illness, and if a rival's milk causes an outbreak, customers and the council will turn away from unlicensed sellers.

**Judgement.** She should comply and sell on safety and reliability, because the risk of closure and the harm to customers outweigh the cost; the venture is viable only if enough customers value that safety.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Naming a venture without checking demand"}
An idea is not an opportunity until customers are willing to pay a price that covers costs. State the evidence of demand.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying risk can be removed"}
Risk can be reduced, not removed. Explain the cost of each protective step too.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing constraints with risks"}
A constraint is a limit set by rules or conditions (a licence law, an ecological limit). A risk is the chance of loss from events such as drought or theft.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Enterprise Skills Paper 1 (appraise a venture from figures, identify risks) and Paper 2 (essays on constraints, ethics and opportunities). The topic is not examined in Business Studies or Cambridge.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/identify*: name the venture, risk or constraint. *Describe*: say what it is with an example from the case. *Examine/discuss/evaluate*: weigh the constraint against the benefit of the venture and give a justified recommendation.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Ventures such as bee farming, mushrooms, vending and software meet local needs with low start-up cost.
- Opportunities are found by observing the local environment and judged by demand, resources, competition, legality and return.
- Risks (production, market, financial, natural, theft) can be reduced but not removed.
- Constraints are political, legal, social, ecological and economic.
- Ethical behaviour builds trust and protects the venture from penalties.
:::
