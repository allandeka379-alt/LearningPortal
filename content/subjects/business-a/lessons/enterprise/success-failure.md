## Introduction

A furniture workshop in Harare wins a large order from a hotel group. The owner buys timber, hires two more workers and delivers on time. The hotel agrees to pay after sixty days. Within a month the workshop is profitable on paper but cannot pay its timber supplier, and the supplier stops deliveries. A business that is making a profit has run out of cash, which is one of the commonest ways businesses fail.

Questions on success and failure appear in ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (as structured parts) and Paper 2 (as essays, especially "discuss the causes of business failure"), and in Cambridge Papers 1 and 2 at AS Level. You should already know what a business is and have met profit and cash in the lesson on business activity. Business finance itself is covered later.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Business failure"}
The closing down of a business because it cannot earn enough income to meet its costs, or cannot pay its debts when they fall due.
:::

:::definition{term="Business success"}
The achievement of a business's objectives, such as survival, profit, growth or customer satisfaction, over a period.
:::

:::definition{term="Liquidation"}
The closing down of a business and the selling of its assets to pay its debts. Any remaining money goes to the owners.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Cambridge detail: insolvency outline"}
**Bankruptcy** applies to an individual or partner who cannot pay debts. **Liquidation** ends a company by selling assets to pay creditors. **Administration** puts a struggling company in the hands of an independent administrator who tries to keep it trading or sell it as a going concern. The detail of procedures is not required.
:::

## Causes of business success

- **Good management**: clear objectives, sound decisions and control of costs.
- **Adequate finance**: enough money to start up and to pay running costs.
- **Understanding the market**: knowing customers, using market research and offering what they want.
- **Innovation**: new products or methods that rivals do not offer.
- **Quality** products and service that bring repeat business.

## Causes of business failure

| Cause | How it leads to failure |
| --- | --- |
| Poor cash management | Cash runs out even if sales are rising; bills cannot be paid |
| Over-expansion (overtrading) | Growth outpaces the finance available |
| Weak management | Poor decisions, weak control of costs, no planning |
| Competition | Rivals take customers or force prices down |
| Changes in the economy or law | Falling incomes, rising interest rates, new taxes or regulations |
| Poor planning | The market, costs or location were misjudged at the start |

Most failures have more than one cause, and a single cause often triggers others: poor planning leads to wrong stock levels, which causes a cash problem.

## Consequences of failure

- **Owners** lose their investment and, if liability is unlimited, may lose personal property.
- **Workers** lose their jobs and income.
- **Suppliers** may not be paid, so their own cash flow suffers.
- **Customers** lose a source of supply and may lose prepayments.
- **The community** loses jobs, spending and tax revenue.

## Reducing the risk of failure

- **Planning**: a realistic business plan, tested against likely changes.
- **Cash flow control**: forecasting, chasing debts, controlling inventory.
- **Market research**: check demand before committing resources.
- **Training**: skilled managers and workers make better decisions.
- **Diversification**: several products or markets so that one failure does not end the business.
- **Contingency plans**: reserves or alternative suppliers if something goes wrong.

None of these removes risk; each costs time or money, so a small business must judge which are worth the cost.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: profit but no cash]
A fictional furniture maker in Harare makes sales of US\$12,000 in a month, of which US\$9,000 is on sixty days' credit and US\$3,000 is paid in cash. Costs paid in the month are materials US\$5,500, wages US\$2,400 and rent US\$600. Opening cash is US\$2,000.

| Item | Profit view (US\$) | Cash view (US\$) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Sales | 12,000 | 3,000 |
| Materials | (5,500) | (5,500) |
| Wages | (2,400) | (2,400) |
| Rent | (600) | (600) |
| Profit for the month / net cash movement | 3,500 | (5,500) |
| Opening cash | | 2,000 |
| Closing cash (overdrawn) | | (3,500) |

1. Profit = 12,000 − 8,500 = US\$3,500.
2. Net cash movement = 3,000 − 8,500 = −US\$5,500, so closing cash = 2,000 − 5,500 = −US\$3,500 (an overdraft).
3. The business is profitable but US\$3,500 short of cash: poor cash management, not lack of profit, would cause failure here.
4. Remedies: ask credit customers for a deposit, shorten credit terms, negotiate longer credit from timber suppliers, or arrange an overdraft in advance.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis of a failing business]
**Context.** Chipo, a fictional owner, opened a bakery in Gweru. She borrowed heavily to buy three delivery vans before she had proved the demand. A competitor then opened nearby and cut prices.

**Point.** The bakery is at risk of failure through over-expansion and competition.

**Application.** The vans increased fixed costs and loan repayments, while the competitor's price cut reduced her sales.

**Analysis.** With fixed costs high and sales falling, her revenue may not cover the repayments. She will then have to borrow more, widening the gap. Skipping market research meant she committed finance before knowing demand.

**Judgement.** She should sell one van, concentrate on her best customers and use market research before expanding again. This reduces fixed costs but means slower growth, which is an acceptable price for survival.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Assuming profit means safety"}
A profitable business can fail if it runs out of cash. Distinguish profit from cash flow in your answer.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing causes without applying them"}
"Poor management" scores little. Say what the manager did (or did not do) in the case and what followed.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting that reducing risk has a cost"}
Market research and training cost money. A good evaluation says whether the cost is justified for a small business.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (state or outline causes; calculate from cash data) and Paper 2 (essay: discuss causes and remedies); Cambridge Papers 1 and 2 (explain, analyse, assess causes of failure and finance-related failure, including the three insolvency terms in outline).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/outline*: name the cause. *Explain/describe*: cause plus effect on the business. *Discuss/evaluate/assess*: weigh causes or remedies, decide which matters most in the case and give a reason (for example, cash flow is usually more urgent than profit).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Success rests on good management, adequate finance, market understanding, innovation and quality.
- Failure causes include poor cash management, over-expansion, weak management, competition, economic or legal change and poor planning.
- Failure harms owners, workers, suppliers, customers and the community.
- Risk is reduced by planning, cash flow control, market research, training, diversification and contingency plans.
- Cambridge outlines bankruptcy, liquidation and administration as results of lack of finance.
:::
