## Introduction

Kudzai Supplies is a fictional distributor of school stationery with a head office in Harare and depots in Bulawayo, Mutare and Gweru. Until last year every price change, every large order and every new customer account had to be approved in Harare. A depot manager in Mutare lost a school contract because approval of a discount took five days. The directors are now considering letting depot managers decide, but the finance specialists at head office warn that prices and credit will become inconsistent. At the same time, the transport supervisors complain that the human resources specialists "tell us what to do but never load a truck". These are three related questions: how far to **centralise** decisions, how to combine **line** and **staff** functions, and how the **informal** organisation affects the formal one.

This lesson applies to ZIMSEC Business Studies (Form 6), Business Enterprise Skills (Form 5) and Cambridge **A Level** (Papers 3 and 4). Informal organisation is ZIMSEC detail. You should know the lessons on delegation and structure.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Centralisation"}
An arrangement in which most important decisions are taken at the top of the organisation, usually at head office.
:::

:::definition{term="Decentralisation"}
An arrangement in which decision-making authority is passed down to lower levels, such as branch, depot or department managers.
:::

:::definition{term="Line function"}
A function that is directly involved in producing or selling the goods and services of the business, for example production, sales and distribution. Line managers have authority over their subordinates.
:::

:::definition{term="Staff function"}
A specialist function that supports and advises the line functions, for example human resources, accounting, legal advice and information technology. Staff managers advise but normally do not command line managers.
:::

:::definition{term="Formal organisation"}
The official structure of a business, shown on its organisational chart, with defined jobs, authority and reporting lines.
:::

:::definition{term="Informal organisation"}
The network of personal relationships and groups which form among employees, outside the official structure, based on friendship, shared interests or working together.
:::

## Centralisation and decentralisation

| | Centralised | Decentralised |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decisions | Made at the top | Made close to the customer or the work |
| Speed | Slower for local issues | Faster for local issues |
| Consistency | High: same policy everywhere | Lower: managers may decide differently |
| Control | Easy for senior management | Harder; needs reporting |
| Motivation | Lower: staff follow orders | Higher: managers have responsibility |
| Cost | Fewer managers needed, but senior time is heavily used | More skilled local managers and training |
| Local knowledge | Often missed | Used |
| Suits | Small or new businesses; high-risk decisions; need for uniform standards (finance, branding, safety) | Large, spread-out businesses; varied local markets; fast-changing conditions |

Most businesses do not choose one or the other completely. They **centralise** matters such as finance, branding and safety standards, and **decentralise** matters such as local prices, service and staffing. The right balance depends on **size**, **geographic spread**, **the skills of local managers**, the **importance and risk** of the decision, and the **need for consistency**.

## Line and staff functions

| Line | Staff |
| --- | --- |
| Directly produce or sell | Support and advise |
| Examples: production, operations, sales, distribution | Examples: human resources, finance and accounts, legal, IT, research |
| Have authority to give orders to subordinates | Advise, or have functional authority only in their specialism (e.g. HR policy) |
| Measured by output, sales and profit | Measured by the quality of advice and support |

**Conflicts between line and staff managers** often arise because:

- line managers feel staff specialists lack practical experience and "interfere";
- staff specialists feel that line managers ignore their advice or resist change;
- staff have no authority to enforce what they recommend, so they rely on persuasion;
- staff may take decisions (such as hiring rules or IT systems) that limit the freedom of line managers;
- they compete for budget, and the cost of staff functions is seen as overhead.

Conflict can be reduced by clear definitions of roles, involving line managers in setting staff policy, good communication and shared objectives.

## Formal and informal organisation (ZIMSEC detail)

The **formal organisation** is on the organisational chart. The **informal organisation** forms around friendship, common interests, the same lunch table or the same trade union. Informal groups have their own **norms** (for example, how fast to work), **leaders** and **communication channels** (the grapevine).

| | Potential benefits | Potential problems |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Informal groups | Satisfy social needs; improve morale; help new staff to settle; pass information quickly; support teamwork | Spread rumours; resist change; set low output norms; exclude some staff; informal leaders may challenge management |

A sensible manager works with the informal organisation: uses informal leaders to explain changes, listens to the grapevine for early signs of trouble and avoids breaking up groups that help morale.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: deciding what to centralise]
**Context.** Kudzai Supplies has four sites and wants to decide where decisions should be made.

| Decision | Centralise or decentralise? | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setting the price list | Centralise, with a discount limit for depots | Consistency and margin control; but allows quick local discounts |
| Approving a discount of up to 5% for a school | Decentralise to depot manager | Local knowledge and speed; the risk is limited |
| Borrowing money | Centralise | High risk and affects the whole business |
| Hiring a delivery driver | Decentralise, following HR policy | The depot knows its needs; policy keeps standards |
| Choosing the accounting system | Centralise | A single system is needed for consolidated records |

1. The Mutare contract was lost because a small discount needed head-office approval. The decision was low in risk, so it should be decentralised.
2. Consistent pricing and finance remain centralised to protect the business as a whole.
3. Judgement: a mixed approach keeps control of important decisions while allowing speed locally. Depot managers must be trained and their decisions reported, because inconsistency and errors are the main risks.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis, line and staff conflict]
**Context.** At Kudzai Supplies the human resources manager (a staff role) introduces a new rule that all drivers must complete 3 days of training before starting work. The transport supervisor (a line role) says that this leaves routes uncovered and ignores her advice that on-the-job training would do.

**Point.** This is a typical line and staff conflict.

**Application.** The HR manager's rule aims at safety and consistent standards across all depots. The transport supervisor is accountable for deliveries and sees only the delay. The HR manager has no authority over the transport supervisor in operational matters, but the rule is company policy.

**Analysis.** The conflict arises from different objectives (standards against delivery) and a lack of consultation. If it is not settled, the supervisor may ignore or evade the rule, or drivers may be put on routes untrained, which is a risk to safety and the company's reputation.

**Judgement.** The rule could be introduced in stages, with the supervisor helping to design the training so that two days are classroom-based and one is on the road with an experienced driver. Involving the line manager gives her ownership and keeps the safety standard.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Equating line with 'important' and staff with 'unimportant'"}
Staff functions are not less important. They support and advise the line. A business with weak finance or HR support will have problems in the line.
:::

:::mistake{title="Assuming decentralisation means no control"}
Decentralised managers still report and are accountable. Senior managers set objectives, limits and standards.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling informal groups harmful"}
Informal groups can improve morale and communication. They harm the business only if their norms conflict with its objectives or resist all change.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (state, differentiate line and staff; short cases) and Paper 2 (essays on centralisation and informal groups); Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2; Cambridge Paper 3 (short case questions) and Paper 4 (essays: evaluate decentralisation).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/identify*: name an example of a line or staff function. *Explain/describe*: say what the term means for the business. *Analyse/examine*: link centralisation to speed, control and motivation. *Evaluate/discuss/justify*: weigh centralisation against decentralisation in the case, naming the factors that tip the balance.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Centralisation keeps decisions at the top for control and consistency; decentralisation passes them down for speed, local knowledge and motivation.
- Most businesses mix the two, depending on size, spread, skills, risk and the need for consistency.
- Line functions produce or sell and have command authority; staff functions support and advise.
- Line and staff conflict because of different objectives, limited staff authority and lack of consultation.
- ZIMSEC detail: the informal organisation of groups and relationships can support morale and communication or resist change.
- Always apply the concept to the case and judge the balance.
:::
