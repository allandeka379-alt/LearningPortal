## Introduction

A fictional mining-equipment company headquartered in Johannesburg, Kopano Mining Supplies, opens a factory in Bulawayo to make drill parts for mines across the region. The factory employs 300 local workers, buys steel from local mills and pays tax to the Zimbabwean government. It also sends part of its profit back to the parent company, sells to customers in several countries and may use methods that local rivals have never seen. Local suppliers welcome the orders; local competitors worry; the government wants jobs and technology but also wants the profit to be reinvested.

Kopano is a **multinational company (MNC)**. MNCs and globalisation appear in Cambridge Paper 3 and Paper 4 (A Level), ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 and 2 (Form 6) and Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2 (Form 5). You should know the ways in which trade barriers and agreements affect firms.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Multinational company (MNC)"}
A business that owns and controls operations (factories, offices, shops) in more than one country, normally with a headquarters in its **home country** and subsidiaries in **host countries**.
:::

:::definition{term="Globalisation"}
The increasing linking of the world's economies, through trade, investment, finance, technology and culture, so that goods, services, money, ideas and people move more freely across borders.
:::

:::definition{term="Profit repatriation"}
The sending of profit earned in a host country back to the home country of the MNC.
:::

:::definition{term="Home country and host country"}
The **home country** is where the MNC is headquartered. A **host country** is one in which it operates a subsidiary.
:::

## Why firms become multinational

- **Larger markets** and sales growth when the home market is saturated.
- **Lower costs**: cheaper labour, land or raw materials, and lower transport costs by producing near the customer.
- **Avoiding trade barriers** such as tariffs and quotas by producing inside the market.
- **Access to resources** and skills not available at home.
- **Spreading risk** across several economies and currencies.
- **Government incentives** such as tax holidays offered by host countries.

## Benefits and challenges for the host country

| | Benefits | Challenges |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jobs and incomes | Direct jobs and wages; jobs in suppliers | Jobs may be low-paid, or senior posts may go to foreigners |
| Technology and skills | New methods, training, management skills | Skills may stay inside the MNC; dependence on foreign technology |
| Tax and foreign currency | Tax revenue; export earnings; investment | Profit repatriation; transfer pricing to move profit to low-tax countries |
| Competition and prices | More choice, lower prices, pressure to improve | Local firms may be driven out of business |
| Environment and culture | Possible cleaner methods | Pollution, resource use, culture and consumption patterns may change |

The **home country** may gain from profit and exports, but may lose jobs if production moves abroad.

## MNCs and governments

Governments want investment and jobs and may offer tax breaks or land, while setting conditions: local ownership, local content, training of local staff, environmental standards, reinvestment of part of profits and limits on repatriation. MNCs bargain for the best terms and can move to another country if conditions are poor, which gives them power. Tensions arise over tax, ownership and control of resources.

## Problems MNCs face abroad

- **Culture**: customs, language and tastes differ, so products and advertising may need adapting.
- **Law and regulation**: unfamiliar employment, tax and product rules.
- **Currency**: exchange-rate changes alter the value of profit sent home.
- **Politics**: instability, changes of policy or hostility to foreign firms.
- **Managing distance**: communication, control of subsidiaries and quality.

## Globalisation

**Causes:** faster transport and communications, the internet and mobile technology, falling trade barriers and agreements, growth of MNCs, free movement of capital and rising incomes in emerging economies.

**Advantages for business:** access to bigger markets and cheaper inputs, economies of scale, the spread of ideas, and more choice for consumers.

**Disadvantages:** greater competition for local firms, dependence on world markets (a crisis spreads quickly), pressure on wages and the environment, and loss of local culture.

Globalisation influences **location**, because firms can place production, call centres or design teams wherever costs and skills are best, and **marketing**, because firms need global brands but must adapt to local tastes ("think global, act local").

:::worked-solution[Example 1: profit earned by a subsidiary]
Kopano's Bulawayo subsidiary earns a profit before tax of US\$2,400,000. The corporate tax rate is 25% (an assumed rate), and the parent company takes 70% of profit after tax back home. (Figures are invented for practice.)

| Subsidiary of a multinational in the host country (fictional) | US\$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Profit before tax | 2,400,000 |
| Corporate tax paid to the host government (25%) | 600,000 |
| Profit after tax | 1,800,000 |
| Profit sent home to the parent company (70%) | 1,260,000 |
| Profit kept and invested in the host country | 540,000 |

**Working.** Tax = 25% of 2,400,000 = US\$600,000. Profit after tax = 2,400,000 − 600,000 = US\$1,800,000. Repatriated profit = 70% of 1,800,000 = US\$1,260,000. Retained = 1,800,000 − 1,260,000 = US\$540,000.

**Interpretation.** The host country gains US\$600,000 in tax and US\$540,000 of reinvestment, but US\$1,260,000 leaves. The government may argue for a lower repatriation share; the MNC may say that it needs a reasonable return for the risk. The benefit to the host country also includes the wages of workers and payments to local suppliers, which are not shown in the table.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis, choosing a location]
**Context.** Kopano is deciding where to make its drill parts for the regional market. In the home country a worker earns US\$10 an hour and makes a part in 2 hours. In Bulawayo a worker earns US\$3 an hour but needs 3 hours per part because of lower experience, and transport to the main mines costs US\$4 a part. (Figures are invented for practice.)

| Cost per part | Home country (US\$) | Bulawayo (US\$) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Labour (wage × hours) | 20 | 9 |
| Transport to the mines | 0 | 4 |
| Total labour and transport | 20 | 13 |

**Point.** Bulawayo has the lower cost per part (US\$13 against US\$20), a saving of US\$7 or 35%.

**Application.** Labour is 10 × 2 = US\$20 at home and 3 × 3 = US\$9 in Bulawayo; adding transport gives US\$13. Producing near the mines also avoids tariffs on imports to the region.

**Analysis.** The saving may be smaller if training is costly or quality problems appear. Political risk, power cuts and the exchange rate could alter the gain, and the government may require local ownership or reinvestment.

**Judgement.** Kopano should locate in Bulawayo if the cost advantage remains after training and risk are allowed for, and if it can agree acceptable terms with the government. A smaller pilot plant would let it test productivity before committing.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling every large firm an MNC"}
An MNC owns operations in more than one country. A large firm that only exports from one country is not necessarily multinational.
:::

:::mistake{title="Seeing MNCs as all good or all bad"}
MNCs bring jobs and technology but may also repatriate profit and crowd out local firms. Weigh the effects for the specific case and say who gains and who loses.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing globalisation with trade only"}
Globalisation also covers investment, finance, technology, people and culture, not just trade in goods.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
Cambridge Paper 3 and Paper 4 (A Level): short cases on MNC benefits or a location calculation, and evaluation of the effect of an MNC or globalisation on a business. ZIMSEC Business Studies (Form 6) and Business Enterprise Skills (Form 5) Papers 1 and 2: structured questions on the benefits and drawbacks of MNCs to Zimbabwe and essays on globalisation.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/outline*: name a reason or a benefit. *Explain/describe*: say how it works and apply it to the case. *Analyse*: build a chain (MNC enters, local firms face competition, prices fall, some firms close). *Discuss/evaluate/assess*: balance benefits and costs for host and home countries and local firms and give a justified judgement.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- An MNC owns operations in more than one country; firms go multinational for markets, lower costs, avoiding barriers, resources and spreading risk.
- Host countries gain jobs, technology, tax and competition, but face profit repatriation, pressure on local firms and environmental risks.
- Governments and MNCs bargain over tax, ownership and reinvestment.
- MNCs face problems of culture, law, currency and politics abroad.
- Globalisation is the growing linkage of economies through trade, investment, technology and culture; it affects where firms locate and how they market.
:::
