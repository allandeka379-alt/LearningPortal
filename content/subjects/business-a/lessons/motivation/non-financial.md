## Introduction

Nyasha manages a fictional bottling plant near Gweru. Her workers are paid fairly, yet the same ones stand at the same filling machine all day, absence is rising and ideas for improvement never reach her. A pay rise would cost money and might not change the way the work feels. There are cheaper and often more lasting options: changing the job itself, giving workers a say, recognising achievement and improving conditions. These are the non-financial methods of motivation.

Non-financial methods are tested in ZIMSEC Business Studies and Business Enterprise Skills (Form 5) and in Cambridge AS Level Papers 1 and 2. Questions ask you to explain a method, to choose one for a stated business and to judge its costs and limits. You should have met financial methods in the previous lesson and the idea of motivation in general.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
| ZIMSEC | Cambridge |
| --- | --- |
| Job rotation; job enlargement; job enrichment; team working; delegation; participation in management; works committees | Job rotation, enlargement and enrichment; teamworking; empowerment; employee participation; quality circles |

Both boards examine the same methods. The theories that support them (Herzberg, McClelland, Maslow) are covered in later lessons.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Job rotation"}
Moving employees between different jobs at the same level, for a period, so that they learn several tasks and reduce boredom.
:::

:::definition{term="Job enlargement"}
Adding more tasks of a similar level to a job, so that the employee does a wider range of work. It adds variety but not extra responsibility.
:::

:::definition{term="Job enrichment"}
Giving a job more responsibility, planning and decision making, so that the employee has more control and achievement. It makes the job deeper.
:::

:::definition{term="Empowerment"}
Giving employees the authority and resources to make decisions about their own work without seeking approval.
:::

:::definition{term="Employee participation"}
Involving employees in making decisions that affect them and the business.
:::

## Changing the job

| Method | What changes | Example | Benefits | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Job rotation | Which task is done, taking turns | A packer spends a week each on filling, labelling and packing | Less boredom; staff learn several jobs; cover for absence | Training cost; productivity may fall at first; tasks are no more demanding |
| Job enlargement | More tasks of the same level | A cashier also stacks shelves | More variety | May feel like extra work for the same pay |
| Job enrichment | More responsibility and control | A machine operator plans the day's schedule and checks quality | Greater interest, achievement and ownership; consistent with Herzberg's motivators | Needs training, trust and sometimes more pay; not all staff want responsibility |

**Team working.** Employees work in groups with a shared goal and some control over how they do the task. Teams create a sense of belonging, share skills and encourage mutual support. The risks are social loafing (one person relying on others), conflict and slower decisions.

**Empowerment.** Staff can act without waiting for approval, for example a receptionist who is allowed to give a refund up to a set limit. It speeds service and shows trust, but needs training, clear limits and a willingness by managers to let go.

**Recognition, status and conditions.** Thanks, awards, titles, a good workplace, fair treatment, adequate equipment and safe conditions show that employees are valued. **Training and promotion** show a future in the business. These cost little relative to pay, but must be genuine and consistent.

## Employee participation in management

| Method | How it works | Benefits | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Works council / workers' committee | Representatives of workers and managers meet regularly to discuss matters of common concern | Two-way communication; early warning of problems | Slows decisions; may be ignored if managers do not act |
| Suggestion scheme | Staff submit ideas, often with a reward for adopted ideas | Cheap source of ideas from those who know the job | Ideas may be ignored, discouraging staff; judging them takes time |
| Quality circle | A small group from the same area meets regularly to find and solve quality problems | Uses workers' knowledge; improves quality and morale | Takes time; needs training and management support |
| Worker director | An employee representative sits on the board | Voice at the top; trust | Conflict of loyalties; rare in small firms |

Participation works best when managers listen and act on the advice. If workers are asked for ideas and nothing happens, motivation falls.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: measuring the effect of team working]
Nyasha reorganises the filling line into teams of four. Before the change, 8 workers filled 1,200 crates a week. After the change, the same 8 workers fill 1,440 crates a week.

| | Crates a week | Workers | Output per worker |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Before | 1,200 | 8 | 150 |
| After | 1,440 | 8 | 180 |
| Change | | | +30 (+20%) |

1. Output per worker before = 1,200 ÷ 8 = 150 crates.
2. Output per worker after = 1,440 ÷ 8 = 180 crates.
3. Percentage change = (180 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 = 20%.
4. The rise is consistent with better motivation, but other causes (a new machine, a seasonal rise) must be ruled out before the change is credited to team working. The cost, mainly training time, should be compared with the gain.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis of a participation scheme]
**Context.** Nyasha introduces a suggestion scheme at the plant. In the first month 40 ideas are submitted, but only 3 receive a reply, and none has been used.

**Point.** The scheme as run is more likely to lower motivation than to raise it.

**Application.** Workers who take time to suggest ideas and hear nothing will conclude that managers are not interested.

**Analysis.** A participation scheme works only when managers respond. The 37 unanswered ideas are a loss of both ideas and goodwill. A team with time set aside to review ideas, quick replies and a small reward when an idea saves money would show that suggestions are valued.

**Judgement.** The scheme should continue but with a named manager who answers every idea within two weeks and reports what has been adopted. If the response stays poor, the plant would be better off not running it.
:::

## Choosing a method

| Situation | Suitable method |
| --- | --- |
| Boredom in repetitive work | Job rotation or enlargement |
| Skilled staff who want more responsibility | Job enrichment, empowerment |
| Need for ideas and improvement | Suggestion scheme, quality circle |
| Poor communication and mistrust | Works council, regular meetings |
| Need to retain talent | Training, promotion, recognition |

Non-financial methods are not free, nor do they replace fair pay: if pay is felt to be unfair, no job redesign will motivate (Herzberg's hygiene factors). Their effect is slower and harder to measure than a bonus.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing enlargement with enrichment"}
Enlargement adds *more tasks* at the same level (wider). Enrichment adds *responsibility and control* (deeper). Do not use them as synonyms.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying non-financial methods cost nothing"}
They cost training time, managers' time and sometimes lost output during change. Weigh these costs.
:::

:::mistake{title="Assuming participation always works"}
If managers do not act on suggestions, participation lowers motivation. A good answer says what is needed for it to work.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (state, describe methods; distinguish enlargement and enrichment) and Paper 2 (essays on motivation and participation); Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2; Cambridge Paper 1 (define, explain a method) and Paper 2 (assess which method suits a business in a data response).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/outline*: name the method and a feature. *Explain/describe*: say how it motivates, with an example from the case. *Analyse/examine*: link the method to motivation, productivity and cost. *Discuss/evaluate/assess*: weigh benefits against costs and limits, choose the method that fits the case and justify it.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Job rotation moves staff between tasks; enlargement adds similar tasks; enrichment adds responsibility and control.
- Team working, empowerment, recognition, good conditions, training and promotion also motivate.
- Participation includes works councils, suggestion schemes, quality circles and worker directors.
- Participation works only when managers listen and act on what they hear.
- Non-financial methods cost time and training and work slowly; they do not replace fair pay.
- Choose a method by the problem: boredom, lack of ideas, mistrust or loss of talent.
:::
