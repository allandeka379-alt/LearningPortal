## Introduction

Two fictional millers in Gweru sell the same type of maize meal. One packs it in plain sacks and sells it by weight to shop owners. The other packs it in a bright 25 kg bag with a clear name, "Mhuri Meal", a picture of a family, a recipe on the back, a resealable top and a promise of "no stones, no weevils". The second bag costs more to make, but families ask for it by name and shops pay a higher price. The maize meal inside is much the same. The difference is in the **product** as customers see it: its features, brand, packaging and the image it gives.

This lesson applies to ZIMSEC Business Studies and Business Enterprise Skills (Form 6) and Cambridge **AS** (Papers 1 and 2). It introduces the marketing mix (the 4Ps, and for ZIMSEC the extended 7Ps) and develops the product. Price, promotion and place have their own lessons.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Marketing mix"}
The combination of decisions on product, price, promotion and place (and, for services, people, processes and physical evidence) made to meet the needs of a target market.
:::

:::definition{term="Product differentiation"}
Making a product different from competing products in a way that customers value, so that it is seen as distinct.
:::

:::definition{term="Unique selling point (USP)"}
A feature of a product that makes it different from and better than competing products in the customer's eyes.
:::

:::definition{term="Brand"}
A name, symbol, design or combination that identifies a product and distinguishes it from rivals, and carries an image in customers' minds.
:::

## The marketing mix

| P | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Product | What is sold: features, quality, design, brand, packaging, range, after-sales service |
| Price | List price, discounts, credit terms, pricing strategy |
| Promotion | Advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, public relations, digital |
| Place | Distribution channels, outlets, stock, transport |
| People (ZIMSEC) | Staff who deliver a service: skills, attitude, appearance |
| Processes (ZIMSEC) | How the service is delivered: booking, waiting time, payment |
| Physical evidence (ZIMSEC) | Tangible clues of quality in a service: premises, uniforms, receipts, website |

The mix is **integrated** when each P supports the same positioning: a premium product needs a premium price, selective outlets and quality promotion. A mix that sends conflicting messages (a luxury brand sold in a discount store) confuses customers.

## Goods and services

| | Goods | Services |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | Tangible: can be seen and touched | Intangible: cannot be stored or touched |
| Ownership | Passes to the buyer | Not transferred; the customer uses it |
| Inventory | Can be stored | Cannot be stored (an empty seat is lost) |
| Quality | Can be checked before sale | Varies with the people; hard to check in advance |

Most products are a mix: a restaurant sells food (tangible) and service (intangible). Because services are intangible, the extra three Ps (people, processes, physical evidence) matter more.

## The product: features, differentiation and the USP

A product has **tangible attributes** (size, materials, design, packaging) and **intangible attributes** (brand image, reliability, status, service). Customers buy a "bundle of benefits", not only the physical item. A firm can differentiate by quality, design, features, brand, service, packaging, convenience or ethics (for example, locally grown). A **USP** might be "the only battery that works in a solar-powered torch", "delivery within an hour" or "made by women's cooperatives in Chipinge". A good USP is **real**, **valued by customers** and **hard to copy**.

**Benefits of differentiation:** less price competition, higher prices and profit, customer loyalty, and a more defined brand. **Costs:** research, design, quality control and promotion; the difference may be copied; customers may not value it.

## Branding

A brand identifies the product and builds an image. **Benefits:** easier recognition and recall; loyalty and repeat purchase; a **price premium**; a platform for new products; trust in quality; legal protection of the name. **Costs and risks:** the cost of building and protecting a brand; a quality failure damages the whole brand; brands may become dated.

## Packaging

Packaging has several roles: **protect** the product; **preserve** it (shelf life); **inform** (ingredients, instructions, legal information); **promote** and attract attention on the shelf; **convenience** (easy to carry or open); and **position** the product (luxury or economy). Costs: materials and design, extra weight in transport, waste and environmental concerns. Packaging should match the positioning and be recyclable where possible.

## Product development and the mix

Product development keeps the product competitive as tastes change and the product ages. New features, new sizes or a new version extend the life of the product. It is costly and risky, and the lesson on new product development covers it in detail.

### Choosing the mix for a context

The right mix depends on the **target market**, the **product** (a durable good or a service), the **competitors**, the **resources** of the firm and the **objectives**. A small firm with limited cash may rely on a distinctive product, low-cost promotion by word of mouth and direct sales; a large firm may use mass advertising and wide distribution.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: the value of branding and packaging]
**Context.** The fictional miller "Mhuri Meal" sells a plain 25 kg bag at US\$14. A branded bag costs US\$1.10 more to pack, and the miller can charge US\$16.80. It sells 10,000 bags a year and spends US\$6,000 on a one-off branding campaign.

| Item | Unbranded | Branded |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Selling price per 25 kg bag (US\$) | 14.00 | 16.80 |
| Extra packing cost per bag (US\$) | — | 1.10 |
| Extra profit per bag (US\$) | — | 1.70 |

1. Price premium = (16.80 − 14.00) ÷ 14.00 × 100 = 20%.
2. Extra profit per bag = 16.80 − 14.00 − 1.10 = US\$1.70.
3. Extra profit on 10,000 bags = 1.70 × 10,000 = US\$17,000.
4. After the campaign cost: 17,000 − 6,000 = US\$11,000.
5. **Judgement.** The branding is profitable on these assumptions, but they depend on customers continuing to pay a 20% premium and on volume not falling. If a rival copies the bag and cuts its price, the premium may disappear, so the miller must keep the quality and the recipes that give the brand its value.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis, the extended mix for a service]
**Context.** A fictional guest lodge in Victoria Falls wants to position itself as friendly and good value for families. It sells rooms, meals and tours.

| P | Decision for the lodge |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Family rooms; safe play area; local food |
| Price | Child discounts; a package price for room, meals and a tour |
| Promotion | Family blogs, travel agents, referrals from past guests |
| Place | Online booking; a shuttle from the airport |
| People | Staff trained to welcome children and answer questions |
| Processes | Quick check-in; easy online payment |
| Physical evidence | Clean, welcoming rooms; photographs of families on the website |

**Point.** A service is judged on people, process and evidence as well as the room itself.

**Application.** A family choosing between lodges cannot inspect the rooms in advance, so photographs, reviews and the manner of staff replies to messages affect the choice.

**Analysis.** If the lodge's website promises a friendly welcome but check-in is slow and the staff are untrained, the mix is not integrated, and guests will give poor reviews that harm bookings.

**Judgement.** The lodge should invest first in staff training and a quick booking process, because these cost little and affect every guest, and then in more advertising.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing the Ps without applying them"}
"Product, price, promotion, place" earns little. Say what the decision is for **this** business and why it fits the target market.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing a brand with a product"}
A product is the item sold; a brand is the name and image attached to it. A branded product can sell at a premium, but only if customers believe the image.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting that the mix must be integrated"}
A high price with cheap packaging and discount promotion contradicts itself. Always check that the Ps support the same positioning.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Business Studies and Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2 ask you to *state*, *describe* and *discuss* the 4Ps and 7Ps and the roles of branding and packaging; Cambridge Papers 1 and 2 ask you to *identify*, *explain*, *analyse* and *assess* differentiation, branding and the mix. Use the context (target market, product, resources). For *assess* or *evaluate*, weigh the benefit (a price premium, loyalty) against the cost and the risk (copying, quality failure), and give a judgement with a condition.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The marketing mix is product, price, promotion and place; ZIMSEC adds people, processes and physical evidence for services.
- Differentiation and a USP make a product distinct and allow a higher price, but cost money and can be copied.
- Branding builds recognition, loyalty and a price premium; packaging protects, informs, promotes and positions.
- Services are intangible, so people, processes and physical evidence matter.
- The mix must be integrated and suited to the target market, resources and objectives.
:::
