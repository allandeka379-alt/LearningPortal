## Introduction

Chipo runs a fictional hardware shop in Chinhoyi. Five years ago she kept her stock records in a ledger, counted money by hand and took orders by telephone. Today she uses a till linked to a stock programme that tells her when cement runs low, takes mobile-money payments, sends order messages to suppliers and keeps customer details for deliveries. A lost password and a day without internet showed her the other side: when the system fails, the shop nearly stops.

Information and communication technology (ICT) is part of almost every business. This topic is tested in ZIMSEC Business Studies Papers 1 and 2 (Form 5); it is not a Business Enterprise Skills or Cambridge topic in this form, although Cambridge candidates will meet ICT in operations and marketing lessons. You should already know the main functional areas of a business: finance, marketing, human resources and operations. E-marketing and ICT in operations (ERP) are covered in separate lessons.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Information and communication technology (ICT)"}
The use of computers, software, networks, telecommunications and the internet to collect, store, process, send and receive information.
:::

:::definition{term="Hardware and software"}
**Hardware** is the physical equipment: computers, tablets, printers, scanners, tills, servers, phones. **Software** is the programs that tell hardware what to do, for example accounting packages, word processors, spreadsheets, databases and payroll programs.
:::

:::definition{term="Network, internet and mobile technology"}
A **network** links computers so they can share files and equipment (a local area network inside a building). The **internet** is a worldwide network that allows email, websites, online banking and e-commerce. **Mobile technology** covers mobile phones, tablets and mobile money which allow business to be done anywhere.
:::

:::definition{term="Data protection"}
Rules and practices that keep personal and business information accurate, private and secure, and that limit how it is collected and used.
:::

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC detail"}
This lesson is for ZIMSEC Business Studies (Form 5). It is not a Cambridge or Business Enterprise Skills heading. The syllabus asks for ICT resources, the role of ICT in the functional areas, advantages and disadvantages of ICT, and data protection and security in outline.
:::

## ICT resources

| Resource | Examples | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hardware | Computers, tablets, printers, scanners, barcode readers, tills, servers | Entering, storing and printing information |
| Software | Word processor, spreadsheet, database, accounting and payroll packages, design software | Processing information and automating tasks |
| Networks | Local area network, wireless network | Sharing data and equipment between users |
| Internet | Email, websites, social media, online banking | Communication, research, selling and buying |
| Mobile technology | Smartphones, mobile money, apps | Communication and payment while on the move |

## The role of ICT in functional areas

- **Finance and accounting**: accounting packages record transactions, produce statements and ratios, and support budgets; spreadsheets prepare forecasts; online banking and mobile money speed up payments and receipts.
- **Marketing**: databases of customers help to target promotion; websites and social media advertise and sell; software analyses survey results.
- **Human resources**: records of employees, payroll, job advertising and online application, and online training.
- **Operations**: stock control with barcodes, scheduling and design with computer programs, automated machines and quality checks.
- **Communication**: email, messaging and video conferencing connect staff, suppliers and customers inside and outside the firm.

## Advantages of ICT

- **Speed**: tasks that took hours are done in minutes.
- **Accuracy**: calculations are consistent and errors are reduced.
- **Lower costs**: less paper and labour for routine tasks.
- **Better information** for decisions, and wider markets through the internet.
- **Communication**: instant contact across distance.

## Disadvantages of ICT

- **Cost** of equipment, software, updates and training.
- **Skills**: staff need training; a shortage of skilled people limits benefit.
- **Security risks**: hacking, viruses, theft of data and fraud.
- **Dependence**: power cuts or network failure can halt operations.
- **Job effects**: routine jobs may disappear, though new ones appear; staff may resist change.

## Data protection and security (outline)

Businesses hold information about customers, workers and finances. To protect it they use **passwords and access levels**, **backups** stored away from the main computer, **antivirus software and firewalls**, **encryption** of sensitive data, careful control of who may see records, and staff training. Data protection also means collecting only what is needed, keeping it accurate and not sharing it without permission. Failure can lead to fraud, loss of customer trust and legal action.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: computerising payroll]
Chipo's shop pays six workers. A clerk, paid US\$5 an hour, spends 30 hours a month preparing the payroll by hand. With a payroll program the job takes 4 hours a month, but the program costs US\$40 a month. A suitable computer costs US\$900 (bought once). (Figures are invented for practice.)

| Yearly cost of payroll | Manual (US\$) | Computerised (US\$) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Clerk's time (US\$5 an hour) | 1,800 | 240 |
| Software subscription (US\$40 a month) | 0 | 480 |
| Total yearly cost | 1,800 | 720 |
| Yearly saving | | 1,080 |

**Working.** Manual cost = 30 hours × US\$5 × 12 months = US\$1,800. Computerised: clerk 4 × 5 × 12 = US\$240, software 40 × 12 = US\$480, total US\$720. Saving = 1,800 − 720 = US\$1,080 a year. The computer costs US\$900, so it is repaid in 900 ÷ 1,080 = 0.83 years, which is 10 months.

**Interpretation.** The saving repays the equipment in under a year, so on cost alone the change is worthwhile. Other benefits include fewer errors and easier records; risks include loss of data if no backup is made, and the clerk's need for training.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis, security and dependence]
**Context.** A school tuck shop run by students (a fictional enterprise at a school in Gweru) keeps all its sales and stock records on a single laptop and accepts mobile-money payments. One week the laptop is stolen, and the mobile-money account is used by someone who knew the password.

**Point.** The shop's use of ICT brought speed and convenience, but its poor security and its dependence on one machine caused loss.

**Application.** Records of sales and stock were lost because there was no backup, so the shop could not tell what it was owed or how much stock remained. The password was shared and easy to guess, which allowed misuse of the mobile-money account.

**Analysis.** Backups on a separate device or online storage would have allowed the records to be restored. Separate passwords, a locked cupboard and limiting who can approve payments would have reduced the risk of fraud. These measures cost little compared with the loss and the damage to customers' trust.

**Judgement.** ICT is still worth using, because its benefits in speed and accuracy are large, but only if the shop spends modestly on security, backups and training. A business that depends on one machine and one password has accepted a risk that is easily avoided.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing ICT items without linking to a business function"}
"Computers, printers and the internet" is a list of resources. Say what each is used for in finance, marketing, HR or operations, and what difference it makes.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying ICT always saves money"}
ICT has high start-up and training costs and needs maintenance. Weigh savings against costs, and refer to the size of the business and the time period.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring data protection"}
Security is part of the topic. When a case mentions customer records or payments, mention passwords, backups, access control or privacy as appropriate.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Paper 1 (structured questions and the case study) and Paper 2 (data response and essays). It is not tested in Business Enterprise Skills or Cambridge papers as a separate topic.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/outline*: name an ICT resource, advantage or security measure. *Describe*: say what the ICT does in a function. *Discuss/examine*: weigh advantages against disadvantages for the business in the case and reach a judgement that depends on size, cost and skills. *Evaluate*: decide whether the business should adopt ICT and give a reason.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- ICT resources include hardware, software, networks, the internet and mobile technology.
- ICT supports finance, marketing, human resources, operations and communication.
- Advantages are speed, accuracy, lower cost, better information and communication; disadvantages are cost, skills, security risk, dependence and job effects.
- Data protection and security rely on passwords, backups, antivirus and firewalls, encryption, access control and training.
- Whether ICT pays depends on cost, the size of the business, the skills available and the time period.
:::
