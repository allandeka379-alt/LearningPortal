## Introduction

Nyasha Dube (a fictional owner) has a poultry business near Gweru. Last year it sold chickens worth US\$96,000. This year she says "we want to grow", but her workers do not know whether that means ten more birds a week or a new farm. When she changes the statement to "increase sales by 20% by 31 December, with a quarterly target for each team", the workers can plan, the manager can set a budget, and she can tell at the end of the year whether she succeeded.

Turning vague hopes into measurable targets, and judging decisions against them, is the subject of this lesson. It also covers the ethical limits on how a firm may pursue its objectives. The topic is tested in Cambridge Papers 1 and 2 (AS Level), ZIMSEC Business Studies Papers 1 and 2 and Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2. You should already know the types of business objective.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="SMART objective"}
An objective that is **S**pecific, **M**easurable, **A**chievable, **R**ealistic and **T**ime-limited. Some textbooks use "Agreed" or "Attainable" for A and "Relevant" for R; the exam accepts any sensible wording, but you must be able to explain each letter.
:::

:::definition{term="Budget and target"}
A **target** is a measurable level of performance to be reached by a given date. A **budget** is a financial plan that sets the income or spending allowed to reach the objective.
:::

:::definition{term="Business ethics"}
The moral principles that guide the behaviour of a firm, such as honesty, fair dealing, fair treatment of workers and concern for the environment, going beyond what the law requires.
:::

:::definition{term="Unhu/ubuntu/vumunhu (ZIMSEC detail)"}
The Zimbabwean and southern African value that a person becomes fully human through relationships with others. In business it means respect, honesty, caring for the community, sharing and fairness.
:::

## The role of objectives in decision-making

A rational decision follows stages:

1. **Set the objective** (what must be achieved?).
2. **Gather information** (market research, costs, competitors).
3. **Identify the options** (different ways to reach the objective).
4. **Analyse and choose** (compare the options against the objective, cost and risk).
5. **Implement** the decision.
6. **Review** the result against the objective and learn from it.

The objective is both the **starting point** and the **test** of a decision. If the objective is vague, managers cannot say whether a decision was good. Real decisions are also affected by limited information, time, and managers' intuition.

## From objectives to targets and budgets

Large objectives are broken into targets for departments, teams and periods, and each target gets a budget. This is how senior managers **delegate** and how the whole organisation is made to work towards one objective.

## SMART objectives

| Letter | Meaning | Weak example | SMART example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specific | Says exactly what is to be achieved | "Do better" | "Increase egg sales" |
| Measurable | Has a number | "Sell more eggs" | "Sell 30% more eggs" |
| Achievable | Staff can do it | "Double sales next week" | "Raise sales by 20%" |
| Realistic | Fits the resources and market | "Capture the whole country's market" | "Gain two new supermarket customers" |
| Time-limited | Has a deadline | "Some time soon" | "By 31 December" |

SMART objectives help managers to monitor progress and to motivate workers, because everyone knows what is expected.

## Communicating objectives

Objectives work only if the people who must achieve them know them. Good communication gives employees direction and a sense of involvement; unclear or secret objectives cause confusion and conflict. If targets are set by managers without consultation, workers may see them as unfair and be demotivated; if targets are too easy, there is no stretch; if too hard, workers may give up.

## Constraints on objectives (ZIMSEC)

A firm cannot pursue any objective it likes. **Finance** limits what it can afford; **the law** forbids some actions and demands others; **competition** limits prices and sales; **stakeholders** (workers, lenders, the community) may resist; and **resources** such as skills, machines and time restrict output. A realistic objective is set within these constraints.

## Ethics and business objectives

Ethical behaviour includes fair dealing with suppliers and customers, honest advertising, fair treatment of workers and care for the environment. Ethics influence objectives: a firm that values honesty will not mislead customers, even if that would raise sales.

| | Possible benefits | Possible costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Behaving ethically | Better reputation, loyal customers and workers, fewer legal problems, easier access to finance | Higher costs, slower growth, loss of sales to less ethical rivals in the short run |
| Behaving unethically | Lower costs and higher short-term profit | Prosecution, fines, boycotts, loss of trust, difficulty in recruiting |

In ZIMSEC, **unhu/ubuntu/vumunhu** is applied to business: respecting customers and employees, paying suppliers on time, selling safe goods and contributing to the community.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: from objective to target and budget]
Nyasha's poultry business sold goods worth US\$96,000 last year. Her objective is to increase sales by 20% by 31 December. She spreads the yearly target across the quarters, with more in the last quarter when demand is higher, and sets an advertising budget of 5% of the sales target.

| Quarter | Share of yearly sales (%) | Sales target (US\$) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 20 | 23,040 |
| 2 | 20 | 23,040 |
| 3 | 25 | 28,800 |
| 4 | 35 | 40,320 |
| Year | 100 | 115,200 |

**Working.** Yearly target = 96,000 × 1.20 = US\$115,200. Quarter 1 = 115,200 × 20% = US\$23,040, and the other quarters are found in the same way. The advertising budget = 5% × 115,200 = US\$5,760.

**Interpretation.** The objective is now specific, measurable and time-limited. If sales in Quarter 1 are only US\$20,000, Nyasha can see within three months that she is behind and respond (more promotion, new customers) instead of discovering the shortfall in December.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis, an ethical dilemma]
**Context.** Sipho (a fictional manager) runs a small factory in Bulawayo producing tinned beans. The firm faces a cash shortage. A supplier offers cheaper beans but they are old, and the tins have a "best before" date that Sipho could print later than the true date. Selling more now would help him meet his profit objective.

**Point.** Printing a false date is unethical, and probably illegal, even though it supports the profit objective.

**Application.** The cost saving could add to profit in the short term. But if customers fall ill, the firm faces claims, loss of reputation and possibly closure. Workers who know about the practice may lose trust in management.

**Analysis.** Profit gained by deceiving customers is short term, while the damage lasts for years. A firm guided by unhu would put honesty and the health of customers first, and look for other solutions such as negotiating payment terms with its supplier or a short-term loan.

**Judgement.** Sipho should not use the old beans. The profit objective is legitimate, but it is limited by ethics and the law, and the long-term cost of losing customer trust is greater than the short-term saving.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Writing objectives that cannot be measured"}
"Be the best" or "increase profit" has no number or date. Add a figure and a deadline so that it can be called SMART.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying ethics always lowers profit"}
Ethical behaviour may raise costs, but it can also win loyal customers and avoid fines. A good answer weighs both and refers to the time period.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up an objective with a budget"}
An objective states what is to be achieved; a budget states the money available or expected. A budget supports an objective but is not the same thing.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
Cambridge Papers 1 and 2 (AS Level): the stages of decision-making, SMART objectives, communication of objectives and the effect of ethics, usually inside a data response. ZIMSEC Business Studies and Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2: SMART objectives and constraints in structured questions, ethics and unhu/ubuntu in essays.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/outline*: name the SMART letters or constraints. *Explain*: link each point to the case. *Analyse*: follow the effect of an ethical or unethical choice on customers, workers and profit. *Assess/discuss/evaluate*: balance short-term gains against long-term costs and give a judgement with a condition.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Objectives start and test the stages of decision-making: set objective, gather information, identify options, choose, implement, review.
- Objectives are turned into targets and budgets for departments and periods.
- SMART means specific, measurable, achievable, realistic and time-limited.
- Objectives must be communicated; constraints of finance, law, competition, stakeholders and resources limit what is possible (ZIMSEC).
- Ethics (and unhu/ubuntu) limit how a firm pursues its objectives; ethical behaviour can cost money in the short term but protects reputation.
:::
