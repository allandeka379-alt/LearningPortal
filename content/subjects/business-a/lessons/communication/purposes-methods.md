## Introduction

The manager of a fictional bakery in Bulawayo, Mrs Ncube, decides to change the start of the early shift from 04:00 to 03:30 because of a big weekend order. She tells the supervisor in passing. The supervisor tells two bakers, who tell others, and by Saturday some bakers arrive at 04:00 and some at 03:30. The bread is late, the customer is angry and two bakers feel blamed. The decision was good, but the communication was not. In business, a decision is only as good as the way it is passed to the people who must act on it.

This lesson applies to ZIMSEC Business Studies (Forms 5 and 6), Business Enterprise Skills (Form 5) and Cambridge **A Level** (Papers 3 and 4). Drafting documents and the media of communication are ZIMSEC detail. A second lesson covers channels and barriers.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Communication"}
The exchange of information, ideas or instructions between a sender and a receiver, so that the message is understood.
:::

:::definition{term="Communication process"}
The sequence by which a sender encodes a message, sends it through a medium to a receiver, who decodes it and gives feedback.
:::

:::definition{term="Medium"}
The means by which a message is carried, such as a telephone, letter, email, radio or notice board.
:::

:::definition{term="Feedback"}
The receiver's response, which tells the sender whether the message has been received and understood.
:::

## When communication is essential

Communication is essential when a business needs to:

- give **instructions** and targets to staff (for example a new production schedule);
- **inform** staff, customers and suppliers (price changes, delivery dates, safety rules);
- **motivate** and recognise staff, and keep morale high;
- **consult** staff before a change, and collect **ideas** and **complaints**;
- manage **change**, such as a move of premises or the introduction of new machinery;
- deal with **customers**: quotations, orders, complaints and after-sales service;
- keep **records** for legal, tax and audit purposes;
- coordinate **departments**, so that production, sales and finance work together.

Poor communication leads to mistakes, delays, conflict, loss of customers and low morale.

## The communication process model

| Stage | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sender | The person who wants to pass on the message | Mrs Ncube |
| Message | The information, instruction or idea, in words or symbols | "Shift starts at 03:30 on Saturday" |
| Medium | The means used to carry the message | A written notice and a team briefing |
| Receiver | The person or group for whom the message is meant | The bakers |
| Feedback | The response which shows the message was understood | A baker repeats the time or asks about overtime pay |

Communication is **effective** only when the receiver understands the message as the sender meant it. Without feedback the sender cannot be sure. **Barriers** (jargon, noise, language, poor medium, distrust) can interfere at any stage. These are covered in the lesson on channels and barriers.

## Methods of communication

| Method | Examples | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spoken (verbal) | Face-to-face talk, meetings, telephone calls | Fast; immediate feedback; personal; tone of voice | No record unless noted; may be misremembered; hard to reach many people |
| Written | Memos, letters, reports, notices, invoices | Permanent record; precise; can be checked | Slower; no immediate feedback; may not be read; can be impersonal |
| Electronic | Email, text messages, group messages, video calls, the internet | Fast, cheap to many people; can attach documents; records kept | Needs equipment and network; overload; security risks; less personal |
| Visual | Charts, graphs, diagrams, posters, signs, photographs | Simple and quick to understand; helps with numbers and instructions | Limited detail; can be misread; needs careful design |

**Media of communication** include newspapers, radio, television, cell phones and the internet. These are used mainly for communicating with customers and the public, for example advertising a vacancy or announcing a product recall. Their reach differs: radio reaches rural areas without internet; the internet is cheap but only reaches people with access.

## Choosing a method and medium

A business should choose the method by asking:

- **Purpose**: is it an instruction, a warning, a request or a complaint?
- **Audience**: who is the receiver, how many, where, and what do they understand?
- **Urgency**: does the message need to be received immediately?
- **Need for a record**: is a written record required, for example a warning to an employee?
- **Complexity**: does the receiver need to see figures, or ask questions?
- **Cost**: of equipment, printing, airtime or time spent.
- **Confidentiality**: should others be able to see the message?

For example, a redundancy notice is delivered **face to face and followed in writing**, whereas a sales figure for the week can be shown in a chart on a notice board.

## Drafting business communications (ZIMSEC detail)

| Document | Purpose | Main features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Memorandum (memo) | Internal message between people in the same business | Heading "Memorandum"; To, From, Date, Subject; short body; no formal salutation |
| Business letter | External message to a customer or supplier | Sender's address, date, receiver's address, salutation, subject line, body, closing, signature and name |
| Email | Fast written message, internal or external | Clear subject line; brief paragraphs; polite greeting and closing; attachments named |
| Statement | A summary of account for a customer showing amounts owed | Customer name, period, invoices and payments, balance due |
| Invoice | A bill sent by the seller to the buyer | Seller and buyer, invoice number, date, goods and quantities, prices, total, payment terms |

A good document is **clear, accurate, brief, polite and complete**, uses simple language and has the key information first.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: drafting a memo]
**Context.** The manager of a fictional bakery asks the supervisor to draft a memo to all bakers about the change of shift.

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Heading | **MEMORANDUM** |
| To | All bakers |
| From | Mrs Ncube, Manager |
| Date | 12 March 2026 |
| Subject | Change of start time for Saturday 14 March 2026 |
| Body | The start time on Saturday 14 March 2026 is 03:30, not 04:00, because of a large weekend order. Please confirm with your supervisor by Friday that you can attend. Extra hours will be paid as overtime. |
| Signature | Mrs Ncube |

1. The memo is short and gives the date, the time, the reason and what the receiver must do.
2. It is a written method with a record, so there is no confusion about the time.
3. It lacks immediate feedback, so the manager asks bakers to confirm with the supervisor.
4. Judgement: for a time-sensitive change affecting pay, a memo plus a short spoken briefing gives both a record and feedback.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: case analysis, choosing a method]
**Context.** A fictional clothing wholesaler in Harare has three messages to send this week. (1) A warning to a worker who has been late four times. (2) A change in delivery times to 120 retailers across the country. (3) An explanation to a team of tailors of a new cutting pattern.

| Message | Best method | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warning to a late worker | Face-to-face meeting, followed by a written warning | Sensitive and confidential; needs a record and the worker's response |
| New delivery times to 120 retailers | Email or group text message, with a follow-up call to key customers | Large, scattered audience; low cost; fast; written record |
| New cutting pattern | Demonstration with a visual diagram and a spoken explanation | Practical; tailors can ask questions; diagrams show the shape |

**Point.** No single method suits every message.

**Application.** Each choice reflects purpose, audience, cost and need for a record.

**Analysis.** A text message to retailers would be fast but may not reach those with no network, so key customers are also telephoned. A written warning alone, without a meeting, would be impersonal and give no chance to respond.

**Judgement.** The wholesaler should match the method to the message and combine methods when a message is important, though this takes more time and cost.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Leaving out feedback"}
The process model has five parts. Without feedback the sender does not know whether the message was understood, so communication is incomplete.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying that one method is always best"}
Email is quick but unsuitable for sensitive news; a meeting gives feedback but leaves no record. Choose by purpose, audience, urgency and cost.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing method with medium"}
The method is the type (spoken, written, electronic, visual). The medium is the means, such as a telephone, letter or email.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers test this"}
ZIMSEC Business Studies Papers 1 and 2 (the process model, methods, and drafting a memo, letter or email); Business Enterprise Skills Papers 1 and 2; Cambridge Paper 3 (short case questions) and Paper 4 (essays: assess the importance of effective communication).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State/identify*: name a stage or a method. *Explain/describe*: say what a method involves and how it suits the case. *Analyse/examine*: trace the effect of poor or good communication. *Evaluate/discuss/justify*: weigh methods against purpose, audience and cost and recommend one, with a reason.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Communication is essential for instructions, information, motivation, consultation, change, customer service and records.
- The process model: sender, message, medium, receiver, feedback; communication succeeds only if the message is understood.
- Methods: spoken, written, electronic and visual; each has strengths and weaknesses.
- Choose the method by purpose, audience, urgency, need for a record, complexity, cost and confidentiality.
- ZIMSEC detail: drafting memos, letters, emails, statements and invoices, and using media such as newspapers, radio, cell phones and the internet.
- Combine methods for important messages, and always seek feedback.
:::
