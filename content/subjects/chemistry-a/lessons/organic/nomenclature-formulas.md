## Introduction

The ethanol blended into petrol in Zimbabwe is made by fermenting sugar from sugar cane grown in the Lowveld. A chemist calls it ethanol, writes it as $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$, and immediately knows three things: it has two carbon atoms, it contains the –OH functional group, and it belongs to the alcohols, so it will behave like every other alcohol. That is the power of systematic naming and formulae: one name or formula carries the structure, and the structure predicts the chemistry.

This lesson is the toolkit for the whole of organic chemistry at A Level. You should already know that carbon forms four covalent bonds, what single and double bonds are, and how to calculate relative molecular mass. Every later organic lesson assumes you can read and write the names and formulae here without hesitation.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Hydrocarbon"}
A compound made of carbon and hydrogen only.
:::

:::definition{term="Homologous series"}
A family of organic compounds with the same functional group and the same general formula, in which each member differs from the next by a $\ce{CH2}$ unit; members show a gradual trend in physical properties and have similar chemical properties.
:::

:::definition{term="Functional group"}
The atom or group of atoms in a molecule that is responsible for its characteristic chemical reactions.
:::

:::definition{term="Saturated / unsaturated"}
A saturated compound has only single carbon–carbon bonds; an unsaturated compound contains at least one carbon–carbon multiple bond (C=C or C≡C).
:::

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
Both boards: naming up to six carbons for the main functional groups and all the formula types. ZIMSEC asks for alkane names up to C₁₀ (decane). Cambridge A Level adds the naming of benzene derivatives such as 3-nitrobenzoic acid (see the lesson on the structure of benzene).
:::

## Five ways to write a formula

| Formula type | What it shows | Example: butan-1-ol |
|---|---|---|
| Molecular | the actual number of each type of atom | $\ce{C4H10O}$ |
| Empirical | the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms | $\ce{C4H10O}$ (already simplest) |
| General | the formula of a whole homologous series | $\ce{C_nH_{2n+1}OH}$ |
| Structural | the minimum detail to show the arrangement of atoms | $\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH2OH}$ |
| Displayed | every atom and every bond drawn out | each C–H, C–C, C–O and O–H bond shown |
| Skeletal | carbon chain as a zigzag of lines; H on carbon omitted; other atoms shown | a zigzag of four carbons ending in OH |

Because drawings are not shown in this course, displayed and skeletal formulae are described in words. In a skeletal formula every line end and every corner is a carbon atom, and each carbon carries enough hydrogen atoms to make four bonds.

## Functional groups and general formulae

| Homologous series | Functional group | General formula | Suffix or prefix | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| alkanes | C–C and C–H only | $\ce{C_nH_{2n+2}}$ | -ane | propane $\ce{CH3CH2CH3}$ |
| alkenes | C=C | $\ce{C_nH_{2n}}$ | -ene | propene $\ce{CH3CH=CH2}$ |
| halogenoalkanes | C–X (X = F, Cl, Br, I) | $\ce{C_nH_{2n+1}X}$ | chloro-, bromo-, iodo- | 2-bromopropane $\ce{CH3CHBrCH3}$ |
| alcohols | –OH | $\ce{C_nH_{2n+1}OH}$ | -ol | propan-2-ol $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH3}$ |
| aldehydes | –CHO | $\ce{C_nH_{2n+1}CHO}$ | -al | propanal $\ce{CH3CH2CHO}$ |
| ketones | C=O within the chain | $\ce{C_nH_{2n}O}$ ($n \geq 3$) | -one | propanone $\ce{CH3COCH3}$ |
| carboxylic acids | –COOH | $\ce{C_nH_{2n+1}COOH}$ | -oic acid | propanoic acid $\ce{CH3CH2COOH}$ |
| esters | –COO– | $\ce{C_nH_{2n}O2}$ | -yl …-oate | methyl ethanoate $\ce{CH3COOCH3}$ |
| amines | –NH₂ | $\ce{C_nH_{2n+1}NH2}$ | -amine | ethylamine $\ce{CH3CH2NH2}$ |
| nitriles | –C≡N | $\ce{C_nH_{2n+1}CN}$ | -nitrile | propanenitrile $\ce{CH3CH2CN}$ |

Note that the carbon of –CHO, –COOH and –CN is counted in the stem: $\ce{CH3CH2CN}$ has three carbons, so it is **propane**nitrile.

## Naming step by step

The stems are meth- (1), eth- (2), prop- (3), but- (4), pent- (5), hex- (6), hept- (7), oct- (8), non- (9), dec- (10).

1. Find the **longest continuous carbon chain** that contains the functional group. It gives the stem.
2. Number the chain from the end that gives the **functional group** (or the C=C) the lowest number.
3. Name side chains (methyl, ethyl) and halogens as prefixes, each with its position number, in **alphabetical order** (bromo before chloro before methyl).
4. Use di-, tri-, tetra- for repeated groups, with one number for each (2,2-dimethyl…); separate numbers from numbers with commas and numbers from words with hyphens.
5. For esters, name the alkyl group from the alcohol first, then the acid part with -oate: $\ce{CH3CH2COOCH3}$ is methyl propanoate.

:::example{title="Example 1: naming from structural formulae"}
1. $\ce{CH3CH(CH3)CH2CH3}$: longest chain four carbons (butane); a methyl group on carbon 2 → **2-methylbutane**.
2. $\ce{CH3CH2CH=CHCH3}$: five carbons with C=C; number from the right so the double bond starts at carbon 2 → **pent-2-ene**.
3. $\ce{CH3CHClCH2CH2Br}$: four carbons; numbering from the bromine end gives 1-bromo-3-chloro (positions 1 and 3), lower than 2-chloro-4-bromo → **1-bromo-3-chlorobutane** (alphabetical: bromo first).
4. $\ce{(CH3)3COH}$: three-carbon chain with OH on the middle carbon, which also carries a methyl group → **2-methylpropan-2-ol**.
5. $\ce{CH3CH2COCH2CH3}$: five carbons, C=O on carbon 3 → **pentan-3-one**.
:::

## Primary, secondary and tertiary

For alcohols and halogenoalkanes, count the alkyl groups (carbon atoms) attached to the carbon that carries the functional group:

| Class | Carbons bonded to the C–OH or C–X carbon | Example |
|---|---|---|
| primary (1°) | one (or none, for methanol / halogenomethanes) | butan-1-ol $\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH2OH}$ |
| secondary (2°) | two | butan-2-ol $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3}$ |
| tertiary (3°) | three | 2-methylpropan-2-ol $\ce{(CH3)3COH}$ |

Amines are classified differently (by the number of alkyl groups on the nitrogen); that is covered with the amines.

## Molecular and empirical formulae from a structure

:::worked-solution[Example 2: formulae of ethyl propanoate]
Ethyl propanoate is $\ce{CH3CH2COOCH2CH3}$. Data: Ar C = 12, H = 1, O = 16.

1. Count atoms: C: 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 5; H: 3 + 2 + 2 + 3 = 10; O: 2. Molecular formula $\ce{C5H10O2}$.
2. The numbers 5, 10 and 2 have no common factor except 1, so the empirical formula is also $\ce{C5H10O2}$.
3. $M_\text{r} = 5(12) + 10(1) + 2(16) = 102$.
4. Percentage of carbon by mass $= \dfrac{60}{102} \times 100 = 58.8\%$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: from percentage composition to a structure]
A neutral compound contains 54.5% C, 9.1% H and 36.4% O by mass; $M_\text{r} = 88$. It is an ester. Data: Ar C = 12, H = 1, O = 16.

1. Divide by Ar: C 54.5/12 = 4.54; H 9.1/1 = 9.1; O 36.4/16 = 2.275.
2. Divide by the smallest (2.275): C 2.00; H 4.00; O 1.00. Empirical formula $\ce{C2H4O}$, $M_\text{r}$ of the empirical unit = 44.
3. 88 ÷ 44 = 2, so the molecular formula is $\ce{C4H8O2}$.
4. Esters fit $\ce{C_nH_{2n}O2}$; possible esters are ethyl ethanoate $\ce{CH3COOCH2CH3}$, methyl propanoate $\ce{CH3CH2COOCH3}$, propyl methanoate and 1-methylethyl methanoate. Further data (a spectrum or hydrolysis products) would decide.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Not counting the functional-group carbon"}
$\ce{CH3CH2CH2COOH}$ is butanoic acid, not propanoic acid: the COOH carbon is carbon 1.
:::

:::mistake{title="Numbering from the wrong end"}
The functional group (or C=C) takes the lowest number, not the side chain. $\ce{CH2=CHCH(CH3)CH3}$ is 3-methylbut-1-ene.
:::

:::mistake{title="Choosing a chain that is not the longest"}
A chain written bent is still one chain. $\ce{CH3CH(CH2CH3)CH3}$ is 2-methylbutane, not 2-ethylpropane.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ester names back to front"}
In $\ce{CH3COOCH2CH3}$ the ethyl group comes from ethanol and the ethanoate from ethanoic acid: ethyl ethanoate. The alkyl group attached to the single-bonded O is named first.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Where this is tested"}
Every organic question on ZIMSEC Papers 1-3 and Cambridge Papers 1, 2 and 4 assumes this lesson: *name* means the full IUPAC name with numbers; *give the structural formula* means an unambiguous condensed formula such as $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH3}$, not $\ce{C3H8O}$.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Formula commands"}
*Deduce the molecular formula* from a skeletal or displayed description: count carbons at every corner and end, then add hydrogens so each carbon has four bonds. *Deduce the empirical formula*: divide by the highest common factor. Check your answer against the general formula of the series.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Classifying"}
When asked to *state whether an alcohol is primary, secondary or tertiary*, look only at the carbon carrying the OH and count the carbons bonded to it.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A homologous series shares a functional group and a general formula; members differ by $\ce{CH2}$.
- Molecular, empirical, general, structural, displayed and skeletal formulae show increasing or decreasing detail; know what each one shows.
- Name from the longest chain containing the functional group, numbering to give it the lowest number; prefixes go in alphabetical order.
- Count the carbon of –CHO, –COOH and –CN in the stem; esters are named alkyl …-oate.
- Primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols and halogenoalkanes have one, two and three carbons on the functional-group carbon.
:::
