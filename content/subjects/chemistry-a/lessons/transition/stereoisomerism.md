## Introduction

Two compounds can have exactly the same formula, the same atoms and the same bonds, and yet one is a life-saving cancer drug while the other is far less useful. That is the story of $\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$: the isomer called cisplatin is on the World Health Organization list of essential medicines and is used to treat several cancers, while its trans isomer is not used clinically.

The difference is only the arrangement of the ligands in space. This lesson shows how complexes form cis/trans and optical isomers, how to describe them without drawings, and how to decide whether an isomer is polar. You should know the shapes of complexes, bidentate ligands, and stereoisomerism in organic compounds (cis/trans isomers of alkenes and chiral centres).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Stereoisomers"}
Compounds with the same molecular formula and the same structural formula (the same atoms bonded to each other) but a different arrangement of atoms in space.
:::

:::definition{term="Cis/trans (geometrical) isomers"}
Stereoisomers that differ in whether two identical ligands are next to each other (cis, at 90°) or opposite each other (trans, at 180°) around the central ion.
:::

:::definition{term="Optical isomers (enantiomers)"}
Stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other. A complex that has optical isomers is chiral: it has no plane of symmetry.
:::

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
Both boards: cis/trans isomerism in square planar and octahedral complexes, optical isomerism in octahedral complexes with bidentate ligands, cisplatin in outline and the overall polarity of isomers (Cambridge A Level, Paper 4; ZIMSEC Form 6). In the examination you may be asked to draw isomers; here they are described in words and tables, and you should practise turning these descriptions into three-dimensional sketches.
:::

## Cis/trans isomerism in square planar complexes

In a square planar complex the four ligand positions lie at the corners of a square around the metal ion. Two positions next to each other are at 90°; two opposite each other are at 180°.

For a complex of the type MA₂B₂, such as $\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$:

| Isomer | Arrangement | Cl-Pt-Cl angle | Overall polarity |
|---|---|---|---|
| cis (cisplatin) | the two Cl ligands are on adjacent corners; the two NH₃ ligands on the other two adjacent corners | 90° | polar: the Pt-Cl bond dipoles do not cancel |
| trans (transplatin) | the two Cl ligands are on opposite corners, and so are the two NH₃ ligands | 180° | non-polar: equal and opposite bond dipoles cancel |

Tetrahedral complexes do not show cis/trans isomerism, because every position in a tetrahedron is adjacent to every other one.

## Cisplatin as an anticancer drug (outline)

Cisplatin is given by injection. Inside cells the chloride concentration is lower than in blood, so water molecules replace the chloride ligands. The platinum then bonds to nitrogen atoms of two guanine bases that lie next to each other on the same strand of DNA. This bends the DNA so that it cannot be copied, and the cancer cell cannot divide and dies.

Only the cis isomer can bond to two neighbouring bases in this way, because its two leaving groups are at 90° to each other. The drug also harms healthy, rapidly dividing cells, so it has side effects such as nausea and kidney damage, and doses are carefully controlled.

## Cis/trans isomerism in octahedral complexes

An octahedral complex of the type MA₄B₂, such as $\ce{[Co(NH3)4(H2O)2]^2+}$, has two isomers:

| Isomer | Arrangement of the two water ligands | Angle between them | Overall polarity |
|---|---|---|---|
| cis | on adjacent positions | 90° | polar |
| trans | on opposite positions (one above and one below the plane of the four NH₃ ligands) | 180° | non-polar |

Bidentate ligands always span two **adjacent** positions (cis), because they are too short to reach across the metal ion. So $\ce{[Ni(en)2(H2O)2]^2+}$ (en = 1,2-diaminoethane) has a trans isomer, with the two water molecules opposite each other and the two en ligands in a plane, and a cis isomer, with the two water molecules next to each other.

## Optical isomerism

An octahedral complex with **three bidentate ligands**, such as $\ce{[Ni(en)3]^2+}$, has no plane of symmetry. The three en ligands wrap around the metal ion like the blades of a propeller, which can twist either clockwise or anticlockwise. The two forms are non-superimposable mirror images: a pair of optical isomers.

The cis isomer of $\ce{[Ni(en)2(H2O)2]^2+}$ is also chiral, so it exists as two optical isomers. The trans isomer has a plane of symmetry (the plane containing the two en ligands), so it is not chiral.

:::example{title="Example 1: counting the stereoisomers of [Ni(en)2(H2O)2]2+"}
Deduce how many stereoisomers $\ce{[Ni(en)2(H2O)2]^2+}$ has, and describe them.

1. Each en ligand occupies two adjacent positions; the two water molecules occupy the remaining two positions.
2. If the two water molecules are opposite each other (trans), the two en ligands lie in one plane. This isomer has a plane of symmetry, so it has no optical isomer: **1** stereoisomer.
3. If the two water molecules are adjacent (cis), the two en ligands are twisted out of a single plane. There is no plane of symmetry, so the mirror image cannot be superimposed: **2** optical isomers.
4. Total: 1 + 2 = **3** stereoisomers.
:::

:::example{title="Example 2: deciding the overall polarity"}
State which isomers of $\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$ and $\ce{[Co(NH3)4(H2O)2]^2+}$ have an overall dipole.

1. Each metal-ligand bond is polar, and Pt-Cl and Pt-N bonds have different dipoles.
2. In the trans isomers, identical ligands are opposite each other, so their bond dipoles are equal and opposite and cancel. Trans-$\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$ and trans-$\ce{[Co(NH3)4(H2O)2]^2+}$ have no overall dipole.
3. In the cis isomers, identical ligands are at 90°, so their dipoles add to a resultant: cis-$\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$ and cis-$\ce{[Co(NH3)4(H2O)2]^2+}$ are polar.
4. A consequence: cisplatin is more soluble in water than transplatin.
:::

## Summary table

| Complex type | Example | Number of stereoisomers |
|---|---|---|
| square planar MA₂B₂ | $\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$ | 2 (cis and trans) |
| octahedral MA₄B₂ | $\ce{[Co(NH3)4(H2O)2]^2+}$ | 2 (cis and trans) |
| octahedral M(AA)₂B₂ (AA bidentate) | $\ce{[Ni(en)2(H2O)2]^2+}$ | 3 (trans, and a pair of cis enantiomers) |

| tetrahedral MA₂B₂ | $\ce{[ZnCl2(NH3)2]}$ | 1 (no stereoisomers) |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Expecting cis/trans isomers in tetrahedral complexes"}
In a tetrahedron all four positions are adjacent, so there is only one arrangement of MA₂B₂.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting a bidentate ligand across the metal ion"}
A bidentate ligand such as en always occupies two adjacent (cis) positions; it cannot span 180°.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling a complex with optical isomers 'having a chiral carbon'"}
Chirality in $\ce{[Ni(en)3]^2+}$ comes from the arrangement around the metal ion as a whole (no plane of symmetry), not from a chiral carbon atom.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers"}
Cambridge Paper 4 (often with a request to draw three-dimensional diagrams); ZIMSEC Papers 1, 2 and 3 Section D.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*Draw* or *describe* isomers by showing which positions identical ligands occupy (adjacent or opposite) and the bond angle between them. *Explain* why a complex is chiral by stating that it has no plane of symmetry and that its mirror image is non-superimposable. *Deduce* polarity by deciding whether bond dipoles cancel. *Suggest* why only cisplatin works: its two chloride ligands are on adjacent positions, so it can bind to two neighbouring bases on DNA.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Square planar MA₂B₂ and octahedral MA₄B₂ complexes have cis (90°) and trans (180°) isomers; tetrahedral complexes do not.
- Cisplatin, cis-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂], loses its chloride ligands in cells and binds to two adjacent guanine bases on DNA, stopping cancer cells dividing.
- Bidentate ligands occupy adjacent positions; [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ and cis-[Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ have no plane of symmetry and exist as pairs of optical isomers.
- [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ has three stereoisomers: trans (not chiral) and two cis enantiomers.
- Cis isomers are polar; in trans isomers equal bond dipoles cancel.
:::
