## Introduction

Nyasha and two friends in Mutare build a revision app for ZIMSEC learners. The app works well, yet after a month only a handful of people have installed it. The problem is not the program: nobody knows it exists, the price is unclear and learners do not know where to get it. A good product that is not marketed is like a shop with no sign on the door.

**Marketing** is everything a business does to find out what customers want, to offer it at the right price in the right place and to tell customers about it. This lesson covers the four Ps, market research, branding, digital marketing, how to write a simple marketing plan and strategy, and how to safeguard an innovation. You should already know the e-business models and basic ideas of costs and profit from the previous lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Marketing"}
The activities of finding out what customers need and then creating, pricing, distributing and promoting a product or service to satisfy them at a profit.
:::

:::definition{term="Marketing mix (the 4Ps)"}
The four elements a business controls to reach its customers: product, price, place and promotion.
:::

:::definition{term="Market research"}
Collecting and analysing information about customers, competitors and the market to help decisions about what to sell and how.
:::

:::definition{term="Brand"}
The name, logo, style and reputation that make a product or business recognisable and different from its competitors.
:::

:::definition{term="Digital marketing"}
Promoting products using electronic channels such as search engines, social media, email, messaging apps and web sites.
:::

## The 4Ps

| P | Question it answers | ICT example (revision app) |
|---|---|---|
| **Product** | What are we selling and what makes it useful? | Past-paper questions with marking, works offline on a phone |
| **Price** | What will customers pay? | A one-off fee low enough for a learner, with a free sample |
| **Place** | Where and how can customers get it? | Download from an app store, mobile money payment, copies at school computer rooms |
| **Promotion** | How do customers learn about it? | Social media posts, posters at schools, demonstrations, word of mouth |

The four Ps must fit together: a very cheap price with a poor product, or a good product that cannot be found, will fail.

## Market research

Research reduces guesswork. Methods include **questionnaires** (many people answer the same questions), **interviews** (detailed answers from a few people), **observation** (watching how customers behave), **focus groups**, and **desk research** (using existing reports and web sites). A sample should be chosen so that it represents the customers.

:::worked-solution{title="Reading a survey"}
Nyasha asks 120 learners whether they would buy the app.

| Answer | Number of people | Percentage |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Yes | 66 | 55% |
| Maybe | 30 | 25% |
| No | 24 | 20% |

1. Total = 66 + 30 + 24 = 120.
2. Percentage who say yes = 66 ÷ 120 × 100 = 55%.
3. The 25% who say "maybe" may be won with a free sample or a trial.
4. Caution: saying yes in a survey is not the same as paying. The sample should also include learners from different schools and forms, or the result may mislead.
:::

## Pricing and branding

A common method is **cost-plus pricing**: the price is the cost plus a mark-up percentage. Other methods look at what competitors charge or at what customers are willing to pay.

:::worked-solution{title="Cost-plus pricing"}
A phone cover costs US\$4.50 to make and the owner adds a mark-up of 40%.

1. Mark-up = 4.50 × 40 ÷ 100 = US\$1.80.
2. Selling price = 4.50 + 1.80 = US\$6.30.
3. Profit on each cover = 6.30 − 4.50 = US\$1.80.
:::

**Branding** gives a product a name, a logo, colours, a slogan and a reputation. A consistent brand builds trust, makes the product easy to recognise and lets the business charge for quality. Poor service damages the brand more quickly than advertising can repair it.

## Digital marketing

Digital marketing is cheap to start and can be measured. Typical measures are:

- **Click-through rate** = clicks ÷ times the advert was shown × 100.
- **Conversion rate** = sales ÷ clicks × 100.
- **Cost per sale** = advert cost ÷ number of sales.

:::worked-solution{title="Was the advert worth it?"}
An advert for the phone covers (price US\$6.30, cost US\$4.50) costs US\$20. It is shown 4000 times, receives 160 clicks and leads to 16 sales.

1. Click-through rate = 160 ÷ 4000 × 100 = 4%.
2. Conversion rate = 16 ÷ 160 × 100 = 10%.
3. Cost per sale = 20 ÷ 16 = US\$1.25.
4. Revenue = 16 × 6.30 = US\$100.80; cost of the covers = 16 × 4.50 = US\$72.
5. Profit from the campaign = 100.80 − 72 − 20 = US\$8.80, so the advert was worth running.
:::

## A marketing plan and strategy

A simple plan has these parts: the **objective** (for example, 200 downloads in two months); the **target market** (who the customers are); the **marketing mix** (the 4Ps); the **budget**; the **timetable**; and how success will be **measured**. A **strategy** is the broad approach, such as offering a free trial to learners at ten schools and using word of mouth.

:::example{title="A strategy for a school software supplier"}
A small firm in Gweru sells attendance software to schools. Its *target market* is secondary schools with a computer room. Its *product* works with a single computer; its *price* is a yearly licence per school; its *place* is direct sales, by visiting schools and by mobile payment; and its *promotion* is a demonstration at the district head teachers' meeting and a free month's trial. It will *measure* success by the number of trials that become paying customers.
:::

## Safeguarding an innovation

An innovator should protect the idea before sharing it widely. Methods include: **copyright** for the program code and written material; **patents** for a new invention (where the law allows); **trademarks** for the name and logo; **confidentiality agreements** with partners and staff; and keeping key details secret. Keep records that show who created the work and when.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing the 4Ps without applying them"}
Write what each P means for the product in the question, for example "Place: the app is sold through mobile money and school computer rooms".
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing promotion with marketing"}
Promotion is only one of the four Ps. Marketing also includes research, product, price and place.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating a survey as proof of sales"}
People may say they would buy and then not do so. The result is a guide, not a guarantee.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Paper 1 asks you to *state* or *explain* the 4Ps, *describe* market research methods, *calculate* a selling price or percentage from data, and *design* a simple marketing plan for an ICT product. Use the pattern: objective, target market, the four Ps, how success is measured. In calculations show the formula and units, and state the conclusion in words.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Marketing finds out what customers need and provides, prices and promotes a product or service; the mix is product, price, place and promotion.
- Market research uses questionnaires, interviews, observation and existing reports; results are a guide, not proof.
- Cost-plus pricing adds a mark-up percentage to the cost; branding builds recognition and trust.
- Digital marketing is measured by click-through rate, conversion rate and cost per sale.
- Safeguard an innovation with copyright, patents, trademarks and confidentiality.
:::
