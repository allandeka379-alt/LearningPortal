## Introduction

Tatenda is a junior programmer at a company in Harare that writes software for clinics. While testing, he notices that patient names are visible to anyone who knows a simple web address. His manager says, "We have to release on Friday; fix it later." Tatenda has to decide whether to speak up, stay silent or fix it quietly. Nothing in the program's code tells him what to do: the answer depends on **ethics**.

Ethics is the study of what is right and wrong. Law tells us what we *must* do; ethics asks what we *should* do, and some things are legal but unethical. Computing professionals are trusted with other people's data, money and safety, so they need a shared standard of behaviour. This lesson explains the purpose of ethics for a professional, the codes of professional bodies, how to apply a code to a situation, business ethics and the African principle of unhu/ubuntu. You should already know the meaning of privacy and integrity of data.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Ethics"}
The principles of right and wrong that guide how people behave. In computing they guide how technology, data and systems are designed and used.
:::

:::definition{term="Professional code of ethics"}
A written set of standards of conduct that members of a professional body agree to follow, covering matters such as the public interest, competence, honesty and confidentiality.
:::

:::definition{term="Professional body"}
An organisation of people in the same profession that sets standards, supports members and can discipline them. Examples are the British Computer Society (BCS), the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) and, in Zimbabwe, the Computer Society of Zimbabwe.
:::

:::definition{term="Unhu/ubuntu"}
An African ethical principle that a person becomes fully human through relationships with others: it stresses respect, honesty, community, responsibility and care for other people.
:::

## Why computing professionals need ethics

- **Trust.** Customers cannot check the code themselves. They trust professionals not to misuse their data or to hide faults.
- **Power and impact.** A programming error in a bank, a clinic or a vehicle can harm many people at once.
- **Gaps in the law.** Technology changes faster than laws, so professionals must use judgement in new situations such as artificial intelligence, facial recognition or large collections of personal data.
- **Reputation.** An ethical profession is respected and trusted, which benefits employers and clients.

## Professional bodies and codes of ethics

Joining a professional body shows a commitment to standards. It usually gives access to training and a code of conduct, and a body can remove a member who breaks the code. The main points common to most codes are:

| Principle | What it asks of a professional | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Public interest** | Put the safety and wellbeing of the public ahead of personal or employer interests | Report a fault that could endanger patients, even if it delays the release |
| **Competence** | Work only within the limits of your knowledge, and keep your skills up to date | Admit that you do not know how to secure a database and ask for help |
| **Confidentiality** | Keep private information of clients and users secret and use it only for its purpose | Do not read or share customers' records without a reason |
| **Honesty and integrity** | Be truthful about what a system can do, do not falsify results or take credit for others' work | Report test results accurately, including failures |
| **Respect for the law and for others** | Obey laws, respect intellectual property and treat colleagues and users fairly | Use only licensed software and give credit for code you reuse |

## Applying a code to a situation

A simple method is to ask four questions: *Who could be affected? Is anyone harmed or deceived? Which principles of the code apply? What are the alternatives and their consequences?*

:::worked-solution{title="Deciding what Tatenda should do"}
1. **Who is affected?** Patients, whose names are exposed; the clinics; the company, which may be fined or lose trust; Tatenda himself.
2. **Is anyone harmed or deceived?** Patients' privacy is at risk, and releasing without saying so would deceive the clinics.
3. **Which principles apply?** Public interest (protect patients), confidentiality (patient data), honesty (do not hide a known fault).
4. **Alternatives.** (a) Say nothing: the patients stay at risk and Tatenda breaks the code. (b) Fix it quietly: better, but the manager's decision to release is not corrected and the problem may be repeated. (c) Report it to the manager in writing, explain the risk, and ask for the fix to be done before release; if it is ignored, report to a senior manager or the professional body.
5. **Decision.** Alternative (c) follows the code: it protects the public, is honest and gives the company the chance to act.
:::

:::example{title="Ethical or unethical?"}
**A.** A web developer copies a function from an open-source project, keeps the licence notice and credits the author. *Ethical*: it respects the licence and the author's rights.

**B.** A systems administrator at a school reads a teacher's private email to satisfy her curiosity. *Unethical*: it breaks confidentiality, even though she had the technical ability to do so.

**C.** A company sells an app and claims that it "never shares your data", while in fact it sells user data to advertisers. *Unethical and possibly illegal*: it is dishonest.

**Impact.** Ethical behaviour builds trust and protects people. Unethical behaviour harms individuals, can lead to dismissal, removal from a professional body, fines or prosecution, and damages the reputation of the whole profession.
:::

## Business ethics and unhu/ubuntu

The ZIMSEC syllabus names four attributes of business ethics.

- **Fairness**: treating customers, employees and competitors justly, for example charging every customer the same advertised price.
- **Firmness**: keeping to principles under pressure, for example refusing to bribe an official to win a contract.
- **Honesty**: telling the truth about products, prices and faults.
- **Self-motivation**: working responsibly and diligently without needing to be forced.

**Unhu/ubuntu** adds that an action is judged by its effect on the community. A computing professional who respects unhu asks how a decision will affect others, treats people with dignity, keeps promises and shares knowledge.

## Existing and emerging technologies

Ethical questions arise with every technology: **social media** (privacy, misinformation), **artificial intelligence** (bias in decisions, jobs, who is responsible for mistakes), **surveillance and facial recognition** (privacy against safety), **big data** (consent and use of personal data) and the **internet of things** (devices that collect data in homes). The same four questions help: who is affected, who may be harmed, which principles apply and what are the alternatives.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
Both boards need the purpose of ethics, the idea of a professional body (BCS, IEEE; the Computer Society of Zimbabwe for local examples), the points of a code of ethics and the impact of ethical or unethical behaviour in a scenario. ZIMSEC adds the business ethics of fairness, firmness, honesty and self-motivation, and unhu/ubuntu. Cambridge links AI to its own topic.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying that something is ethical because it is legal"}
Law is the minimum standard. Reading a colleague's private files may not lead to prosecution, yet it breaks the code of confidentiality.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing a code of ethics without a point from it"}
Name the principle (public interest, competence, confidentiality or honesty) and link it to the situation, rather than writing "he behaved badly".
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the impact"}
When asked for the impact of acting unethically, say who is harmed (users, the company, the profession) and the consequences (loss of trust, dismissal, legal action).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Paper 1 and Cambridge Paper 1 give a short situation and ask whether the action is ethical, which point of a code it breaks or keeps, and what the impact is. Answer in three steps: say ethical or unethical, name the principle, explain the effect on the people involved. For *state the purpose of a code of ethics* give two ideas such as guiding behaviour and protecting the public. Do not use real names of companies when describing misbehaviour.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Ethics asks what people *should* do; computing professionals need it because they are trusted with data and systems and the law cannot cover every case.
- Professional bodies such as the BCS, IEEE and the Computer Society of Zimbabwe publish codes of ethics: public interest, competence, confidentiality and honesty.
- To judge a situation, ask who is affected, who is harmed, which principles apply and what the alternatives are.
- Business ethics include fairness, firmness, honesty and self-motivation; unhu/ubuntu judges actions by their effect on the community.
- Unethical behaviour harms people and can lead to dismissal, expulsion from the body, fines and loss of trust.
:::
