## Introduction

A learner in Hwange posts a photograph of her school's science fair and hundreds of people see it within an hour. A small business in Mutare sells clothes through a social media page and reaches customers in other towns. In the same week a false message about a "dangerous vaccine" spreads through a group chat, and a learner is bullied by classmates online. At the end of term the school also throws a pile of broken monitors and old computers behind a store room.

These everyday scenes show two ethical areas of computing: the **social impact** of social media and the **environmental impact** of computer use and disposal. This lesson sets out the benefits and dangers of social media, the environmental effects of computers, how e-waste should be managed with the 3Rs, and the role of the Environmental Management Agency (EMA). You should know what ethics and computer crime mean from earlier lessons.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Social media"}
Web sites and apps that let people create and share content and take part in online communities, such as messaging groups, video and photograph sharing and networking sites.
:::

:::definition{term="E-waste"}
Discarded electrical and electronic equipment, such as computers, phones, monitors, batteries and printers, which is broken, obsolete or no longer wanted.
:::

:::definition{term="The 3Rs"}
Reduce (use and buy less), reuse (use again, repair or donate) and recycle (break down a product so that its materials can be made into new things).
:::

:::definition{term="Defamation"}
Making a false statement about a person that harms their reputation.
:::

## Social media: benefits and dangers

| Benefits | Dangers |
|---|---|
| Quick, cheap communication with family, friends and groups | **Misinformation**: false stories spread faster than corrections |
| Enterprise: small businesses advertise and sell to many customers | **Cyberbullying and harassment**, which can harm a victim's health and school work |
| Education: study groups, videos and sharing notes | **Privacy**: personal details, photographs and locations can be seen, copied or misused |
| Community, public information and emergency alerts | **Addiction**: hours of scrolling reduce sleep, study time and face-to-face contact |
| A voice for people and campaigns | **Defamation and illegal content** posted by users; fraud and scams aimed at users |

:::keyfact{title="Using social media responsibly"}
Check a story with another reliable source before sharing; think about whether a post could hurt someone; keep privacy settings tight; do not post other people's pictures without their permission; report abuse and block bullies; and limit the time spent online.
:::

## Environmental effects of computer use

- **Energy.** Computers, servers and networks use electricity, much of it produced by burning fuel, so energy use adds to pollution and costs money. Equipment left on when it is not needed wastes energy.
- **Raw materials.** Making devices uses metals, plastics and water, and mining and manufacturing can damage land and water.
- **E-waste.** Equipment is replaced quickly, so large amounts become waste.
- **Toxic materials.** Many devices contain substances such as lead and mercury, and batteries contain harmful metals. If devices are dumped or burned, these can enter soil, water and air and harm people and animals.

:::worked-solution{title="Energy wasted by idle computers"}
A school laboratory has 30 computers. Each uses 0.05 kW when left on but idle, and they are idle for 18 hours on each of the 5 weekdays. Electricity costs US\$0.12 per kWh. (Energy in kWh = power in kW × time in hours.)

| Quantity | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Energy used per day by idle computers | 30 × 0.05 kW × 18 h | 27 kWh |
| Energy per week (5 weekdays) | 27 × 5 | 135 kWh |
| Energy per term (13 weeks) | 135 × 13 | 1755 kWh |
| Cost per week at US\$0.12 per kWh | 135 × 0.12 | US\$16.20 |

1. Switching the computers off at the end of the day saves this energy and the money.
2. It is an example of **reduce**: using less energy to do the same job.
:::

## E-waste management and the 3Rs

1. **Reduce.** Buy only what is needed, choose durable equipment, switch off idle devices, and avoid replacing equipment that still does its job.
2. **Reuse.** Repair faulty equipment, upgrade memory or storage, and donate working computers to schools and clinics. Remove personal data first.
3. **Recycle.** Take equipment to an authorised collector that separates the metals, glass and plastics for reuse and handles harmful parts safely.

:::worked-solution{title="How much e-waste can be recovered?"}
A school replaces 40 computers, each of mass 9 kg. An authorised recycler recovers 65% of the mass for reuse.

1. Total mass = 40 × 9 = 360 kg.
2. Mass recovered = 360 × 0.65 = 234 kg.
3. Mass that cannot be recovered and goes to safe disposal = 360 − 234 = 126 kg.
4. If the school had donated 10 working computers instead of recycling them, that would be reuse, which keeps 90 kg in use and is better than recycling because the machines are not broken down.
:::

## Law, the EMA and responsible disposal

Environmental laws in Zimbabwe set out how waste must be handled and make polluting an offence. The **Environmental Management Agency (EMA)** is the national body that enforces environmental law: among other things it promotes proper waste management, can require organisations to manage their waste responsibly, and can take action against polluters. The details of the laws are described here in outline only, and a reviewer or teacher should confirm them.

Responsible disposal means:

- never burning or dumping computers, batteries or toner;
- using an authorised collection or recycling service, or returning equipment to the seller where this is offered;
- erasing or destroying data on disks before equipment leaves the school;
- keeping a record of what was disposed of and how.

:::example{title="Planning for the end of a laboratory's life"}
The school in Gweru has 25 old computers. The ICT head separates them into three groups. Eight still work and are checked, wiped and donated to a rural primary school (**reuse**). Ten can be repaired with new memory and used in the library (**reuse** through repair). The last seven are broken: they are sent to an authorised recycler (**recycle**). The head also changes the purchasing policy to buy fewer, longer-lasting machines and to switch computers off at night (**reduce**).
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing only the dangers of social media"}
Balanced answers give both benefits and dangers. A question that says "discuss" expects both sides and a conclusion.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying that recycling is the same as reuse"}
Reuse means using the item again, perhaps after repair. Recycling breaks the item down and uses its materials to make something new.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the data on old equipment"}
Before donating, selling or recycling a computer, its data must be erased or its disk destroyed, otherwise privacy is breached.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Paper 1 asks you to *state* benefits or dangers of social media, to *explain* how computers affect the environment, to *describe* e-waste management using the 3Rs and to *state the role of EMA*. Give a developed point, not a single word: "cyberbullying, which harms a victim's mental health and school work". For calculations of energy use remember kWh = kW × hours. In a *discuss* question, give two benefits, two dangers and a conclusion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Social media gives quick communication, enterprise and education, but also misinformation, cyberbullying, privacy risks, addiction and defamation.
- Computers affect the environment through energy use, raw materials, e-waste and toxic materials.
- Energy in kWh = power in kW × time in hours; switching off idle equipment saves energy and money.
- The 3Rs are reduce, reuse and recycle; reuse (including repair and donation) comes before recycling.
- EMA enforces environmental law; equipment must be disposed of through authorised services after erasing the data.
:::
