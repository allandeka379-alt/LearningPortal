## Introduction

Every living organism is made of one or more **cells**. A bacterium is a single cell; a maize plant or an elephant is made of billions of them. The cell is the basic unit of both **structure** (organisms are built from cells) and **function** (the processes of life, such as respiration, happen inside cells). New cells are only ever made when existing cells divide.

Most cells are far too small to see with the naked eye, so you study them with a light microscope or a bioviewer. In this lesson you will learn the structures inside plant, animal and bacterial cells, what each one does, how the three types of cell differ, and how to prepare slides and identify structures in micrographs. You should already know the seven characteristics of living organisms.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Cell"}
The smallest unit of structure and function of a living organism.
:::

:::definition{term="Organelle"}
A structure inside a cell that has a particular job, such as the nucleus, a mitochondrion or a chloroplast.
:::

:::definition{term="Partially permeable"}
Allowing some substances to pass through but not others. The cell membrane is partially permeable; the cell wall of a plant is fully permeable.
:::

## The structures of cells and their functions

| Structure | Function | Plant | Animal | Bacterium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cell membrane | Controls which substances enter and leave the cell | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Cytoplasm | Jelly-like material where most of the cell's chemical reactions take place | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Ribosomes | Make proteins (protein synthesis) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Nucleus | Contains the genetic material (DNA in chromosomes), which controls the cell's activities | Yes | Yes | No |
| Mitochondria | Carry out aerobic respiration, releasing energy | Yes | Yes | No |
| Cell wall | Strengthens the cell, keeps its shape and stops it bursting when it takes in water | Yes (cellulose) | No | Yes (not cellulose) |
| Chloroplasts | Contain chlorophyll, which absorbs light for photosynthesis | Some cells | No | No |
| Large permanent vacuole | Holds cell sap (water with dissolved sugars and salts); keeps the cell firm | Yes | No (only small, temporary vacuoles) | No |
| Circular DNA | A single loop of DNA, free in the cytoplasm, that controls the cell | No | No | Yes |
| Plasmids | Small extra rings of DNA carrying a few genes | No | No | Yes |

:::keyfact
Plant cells have everything an animal cell has (membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes) **plus** a cellulose cell wall, a large permanent vacuole and, in green parts, chloroplasts.
:::

## Plant and animal cells compared

A typical plant cell, such as a palisade cell from a leaf, has a regular, box-like shape because of its wall. Its large vacuole pushes the cytoplasm and nucleus to the edge. A typical animal cell, such as a cheek cell, has no wall, so its shape is irregular, and its nucleus is usually near the middle. Plant cells store carbohydrate as starch grains; animal cells store it as small granules of glycogen.

![Plant cell and animal cell side by side: both have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes; only the plant cell also has a cellulose cell wall, a large permanent vacuole full of cell sap and chloroplasts.](media:diagrams/plant-animal-cell.png "Figure: a plant cell and an animal cell with their main structures labelled")

Not every plant cell has chloroplasts. Cells in roots and in the white storage leaves of an onion bulb receive no light, so they have none.

## Bacterial cells

Bacteria are much smaller than plant and animal cells, usually 1–5 micrometres long, compared with roughly 50 micrometres across for a cheek cell. A bacterial cell has a cell wall (not made of cellulose), a cell membrane, cytoplasm and ribosomes. It has **no nucleus**: its DNA is a single circular chromosome lying in the cytoplasm, and it may also have small rings of DNA called **plasmids**. It has no mitochondria and no chloroplasts.

![Bacterial cell: a cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm with ribosomes, a single loop of circular DNA lying free in the cytoplasm and small rings called plasmids; there is no nucleus and no mitochondria.](media:diagrams/bacterial-cell.png "Figure: a bacterial cell has circular DNA and plasmids but no nucleus")

## Preparing and observing cells

:::example{title="Onion epidermis cells"}
1. Peel a thin, almost transparent layer (the epidermis) from the inner surface of one of the fleshy leaves of an onion bulb.
2. Lay it flat in a drop of iodine solution on a clean slide. The stain makes the nucleus show up clearly.
3. Lower a coverslip gently at an angle using a mounted needle, so that no air bubbles are trapped.
4. Look first with the low-power objective, then switch to high power.

You see regular, brick-shaped cells in rows, each with a cell wall, a stained nucleus at the edge, a thin layer of cytoplasm and a large vacuole. There are no chloroplasts.
:::

:::example{title="Cheek cells"}
Gently rub the inside of your cheek with a clean cotton bud, smear it on a slide, add a drop of methylene blue stain and a coverslip. The cells are flat and irregular in shape, each with a dark-blue nucleus near the centre, and there is no cell wall and no large vacuole. Put used cotton buds straight into disinfectant.
:::

## Identifying structures in a micrograph

Exam questions often give a photograph taken through a microscope (a micrograph) with labelled parts. Use shape and position as clues.

:::example{title="A cell from the palisade layer of a leaf"}
A micrograph shows a long, box-shaped cell with these labelled parts:

- **A**: a thick, clearly visible outer boundary;
- **B**: a thin line lying just inside A;
- **C**: a large, clear area filling most of the centre of the cell;
- **D**: many small oval bodies in the cytoplasm, near the edge of the cell;
- **E**: one large rounded body at the side of the cell.

A is the **cell wall** (the outermost layer of a plant cell). B is the **cell membrane**, pressed against the wall. C is the **large permanent vacuole**, which fills the centre. D are **chloroplasts**, and E is the **nucleus**, pushed to the side by the vacuole. Mitochondria and ribosomes are too small to identify clearly with a light microscope.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking plant cells have no mitochondria"}
Plant cells respire all the time, so they have mitochondria, just like animal cells. Chloroplasts do not replace them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking all plant cells have chloroplasts"}
Root cells and onion bulb cells have no chloroplasts because they receive no light.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the cell wall and the cell membrane"}
The cell wall is fully permeable and gives strength and shape. The cell membrane is partially permeable and controls what enters and leaves. Animal cells have a membrane but no wall.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying bacteria have no DNA"}
Bacteria have no nucleus, but they do have DNA: a circular chromosome and often plasmids.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying mitochondria make energy"}
Energy cannot be made. Mitochondria **release** energy from nutrients in aerobic respiration.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Compare" means give a point about each cell: "the plant cell has a cellulose cell wall but the animal cell does not". A list of plant features alone is not a comparison.
:::

:::exam-tip
Learn the functions in their exam form: the nucleus **contains genetic material that controls the activities of the cell**; the membrane **controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell**; ribosomes are **the site of protein synthesis**.
:::

:::exam-tip
When drawing cells you have observed, draw only what you can see, with clear lines and ruled labels, and do not add structures from memory.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The cell is the basic unit of structure and function; new cells come only from the division of existing cells.
- Plant and animal cells both have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, a nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes.
- Plant cells also have a cellulose cell wall, a large permanent vacuole and (in green parts) chloroplasts.
- Bacterial cells have a cell wall, membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, circular DNA and plasmids, but no nucleus or mitochondria.
- Slides: thin specimen, a stain (iodine or methylene blue), coverslip lowered at an angle, low power before high power.
:::
