## Introduction

Some of the carbon atoms in your body may once have been in a breath of carbon dioxide breathed out by a cow in Masvingo, or in smoke from a cooking fire in Mutare. Every carbohydrate, fat and protein in living things contains carbon, but the Earth has a fixed amount of it. Life can continue only because carbon is **recycled** over and over again between the air, living organisms, dead remains and rocks.

This journey is the **carbon cycle**. You should already know the word equations for photosynthesis and respiration, and that decomposers feed on dead material.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Carbon cycle"}
The continuous movement of carbon between the atmosphere, living organisms, dead remains and fossil fuels.
:::

:::definition{term="Decomposition"}
The breakdown of dead organisms and waste by decomposers (bacteria and fungi), which release carbon dioxide by respiration.
:::

:::definition{term="Fossil fuels"}
Coal, oil and natural gas, formed over millions of years from the remains of organisms that were not fully decomposed.
:::

:::definition{term="Combustion"}
Burning: a fuel reacts with oxygen and releases carbon dioxide and energy.
:::

## Where carbon is found

- In the **air**, as carbon dioxide: only about 0.04% of the atmosphere, but vital for photosynthesis. It also dissolves in the water of lakes and oceans.
- In **living organisms**, in carbohydrates, fats and proteins.
- In **dead remains**, dung and the humus of the soil.
- In **fossil fuels**: coal, oil and natural gas, stored underground for millions of years.

## The processes of the carbon cycle

| Process | Carbon moves from | Carbon moves to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photosynthesis | Carbon dioxide in the air | Glucose and other organic compounds in producers |
| Feeding | Organic compounds in plants (or animals) | Organic compounds in the animals that eat them |
| Respiration (plants, animals and decomposers) | Organic compounds, such as glucose | Carbon dioxide in the air |
| Death and decomposition | Dead organisms and waste | Carbon dioxide, released by the decomposers' respiration |
| Fossilisation | Remains that are not decomposed, buried for millions of years | Coal, oil and natural gas |
| Combustion | Fossil fuels and wood | Carbon dioxide in the air |

Of the processes in the table, **photosynthesis** is the only one that removes carbon dioxide from the air (some carbon dioxide also dissolves in lakes and oceans). **Respiration**, **decomposition** (through the respiration of decomposers) and **combustion** all return it.

![Carbon cycle: plants take carbon dioxide from the air by photosynthesis, animals get carbon by feeding, respiration by plants, animals and decomposers returns carbon dioxide to the air, dead remains are broken down by decomposers or fossilised into fossil fuels, and combustion of fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide.](media:diagrams/carbon-cycle.png "Figure: the carbon cycle, showing photosynthesis, feeding, respiration, decomposition, fossilisation and combustion")

Fossil fuels form when dead organisms are buried in places where decomposition cannot happen fully, for example because there is no oxygen, as in waterlogged swamps. Over millions of years, heat and pressure turn the remains into coal, oil or gas. The coal mined at Hwange formed in this way from swamp plants that lived hundreds of millions of years ago.

:::example{title="Example 1: following one carbon atom"}
1. A carbon atom in a molecule of carbon dioxide diffuses into a maize leaf.
2. By photosynthesis it becomes part of a glucose molecule, which is converted to starch and stored in a maize grain.
3. The grain is ground into mealie-meal and cooked into sadza. A person eats it, and the starch is digested to glucose and absorbed into the blood.
4. In a muscle cell, the glucose is used in aerobic respiration, and the carbon atom leaves the body as carbon dioxide in the person's breath.
5. Back in the air, it may enter another leaf, and the cycle begins again.
:::

## Carbon dioxide over a day

In a field of growing maize, the concentration of carbon dioxide in the air between the plants changes from hour to hour.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: reading carbon dioxide data]
Students measured the carbon dioxide concentration among maize plants: 460 parts per million (ppm) at midnight, 470 ppm at 06:00, 380 ppm at 12:00 and 410 ppm at 18:00.
1. Highest at 06:00: all night the plants, animals and soil organisms respired, adding carbon dioxide, and no photosynthesis removed it.
2. Lowest at 12:00: in bright light the maize photosynthesised faster than all the organisms respired, so it removed carbon dioxide.
3. Percentage fall from 06:00 to 12:00 $= \dfrac{470 - 380}{470} \times 100 = 19.1\%$.
4. Respiration continues all the time; photosynthesis only in the light.
:::

## How humans change the carbon cycle

Human activities are adding carbon dioxide to the air faster than photosynthesis removes it:

- **Burning fossil fuels.** Coal-fired power stations, such as the one at Hwange, and vehicles burning petrol and diesel release carbon that was locked away for millions of years.
- **Deforestation.** Woodland is cut for farmland, firewood and the curing of tobacco. Fewer trees remain to remove carbon dioxide by photosynthesis, and burning or rotting the wood releases the carbon stored in it.
- **Veld fires.** Burning grassland and woodland releases carbon dioxide quickly, and damaged trees photosynthesise less.

:::example{title="Example 3: firewood from a woodlot or coal from a mine?"}
When eucalyptus from a farm woodlot is burned to cure tobacco, the carbon dioxide released was taken from the air by the trees as they grew only a few years earlier. If new trees are planted to replace them, they take it back again. When coal is burned, the carbon released had been out of the cycle for millions of years, so it adds **extra** carbon dioxide to the air. Burning indigenous woodland without replanting is worse still: the carbon is released, and the trees that would have absorbed carbon dioxide are gone.
:::

Rising carbon dioxide concentration in the air is the main cause of the **enhanced greenhouse effect**, which you will study later.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking plants only photosynthesise"}
Plants **respire all the time**, day and night. In the light they usually photosynthesise faster than they respire, so overall they take in carbon dioxide.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving out the decomposers"}
Decomposers return carbon to the air through their **respiration** as they feed on dead material. Without them, carbon would stay locked in dead remains.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying fossil fuels form quickly"}
Fossil fuels take **millions of years** to form. That is why they are non-renewable and why burning them adds carbon to the air much faster than it can be removed.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying energy is recycled in the carbon cycle"}
Carbon is recycled. Energy is not: it flows through the ecosystem and is lost as heat.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Name the processes exactly: **photosynthesis**, **respiration**, **feeding** (or consumption), **decomposition**, **fossilisation** and **combustion**. "Breathing" is not the same as respiration.
:::

:::exam-tip
When a question shows a carbon cycle diagram, check the direction of each arrow: towards the air (respiration, combustion, decomposition) or away from it (photosynthesis).
:::

:::exam-tip
To explain a rise in atmospheric carbon dioxide, give a cause **and** a reason: "burning coal releases carbon dioxide" and "deforestation means less photosynthesis".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Carbon is recycled between the air (as carbon dioxide), living organisms, dead remains and fossil fuels.
- Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the air; respiration, decomposition and combustion return it.
- Feeding passes carbon compounds along food chains; decomposers respire the carbon in dead material back to the air.
- Remains that are not decomposed can form fossil fuels over millions of years.
- Burning fossil fuels, deforestation and veld fires increase the carbon dioxide concentration of the air.
:::
