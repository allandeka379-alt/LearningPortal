## Introduction

Every day your kidneys filter about 180 dm³ of liquid out of your blood, more than the whole volume of your blood many times over, yet you produce only about 1.5 dm³ of urine. The kidneys keep almost everything useful and remove only the wastes and the excess.

This lesson explains how the body gets rid of the waste products of its chemical reactions. You should already know how proteins are digested to amino acids and how the blood transports substances.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Excretion"}
The removal from the body of the waste products of metabolism (the chemical reactions in cells), of toxic substances and of substances in excess of requirements.
:::

:::definition{term="Urea"}
A nitrogen-containing waste made in the liver from excess amino acids and removed by the kidneys in urine.
:::

:::definition{term="Deamination (Supplement)"}
The removal of the nitrogen-containing part of amino acids, forming urea.
:::

:::definition{term="Osmoregulation (ZIMSEC)"}
Keeping the water content (and so the concentration) of the blood constant.
:::

Remember that **egestion** (passing out undigested food as faeces) is not excretion: undigested food was never part of the body's cells.

## Excretory organs

| Organ | What it excretes |
| --- | --- |
| Lungs | Carbon dioxide (from respiration) |
| Kidneys | Urea, excess water and excess ions, in urine |
| Skin (ZIMSEC) | Water, salts and a little urea, in sweat |
| Liver | Makes urea from excess amino acids; also removes some wastes in bile |

## The urinary system

Labelled parts, in the order urine passes through them:

- **Kidneys**: two bean-shaped organs at the back of the abdomen. Blood containing urea arrives in the **renal artery**; filtered blood leaves in the **renal vein**.
- **Ureters**: one from each kidney, carrying urine to the bladder.
- **Bladder**: a muscular bag that stores urine.
- **Urethra**: carries urine from the bladder out of the body.

![The urinary system: two kidneys, each receiving blood with urea through a renal artery and returning filtered blood by a renal vein, a ureter from each kidney carrying urine to the bladder, and the urethra carrying urine from the bladder out of the body.](media:diagrams/urinary-system.png "Figure: the urinary system with the blood vessels of the kidneys")

A kidney cut lengthways shows an outer **cortex**, an inner **medulla** and (ZIMSEC) the **pelvis**, the funnel-shaped space where urine collects before it enters the ureter.

## How the kidneys make urine (Supplement)

Each kidney contains about a million tiny tubules called **nephrons**, which start in the cortex and loop into the medulla.

1. **Filtration.** Blood enters a knot of capillaries, the **glomerulus**, at high pressure. Water, glucose, urea and mineral ions are forced out of the blood into the cup-shaped capsule around it. Blood cells and large proteins are too big and stay in the blood. The liquid that passes through is the **filtrate**.
2. **Reabsorption.** As the filtrate flows along the tubule, **all of the glucose**, **some of the ions** and **most of the water** are taken back into the blood capillaries that surround the tubule.
3. **Urine.** What is left (urea, excess water and excess ions) flows into a collecting duct, then to the pelvis, the ureter and the bladder.

![A nephron: filtration at the glomerulus forces water, glucose, urea and ions out of the blood into the capsule while blood cells and protein stay in the blood; reabsorption along the tubule returns all the glucose, most of the water and some ions to the blood capillaries; urea, excess water and ions continue down the collecting duct to the pelvis as urine.](media:diagrams/nephron-structure.png "Figure: a nephron showing filtration, reabsorption and the formation of urine")

:::worked-solution[Example 1: how much water is reabsorbed?]
A person's kidneys produce 180 dm³ of filtrate a day but only 1.5 dm³ of urine. What percentage of the filtrate is reabsorbed?
1. Volume reabsorbed $= 180 - 1.5 = 178.5\ \text{dm}^3$.
2. Percentage $= \dfrac{178.5}{180} \times 100 = 99.2\%$.
3. Almost all the filtrate returns to the blood; without reabsorption we would lose our body's water in minutes.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: reading a kidney data table]
Typical concentrations (g per dm³) in plasma, filtrate and urine are: glucose 1.0, 1.0 and 0; protein 80, 0 and 0; urea 0.3, 0.3 and 20.
1. **Protein:** in plasma but not in filtrate, because protein molecules are too large to be filtered.
2. **Glucose:** filtered (same in plasma and filtrate) but absent from urine, because all of it is reabsorbed.
3. **Urea:** filtered, then concentrated as water is reabsorbed: $\dfrac{20}{0.3} = 67$, so urine has about 67 times the concentration in plasma.
:::

## The liver and urea (Supplement)

Amino acids absorbed from the small intestine are carried to the liver. The liver **assimilates** many of them into proteins, including the plasma proteins of the blood. The body cannot store excess amino acids, so the liver **deaminates** them: it removes the nitrogen-containing part, which is turned into **urea**. The rest of each molecule can be respired or stored as glycogen or fat.

Urea dissolves in the blood plasma and is carried to the kidneys. It must be excreted because it is **toxic**: if its concentration in the blood rose, it would damage cells.

:::keyfact{title="Urea is made in the liver, not the kidneys"}
The liver makes urea; the kidneys only remove it from the blood.
:::

## Water, pH and the skin (ZIMSEC)

**Osmoregulation.** The kidneys keep the water content of the blood constant by changing how much water they reabsorb. After drinking a lot, little water is reabsorbed, so you produce a **large volume of dilute urine**. On a hot day in the Zambezi Valley, when you lose a lot of water in sweat, more water is reabsorbed and you produce a **small volume of concentrated, dark urine**. A hormone from the pituitary gland, ADH, controls how much water is reabsorbed.

**pH regulation.** The kidneys remove excess hydrogen ions (acid) from the blood in urine, helping to keep the blood's pH constant.

**The skin.** Sweat glands excrete water, salts and a little urea, although the main job of sweating is to cool the body.

## Kidney failure and dialysis (ZIMSEC)

If both kidneys fail, urea, excess water and excess ions build up in the blood, which is fatal unless treated. There are two treatments: a **kidney transplant** or regular **dialysis**.

In a **dialysis machine**:

1. Blood from a vein in the patient's arm flows through tubing made of a **partially permeable membrane**.
2. The tubing is surrounded by **dialysis fluid**, which contains glucose and ions at the **same concentrations as normal blood plasma**, but **no urea**.
3. **Urea diffuses** out of the blood into the fluid, down its concentration gradient. Excess ions also diffuse out until their concentrations match normal levels.
4. **Glucose does not diffuse out**, because its concentration is the same on both sides. Blood cells and proteins are too large to cross the membrane.
5. The fluid flows in the **opposite direction** to the blood and is constantly replaced, keeping the concentration gradient for urea steep.
6. The cleaned blood returns to the patient's vein. A session lasts several hours and is repeated about three times a week.

A transplant lets the patient live a normal life without dialysis, but suitable donors are scarce and the patient must take drugs for life to stop the new kidney being rejected.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the ureter and the urethra"}
Ureters run from the kidneys to the bladder; the urethra runs from the bladder to the outside.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying glucose is normally found in urine"}
In a healthy person all the filtered glucose is reabsorbed. Glucose in urine is a sign of diabetes.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying blood cells and proteins are filtered"}
They are too large to pass out of the glomerulus, so they stay in the blood.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying dialysis fluid contains no glucose"}
(ZIMSEC) It contains glucose at the normal blood concentration, so the patient does not lose glucose.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Learn the three reabsorption phrases exactly: **all** the glucose, **some** of the ions, **most** of the water.
:::

:::exam-tip
In dialysis questions, explain every movement by **diffusion** and **concentration gradients**: urea leaves because there is none in the fluid; glucose stays because its concentrations are equal.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked why urea must be excreted, say it is **toxic** (it would damage cells if it built up).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Excretion removes waste products of metabolism, toxic substances and excess substances: carbon dioxide by the lungs; urea, excess water and ions by the kidneys.
- Urine passes from the kidneys along the ureters to the bladder and out through the urethra; the kidney has a cortex, a medulla and (ZIMSEC) a pelvis.
- Supplement: the glomerulus filters water, glucose, urea and ions from the blood; all the glucose, some ions and most water are reabsorbed; the rest forms urine.
- Supplement: the liver assimilates amino acids into proteins and deaminates the excess to form urea, which is toxic.
- ZIMSEC: the kidneys control water content (osmoregulation) and pH; dialysis removes urea by diffusion into fluid containing normal glucose and ions but no urea.
:::
