## Introduction

Wherever there is life on Earth, there is water. Water makes up about 60% of the mass of an adult human, and more than 90% of a lettuce leaf. The cytoplasm of every cell is mostly water, and most of the chemical reactions of life happen in it.

In this lesson you will learn what water is made of and how its properties make it so useful to living things: as a solvent for digestion, excretion and transport, as a raw material for photosynthesis, in controlling temperature and as a place to live. You need no earlier biology for this lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Solvent"}
A liquid in which other substances dissolve. Water is the solvent in all living organisms.
:::

:::definition{term="Solute"}
A substance that dissolves in a solvent, such as glucose or salt in water.
:::

:::definition{term="Solution"}
A mixture formed when a solute dissolves in a solvent.
:::

:::definition{term="Specific heat capacity"}
The amount of heat energy needed to raise the temperature of one kilogram of a substance by one degree Celsius. Water's is high: it takes a lot of energy to warm it up.
:::

## What water is made of

A water molecule ($\ce{H2O}$) is made of two hydrogen atoms joined to one oxygen atom. Pure water is a colourless, clear liquid between 0 °C and 100 °C, the range of temperatures at which most life exists.

## Water as a solvent

Many of the substances that organisms need, or need to get rid of, dissolve in water: glucose, amino acids, mineral ions, some vitamins (such as vitamin C), oxygen, carbon dioxide and urea. Because they are in solution, they can react, move and be carried around.

| Process | How water helps |
| --- | --- |
| Reactions in cells | Most chemical reactions take place in solution in the cytoplasm |
| Digestion | Digestive juices are mostly water; enzymes act on food in solution, and the small, soluble products dissolve so they can be absorbed |
| Transport in animals | Blood plasma carries dissolved glucose, amino acids, mineral ions, hormones, urea and carbon dioxide |
| Transport in plants | Xylem carries water with dissolved mineral ions; phloem carries dissolved sucrose and amino acids |
| Excretion | Urea and excess salts are removed from the body dissolved in water, as urine |

:::keyfact
Blood cells are not dissolved in plasma: they are carried along **suspended** in it. Fats and oils do not dissolve in water at all.
:::

## Other properties of water and their roles

| Property | Role in living organisms |
| --- | --- |
| Transparent: light passes through it | Water plants and algae can photosynthesise below the surface of a lake; light passes through the watery parts of the eye to the retina |
| High specific heat capacity: it warms up and cools down slowly | Lakes and rivers keep a steady temperature, giving a stable habitat; organisms, which are mostly water, are protected from sudden temperature changes |
| Takes in a lot of heat when it evaporates | Evaporation of sweat cools the skin; evaporation from leaves (transpiration) cools a plant |
| Raw material for photosynthesis | Plants combine water with carbon dioxide to make glucose |
| Liquid in which organisms can live and move | A habitat for fish, hippos, crocodiles, frogs and water plants; water also helps to support their bodies |

:::example{title="A hot day beside a lake"}
On a sunny day the air beside a large lake may warm up by more than ten degrees between dawn and early afternoon, while the lake water warms by only a degree or two. Because water has a high specific heat capacity, a great deal of energy is needed to raise its temperature. Fish and other aquatic organisms therefore live in conditions that change only slowly, so their enzymes keep working steadily.
:::

:::example{title="Digesting a meal of sadza"}
Sadza is mostly starch, which does not dissolve. Saliva, which is mostly water, wets the food, and the amylase dissolved in it starts to digest the starch. Further down the gut, enzymes in watery digestive juices break the food down into small, soluble molecules such as glucose. These dissolve, pass through the wall of the small intestine and are carried away dissolved in the blood plasma.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying red blood cells are dissolved in plasma"}
Cells cannot dissolve. Red and white blood cells are suspended in the plasma and carried along by it.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying water heats up quickly"}
It is the other way round: water has a high specific heat capacity, so its temperature changes slowly.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting that water is a reactant"}
Water is not only a solvent. It is also used up in reactions such as photosynthesis and digestion.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When you explain a role of water as a solvent, name the dissolved substance and where it goes: "glucose is carried dissolved in the plasma from the small intestine to the liver".
:::

:::exam-tip
Link each property to a role with "so": "water is transparent, so light reaches plants growing under water".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A water molecule is made of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom; most of every cell is water.
- Water is a solvent: reactions, digestion, transport in plasma, xylem and phloem, and excretion of urea all depend on substances dissolved in water.
- Water is transparent, so aquatic plants can photosynthesise below the surface.
- Its high specific heat capacity keeps the temperature of water bodies and organisms steady; evaporation of water cools organisms.
- Water is a raw material for photosynthesis and a habitat for many organisms.
:::
