## Introduction

A plate of sadza with beans and covo contains three groups of large molecules that every organism needs: **carbohydrates**, **proteins** and **lipids** (fats and oils). Living things build these large molecules from small units, break them down again when they need energy or raw materials, and rebuild them into their own tissues.

In this lesson you will learn which elements each group contains, which small units they are made from, and what they do. ZIMSEC candidates also need to classify carbohydrates and describe the functions of all three groups. You should already know that living things are made of cells and that cells carry out chemical reactions.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Carbohydrate"}
A compound of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, usually with twice as many hydrogen atoms as oxygen atoms, such as glucose, $\ce{C6H12O6}$. Sugars, starch, glycogen and cellulose are carbohydrates.
:::

:::definition{term="Protein"}
A large molecule made of one or more chains of amino acids. Proteins contain carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen, and sometimes sulfur.
:::

:::definition{term="Lipid"}
Fats (solid at room temperature) and oils (liquid at room temperature). They are made from fatty acids and glycerol and contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, with much less oxygen than carbohydrates.
:::

## The elements

| Group | Elements |
| --- | --- |
| Carbohydrates | Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen |
| Lipids (fats and oils) | Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen |
| Proteins | Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen (sometimes sulfur) |
| Nucleic acids (ZIMSEC) | Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus |

:::keyfact
Nitrogen is the element that proteins contain but carbohydrates and lipids do not. Plants get it from nitrate ions in the soil, which is why nitrogen fertiliser helps crops to make proteins and grow.
:::

## Large molecules from small units

| Large molecule | Built from | Found in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starch | Many glucose molecules | Food store in plants: maize grains, potatoes, cassava |
| Glycogen | Many glucose molecules | Food store in animals (liver and muscles) and fungi |
| Cellulose | Many glucose molecules | Plant cell walls |
| Proteins | Amino acids (about 20 kinds) | Every cell: enzymes, muscle, hair, antibodies |
| Fats and oils | Fatty acids and glycerol | Fat under the skin, seeds such as groundnuts, cooking oil |

Think of starch as a long chain of identical beads (glucose units), sometimes with branches. A protein is a chain made from about 20 different kinds of bead (amino acids); the order of the beads decides which protein it is. A fat molecule is one glycerol with three fatty acids attached.

:::example{title="What happens to sadza and beans"}
In digestion, the starch in sadza is broken down into glucose and the proteins in beans are broken down into amino acids. These small, soluble molecules are absorbed into the blood. Body cells then use them: glucose is respired for energy or joined into glycogen in the liver and muscles, and amino acids are joined in new orders to make the body's own proteins, such as enzymes and muscle proteins.
:::

## Classifying carbohydrates (ZIMSEC)

| Type | Meaning | Examples | Properties |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monosaccharides | Single sugar units | Glucose, fructose, galactose | Sweet, soluble in water; all are reducing sugars |
| Disaccharides | Two sugar units joined | Maltose (glucose + glucose), sucrose (glucose + fructose), lactose (glucose + galactose) | Sweet, soluble; maltose and lactose are reducing sugars, sucrose is not |
| Polysaccharides | Many sugar units joined | Starch, glycogen, cellulose | Not sweet, insoluble |

Being insoluble makes polysaccharides good for storage: they do not dissolve and leak out of cells, and they do not affect the movement of water by osmosis.

## Functions in organisms (ZIMSEC)

| Group | Functions | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carbohydrates | Main source of energy in respiration; energy storage; structure | Glucose respired in cells; starch in maize grains; glycogen in liver; cellulose in cell walls (and fibre in the diet) |
| Proteins | Growth and repair (making new cells and tissues); enzymes; structure; transport; defence; some hormones | Muscle proteins; amylase; keratin in hair and nails; collagen in skin; haemoglobin; antibodies; insulin |
| Lipids | Energy store (about twice the energy per gram of carbohydrate); insulation; protection; cell membranes; waterproofing | Fat under the skin keeps heat in; fat around the kidneys cushions them; waxy cuticle on leaves |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying fats are made of glucose"}
Fats and oils are made from fatty acids and glycerol. Starch, glycogen and cellulose are the molecules made of glucose.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving nitrogen out of proteins"}
Proteins contain carbon, hydrogen, oxygen **and nitrogen** (and sometimes sulfur). Writing only C, H and O loses the mark.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting glycogen in plants"}
Plants store starch; animals and fungi store glycogen. Both are made of glucose.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking humans get energy from cellulose"}
Humans cannot digest cellulose, so it passes through the gut as fibre. It helps food move through the intestines but supplies no energy.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"State the elements in a protein": give all four, carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen. Mentioning that some proteins also contain sulfur is a bonus, not a substitute.
:::

:::exam-tip
Learn the pairs as a set: starch, glycogen and cellulose from glucose; proteins from amino acids; fats and oils from fatty acids and glycerol.
:::

:::exam-tip
When you describe a function, add an example: "proteins act as enzymes, for example amylase".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Carbohydrates and lipids contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen; proteins also contain nitrogen and sometimes sulfur.
- Starch, glycogen and cellulose are made from glucose; proteins from amino acids; fats and oils from fatty acids and glycerol.
- ZIMSEC: monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose) and disaccharides (maltose, sucrose, lactose) are sweet and soluble; polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose) are insoluble.
- Carbohydrates supply and store energy and form cell walls; proteins are used for growth, enzymes, structure, transport and antibodies.
- Lipids store about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrates and are used for insulation, protection, membranes and waterproofing.
:::
