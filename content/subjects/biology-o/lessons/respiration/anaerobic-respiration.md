## Introduction

At the end of a 400 m race your legs burn and you keep gasping for breath long after you have stopped running. In a kitchen in Gweru, bread dough left in a warm place doubles in size. Both happen because cells are releasing energy from glucose **without enough oxygen**.

This lesson explains anaerobic respiration in yeast and in muscles, and why you go on breathing hard after exercise. You should already know the word equation for aerobic respiration and that respiration is controlled by enzymes.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Anaerobic respiration"}
The chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy without using oxygen.
:::

:::definition{term="Lactic acid"}
The product of anaerobic respiration in muscle cells.
:::

:::definition{term="Oxygen debt (Supplement; ZIMSEC)"}
The extra oxygen needed after vigorous exercise to remove the lactic acid that built up in the muscles and blood.
:::

:::definition{term="Muscle fatigue (ZIMSEC)"}
The loss of a muscle's ability to contract strongly, caused partly by a build-up of lactic acid.
:::

## Less energy per glucose molecule

Anaerobic respiration releases **much less energy** per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration. Without oxygen, glucose is only **partly** broken down: the products (alcohol or lactic acid) still contain a lot of chemical energy. Alcohol, for example, can be burned as a fuel.

## Anaerobic respiration in yeast (and plants)

Yeast can respire aerobically when oxygen is present, and anaerobically when it is not.

:::formula{name="Anaerobic respiration in yeast: word equation"}
$$
\text{glucose} \rightarrow \text{alcohol (ethanol)} + \text{carbon dioxide}
$$
:::

:::formula{name="Anaerobic respiration in yeast: balanced equation (Supplement)"}
$$
\ce{C6H12O6 -> 2C2H5OH + 2CO2}
$$
:::

**ZIMSEC:** plant cells respire anaerobically in the same way when oxygen is short, for example in the roots of crops standing in waterlogged soil. They produce ethanol and carbon dioxide, and the small amount of energy released is not enough for the roots to absorb mineral ions well, so the crop grows poorly.

**Uses of yeast:**

- **Baking:** carbon dioxide bubbles make bread dough rise; the alcohol evaporates when the bread is baked.
- **Brewing:** the alcohol is the useful product, in beer and wine and in traditional opaque beer brewed from sorghum or maize.
- **Biofuel:** ethanol made by yeast from sugar cane can be mixed with petrol.

(Some bacteria respire anaerobically to produce lactic acid instead. That is what makes sour milk (maas) and maheu taste sour.)

:::worked-solution[Example 1: using the yeast equation (Supplement)]
A yeast culture respires 10 molecules of glucose anaerobically. How many molecules of carbon dioxide and of ethanol are produced?
1. From the equation, 1 glucose gives 2 ethanol and 2 carbon dioxide.
2. Carbon dioxide: $10 \times 2 = 20$ molecules.
3. Ethanol: $10 \times 2 = 20$ molecules.
:::

## Anaerobic respiration in muscles

During vigorous exercise the heart and lungs cannot deliver oxygen to the muscles fast enough for all the energy to come from aerobic respiration. The muscle cells then also respire anaerobically:

:::formula{name="Anaerobic respiration in muscles: word equation"}
$$
\text{glucose} \rightarrow \text{lactic acid}
$$
:::

No carbon dioxide is produced. **Lactic acid** builds up in the muscles and the blood. **(ZIMSEC)** It contributes to **muscle fatigue**: the muscles ache, may cramp and cannot keep contracting strongly.

### The oxygen debt (Supplement; ZIMSEC)

After the exercise, the lactic acid must be removed, and this needs extra oxygen: the **oxygen debt**. It is repaid in three ways:

1. The **heart rate stays high**, carrying lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the **liver**.
2. **Breathing stays faster and deeper**, supplying the extra oxygen.
3. In the liver, the lactic acid is broken down by **aerobic respiration** (the syllabus describes it this way).

:::worked-solution[Example 2: measuring an oxygen debt]
After a race, Chipo's oxygen uptake was above her resting rate for five minutes. The extra oxygen she took in during each minute was 1.6, 0.9, 0.5, 0.2 and 0.0 dm³. What was the size of her oxygen debt?
1. Add the extra oxygen for each minute: $1.6 + 0.9 + 0.5 + 0.2 + 0.0$.
2. Oxygen debt $= 3.2\ \text{dm}^3$.
3. The debt was repaid fastest in the first minute, then more and more slowly.
:::

## Aerobic and anaerobic respiration compared

| | Aerobic | Anaerobic (muscles) | Anaerobic (yeast) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oxygen needed? | Yes | No | No |
| Products | Carbon dioxide and water | Lactic acid | Alcohol (ethanol) and carbon dioxide |
| Energy per glucose | A relatively large amount | Much less | Much less |
| Glucose broken down | Completely | Partly | Partly |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Giving muscles carbon dioxide as a product"}
Anaerobic respiration in human muscles produces **lactic acid only**, no carbon dioxide.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving yeast lactic acid as a product"}
Yeast produces **alcohol (ethanol) and carbon dioxide**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying anaerobic respiration releases no energy"}
It releases **much less** energy per glucose molecule, but it does release some.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying bread contains alcohol"}
The alcohol made by yeast evaporates as the bread bakes; the carbon dioxide is what makes the dough rise.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Learn the three word equations exactly and say **which organism** each one belongs to. (Supplement) Write the yeast equation with the 2s in the right places: $\ce{C6H12O6 -> 2C2H5OH + 2CO2}$.
:::

:::exam-tip
To explain the oxygen debt, give all three parts: fast heart rate (carries lactic acid to the liver), deep fast breathing (supplies oxygen), and lactic acid broken down in the liver.
:::

:::exam-tip
"Compare" questions need both sides in each point: "aerobic respiration uses oxygen, **whereas** anaerobic respiration does not".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Anaerobic respiration releases energy without oxygen, but much less per glucose molecule, because glucose is only partly broken down.
- Yeast (and plant cells): glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide; (Supplement) $\ce{C6H12O6 -> 2C2H5OH + 2CO2}$.
- Muscles during vigorous exercise: glucose → lactic acid.
- Lactic acid builds up in muscles and blood, causing fatigue (ZIMSEC) and an oxygen debt, repaid by a fast heart rate, deep breathing and breakdown of lactic acid in the liver.
- Yeast's carbon dioxide makes bread rise; its alcohol is used in brewing and as a fuel.
:::
