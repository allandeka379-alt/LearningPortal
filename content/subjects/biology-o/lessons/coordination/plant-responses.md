## Introduction

Put a tray of bean seedlings on a classroom windowsill in Gweru and within a day or two every shoot leans towards the window. Plant maize grains upside down and the roots still curve round to grow downwards. Plants cannot walk towards what they need, but they can **grow** towards it. These slow, directional growth responses are called **tropisms**, and they are controlled by chemicals called **plant hormones**.

In this lesson you will learn the two main tropisms, how to investigate them, and how the hormone **auxin** causes a shoot to bend. ZIMSEC candidates also learn about other plant hormones and how farmers and fruit sellers use them.

:::note{title="Syllabus"}
The explanation of tropisms using **auxin** is Cambridge **Supplement** content and is part of the ZIMSEC syllabus. The sections on **gibberellins, cytokinins, ethylene and commercial uses** are for ZIMSEC candidates. ZIMSEC uses the older name **geotropism** for gravitropism.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Tropism"}
A growth response of part of a plant to a stimulus that comes from one direction. Growth **towards** the stimulus is positive; growth **away** from it is negative.
:::

:::definition{term="Phototropism"}
A growth response in which parts of a plant grow towards or away from the direction from which light is coming.
:::

:::definition{term="Gravitropism (geotropism)"}
A growth response in which parts of a plant grow towards or away from gravity.
:::

:::definition{term="Plant hormone"}
A chemical made in small amounts in one part of a plant and carried to other parts, where it controls growth and development. Auxin is one example.
:::

## The two main tropisms

| Part | Response to light | Response to gravity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shoot | Grows towards light: **positively phototropic** | Grows upwards, away from gravity: **negatively gravitropic** |
| Root | In many plants grows away from light: negatively phototropic | Grows downwards: **positively gravitropic** |

These responses help the plant survive. A shoot growing towards light places its leaves where they can absorb more light for **photosynthesis**. A root growing down into the soil **anchors** the plant and reaches **water and mineral ions**.

## Investigating tropisms

:::example{title="Example 1: phototropism in a box with a slit"}
Three pots of young maize seedlings are set up: pot A inside a box with a slit on one side (light from one direction), pot B in a box with no slit (darkness) and pot C on a slowly rotating turntable in the open, so that light reaches all sides equally (control). After three days the seedlings in A have bent towards the slit, those in C have grown straight up, and those in B are tall, thin and pale, with no bending. Light from **one side** causes the bending.
:::

:::example{title="Example 2: which part detects the light?"}
Four young shoots (coleoptiles) are lit from one side:

1. Untreated shoot: bends towards the light.
2. Tip cut off: does not bend, and grows very little.
3. Tip covered with a foil cap: grows straight up, without bending.
4. Foil collar just below the tip, tip uncovered: bends towards the light.

**Conclusion:** the **tip** detects the light and produces the substance that controls growth; the bending happens in the region just **below** the tip.
:::

:::example{title="Example 3: gravitropism and the clinostat"}
Germinating bean seeds are pinned to a cork disc with their roots pointing sideways, kept moist and placed in the dark (so that light has no effect). One disc is fixed to a **clinostat**, which rotates slowly so that gravity acts on all sides of the roots in turn. A second, stationary disc is the control. After two days the roots on the stationary disc have curved **downwards** and the shoots upwards; the roots on the rotating clinostat have grown straight out sideways. A clinostat does not remove gravity; it stops gravity acting from one direction only.
:::

## How auxin makes a shoot bend (Cambridge Supplement)

1. **Auxin** is made in the shoot tip.
2. It **diffuses** from the tip down into the region of the shoot just behind it.
3. There it stimulates **cell elongation**: the cells take up water and grow longer.
4. If auxin is spread **unequally**, one side of the shoot grows faster than the other, so the shoot bends.

:::worked-solution[Explaining phototropism with auxin]
1. Light shines on the shoot from the left.
2. Auxin moves to, and accumulates on, the shaded (right-hand) side of the shoot.
3. Cells on the shaded side elongate more than cells on the lit side.
4. The shaded side becomes longer, so the shoot bends towards the light.
:::

:::worked-solution[Explaining negative gravitropism of a shoot with auxin]
1. A seedling is laid on its side, so its shoot is horizontal.
2. Gravity causes auxin to accumulate on the **lower** side of the shoot.
3. Cells on the lower side elongate more than those on the upper side.
4. The shoot curves upwards, away from gravity.
:::

## Other plant hormones and their uses (ZIMSEC)

| Hormone | Main effects | Commercial uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Auxins | Cell elongation; tropisms; root formation; fruit development | **Rooting powder** helps stem cuttings form roots; synthetic auxins are **selective weedkillers**; seedless fruit |
| Gibberellins | Stem elongation; seed germination | **Larger, seedless grapes**; speeding up germination of barley for malting |
| Cytokinins | Cell division; delay ageing of leaves | **Tissue culture**: with auxin, make many small plants grow from a few cells; keeping leafy vegetables and cut flowers fresh |
| Ethylene (ethene) | A gas that ripens fruit; causes leaves and fruit to fall | **Ripening** fruit such as bananas at the right time for sale |

:::example{title="Example 4: a selective weedkiller in a maize field"}
A synthetic auxin such as 2,4-D is sprayed on a maize field. Broad-leaved weeds absorb it and grow so fast and abnormally that they twist and die. Maize, a grass with narrow upright leaves, is much less sensitive at the recommended dose, so the crop survives while competition from the weeds is removed.
:::

:::example{title="Example 5: ripening bananas"}
Bananas are harvested while green and hard, so they are not damaged in transport. At the market town they are kept in a closed ripening room with a little **ethylene** gas, and they ripen evenly within a few days. At home, putting a ripe banana in a bag with hard avocados speeds up their ripening, because the ripe banana gives off ethylene.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="The plant moves towards light"}
A tropism is a **growth** response. The shoot bends because one side grows faster than the other, not because it moves like an animal.
:::

:::mistake{title="Light destroys auxin"}
At this level, explain phototropism by auxin **accumulating on the shaded side**, which then elongates more.
:::

:::mistake{title="The tip bends"}
The tip **detects** the light and makes auxin, but the bending happens in the region of elongation **below** the tip.
:::

:::mistake{title="A clinostat removes gravity"}
Nothing on Earth removes gravity. The clinostat rotates the seedling so that gravity acts **equally on all sides**.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Name the response fully: "the shoot is **positively phototropic**", "the root is **positively gravitropic**".
:::

:::exam-tip
For the auxin explanation, examiners look for four steps: made in the **tip**, moves to the **shaded / lower side**, stimulates **cell elongation** there, so the shoot **bends**.
:::

:::exam-tip
In experiments, identify the control (uniform light, a rotating turntable or a stationary clinostat) and say why roots in gravitropism experiments are kept in the dark.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Tropisms are growth responses to a directional stimulus: shoots are positively phototropic and negatively gravitropic; roots are positively gravitropic.
- Tropisms put leaves in the light and roots in the soil, near water and mineral ions.
- The shoot tip detects light; foil caps, tip removal and clinostats are used to investigate tropisms.
- Supplement: auxin made in the shoot tip diffuses back, collects on the shaded or lower side and stimulates cell elongation there, so the shoot bends.
- ZIMSEC: auxins (rooting powder, weedkillers), gibberellins (larger seedless grapes, germination), cytokinins (tissue culture, cell division) and ethylene (fruit ripening).
:::
