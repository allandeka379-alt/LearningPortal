## Introduction

Walk out of a dim classroom into the midday sun in Bulawayo and you squint for a moment, then you can see comfortably again. Look up from your notes to read the board and the words are sharp almost at once. Your eyes are adjusting all the time, without you deciding anything.

The eye is a **sense organ**: a group of **receptor cells** that respond to one kind of stimulus, together with the structures that help them work. Other sense organs respond to other stimuli: the ears to sound, the skin to touch, pressure and temperature, and the tongue and nose to chemicals.

You should already know what a receptor and a reflex are (see the lesson on the nervous system).

:::note{title="Syllabus"}
Both boards: the parts of the eye, their functions and the pupil reflex. **Cambridge Supplement**: the iris muscles, accommodation, rods and cones and the fovea; ZIMSEC candidates also need accommodation to explain how images are focused. **ZIMSEC**: short sight and long sight and how they are corrected.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Sense organ"}
A group of receptor cells that respond to a specific stimulus, such as light, sound, touch, temperature or chemicals.
:::

:::definition{term="Refraction"}
The bending of light as it passes from one transparent substance into another. The eye refracts light so that it comes to a focus on the retina.
:::

:::definition{term="Pupil reflex"}
The automatic change in the size of the pupil in response to the intensity of light: smaller in bright light, larger in dim light.
:::

:::definition{term="Accommodation"}
The change in the shape of the lens that keeps light from near and distant objects focused on the retina.
:::

## The parts of the eye

Light passes through the cornea, the aqueous humour, the pupil, the lens and the vitreous humour before it reaches the retina.

| Part | What it is | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cornea | Transparent, curved front of the eye | Refracts light entering the eye; it does most of the bending |
| Iris | Coloured ring of muscle | Controls the size of the pupil, and so how much light enters |
| Pupil | Hole in the centre of the iris | Lets light into the eye |
| Lens | Transparent, flexible and curved on both sides | Changes shape to focus light accurately on the retina |
| Ciliary muscles | Ring of muscle around the lens | Change the shape of the lens |
| Suspensory ligaments | Fibres joining the lens to the ciliary muscles | Hold the lens in place |
| Retina | Light-sensitive layer at the back | Contains rods and cones, which turn light into nerve impulses |
| Fovea | Small area of the retina, opposite the lens | Packed with cones: gives the sharpest, most detailed colour vision |
| Blind spot | Where the optic nerve leaves the retina | Has no rods or cones, so no image is detected there |
| Optic nerve | Bundle of sensory neurones | Carries impulses from the retina to the brain |
| Sclera | Tough white outer coat | Protects the eye and keeps its shape |
| Choroid | Dark layer with blood vessels behind the retina | Supplies the retina; its dark pigment stops light reflecting inside the eye |
| Aqueous and vitreous humour | Clear watery fluid in front of the lens; clear jelly behind it | Keep the eyeball in shape and let light through |

## How an image is formed

The **cornea** does most of the refraction and the **lens** does the fine focusing, so that light from each point of an object meets at one point on the retina. The image formed is **inverted** (upside down) and **smaller** than the object. The retina sends impulses along the optic nerve and the **brain** interprets them, so you see the object the right way up.

:::example{title="Example 1: a tree on the skyline"}
Light from the top of a distant msasa tree is refracted by the cornea and lens and lands near the **bottom** of the retina; light from the base of the trunk lands near the top. The image on the retina is upside down and much smaller than the tree, but the brain interprets it correctly.
:::

## The pupil reflex

In **bright light** the pupil becomes **smaller** (constricts), so less light enters and the retina is protected from damage. In **dim light** the pupil becomes **larger** (dilates), so more light enters and you can still see. This is a reflex: the receptors are in the retina and the effectors are the muscles of the iris.

### The iris muscles (Cambridge Supplement)

The iris contains two sets of muscles that work **antagonistically**: when one set contracts, the other relaxes.

| Light | Circular muscles | Radial muscles | Pupil |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bright | Contract | Relax | Constricts (smaller) |
| Dim | Relax | Contract | Dilates (larger) |

:::example{title="Example 2: into a dark room"}
Tatenda walks from bright sunshine into a dark storeroom. At first he can hardly see. Within seconds his **radial muscles contract** and his **circular muscles relax**, so his pupils **dilate**. More light reaches his retina and the room comes into view.
:::

## Accommodation: focusing near and far

Light from a **distant** object arrives almost parallel and needs only a little refraction. Light from a **near** object is spreading out (diverging) and needs more refraction. The lens changes shape to provide it.

| Object | Ciliary muscles | Suspensory ligaments | Lens | Refraction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Near | Contract | Slacken (loose) | Becomes fatter, more curved | More |
| Distant | Relax | Pulled taut (tight) | Pulled thinner, less curved | Less |

:::example{title="Example 3: from book to board"}
Farai reads her textbook, then looks up at the board at the far end of the classroom.

1. While she reads, her ciliary muscles are **contracted**, the suspensory ligaments are **slack** and her elastic lens is **fat**, refracting the diverging light strongly.
2. When she looks up, her ciliary muscles **relax**.
3. The suspensory ligaments are pulled **taut** and pull the lens **thinner**.
4. The thinner lens refracts the light less, and the board is focused on her retina.
:::

## Rods, cones and the fovea (Cambridge Supplement)

| Feature | Rods | Cones |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light needed | Work in dim light (very sensitive) | Need bright light |
| Vision | Black and white only | Colour: three types respond to different colours of light (red, green and blue) |
| Where | Throughout the retina, more towards the edges; none at the fovea | Concentrated at the fovea |

When you look straight at something, its image falls on the **fovea**, where cones are closely packed. That is why you see the most detail there.

:::example{title="Example 4: colours at night"}
On a moonlit night in a village with no streetlights, you can see shapes but hardly any colours: only the rods are working. A faint star is easier to see slightly out of the corner of your eye, because its light then falls on the edge of the retina, where the sensitive rods are.
:::

## Short sight and long sight (ZIMSEC)

| Defect | What the person sees | Cause | Where light focuses | Correcting lens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short sight | Near objects clearly; distant objects blurred | Eyeball too long, or lens too strongly curved | In front of the retina | **Diverging (concave)**: spreads the light out a little first |
| Long sight | Distant objects clearly; near objects blurred | Eyeball too short, or lens not curved enough | Behind the retina | **Converging (convex)**: bends the light inwards first |

:::example{title="Example 5: two eye tests"}
Chipo can read her notes easily but cannot read the board from the back of the class: she is **short-sighted** and is given **concave** lenses. Her grandfather can read a bus sign across the road but holds the newspaper at arm's length: he is **long-sighted**. As people age the lens becomes less elastic and cannot become fat enough for near objects, so many older people need **convex** reading glasses.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the pupil contracts"}
The pupil is a hole and cannot contract. Muscles contract; the pupil **constricts** (gets smaller) or **dilates** (gets larger).
:::

:::mistake{title="Getting the iris muscles the wrong way round"}
Bright light: **circular** muscles contract. Dim light: **radial** muscles contract. Radial muscles run outwards like the spokes of a wheel, so when they shorten they pull the pupil wider.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking the ligaments tighten for near objects"}
For a **near** object the ciliary muscles **contract**, which **slackens** the ligaments and lets the lens become fat. It feels backwards, so learn the table.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the lens does all the focusing"}
The cornea does most of the refraction; the lens makes the fine adjustments.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the lenses for short and long sight"}
Short sight: light focuses **in front of** the retina, corrected with a **diverging (concave)** lens. Long sight: **behind** the retina, corrected with a **converging (convex)** lens.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For accommodation, examiners want all three steps in order: **ciliary muscles**, **suspensory ligaments**, **lens shape**, and then the effect on **refraction**.
:::

:::exam-tip
For the pupil reflex, say what happens to the pupil **and** why: "constricts, so less light enters and the retina is not damaged".
:::

:::exam-tip
When comparing rods and cones, give matched pairs: light needed, colour or not, where they are found.
:::

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC sight-defect questions: state where the light is focused, give a cause, and name the correcting lens with what it does to the light rays.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A sense organ is a group of receptor cells responding to a specific stimulus; the eye detects light.
- The cornea and lens refract light to form an inverted, smaller image on the retina; the optic nerve carries impulses to the brain.
- Pupil reflex: bright light, pupil constricts (circular muscles contract); dim light, pupil dilates (radial muscles contract).
- Near objects: ciliary muscles contract, ligaments slacken, lens fatter. Distant objects: ciliary muscles relax, ligaments taut, lens thinner.
- Rods work in dim light (black and white); cones need bright light and give colour vision, packed at the fovea.
- ZIMSEC: short sight is corrected with a diverging (concave) lens, long sight with a converging (convex) lens.
:::
