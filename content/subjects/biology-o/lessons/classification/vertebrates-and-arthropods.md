## Introduction

The animal kingdom is huge, so it is divided into smaller groups. Two of these groups contain most of the animals you know. **Vertebrates** are animals with a backbone (vertebral column): mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians and fish. **Arthropods** have no backbone; instead they have a hard outer skeleton and jointed legs: insects, arachnids, crustaceans and myriapods.

Zimbabwe has a wonderful range of both. In a single day at Lake Kariba you might see a hippopotamus, an African fish eagle, a Nile crocodile, a frog, a tigerfish, tsetse flies, a scorpion, a freshwater crab and a giant millipede (chongololo). In this lesson you will learn the features that place each of them in its group, so that you can classify any animal from a description, a drawing or a photograph.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Vertebrate"}
An animal with a backbone (vertebral column).
:::

:::definition{term="Arthropod"}
An animal without a backbone that has a hard exoskeleton, a segmented body and jointed legs.
:::

:::definition{term="Exoskeleton"}
A hard outer covering that supports and protects the body of an arthropod.
:::

## The five vertebrate groups

| Group | Main features | Local examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mammals | Hair or fur on the skin; mammary glands that produce milk for the young; external ears (pinnae) | Elephant, baboon, bat, pangolin, human |
| Birds | Feathers; a beak with no teeth; front limbs are wings; lay eggs with hard shells | African fish eagle, guineafowl, ostrich |
| Reptiles | Dry skin covered with scales; lay eggs with tough, usually leathery shells, on land | Nile crocodile, monitor lizard, python, tortoise |
| Amphibians | Moist skin with no scales; lay eggs without shells in water; young (tadpoles) live in water | Frogs, toads |
| Fish | Wet scales; fins; gills for breathing in water | Tigerfish, kapenta, Kariba bream (catfish are also fish, although their skin has no scales) |

:::keyfact
The features that decide the group are those found **only** in that group: hair and mammary glands (mammals), feathers (birds), dry scales (reptiles), moist scale-free skin (amphibians) and wet scales with fins (fish).
:::

Mammals and birds also keep a constant body temperature, while reptiles, amphibians and fish do not. Most mammals give birth to live young that are fed on milk.

## The four arthropod groups

| Group | Body parts | Legs | Antennae | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insects | Three: head, thorax and abdomen | Three pairs (six legs) | One pair | Tsetse fly, mosquito, honeybee, termite, locust |
| Arachnids | Two: cephalothorax and abdomen | Four pairs (eight legs) | None | Spiders, scorpions, ticks |
| Crustaceans | Varies; many have a hard shell (carapace) over the front of the body | More than four pairs | Two pairs | Freshwater crab, crayfish, shrimp, woodlouse |
| Myriapods | Many similar segments | One or two pairs on most segments | One pair | Millipedes (chongololo), centipedes |

:::note{title="Wings"}
Most adult insects have two pairs of wings, but flies such as the tsetse fly and the mosquito have only one pair, and worker termites have none. Wings are therefore not a reliable way to recognise an insect: count the legs and the body parts instead.
:::

## Classifying unfamiliar animals

Work through the features in order. First decide whether the animal has a backbone or a jointed exoskeleton, then look for the features that belong to only one group.

:::example{title="A hairy spider in a garden in Harare"}
A large, hairy baboon spider has a body in two parts, eight jointed legs and no antennae. Two body parts, four pairs of legs and no antennae: it is an **arachnid**. Being hairy does not make it a mammal, because it has an exoskeleton, not a backbone.
:::

:::example{title="A bat at dusk"}
A bat flies, but it has fur, external ears, and the mother feeds her young on milk. Hair and mammary glands make it a **mammal**, not a bird. It has no feathers and no beak.
:::

:::example{title="An animal under a log"}
A small grey animal under a damp log has a hard, segmented exoskeleton, seven pairs of legs and two pairs of antennae (one pair very short). More than four pairs of legs and two pairs of antennae: it is a **crustacean**, a woodlouse, one of the few crustaceans that live on land.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling spiders insects"}
Spiders have eight legs, two body parts and no antennae: they are arachnids. Insects have six legs, three body parts and antennae.
:::

:::mistake{title="Classifying by habitat"}
A crocodile spends much of its time in water, but it has dry scales: it is a reptile, not a fish or an amphibian. A bat flies, but it is a mammal. Use body features, not where the animal lives or how it moves.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up millipedes and insects"}
Millipedes and centipedes have many segments with legs on most of them. They are myriapods, not insects.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking all scaly animals are reptiles or fish"}
Pangolins are covered with large scales, but they have hair on parts of the body and feed their young on milk: they are mammals.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When asked to classify an animal, name the group **and** quote the feature you used: "an insect, because it has three pairs of legs and three body parts".
:::

:::exam-tip
For arthropods, count three things: body parts, pairs of legs and pairs of antennae. These three counts separate all four groups.
:::

:::exam-tip
Read every feature given in the question. Examiners often include a misleading one (such as scales on a pangolin or flight in a bat) alongside the feature that decides the group.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Vertebrates have a backbone: mammals (hair, mammary glands, external ears), birds (feathers, beak, hard-shelled eggs), reptiles (dry scales, eggs with tough shells laid on land), amphibians (moist skin, eggs in water) and fish (wet scales, fins, gills).
- Arthropods have an exoskeleton, a segmented body and jointed legs.
- Insects: three body parts, three pairs of legs, one pair of antennae. Arachnids: two body parts, four pairs of legs, no antennae.
- Crustaceans: two pairs of antennae and more than four pairs of legs. Myriapods: many segments with legs and one pair of antennae.
- Classify by body features, not by habitat or the way an animal moves.
:::
