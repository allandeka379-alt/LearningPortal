## Introduction

The largest groups in classification are called **kingdoms**. The system used in both the ZIMSEC and Cambridge syllabuses has five: animals, plants, fungi, prokaryotes and protoctists. ZIMSEC also uses the Latin names Animalia, Plantae, Fungi, Prokaryotae (or Monera) and Protoctista (or Protista).

To place an organism in a kingdom, you look for **diagnostic features**: features such as whether its cells have a nucleus, what its cell walls are made of and how it feeds. For Cambridge candidates, the features of the animal and plant kingdoms are Core; the other kingdoms, the plant groups and viruses are Supplement. You should already know the parts of a cell: nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, cell wall and chloroplasts.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Kingdom"}
One of the five largest groups into which living organisms are classified.
:::

:::definition{term="Prokaryote"}
A single-celled organism whose cell has no nucleus: its DNA is a circular chromosome lying free in the cytoplasm.
:::

:::definition{term="Hypha"}
One of the thread-like filaments that make up the body of most fungi. A network of hyphae is called a mycelium.
:::

:::definition{term="Saprotrophic nutrition"}
Feeding on dead or decaying organic matter by releasing enzymes onto it and absorbing the soluble products of digestion.
:::

:::definition{term="Virus"}
A particle made of genetic material (DNA or RNA) inside a protein coat, which can reproduce only inside the living cells of a host.
:::

## Animals and plants

:::keyfact{title="Main features of animals"}
Multicellular; cells have a nucleus but **no cell wall** and no chlorophyll; they feed on organic substances made by other organisms; most move from place to place, and they usually have a nervous system to coordinate responses.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Main features of plants"}
Multicellular; cells have a nucleus, a **cellulose cell wall** and chloroplasts containing **chlorophyll**; they make their own food by photosynthesis; they store carbohydrate as starch or sucrose.
:::

## The five kingdoms

| Kingdom | Cells and body | Cell wall | Nutrition | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Animals | Many cells, each with a nucleus | None | Feed on other organisms or their products | Cattle, tigerfish, tsetse fly, humans |
| Plants | Many cells, each with a nucleus and chloroplasts | Cellulose | Photosynthesis | Maize, msasa, ferns |
| Fungi | Usually a mycelium of hyphae (yeast is single-celled); cells have nuclei | Chitin | Saprotrophic or parasitic; no chlorophyll; store glycogen | Bread mould (*Mucor*), yeast, mushrooms |
| Prokaryotes | One cell with **no nucleus**; circular DNA and plasmids | Present, not cellulose | Varied; some photosynthesise | Bacteria such as *Lactobacillus* (in yoghurt) and the bacterium that causes cholera |
| Protoctists | Mostly one cell with a nucleus; a few are multicellular | Some have one | Some photosynthesise (plant-like); some feed on other organisms (animal-like) | *Amoeba*, *Plasmodium* (the malaria parasite), *Chlorella* |

:::note{title="Why fungi are not plants"}
Fungi were once grouped with plants because they do not move about and some have cell walls. But fungi have no chlorophyll and cannot photosynthesise, their walls are made of chitin rather than cellulose, and they store glycogen rather than starch.
:::

## Classifying unfamiliar organisms

:::example{title="Green cells in pond water"}
A microscopic organism from a pond near Gweru is a **single cell** with a nucleus, a cell wall and chloroplasts. It has a nucleus, so it is not a prokaryote. It photosynthesises, but plants are multicellular, so it is not a plant. It is a **protoctist**, for example *Chlorella*.
:::

:::example{title="Fluffy growth on stale bread"}
White threads spread over a slice of old bread, with tiny black dots on upright threads. The threads are hyphae, the black dots hold spores, and the organism has no chlorophyll and digests the bread. It is a **fungus**, the bread mould *Mucor*.
:::

:::example{title="A cell without a nucleus"}
An electron micrograph shows a cell about 2 μm long with a cell wall, cytoplasm, ribosomes, a loop of DNA and some small rings of DNA (plasmids), but no nucleus and no mitochondria. It is a **prokaryote**: a bacterium.
:::

## Plant groups: ferns and flowering plants (Supplement)

**Ferns** have roots, stems and leaves called fronds, which are often finely divided and uncurl as they grow. They do not produce flowers or seeds. Instead they reproduce by **spores**, made in small brown clusters on the underside of the fronds.

**Flowering plants** reproduce sexually using flowers, and their seeds develop inside fruits. They are split into two groups:

| Feature | Monocotyledons | Dicotyledons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seed leaves (cotyledons) in the seed | One | Two |
| Leaf veins | Parallel | Branching (a network) |
| Flower parts (such as petals) | In threes or multiples of three | In fours or fives, or multiples of them |
| Examples | Maize, sorghum, rice, onions, lilies | Beans, groundnuts, sunflowers, tomatoes, msasa |

## Viruses (Supplement)

Viruses are not made of cells. Each virus particle is just **genetic material** (DNA or RNA) surrounded by a **protein coat**. It has no cytoplasm, no cell membrane, no ribosomes and no nucleus, and it is far smaller than a bacterium: most viruses are between about 20 and 300 nanometres across.

A virus can only reproduce inside the living cells of a host. It enters a cell and uses the cell's materials and enzymes to make copies of itself, which usually damages or kills the cell. Outside a host cell a virus shows none of the characteristics of life, so viruses are **not placed in any kingdom**.

Viruses cause many diseases: HIV, measles and influenza in humans, foot-and-mouth disease in cattle, and tobacco mosaic and maize streak in crops.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling fungi plants"}
Fungi have no chlorophyll, their cell walls are made of chitin, and they feed saprotrophically. They are a separate kingdom.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying bacteria have no DNA"}
Prokaryotes have no **nucleus**, but they do have DNA: a circular chromosome free in the cytoplasm, plus small rings called plasmids.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling viruses cells"}
A virus is only genetic material in a protein coat. It has no cytoplasm or membrane and cannot reproduce on its own.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting every green organism in the plant kingdom"}
Single-celled organisms with chloroplasts, such as *Chlorella*, are protoctists. Plants are multicellular.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When you classify an organism, state the kingdom **and** the feature that decides it: "a prokaryote, because the cell has no nucleus".
:::

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC accepts either set of names (Prokaryotae or Monera, Protoctista or Protista). Spell them carefully.
:::

:::exam-tip
For monocotyledons and dicotyledons, the two features you can see on the plant are the leaf veins and the number of flower parts.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The five kingdoms are animals, plants, fungi, prokaryotes (Monera) and protoctists (Protista).
- Animals have no cell walls and feed on other organisms; plants have cellulose walls and chlorophyll and photosynthesise.
- Fungi are made of hyphae (or single cells, like yeast), have chitin walls and feed saprotrophically; prokaryotes have no nucleus; protoctists are mostly single cells with a nucleus.
- Supplement: ferns reproduce by spores; flowering plants are monocotyledons (parallel veins, flower parts in threes) or dicotyledons (branching veins, flower parts in fours or fives).
- Supplement: viruses are genetic material in a protein coat, reproduce only inside host cells and belong to no kingdom.
:::
