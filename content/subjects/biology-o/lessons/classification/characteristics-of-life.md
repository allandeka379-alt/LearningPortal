## Introduction

An elephant drinking at a pan in Hwange National Park, a maize plant in a field near Chinhoyi and a single bacterium in a drop of pond water look nothing alike. Yet they all share a set of features that a lorry, a stone or a fire do not. These features, the **characteristics of living organisms**, are where biology begins.

There are seven of them. In the exam you must be able to name them, give their **precise** meanings (examiners look for particular key words) and use them to decide whether something is alive. You do not need any earlier biology for this lesson.

## Key definitions

The first letters spell **MRS GREN**, a useful memory aid.

:::definition{term="Movement"}
An action by an organism, or by part of an organism, that causes a change of position or place.
:::

:::definition{term="Respiration"}
The chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism.
:::

:::definition{term="Sensitivity"}
The ability to detect changes in the internal or external environment (stimuli) and to respond to them.
:::

:::definition{term="Growth"}
A permanent increase in size and dry mass.
:::

:::definition{term="Reproduction"}
The processes that make more of the same kind of organism.
:::

:::definition{term="Excretion"}
The removal from an organism of the waste products of metabolism (the chemical reactions in cells), toxic materials and substances in excess of requirements.
:::

:::definition{term="Nutrition"}
Taking in materials for energy, growth and development.
:::

## The characteristics in animals and plants

Plants show all seven characteristics too, although some are harder to see.

| Characteristic | In an animal | In a plant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movement | A cheetah running; the heart pumping | Leaves turning towards a window; flowers opening in the morning |
| Respiration | In every cell, all the time | In every cell, day and night |
| Sensitivity | Eyes detecting light; skin detecting heat | Shoots growing towards light; roots growing downwards |
| Growth | A calf gaining dry mass as it matures | A maize seedling making new cells and new leaves |
| Reproduction | A cow giving birth to a calf | Maize producing grains that grow into new plants |
| Excretion | Carbon dioxide breathed out; urea in urine | Oxygen from photosynthesis and carbon dioxide from respiration leaving through the leaves |
| Nutrition | A goat eating leaves | Making food by photosynthesis from carbon dioxide, water and light, and absorbing mineral ions |

:::keyfact
Plants respire all the time, day and night. In daylight they also photosynthesise, and this usually hides their respiration because they take in more carbon dioxide than they release.
:::

## The key words examiners look for

### Growth: "permanent" and "dry mass"

A dry bean soaked overnight swells and becomes heavier, but it has only taken in water, which it can lose again. You gain a kilogram of mass when you drink a litre of water, but nobody would say you have grown. The mass of water in an organism changes from hour to hour, so true growth is measured as an increase in **dry mass** (the mass left after all the water is removed) that is **permanent**.

### Respiration is not breathing

Breathing (ventilation) moves air into and out of the lungs. Respiration is a set of **chemical reactions in cells** that release energy from nutrients such as glucose. Plants and bacteria have no lungs, yet every one of their cells respires.

### Excretion is not egestion

Excretion removes substances made by the body's own metabolism, such as carbon dioxide (from respiration) and urea (from breaking down excess amino acids in the liver). Faeces are mostly undigested food that passed through the gut without ever entering the body's cells, so getting rid of them is called **egestion**, not excretion.

## Is it alive?

To decide whether something is alive, check whether it shows **all seven** characteristics. Showing one or two is not enough.

:::example{title="A car"}
A car moves, takes in fuel and gives out waste gases, so it can look a little like a living thing. But it does not grow, it cannot reproduce, and the energy from its fuel is released by burning in the engine, not by chemical reactions in cells. A car is not alive.
:::

:::example{title="A packet of bean seeds"}
Dry seeds show almost no signs of life, but they are alive. They respire very slowly (a sensitive instrument would detect them taking in oxygen), and when they are given water, warmth and oxygen they germinate, grow and eventually reproduce. Seeds are living but **dormant**.
:::

:::example{title="Crystals growing in a solution"}
Salt crystals can get bigger in a concentrated solution, but they have no cells, do not respire, do not respond to stimuli and do not reproduce. Their increase in size is not growth in the biological sense.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Writing 'breathing' for respiration"}
Respiration is the release of energy by chemical reactions in cells. Breathing is a movement of air. If you write "breathing in oxygen" as the meaning of respiration, you lose the mark.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling egestion excretion"}
Removing faeces is egestion. Excretion is the removal of waste made by metabolism, such as carbon dioxide and urea.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving out 'permanent' or 'dry mass' from growth"}
"An increase in size" is not enough. Growth is a **permanent** increase in size and **dry mass**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking plants do not move or excrete"}
Plants move parts of themselves (leaves, flowers, growing shoots and roots), and they excrete oxygen and carbon dioxide through their leaves.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"State the characteristics" needs only the seven names. "Define" or "describe the meaning" needs the precise wording, including the key words in bold in this lesson.
:::

:::exam-tip
In questions about unfamiliar objects (a robot, a fire, a virus), give a characteristic that is clearly missing, such as growth or reproduction, rather than arguing about borderline ones.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The seven characteristics of living organisms are movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion and nutrition (MRS GREN).
- Respiration is chemical reactions in cells that release energy; it is not breathing.
- Growth is a permanent increase in size and dry mass.
- Excretion removes the waste products of metabolism and substances in excess of requirements; removing faeces is egestion.
- Something is living only if it shows all seven characteristics; plants show them all.
:::
