## Introduction

For many years chloroquine was the standard medicine for malaria in Zimbabwe. Over time it stopped curing many malaria infections, so it was replaced by other drugs. Mosquito sprays that once killed nearly every mosquito in a house now leave some alive. Some tuberculosis bacteria can no longer be killed by the usual antibiotics. Nobody designed these changes: they are the result of **natural selection**.

Humans use the same idea on purpose. Over many generations, farmers and breeders have turned wild grasses into high-yielding maize and hardy local cattle into breeds such as the Tuli. This is **artificial selection**, or selective breeding. You should already know about variation, mutation and adaptive features.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Natural selection"}
The process in which individuals with features better suited to their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing on their alleles to the next generation.
:::

:::definition{term="Evolution"}
The change in the adaptive features of a population over time as a result of natural selection.
:::

:::definition{term="Adaptation (Supplement)"}
The process, resulting from natural selection, by which populations become more suited to their environment over many generations.
:::

:::definition{term="Selective breeding (artificial selection)"}
Humans choosing individuals with useful features to breed together, selecting the best of the offspring, and repeating this over many generations.
:::

## How natural selection works

Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace explained natural selection in 1858. It follows from a few simple observations:

1. **Variation**: individuals in a population differ, and much of this variation is genetic (caused by different alleles).
2. **Over-production**: organisms produce far more offspring than the environment can support. A single tigerfish can lay many thousands of eggs.
3. **Struggle for survival**: there is competition for food, water, space and mates, and many individuals are killed by predators or disease. Most offspring die before they reproduce.
4. **Survival of the better adapted**: individuals whose features suit the environment best are more likely to survive and reproduce.
5. **Passing on alleles**: the survivors pass the alleles for their useful features to their offspring.
6. **Over many generations**, the useful alleles become more common in the population. The population becomes better suited to its environment (adaptation), and its features change (evolution).

:::keyfact{title="Natural selection in one line"}
Variation → over-production → struggle for survival → better-adapted individuals survive and reproduce → they pass on their alleles → over many generations those alleles become more common.
:::

## Natural selection happening now

### Antibiotic-resistant bacteria (Supplement)

1. In a large population of bacteria, a random **mutation** gives one bacterium an allele for resistance to an antibiotic. The mutation happens by chance; the antibiotic does not cause it.
2. When a patient takes the antibiotic, the non-resistant bacteria are killed.
3. The resistant bacteria survive. With less competition, they reproduce rapidly by binary fission, passing the resistance allele to all their offspring.
4. Soon most of the bacteria are resistant, and the antibiotic no longer works. Some strains of the bacterium that causes tuberculosis are now resistant to several antibiotics.

To slow the spread of resistance: use antibiotics only when they are needed (they do not work against viruses, such as those causing colds and flu), take the full course as prescribed, and never share or keep leftover antibiotics.

### Insecticides and malaria drugs

The same process explains why some mosquitoes survive the insecticides used to spray houses, and why many malaria parasites became resistant to chloroquine. Each time the chemical is used, it kills the susceptible individuals and leaves the resistant ones to breed.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: one season of spraying]
A population of 10 000 mosquitoes contains 50 that carry an allele for resistance to an insecticide. Spraying kills 98% of the mosquitoes that are not resistant, and none of the resistant ones.
1. Resistant before spraying $= \dfrac{50}{10\,000} \times 100 = 0.5\%$.
2. Non-resistant mosquitoes $= 10\,000 - 50 = 9950$. Survivors $= 2\%$ of 9950 $= 0.02 \times 9950 = 199$.
3. Total survivors $= 199 + 50 = 249$.
4. Resistant after spraying $= \dfrac{50}{249} \times 100 = 20.1\%$.
5. In one spraying the proportion of resistant mosquitoes rose from 0.5% to about 20%. The survivors breed, so the next generation contains far more resistant insects.
:::

## Artificial selection: selective breeding

Breeders follow four steps, again and again:

1. **Choose parents** that show the wanted feature most strongly.
2. **Breed** (cross) them together.
3. **Select the offspring** that show the feature best; discard or do not breed from the rest.
4. **Repeat** for many generations, until the feature is reliable.

:::example{title="Example 2: cattle and crops in Zimbabwe"}
- **Tuli cattle**: from the 1940s, breeders in the dry south of the country, near Gwanda, chose the hardiest and most fertile of the local yellow cattle and bred from them, generation after generation. The Tuli breed copes well with heat, ticks and poor grazing and produces good beef.
- **Mashona and Nkone cattle**, kept for generations by local farmers, are also hardy breeds, and farmers cross them with faster-growing breeds to combine hardiness with productivity.
- **Crops**: plant breeders have produced maize, sorghum and millet varieties that mature early or tolerate drought, which matter more as rains become less reliable, as well as varieties resistant to diseases and pests.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: measuring progress]
A breeder records the mean mass of calves at weaning. It was 160 kg when selection began and 184 kg several generations later. Calculate the percentage increase.
1. Increase $= 184 - 160 = 24\ \text{kg}$.
2. Percentage increase $= \dfrac{24}{160} \times 100 = 15\%$.
:::

## Natural and artificial selection compared (Supplement)

| | Natural selection | Artificial selection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What does the selecting | The environment: predators, disease, climate, competition | Humans |
| Which features are favoured | Those that help survival and reproduction in that environment | Those useful to humans (yield, taste, docility), even if they do not help survival |
| Speed | Usually slow, over many generations | Usually faster, because breeders choose every pair of parents |
| Genetic variation | Variation remains in the population | Variation is reduced; inbreeding can make animals less healthy |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the antibiotic causes the mutation"}
The resistance allele arose by **random mutation** before the antibiotic was used. The antibiotic only **selects**: it kills the bacteria without the allele.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying bacteria become 'immune'"}
Immunity involves antibodies in animals. Bacteria become **resistant**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying individuals evolve"}
An individual cannot change its alleles to suit its environment. **Populations** change over many generations, as the proportion of individuals with useful alleles rises.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling selective breeding genetic modification"}
Selective breeding chooses which existing organisms breed. Genetic modification moves genes directly into an organism's DNA.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In "explain natural selection" answers, use the sequence of key words: **variation**, **over-production**, **competition / struggle for survival**, **better adapted survive**, **reproduce**, **pass on alleles**, **many generations**.
:::

:::exam-tip
For selective breeding, name the feature, then give the steps: choose parents with the feature, breed them, select the best offspring, repeat over many generations.
:::

:::exam-tip
Answer "suggest" questions about resistance with the same logic in a new context: chance mutation → selection by the chemical → survivors reproduce → resistance becomes common.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Natural selection: variation, over-production, a struggle for survival; the better-adapted individuals survive, reproduce and pass on their alleles, which become more common over many generations.
- Evolution is the change in the adaptive features of a population over time as a result of natural selection.
- Antibiotic-resistant bacteria, insecticide-resistant mosquitoes and chloroquine-resistant malaria parasites show natural selection happening now.
- Selective breeding: humans choose parents with useful features, breed them, select the best offspring and repeat, as with Tuli cattle and drought-tolerant maize.
- Supplement: in artificial selection humans choose the features and the process is faster, and it reduces genetic variation.
:::
