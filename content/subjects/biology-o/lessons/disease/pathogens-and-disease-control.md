## Introduction

A protected borehole with a concrete apron, a Blair VIP latrine at the edge of a homestead and a tippy-tap for washing hands beside it: none of these is a medicine, yet together they prevent a great deal of illness. To see why, you need to know what causes disease, how it spreads and how the body defends itself.

In this lesson you will learn what pathogens are, the ways they are passed on, the body's first defences against them, and the public health measures that stop disease spreading.

:::note{title="Syllabus"}
Both boards: pathogens, transmissible diseases, how pathogens are transmitted, the body's defences and the control of the spread of disease. **ZIMSEC**: the meaning of health, personal, domestic and community hygiene, the causes of disease and the difference between infectious and non-infectious diseases.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Pathogen"}
A disease-causing organism, such as a virus, a bacterium, a fungus, a protoctist or a parasitic worm.
:::

:::definition{term="Transmissible disease"}
A disease in which the pathogen can be passed from one host to another. Transmissible diseases are also called infectious or communicable diseases.
:::

:::definition{term="Vector"}
An organism that carries a pathogen from one host to another without itself suffering from the disease, such as the mosquito that carries malaria.
:::

:::definition{term="Health (ZIMSEC)"}
A state of complete physical, mental (including emotional) and social well-being, and not simply the absence of disease.
:::

## Types of pathogen

| Group | Examples of diseases they cause |
| --- | --- |
| Viruses | HIV/AIDS, measles, influenza, COVID-19 |
| Bacteria | Cholera, tuberculosis, typhoid |
| Fungi | Athlete's foot, ringworm, thrush |
| Protoctists | Malaria (caused by *Plasmodium*) |
| Parasitic worms | Bilharzia (caused by *Schistosoma*) |

Most microorganisms are **not** pathogens. Yeast, the bacteria used to make yoghurt and the decomposers in soil are useful.

## How pathogens are transmitted

**Direct transmission**: the pathogen passes straight from one person to another, without anything in between.

- Through **blood** and other **body fluids**, for example HIV passed on by unprotected sex, by sharing needles or from mother to baby.
- By touching infected skin, for example ringworm.

**Indirect transmission**: the pathogen is carried by something else.

- **Contaminated surfaces**, such as cups, towels and door handles.
- **Contaminated food**, for example typhoid bacteria on food handled by someone who did not wash their hands.
- **Contaminated water**, for example cholera bacteria in water polluted with faeces.
- **Animals**, including vectors such as mosquitoes (malaria) and houseflies, which carry bacteria from faeces to food on their legs and mouthparts.
- **Air**: tiny droplets from coughs and sneezes, for example tuberculosis and influenza.

:::example{title="Example 1: flies at a market"}
At a market stall, uncovered meat and cut fruit attract houseflies. A fly that has just landed on faeces in an open drain then lands on the food. Bacteria on its feet and mouthparts are left on the food, and anyone who eats it may become ill. The fly is spreading the pathogen **indirectly**. Covering food and disposing of faeces safely breaks this chain.
:::

## The body's defences

| Defence | Type | How it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skin | Mechanical barrier | A tough outer layer that pathogens cannot get through unless it is cut or damaged |
| Hairs in the nose | Mechanical barrier | Trap dust and pathogens in the air you breathe in |
| Mucus | Chemical barrier | Sticky mucus in the airways traps pathogens; it is moved up to the throat and swallowed or coughed out |
| Stomach acid | Chemical barrier | Hydrochloric acid kills many pathogens in food and drink |
| White blood cells | Cells | Some engulf and digest pathogens; others make antibodies (see the lesson on immunity) |

## Controlling the spread of disease

| Measure | How it stops pathogens reaching people |
| --- | --- |
| Clean water supply | Water is treated (filtered and chlorinated) or taken from protected boreholes; at home it can be boiled or treated with tablets. This kills pathogens such as cholera and typhoid bacteria. |
| Hygienic food preparation | Washing hands, cooking food thoroughly and serving it hot, covering food from flies, keeping raw meat away from cooked food and washing fruit in safe water. |
| Good personal hygiene | Washing hands with soap after using the toilet and before handling food removes pathogens; covering coughs and sneezes reduces droplets. |
| Waste disposal | Refuse is collected, buried or burned and kept in bins with lids, so it does not attract flies and rats or collect rainwater where mosquitoes breed. |
| Sewage treatment | Sewage works kill or remove pathogens before the water returns to rivers; toilets keep faeces away from water, food and flies. |

:::example{title="Example 2: the Blair VIP latrine"}
The Blair ventilated improved pit (VIP) latrine was developed in Zimbabwe in the 1970s. It has a **vent pipe** with a **fly screen** on top, and the inside of the shelter is kept dark. Flies in the pit are attracted to the light at the top of the vent pipe, are trapped by the screen and die there. Air drawn up the pipe also carries smells away. Fewer flies reach the faeces, so fewer pathogens are carried to food.
:::

### Hygiene at three levels (ZIMSEC)

- **Personal hygiene**: washing hands, bathing, brushing teeth, wearing clean clothes.
- **Domestic hygiene**: keeping the home clean, storing drinking water in clean covered containers, using a dish rack, keeping the toilet clean.
- **Community hygiene**: safe water supplies, refuse collection, sewage systems, controlling vectors such as mosquitoes, and **health facilities** such as clinics and community health workers.

## Causes and types of disease (ZIMSEC)

| Cause | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Infection by pathogens | Cholera, malaria, measles |
| Genetic defects (inherited) | Sickle-cell anaemia, haemophilia |
| Chemicals | Lead poisoning, pesticide poisoning |
| Radiation | Skin cancer after years of strong ultraviolet light from the sun |
| Malnutrition | Kwashiorkor, scurvy, rickets, anaemia |
| Degeneration (wearing out with age) | Osteoarthritis of the joints |

**Infectious diseases** are caused by pathogens and can be passed from one person to another. **Non-infectious diseases** cannot be caught from another person: they include **deficiency diseases** (kwashiorkor, scurvy), **genetic diseases** (sickle-cell anaemia) and **cancer**, in which cells divide uncontrollably and form a tumour.

:::example{title="Example 3: sorting diseases"}
A clinic poster lists measles, rickets, tuberculosis, sickle-cell anaemia and lung cancer. Measles (virus) and tuberculosis (bacterium) are **infectious**. Rickets (lack of vitamin D or calcium), sickle-cell anaemia (inherited) and lung cancer (often linked to smoking) are **non-infectious**: you cannot catch them from someone.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking all microorganisms are harmful"}
Only some microorganisms are pathogens. Many are useful in food production, in decomposition and in the gut.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling waterborne disease direct transmission"}
Drinking contaminated water is **indirect** transmission: the pathogen reaches you through the water, not straight from another person.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking clear water is safe water"}
Bacteria are far too small to see. Clear water from an unprotected well can still contain cholera or typhoid bacteria.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying cancer is infectious"}
Cancer cannot be caught from another person. It is a non-infectious disease caused by uncontrolled cell division.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Explain how a measure controls the spread of disease" needs two parts: **what** it does (for example, chlorine kills bacteria) and **why** that stops transmission (people no longer drink live pathogens).
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked about defences, name the defence **and** say how it works: "stomach acid kills pathogens in food", not just "stomach acid".
:::

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC: when classifying a disease, give the reason: "infectious, because it is caused by a pathogen that can be passed on".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A pathogen is a disease-causing organism; a transmissible disease is one whose pathogen can pass from host to host.
- Direct transmission is through blood and body fluids or contact; indirect transmission is through surfaces, food, water, animals (vectors) and the air.
- Defences: skin and nose hairs (mechanical), mucus and stomach acid (chemical), and white blood cells.
- Clean water, hygienic food preparation, personal hygiene, waste disposal and sewage treatment stop pathogens reaching people; the Blair VIP latrine traps flies.
- ZIMSEC: health is physical, mental and social well-being; diseases are caused by infection, genetic defects, chemicals, radiation, malnutrition or degeneration, and are infectious or non-infectious.
:::
