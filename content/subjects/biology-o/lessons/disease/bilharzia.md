## Introduction

On a hot afternoon, children cool off in a dam near Masvingo. Downstream, women wash clothes on the rocks, and on a sugar estate in the Lowveld, workers stand in irrigation canals to clear weeds. None of them can see the tiny larvae swimming in the water, but some of these larvae can burrow through human skin. They cause **bilharzia**, also called **schistosomiasis**.

Bilharzia is common in Zimbabwe, which has many rivers, dams and irrigation schemes, and it affects school-age children most. In this lesson you will learn what causes it, how its life cycle depends on freshwater snails, and how it can be treated and prevented.

You should already know the meaning of pathogen and host (see the lesson on pathogens).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Bilharzia (schistosomiasis)"}
A disease caused by parasitic blood flukes of the genus *Schistosoma*, caught when larvae in fresh water burrow through the skin.
:::

:::definition{term="Intermediate host"}
An organism in which a parasite lives and multiplies for part of its life cycle, but not as an adult. Freshwater snails are the intermediate hosts of *Schistosoma*.
:::

:::definition{term="Cercaria (plural cercariae)"}
The tiny fork-tailed larva released from an infected snail, which swims in the water and burrows through human skin.
:::

## The parasites

*Schistosoma* is a **flatworm** (a fluke). The adult worms are about 1 to 2 cm long and live in pairs, male and female together, inside the **veins** of a person, where they feed on blood and can survive for years.

| | *Schistosoma haematobium* | *Schistosoma mansoni* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form of the disease | Urinary bilharzia | Intestinal bilharzia |
| Where the adults live | Veins around the **bladder** | Veins around the **intestines** |
| How eggs leave the body | In the **urine** | In the **faeces** |
| Main signs | **Blood in the urine**, pain when urinating | Abdominal pain, diarrhoea, blood in the faeces; later an enlarged liver and spleen |

## The life cycle

:::example{title="Example 1: from person to snail to person"}
1. An infected person urinates or defecates in or near fresh water, so ***Schistosoma* eggs** reach the water.
2. Each egg hatches into a tiny swimming larva, which must find the right kind of **freshwater snail** within hours.
3. The larva enters the snail and **multiplies asexually** inside it for several weeks.
4. The snail releases **thousands of cercariae** into the water, especially in warm, sunny hours.
5. A cercaria **burrows through the skin** of a person who is swimming, wading, washing clothes or working in the water.
6. It loses its tail, enters the blood and travels to the veins around the **bladder** or **intestines**, where it grows into an **adult worm**.
7. Adult worms pair up and the females lay eggs. Some eggs pass through the wall of the bladder or intestine and leave in the urine or faeces; many get stuck in the tissues and damage them.
:::

The cycle can only be completed if **eggs reach fresh water**, **suitable snails** are present and **people enter the water**. Breaking any one of these links stops transmission.

## Symptoms

- An itchy rash where the cercariae entered the skin
- Weeks later, fever and aches as the worms mature
- **Urinary bilharzia**: blood in the urine, especially at the end of urination, and pain when urinating; over the years the bladder and kidneys can be damaged and the risk of bladder cancer rises
- **Intestinal bilharzia**: abdominal pain, diarrhoea and blood in the faeces; over the years the liver and spleen become enlarged and damaged
- **Anaemia** and tiredness, from blood loss and because the worms feed on blood; in children, poor growth and difficulty concentrating at school

Blood in the urine is never normal and should always be checked at a clinic.

## Treatment

Bilharzia is treated with **praziquantel** tablets, which kill the adult worms. In Zimbabwe, schools take part in **treatment programmes** in which learners are given praziquantel by health workers. Treatment does not stop a person being infected again if they go back into infected water, so prevention matters too.

## Prevention and control

| Measure | Which link it breaks |
| --- | --- |
| Using toilets or latrines; never urinating or defecating in or near water | Eggs do not reach the water |
| Avoiding swimming, wading and washing in rivers and dams; using borehole or piped water at home | Cercariae cannot reach people's skin |
| Wearing rubber boots and gloves when working in canals | Cercariae cannot reach people's skin |
| Snail control: chemicals that kill snails (molluscicides), clearing weeds from canals, lining canals with concrete, keeping water flowing | No intermediate host, so no cercariae |
| Treating infected people, for example in school programmes | Fewer eggs enter the water |
| Health education | People understand the cycle and change their behaviour |

:::example{title="Example 2: designing a safer irrigation scheme"}
A new irrigation scheme near Chiredzi is being planned. Slow-flowing canals full of weeds would be ideal for snails, and workers would stand in the water every day. The planners line the main canals with **concrete** and keep the water **flowing**, so snails cannot settle; they build **latrines** for the workers near the fields, supply **protective boots**, and arrange regular **testing and treatment**. Each measure removes one link from the life cycle.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying snails cause bilharzia"}
Bilharzia is caused by ***Schistosoma***. The snail is the **intermediate host**, in which the larvae multiply.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying it is caught by eating snails or by mosquito bites"}
People are infected when **cercariae burrow through the skin** in fresh water.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying antibiotics cure bilharzia"}
*Schistosoma* is a worm, not a bacterium. It is treated with **praziquantel**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the two species"}
*S. haematobium*: eggs in the **urine**, blood in the urine. *S. mansoni*: eggs in the **faeces**, damage to the intestines and liver.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Bilharzia is caused by *Schistosoma* blood flukes: *S. haematobium* (urinary, blood in the urine) and *S. mansoni* (intestinal, liver damage).
- Eggs in urine or faeces reach fresh water, larvae multiply in freshwater snails (the intermediate host), and cercariae burrow through the skin of people in the water.
- Symptoms include blood in the urine or faeces, pain, anaemia and long-term damage to the bladder, kidneys, intestines or liver.
- It is treated with praziquantel, including in school treatment programmes.
- Prevention: use toilets, avoid contact with infected water, control snails and treat infected people.
:::
