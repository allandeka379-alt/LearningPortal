## Introduction

Farai's uncle works underground at a mine. He has been coughing for a month, sweats heavily at night and has lost weight. At the clinic, a test on his sputum (the mucus he coughs up) shows **tuberculosis (TB)**. In another town, the water supply has been cut for weeks and a sewer has burst; a few weeks later, dozens of people are admitted with a high fever and stomach pains: **typhoid**.

Both diseases are caused by **bacteria**, but they spread in completely different ways, so they are prevented in different ways. In this lesson you will learn the cause, spread, symptoms, treatment and prevention of each.

You should already know the difference between direct and indirect transmission (see the lesson on pathogens).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Tuberculosis (TB)"}
An infectious disease, usually of the lungs, caused by the bacterium *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* and spread in airborne droplets.
:::

:::definition{term="Typhoid"}
An infectious disease caused by the bacterium *Salmonella* Typhi, spread in food and water contaminated with the faeces or urine of an infected person.
:::

:::definition{term="Carrier"}
A person who has a pathogen in their body and can pass it on, but who has no symptoms.
:::

:::definition{term="BCG"}
The vaccine against tuberculosis, given to babies soon after birth.
:::

## Tuberculosis

### Cause and spread

TB is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. It mainly attacks the **lungs**, but it can also affect the bones, lymph nodes, brain and other organs.

When a person with active TB of the lungs **coughs, sneezes, talks or spits**, tiny droplets containing the bacteria float in the air. Other people **breathe them in**. TB spreads most easily in **crowded, poorly ventilated** places: crowded homes, prisons, mine hostels and closed buses. A form of TB found in cattle can also be caught by drinking **unpasteurised milk** from infected cows.

Many people who breathe in the bacteria do not become ill: their immune system keeps the bacteria inactive. This is **latent TB**, and it has no symptoms and cannot be passed on. It can become **active** TB later if the immune system is weakened, for example by HIV, malnutrition or old age.

### Symptoms and diagnosis

- A **cough lasting more than two weeks**, sometimes with blood in the sputum
- **Night sweats**, fever and chest pain
- **Weight loss**, loss of appetite and tiredness

TB is diagnosed by testing **sputum** samples and sometimes by a chest X-ray.

### TB and HIV

HIV destroys **lymphocytes**, weakening the immune system, so latent TB is much more likely to become active in a person with HIV. TB is one of the most common serious illnesses in people living with HIV. For this reason, people with HIV are checked for TB and may be given medicine to prevent it, and people with TB are offered an HIV test.

### Treatment

TB can be **cured**. Ordinary TB is treated with a combination of **several antibiotics**, taken every day for about **six months**. Patients start to feel better after a few weeks, but they must **finish the whole course**; a health worker or a family treatment supporter often watches each dose being taken. Stopping early allows surviving bacteria to multiply again, and those that survive may be **drug-resistant**. Drug-resistant TB is much harder and slower to treat.

### Prevention

- **BCG vaccination** of babies, which protects young children against the most severe forms of TB
- **Early diagnosis and treatment**, so infectious people quickly stop spreading the bacteria
- **Testing contacts**: family members and workmates of a patient are checked
- **Good ventilation**: opening windows in homes, classrooms and buses
- **Covering the mouth** when coughing and not spitting
- Avoiding overcrowding, eating a balanced diet, and drinking **pasteurised or boiled milk**

:::example{title="Example 1: why miners are at risk"}
Many mine workers breathe in fine rock dust, which can damage their lungs (silicosis) and makes TB more likely. They may also live in crowded hostels and work in confined spaces. Regular TB screening, dust control and good ventilation help to protect them.
:::

## Typhoid

### Cause and spread

Typhoid is caused by the bacterium *Salmonella* Typhi. It enters through the mouth, invades the wall of the intestine and spreads in the blood.

It spreads by the **faecal–oral route**: food or water contaminated with the faeces (or urine) of an infected person. Common routes are drinking water from **shallow wells near latrines** or **burst sewers**, eating food prepared by someone who did not **wash their hands** after using the toilet, food visited by **flies**, and raw vegetables washed in dirty water.

Some people who recover from typhoid remain **carriers**: the bacteria stay in their bodies and pass out in their faeces for months or years, although they look healthy. A carrier who handles food can infect many people.

### Symptoms and diagnosis

Symptoms develop gradually over one to three weeks:

- A **high fever** that rises day by day and lasts for a long time
- Headache, weakness and tiredness
- **Abdominal pain**, loss of appetite, and constipation or diarrhoea

Without treatment, the intestine can bleed or develop holes, which can kill. Typhoid is diagnosed with blood or stool tests. Because malaria also causes fever, a test is needed to tell them apart.

### Treatment and prevention

Typhoid is treated with **antibiotics** chosen by health workers (some strains are resistant to the common ones), together with fluids and rest. It is prevented by:

- **safe water**: boiling, chlorination, protected boreholes and repaired pipes;
- **sanitation**: using toilets and keeping sewers in good repair;
- **handwashing with soap**, especially by food handlers;
- **food hygiene**: cooking food thoroughly, eating it hot, covering it from flies and washing fruit and vegetables in safe water;
- finding and treating **carriers**, and keeping them away from handling food;
- **typhoid vaccines** for people at high risk and in outbreak areas.

## TB and typhoid compared

| Feature | Tuberculosis | Typhoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pathogen | Bacterium, *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* | Bacterium, *Salmonella* Typhi |
| How it spreads | Droplets from coughs and sneezes, breathed in from the air | Contaminated food and water (faecal–oral) |
| Main organs | Lungs | Intestine, then blood |
| Main symptoms | Cough for over two weeks, night sweats, weight loss | Long-lasting high fever, abdominal pain, weakness |
| Treatment | Several antibiotics for about six months | Antibiotics and fluids |
| Vaccine | BCG, given at birth | Typhoid vaccine for people at risk |
| Key prevention | Ventilation, early treatment, covering coughs | Safe water, sanitation, handwashing, food hygiene |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Stopping TB treatment when you feel better"}
Feeling better does not mean all the bacteria are dead. The full course must be finished, or TB can return and become drug-resistant.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking TB is spread by sharing cups and plates"}
TB spreads through the **air** in droplets from coughs. Typhoid is the one spread by contaminated food and water.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking a healthy-looking person cannot spread typhoid"}
**Carriers** look healthy but pass the bacteria in their faeces. Handwashing matters for everyone who prepares food.
:::

:::mistake{title="Assuming every fever is malaria"}
Typhoid, malaria and other infections all cause fever. Only a test can tell which disease it is.
:::

:::

:::exam-tip
When asked why TB patients must complete their treatment, give two ideas: to kill **all** the bacteria (so TB does not return) and to stop **drug-resistant** bacteria developing.
:::

:::exam-tip
Link HIV and TB through the immune system: HIV destroys lymphocytes, so latent TB becomes active.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- TB is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* and spreads in airborne droplets, especially in crowded, poorly ventilated places.
- TB symptoms: a cough lasting over two weeks, night sweats, fever and weight loss; it is cured with several antibiotics taken for about six months, and the course must be completed.
- BCG vaccination, early treatment, testing contacts and good ventilation prevent TB; HIV makes active TB much more likely.
- Typhoid is caused by *Salmonella* Typhi and spreads in contaminated food and water; carriers can spread it without being ill.
- Typhoid causes a long-lasting high fever and abdominal pain; it is treated with antibiotics and prevented by safe water, sanitation, handwashing and food hygiene.
:::
