## Introduction

In 2008 and 2009 Zimbabwe suffered one of the largest cholera outbreaks in Africa's recent history. Water treatment and sewage systems had broken down in many towns, and the disease spread to every province. There have been further outbreaks since. Cholera can kill a healthy adult within hours, yet a cheap drink of salts and sugar in clean water saves most patients.

In this lesson you will learn what causes cholera, how it spreads, how its toxin makes the body lose so much water, and how it is treated and prevented. You should already know about osmosis and the ways pathogens are transmitted (see the lesson on pathogens).

:::note{title="Syllabus"}
**ZIMSEC** (8.11.2): the cause, transmission, symptoms, treatment, prevention and control of cholera. **Cambridge**: cholera is **Supplement** content: a disease caused by a bacterium and transmitted in contaminated water, whose toxin causes chloride ions to be secreted into the small intestine, so that water follows by osmosis.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Cholera"}
An infectious disease of the small intestine, caused by the bacterium *Vibrio cholerae* and spread in water or food contaminated with faeces.
:::

:::definition{term="Toxin"}
A poisonous substance made by a pathogen.
:::

:::definition{term="Dehydration"}
A dangerous loss of water from the body, which also lowers the volume of the blood.
:::

:::definition{term="Oral rehydration solution (ORS)"}
A solution of salts and glucose in clean water, drunk to replace the water and ions lost in diarrhoea.
:::

## Cause and transmission

*Vibrio cholerae* is a comma-shaped bacterium with a flagellum. It multiplies in the small intestine of an infected person and leaves the body in huge numbers in their **faeces**. Some infected people have mild symptoms or none at all, but they still pass bacteria in their faeces.

Cholera spreads by the **faecal–oral route**: bacteria from faeces get into water or food, which someone else swallows.

- Drinking water from **wells, rivers or burst pipes** contaminated with sewage.
- Eating food washed or prepared with contaminated water, handled with **unwashed hands**, or visited by **flies**.
- **Poor sanitation**: open defecation, overflowing latrines and burst sewers, made worse when **heavy rain** washes faeces into water sources and when many people live close together.

:::example{title="Example 1: how an outbreak starts"}
In a crowded suburb, the taps run dry for weeks, so families dig shallow wells. Nearby, a sewer pipe bursts. One person who has returned from an area with cholera uses a blocked toilet. After a storm, sewage seeps into several wells. Within days, many people who drank the well water have watery diarrhoea. Every link in this chain involves **faeces reaching drinking water**.
:::

## How the toxin causes diarrhoea (Cambridge Supplement)

1. Bacteria are swallowed in contaminated water or food. Many are killed by stomach acid, but some reach the **small intestine**.
2. They attach to the lining of the small intestine, multiply and release a **toxin**.
3. The toxin makes the cells of the lining **secrete chloride ions** ($\ce{Cl-}$) into the space inside the intestine.
4. The chloride ions **lower the water potential** of the contents of the intestine.
5. Water moves out of the cells and the blood into the intestine by **osmosis**.
6. The result is large volumes of **watery diarrhoea**, causing **dehydration** and the **loss of ions** (salts) from the blood.

ZIMSEC candidates can describe this more simply: the toxin makes the intestine lose very large amounts of water and salts in the diarrhoea.

## Symptoms

- Sudden, painless, **large volumes of watery diarrhoea**, often pale and cloudy ("rice-water" stools)
- Vomiting, and painful **muscle cramps** caused by the loss of ions
- Signs of **dehydration**: great thirst, a dry mouth, **sunken eyes**, skin that stays pinched when lifted, little or no urine, weakness and a fast heartbeat

Without treatment, a person can lose water so fast that their blood volume falls dangerously, and they may collapse and die within hours. Young children and old people are especially at risk.

## Treatment

- **Oral rehydration solution** replaces the lost **water and ions**. The glucose in it helps the small intestine absorb the salts, and the water follows. ORS sachets are dissolved in safe water and drunk frequently.
- Patients who are severely dehydrated or cannot drink are given fluids directly into a vein (a **drip**) at a clinic or cholera treatment centre.
- **Antibiotics** may be given to severely ill patients to shorten the illness, but it is **rehydration** that saves lives.

:::example{title="Example 2: why plain water is not enough"}
Rudo's young son has had watery diarrhoea all morning. She gives him plain boiled water, but he is still weak and has cramps. At the clinic the nurse explains that he has lost **salts** as well as water, and gives him ORS. The salts replace the lost ions, the glucose helps his intestine absorb them, and water follows. Within hours he can sit up again.
:::

## Prevention and control

| Measure | How it stops cholera spreading |
| --- | --- |
| Safe water | Boiling or chlorinating water kills the bacteria; protected boreholes and repaired pipes keep sewage out |
| Sanitation | Toilets and Blair VIP latrines, and repaired sewers, keep faeces away from water, food and flies |
| Handwashing with soap | Removes bacteria after using the toilet, before handling food and after caring for a patient |
| Food hygiene | Cooking food thoroughly and eating it hot kills the bacteria; covering food keeps flies off; fruit and vegetables are washed in safe water |
| Early treatment and reporting | Treatment centres care for patients and safely dispose of their faeces; health workers trace where cases came from |
| Oral cholera vaccine | Used in outbreak areas to give protection for a period; it adds to, but does not replace, safe water and sanitation |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying cholera is caused by a virus"}
Cholera is caused by a **bacterium**, *Vibrio cholerae*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying antibiotics are the main treatment"}
Most patients survive because of **rehydration** with ORS (or a drip). Antibiotics are only an extra for severe cases.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the bacteria drink the body's water"}
The **toxin** makes the intestine lining secrete chloride ions; **water follows by osmosis**. The bacteria do not absorb the water.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying chloride ions move by osmosis"}
Osmosis is the movement of **water**. The chloride ions are secreted by the cells; water then moves by osmosis.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Cholera is caused by the bacterium *Vibrio cholerae* and spreads by the faecal–oral route in contaminated water and food.
- Supplement: its toxin makes the small intestine secrete chloride ions; water follows by osmosis, causing watery diarrhoea, dehydration and loss of ions from the blood.
- Signs include large volumes of watery diarrhoea, cramps, thirst, sunken eyes and little urine; it can kill within hours.
- Treatment is rehydration with ORS (water, salts and glucose), or a drip in severe cases; antibiotics are only an extra for severe cases.
- Prevention: safe water, toilets and sewers, handwashing with soap, food hygiene, early treatment and, in outbreaks, oral vaccines.
:::
