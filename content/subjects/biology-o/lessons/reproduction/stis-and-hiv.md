## Introduction

HIV remains one of Zimbabwe's most important health challenges, but testing, treatment and prevention programmes have greatly reduced the numbers of new infections and deaths. Many people living with HIV now take daily treatment and live long, healthy lives. Knowing the facts about HIV and other **sexually transmitted infections (STIs)** helps you protect yourself and others, and helps to end the fear and stigma that once surrounded them.

In this lesson you will learn how HIV and other STIs are passed on, how they are **not** passed on, and how their spread is controlled. You should already know how the immune system works and the methods of contraception.

:::note{title="Syllabus"}
Both boards: STIs, HIV as the cause of an STI that may lead to AIDS, how HIV is transmitted, and how the spread of STIs is controlled. **ZIMSEC**: gonorrhoea, syphilis, chancroid, genital warts and genital herpes, with their causes, signs and symptoms, contact tracing and treatment.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Sexually transmitted infection (STI)"}
An infection that is transmitted through sexual contact.
:::

:::definition{term="HIV"}
The human immunodeficiency virus: a pathogen that causes an STI and may lead to AIDS.
:::

:::definition{term="AIDS"}
Acquired immune deficiency syndrome: the late stage of untreated HIV infection, when the immune system is so weak that the person suffers serious infections and some cancers.
:::

:::definition{term="Contact tracing"}
Finding and informing the sexual partners of a person with an STI, so that they can be tested and treated.
:::

## HIV and AIDS

HIV infects and destroys a type of **lymphocyte**, a white blood cell that is essential for making antibodies. Over several years, without treatment, the number of these cells falls and the immune system becomes very weak. The person then develops **opportunistic infections**, such as tuberculosis, pneumonia and thrush: this stage is AIDS.

A person can have HIV for years, look and feel completely well, and still pass the virus on. The only way to know whether someone has HIV is an **HIV test**.

### How HIV is passed on

- **Unprotected sexual intercourse** with a person who has HIV.
- **Blood**: sharing needles and syringes, or a transfusion of blood that has not been screened. Donated blood in Zimbabwe is tested, and health workers use gloves and sterile equipment.
- **From mother to baby** during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding.

### How HIV is not passed on

HIV does **not** spread through hugging, shaking hands, kissing on the cheek, sharing cups, plates or food, using the same toilet, swimming together, or coughing and sneezing. **Mosquitoes do not transmit HIV**: the virus cannot multiply inside them, and a mosquito injects its saliva, not blood from the last person it bit.

## Controlling the spread of HIV and other STIs

| Method | How it reduces spread |
| --- | --- |
| Abstinence | No sexual contact, so no transmission through sex |
| Faithfulness | If two uninfected partners (both tested) have sex only with each other, neither is exposed to an STI from anyone else |
| Fewer sexual partners | Fewer chances of meeting an infected partner |
| Condoms, used correctly every time | A barrier stops body fluids passing between partners |
| Testing | People who know their status can get treatment and protect their partners |
| Antiretroviral therapy (ART) | Keeps the amount of HIV in the blood very low, so the immune system recovers; when the virus is undetectable, HIV is not passed on through sex |
| Prevention of mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT) | ART for mothers and preventive medicine for babies protects newborns |
| Prevention medicines (PrEP) | HIV-negative people at high risk take antiretroviral medicine to prevent infection |
| Contact tracing and early treatment | Partners are found and treated, stopping further spread and reinfection |
| Screening blood and using sterile needles | Prevents transmission through blood |

Medical male circumcision also reduces, but does not remove, a man's risk of HIV infection, so condoms are still needed. ART is **not a cure**: it must be taken every day for life.

## Other sexually transmitted infections (ZIMSEC)

| STI | Pathogen | Main signs and symptoms | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gonorrhoea | Bacterium | Burning pain when passing urine; discharge from the penis or vagina; many women have no symptoms | Antibiotics: curable |
| Syphilis | Bacterium | A painless sore where the bacteria entered, then a rash (including on the palms and soles) and fever; years later, damage to the heart, brain and nerves | Antibiotics (penicillin): curable, especially early |
| Chancroid | Bacterium | Painful, soft sores on the genitals and painful swollen glands in the groin | Antibiotics: curable |
| Genital warts | Virus (human papillomavirus, HPV) | Small lumps on or around the genitals | Warts can be removed, but the virus may stay |
| Genital herpes | Virus (herpes simplex virus) | Painful blisters on the genitals that burst into sores and keep coming back | No cure; antiviral medicine reduces outbreaks |

STIs caused by **bacteria** can be cured with antibiotics; STIs caused by **viruses** cannot be cured, although medicines can control them. Some types of HPV can also cause cancer of the cervix, which is why the HPV vaccine is offered to girls in Zimbabwe. Untreated gonorrhoea can spread to the oviducts or sperm ducts, scar them and cause **infertility**, and syphilis can cross the placenta and harm a baby. STIs that cause sores also make it easier for HIV to be passed on.

:::example{title="Example 1: why partners are traced"}
Tatenda is treated for gonorrhoea at a clinic. If his partner is not also tested and treated, she may develop complications such as infertility, and she could re-infect him as soon as his treatment ends. Contact tracing means the clinic helps him to inform her, and she is tested and treated too.
:::

:::example{title="Example 2: a symptom that disappears"}
A painless sore appears on a man's genitals and heals by itself after a few weeks, so he thinks he is well. In fact this is often the first stage of **syphilis**: the bacteria are still in his body, and without treatment the disease can return years later and damage his heart and brain. Any genital sore should be checked at a clinic.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying HIV and AIDS are the same thing"}
HIV is the **virus**. AIDS is the late stage of untreated HIV infection, when the immune system has been badly damaged.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking you can tell if someone has HIV by looking"}
People with HIV may look and feel completely well for years. Only a test can tell.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking HIV spreads by everyday contact or mosquito bites"}
HIV is passed only through sex, blood, and from mother to baby; not by touching, sharing plates or insect bites.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying antibiotics cure HIV or herpes"}
Antibiotics work only on bacteria. HIV, herpes and genital warts are caused by viruses.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- An STI is an infection transmitted through sexual contact; HIV causes an STI that may lead to AIDS.
- HIV destroys lymphocytes, weakening the immune system; it is passed on by unprotected sex, infected blood and from mother to baby, not by everyday contact or mosquitoes.
- The spread of STIs is controlled by abstinence, faithfulness, condoms, testing, ART, PMTCT, contact tracing and early treatment.
- ART controls HIV but does not cure it; when the virus is undetectable, HIV is not passed on through sex.
- ZIMSEC: gonorrhoea, syphilis and chancroid are bacterial and curable with antibiotics; genital warts, genital herpes and HIV are viral and cannot be cured.
:::
