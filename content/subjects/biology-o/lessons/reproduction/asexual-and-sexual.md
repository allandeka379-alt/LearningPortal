## Introduction

The sugar cane estates around Triangle and Hippo Valley in the Lowveld are not planted with seeds. Workers lay short pieces of cane stem in furrows, and each piece sprouts into new plants. Banana growers plant suckers dug from the base of older plants, and citrus growers join a twig of a good orange variety onto the root of a tougher tree. Yet maize farmers buy fresh hybrid seed every season. Why do farmers use such different methods?

The answer lies in the difference between **asexual** and **sexual** reproduction. In this lesson you will learn how each works, see many local examples and weigh up the advantages and disadvantages of each, for wild plants and for crops.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Asexual reproduction"}
A process that produces genetically identical offspring from one parent. No gametes are involved.
:::

:::definition{term="Sexual reproduction"}
A process in which the nuclei of two gametes (sex cells) fuse to form a zygote, producing offspring that are genetically different from each other.
:::

:::definition{term="Fertilisation"}
The fusion of the nuclei of a male gamete and a female gamete to form a zygote.
:::

:::definition{term="Haploid and diploid (Cambridge Supplement)"}
A **haploid** nucleus has one set of chromosomes, as in gametes. A **diploid** nucleus has two sets of chromosomes, as in the zygote and most body cells.
:::

## Asexual reproduction

Offspring from asexual reproduction form a **clone**: they are genetically identical to the parent and to each other (unless a mutation occurs).

### In microorganisms

- **Bacteria** divide by **binary fission**: one cell copies its DNA and splits into two identical cells.
- **Yeast**, a single-celled fungus, reproduces by **budding**: a small bud grows out of the parent cell and separates.
- **Bread mould** grows thread-like hyphae and produces huge numbers of **spores** asexually; each spore can grow into a new mould.

### Natural vegetative reproduction in plants

| Structure | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stem tuber | Swollen underground stem tip storing starch, with buds ("eyes") | Potato |
| Storage root | Swollen root that can sprout new shoots | Sweet potato |
| Rhizome | Horizontal stem growing underground, with buds at its nodes | Ginger, couch grass |
| Runner | Horizontal stem growing along the ground, forming new plants at its nodes | Strawberry |
| Bulb | Short stem surrounded by fleshy leaf bases that store food, with buds | Onion, garlic |
| Sucker | New shoot growing from buds on the underground stem of the parent | Banana |

### Artificial propagation

- **Stem cuttings**: pieces of stem with buds are planted, as with **cassava** and **sugar cane** (pieces called setts) and **sweet potato** vines. Each piece must include at least one node with a bud.
- **Grafting and budding**: a shoot or bud (the **scion**) of a variety with good fruit is joined to the rooted stem (the **rootstock**) of a hardy, disease-resistant variety, as in **citrus** and **mango**. The cut surfaces must fit tightly so that the transport tissues join.
- **Tissue culture**: tiny pieces of tissue are grown on sterile jelly containing nutrients and plant hormones, producing thousands of identical, disease-free plantlets of bananas or potatoes.

:::example{title="Example 1: grafting an orange tree"}
Tendai cuts a slanting slice from a young rough lemon seedling (the rootstock) and a matching slice from a twig of a good orange variety (the scion). He binds the cut surfaces tightly together with grafting tape so that their tissues grow together. The tree that results has the strong, disease-resistant roots of the rough lemon but produces oranges identical to those of the parent orange tree, and it usually bears fruit years earlier than a tree grown from seed.
:::

## Sexual reproduction

In sexual reproduction, a **male gamete** (the nucleus from a pollen grain in plants, a sperm in animals) fuses with a **female gamete** (the egg cell in an ovule, or an ovum). The zygote grows into an embryo. Because half the genetic material comes from each parent, the offspring show **variation**.

Self-pollination still counts as sexual reproduction: even though only one plant is involved, two gametes fuse.

### Chromosome numbers (Cambridge Supplement)

Gametes are **haploid** and the zygote is **diploid**, so fertilisation restores the full chromosome number each generation.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: counting chromosomes in maize]
A maize leaf cell contains 20 chromosomes (two sets of 10). How many chromosomes are in (a) the male gamete nucleus of a pollen grain, (b) the egg cell and (c) the zygote?

1. Body cells are diploid: 20 chromosomes, so one set is 10.
2. (a) The male gamete is haploid: **10** chromosomes.
3. (b) The egg cell is haploid: **10** chromosomes.
4. (c) The zygote forms when the two nuclei fuse: $10 + 10 = 20$ chromosomes, diploid again.
:::

In humans the numbers are 23 in each gamete and 46 in the zygote.

## Advantages and disadvantages

### Asexual reproduction

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wild populations | Only one parent needed, so no mate or pollinator is required; fast, so an area can be colonised quickly; offspring suit the same conditions as the successful parent; stored food helps the new plants | No variation, so a new disease or a change in conditions can kill them all; offspring grow close to the parent and compete for light, water and minerals |
| Crops | Every plant is identical, with the same good qualities, ripening at the same time; good varieties are kept exactly; crops can mature sooner; seedless crops such as bananas can be grown | Diseases pass to the offspring in tubers, suckers and cuttings; one new disease can destroy the whole crop; no new varieties arise; planting material is bulky to store and move |

### Sexual reproduction

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wild populations | Variation allows the population to adapt to change by natural selection; seeds can be dispersed far from the parent; seeds can survive the dry season | Two gametes (and usually a pollinator or wind) are needed; slower; more energy spent on flowers, pollen and nectar |
| Crops | Variation lets breeders combine useful features, such as drought tolerance and disease resistance, into new varieties; seeds are easy to store and transport | Offspring vary, so quality is less uniform; hybrid seed must be bought each season because seed saved from a hybrid crop gives variable, lower yields |

:::example{title="Example 3: the banana and Panama disease"}
For much of the twentieth century, most bananas exported from Central America were of one variety, Gros Michel, grown from suckers. Every plant was a clone, so none could resist **Panama disease**, a fungal wilt. The disease spread from plantation to plantation and the variety had to be abandoned for commercial export. This shows the main risk of relying on asexual reproduction: no variation means no resistant individuals.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="One plant means asexual"}
A self-pollinated plant reproduces **sexually**, because a male and a female gamete fuse. Asexual reproduction involves no gametes at all.
:::

:::mistake{title="Haploid means half a chromosome"}
Haploid means **one set** of chromosomes (23 in humans), not half of each chromosome.
:::

:::mistake{title="Potatoes are roots"}
A potato is a **stem** tuber: its "eyes" are buds. A sweet potato is a swollen **root**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Asexual reproduction is always better for farmers"}
Clones are uniform, but they are all equally vulnerable to the same disease, and no new varieties arise.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Asexual reproduction: one parent, no gametes, genetically identical offspring (binary fission in bacteria, budding in yeast, spores in moulds, vegetative propagation in plants).
- Natural methods: tubers, storage roots, rhizomes, runners, bulbs, suckers; artificial methods: cuttings, grafting and budding, tissue culture.
- Sexual reproduction: nuclei of two gametes fuse to form a zygote; offspring are genetically different.
- Supplement: gametes are haploid, the zygote is diploid (human 23 and 46).
- Asexual: fast, uniform and reliable, but no variation and diseases spread; sexual: variation and seeds, but slower and needs gametes to meet.
:::
