## Introduction

Between the ages of about 8 and 14, every young person's body begins to change: a growth spurt, new hair, a deeper voice or developing breasts, and new feelings. These changes, called **puberty**, are controlled by **hormones**. In girls, puberty also starts the **menstrual cycle**, a monthly cycle that prepares the body for a possible pregnancy.

These changes are a normal, healthy part of growing up. In this lesson you will learn what the sex hormones do at puberty, what happens in the ovaries and the uterus during the menstrual cycle, and how hormones control it. You should already know the parts of the reproductive systems.

:::note{title="Syllabus"}
Both boards: testosterone and oestrogen at puberty, the menstrual cycle and the roles of oestrogen and progesterone. **Cambridge Supplement**: where oestrogen and progesterone are made, and the roles of FSH and LH. **ZIMSEC**: the phases of the cycle and its fertile and infertile phases.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Puberty"}
The stage of development when the reproductive organs mature and the secondary sexual characteristics develop.
:::

:::definition{term="Secondary sexual characteristics"}
The features that develop at puberty and differ between males and females, apart from the reproductive organs themselves, such as facial hair or breasts.
:::

:::definition{term="Menstrual cycle"}
The monthly cycle of changes in the ovaries and the lining of the uterus, which prepares for a possible pregnancy.
:::

:::definition{term="Ovulation"}
The release of an egg from an ovary.
:::

:::definition{term="Menstruation"}
The breakdown and loss of the lining of the uterus, with a little blood, through the vagina: a period.
:::

## Puberty

Puberty starts when the pituitary gland at the base of the brain begins to release hormones that make the testes or ovaries mature and secrete **sex hormones**. The age varies from person to person, and girls usually start a little earlier than boys.

| Testosterone (from the testes) | Oestrogen (from the ovaries) |
| --- | --- |
| Penis and testes grow; sperm production begins | Breasts develop |
| Hair grows on the face and body | Hips widen |
| Voice deepens as the larynx grows | Uterus and vagina grow |
| Muscles develop and shoulders broaden | Menstrual cycle begins |

In both sexes, pubic and underarm hair grows and there is a growth spurt. Emotional changes, such as mood swings and new feelings, are also a normal part of puberty.

## The menstrual cycle

A cycle lasts **about 28 days** on average, but it varies between women and from month to month. Day 1 is the first day of a period.

| Days (28-day cycle) | In the ovary | In the uterus lining |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 5 | A new follicle (an egg in a fluid-filled space) begins to develop | **Menstruation**: the lining breaks down and is lost |
| 6 to 13 | The follicle matures and secretes oestrogen | The lining is **repaired and thickens** |
| About 14 | **Ovulation**: the egg is released | The lining is thick |
| 15 to 28 | The empty follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, which secretes progesterone | The lining is **maintained**, rich in blood vessels, ready for an embryo |

If the egg is **not fertilised**, the corpus luteum breaks down, the progesterone level falls, the lining breaks down and a new period begins. If an embryo **implants**, progesterone stays high (from the corpus luteum and later the placenta), so the lining is kept and there are no periods during pregnancy.

:::keyfact
In cycles that are longer or shorter than 28 days, ovulation usually happens about **14 days before the next period starts**.
:::

## How hormones control the cycle

**Oestrogen** repairs and thickens the lining of the uterus. **Progesterone** maintains the thick lining.

### FSH and LH (Cambridge Supplement)

| Hormone | Made by | Main roles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) | Pituitary gland | Makes a follicle develop in the ovary and secrete oestrogen |
| Oestrogen | The developing follicle in the ovary | Repairs and thickens the uterus lining; at high levels it reduces FSH and triggers a surge of LH |
| LH (luteinising hormone) | Pituitary gland | A sharp rise (surge) triggers **ovulation**; it also makes the empty follicle become the corpus luteum |
| Progesterone | Corpus luteum; in pregnancy, the **placenta** | Maintains the uterus lining; reduces FSH and LH, so no new egg matures |

:::example{title="Example 1: reading a hormone graph"}
A graph of hormone levels over one 28-day cycle shows:

1. **FSH** rising in the first few days, as a follicle starts to develop.
2. **Oestrogen** rising steadily to a peak around day 12, while the lining thickens.
3. **LH** shooting up to a sharp peak around day 13 to 14: ovulation follows.
4. **Progesterone** low until ovulation, then rising to a peak around day 21, from the corpus luteum.
5. Progesterone falling towards day 28, as the corpus luteum breaks down; the lining is then lost.

:::

## Fertile and infertile phases (ZIMSEC)

An egg survives for only about **one day** after ovulation, but sperm can survive in the female reproductive system for **up to about five days**. So fertilisation is possible only if intercourse happens in the few days **before** ovulation, on the day of ovulation or about a day **after**: this is the **fertile phase**. The rest of the cycle is the **infertile phase**, when pregnancy is unlikely.

:::example{title="Example 2: working out the fertile phase"}
In a regular 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14:

1. Sperm can survive up to five days, so intercourse as early as day 9 could leave live sperm waiting when the egg is released.
2. The egg survives about one day, so it could be fertilised up to day 15.
3. The fertile phase is therefore roughly **days 9 to 15**.

Because the day of ovulation can change from cycle to cycle, the fertile phase cannot be predicted exactly. This is why the "safe period" is not a reliable way to avoid pregnancy.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking every cycle is exactly 28 days"}
Cycles vary between women and from month to month. Ovulation usually happens about 14 days before the next period, so it moves when the cycle length changes.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up ovulation and menstruation"}
Ovulation is the release of an **egg**, around the middle of the cycle. Menstruation is the loss of the **lining**, at the start of the cycle.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying progesterone builds up the lining"}
**Oestrogen** repairs and thickens the lining; **progesterone** maintains it.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying FSH and LH come from the ovaries"}
FSH and LH are made by the **pituitary gland**. The ovaries make oestrogen and progesterone.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When reading a cycle graph, quote **day numbers** and link each change to a cause: "the lining is thickest around day 22 because progesterone is high".
:::

:::exam-tip
Supplement: learn a one-line job and source for each of the four hormones; "LH from the pituitary triggers ovulation" is a typical mark.
:::

:::exam-tip
Questions about periods stopping in pregnancy need the idea that **progesterone stays high**, so the lining is not lost.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- At puberty, testosterone (from the testes) and oestrogen (from the ovaries) cause the secondary sexual characteristics.
- The menstrual cycle lasts about 28 days: menstruation (days 1 to 5), repair and thickening of the lining, ovulation around day 14, then the lining is maintained.
- Oestrogen repairs and thickens the uterus lining; progesterone maintains it; when progesterone falls, the lining is lost.
- Supplement: FSH (pituitary) makes a follicle develop; LH (pituitary) triggers ovulation; oestrogen comes from the follicle and progesterone from the corpus luteum, and in pregnancy from the placenta.
- ZIMSEC: the fertile phase is the few days around ovulation, because sperm survive up to about five days and the egg about one day.
:::
