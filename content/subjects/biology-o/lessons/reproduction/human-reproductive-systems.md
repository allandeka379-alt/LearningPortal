## Introduction

Every person began as a single cell formed when a sperm cell from their father joined an egg cell from their mother. Understanding the reproductive systems helps you to understand the changes of puberty, to look after your own health and to make informed decisions later in life.

In this lesson you will learn the parts of the male and female reproductive systems and what each one does, how sperm and eggs are made, how they meet, and how each is adapted for its job. The lesson uses the correct scientific names, as examiners expect.

:::note{title="Syllabus"}
Both boards: the parts and functions of the male and female reproductive systems, fertilisation, and the adaptations and comparison of sperm and eggs. **ZIMSEC**: how gametes are formed (details of meiosis are not required), the pathway of sperm and the causes of infertility.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Gamete"}
A sex cell. In humans the male gamete is the sperm cell and the female gamete is the egg cell (ovum). Each has half the normal number of chromosomes: 23 instead of 46.
:::

:::definition{term="Fertilisation"}
The fusion of the nucleus of a male gamete (sperm) with the nucleus of a female gamete (egg).
:::

:::definition{term="Zygote"}
The single cell formed at fertilisation, with the full set of 46 chromosomes. It divides to form an embryo.
:::

:::definition{term="Semen"}
The mixture of sperm cells and fluid from the glands that is released from the penis.
:::

## The male reproductive system

Described part by part:

1. **Testes** (singular *testis*): two oval organs that produce **sperm** and the hormone **testosterone**.
2. **Scrotum**: the sac of skin that holds the testes outside the body, keeping them a few degrees cooler than the rest of the body, which is best for sperm production.
3. **Sperm ducts**: tubes that carry sperm from the testes to the urethra.
4. **Prostate gland**: adds fluid to the sperm. The fluid contains nutrients and lets the sperm swim; sperm plus fluid is **semen**.
5. **Urethra**: the tube inside the penis that carries semen, or at other times urine, out of the body (never both at once).
6. **Penis**: becomes erect and places semen in the vagina during sexual intercourse.

## The female reproductive system

1. **Ovaries**: two organs that contain the immature eggs. From puberty, usually one egg is released each month (**ovulation**). The ovaries also make the hormones **oestrogen** and **progesterone**.
2. **Oviducts**: tubes that carry an egg from an ovary to the uterus. Each has a funnel-shaped opening near the ovary and is lined with **cilia**, which, with muscle contractions, move the egg along. **Fertilisation happens in the oviduct.**
3. **Uterus** (womb): a hollow, muscular organ with a soft lining rich in blood vessels. An embryo **implants** in the lining and develops there into a fetus.
4. **Cervix**: a ring of muscle at the lower end (neck) of the uterus. It keeps the fetus in the uterus during pregnancy and widens during birth.
5. **Vagina**: the muscular tube leading from the cervix to the outside. It receives the penis and semen during intercourse and is the birth canal.

## How gametes are made

Gametes are made by a special kind of cell division, **meiosis**, which halves the number of chromosomes, so a sperm or egg has 23 chromosomes (the details are covered in the lesson on mitosis and meiosis).

- From puberty, the testes make **millions of sperm every day**, throughout a man's life.
- A girl is born with all her immature eggs already in her ovaries. From puberty, usually **one egg** matures and is released each month until the menopause.

## The pathway of sperm (ZIMSEC)

:::example{title="Example 1: from testis to oviduct"}
1. Sperm are made in a **testis** and stored in a coiled tube on its surface (the epididymis).
2. During ejaculation they pass along a **sperm duct**.
3. Fluid from the **prostate gland** (and other glands) is added, forming semen.
4. Semen passes along the **urethra** and out of the **penis** into the **vagina**.
5. Sperm swim through the **cervix** and the **uterus** into an **oviduct**.
6. If an egg is in the oviduct, one sperm may fertilise it.
:::

## Adaptations of sperm and egg cells

| Gamete | Feature | How it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sperm | **Flagellum** (tail) | Lashes from side to side so the sperm can swim towards the egg |
| Sperm | Many **mitochondria** in the middle piece | Release energy by respiration for swimming |
| Sperm | **Acrosome** (cap on the head) containing enzymes | The enzymes digest a way through the jelly coat of the egg |
| Sperm | Small and streamlined | Moves easily through fluid |
| Egg | Large, with **energy stores** in the cytoplasm | Supplies the zygote and early embryo until it implants |
| Egg | **Jelly coat** | Changes after one sperm has entered, so no other sperm can enter |
| Both | Nucleus with 23 chromosomes | At fertilisation the zygote gets the full 46 |

## Comparing the gametes

| Feature | Sperm | Egg |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size | Very small, about 0.05 mm long including the tail | Much larger, about 0.1 mm across: just visible to the naked eye as a tiny dot |
| Structure | Head, middle piece and tail | Round, with a jelly coat and no tail |
| Movement | Swims using its flagellum | Cannot move by itself; moved by cilia and muscles in the oviduct |
| Numbers | Tens to hundreds of millions in one ejaculation | Usually one released per month |
| Energy stores | Very few | Large stores in the cytoplasm |

:::example{title="Example 2: why so many sperm?"}
Most of the sperm released never reach the egg: many leak out of the vagina, are killed by its acidic conditions, swim the wrong way or into the wrong oviduct, or tire out. Only a few hundred may get close to the egg, and only one fertilises it. Releasing huge numbers of sperm makes it likely that at least one succeeds.
:::

## Infertility (ZIMSEC)

A couple is said to be **infertile** if they have not achieved a pregnancy after about a year of regular sexual intercourse without contraception. The cause may lie with the woman, with the man, or with both; infertility is a medical condition, not anyone's fault.

| In women | In men |
| --- | --- |
| **Blocked or scarred oviducts**, often after an untreated sexually transmitted infection such as gonorrhoea | **Low sperm count**, or sperm that are abnormal or swim poorly |
| Not ovulating, for example because of a hormone imbalance or being very underweight or overweight | **Blocked sperm ducts**, for example after an untreated infection |
| Problems with the uterus | Damage to the testes, for example by mumps after puberty |
| Increasing age | Overheating of the testes, smoking, heavy drinking and drug misuse |

Doctors test both partners. Treatments include antibiotics for infections, hormones that stimulate ovulation, surgery to open blocked tubes and **in vitro fertilisation (IVF)**, in which eggs are fertilised outside the body and an embryo is placed in the uterus. Avoiding and promptly treating sexually transmitted infections prevents many cases.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying fertilisation happens in the uterus"}
Fertilisation happens in the **oviduct**. The embryo then moves down to the uterus and implants there.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling the whole of semen 'sperm'"}
Semen is sperm **plus** fluid from the prostate gland and other glands.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the egg swims to the sperm"}
The egg cannot move by itself. It is moved along the oviduct by **cilia** and muscle contractions.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying fertilisation is when a sperm touches the egg"}
Fertilisation is the **fusion of the nuclei** of the sperm and the egg.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Male: testes make sperm and testosterone; the scrotum keeps them cool; sperm ducts carry sperm; the prostate gland adds fluid to make semen; the urethra and penis carry semen out.
- Female: ovaries release eggs and make hormones; oviducts carry eggs and are where fertilisation happens; the uterus holds the developing fetus; the cervix and vagina lead to the outside.
- Fertilisation is the fusion of the nuclei of a sperm and an egg, forming a zygote.
- Sperm have a flagellum, many mitochondria and an acrosome with enzymes; eggs are large, with energy stores and a jelly coat that changes after fertilisation.
- ZIMSEC: sperm pass from testis to sperm duct, urethra, vagina, cervix, uterus and oviduct; infertility can be caused by blocked tubes, low sperm counts, hormone problems and untreated infections.
:::
