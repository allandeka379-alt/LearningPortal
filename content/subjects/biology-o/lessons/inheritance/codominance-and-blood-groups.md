## Introduction

Before a patient at Parirenyatwa Hospital in Harare receives a blood transfusion, the blood is tested to find its **blood group**. Giving the wrong group can be dangerous, so blood banks label every bag A, B, AB or O. Your blood group was decided at fertilisation by the alleles you inherited from your parents.

The ABO blood groups follow rules a little different from the polled and horned cattle of simple dominance: two of the alleles are **codominant**. For Cambridge candidates this is **Supplement** content; ZIMSEC candidates study it as part of monohybrid inheritance. You should already be able to draw a genetic diagram for a monohybrid cross.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Codominance"}
A situation in which both alleles in a heterozygous organism contribute to the phenotype.
:::

:::definition{term="ABO blood groups"}
The four blood groups A, B, AB and O, controlled by one gene with three alleles: $\text{I}^\text{A}$, $\text{I}^\text{B}$ and $\text{I}^\text{o}$.
:::

:::definition{term="Multiple alleles"}
A gene that has more than two alleles in a population. Each person still carries only **two** of them, one from each parent.
:::

## The ABO alleles

The gene for the ABO blood groups has three alleles:

- $\text{I}^\text{A}$ leads to a marker called antigen A on the surface of the red blood cells;
- $\text{I}^\text{B}$ leads to antigen B;
- $\text{I}^\text{o}$ leads to neither antigen.

$\text{I}^\text{A}$ and $\text{I}^\text{B}$ are **codominant**: a person who has both makes both antigens and has blood group AB. Both are **dominant** to $\text{I}^\text{o}$, which is **recessive**.

:::keyfact{title="The six genotypes and four blood groups"}
| Genotype | Blood group (phenotype) |
| --- | --- |
| $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{A}$ or $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{o}$ | A |
| $\text{I}^\text{B}\text{I}^\text{B}$ or $\text{I}^\text{B}\text{I}^\text{o}$ | B |
| $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{B}$ | AB |
| $\text{I}^\text{o}\text{I}^\text{o}$ | O |
:::

Notice that groups A and B each have two possible genotypes, but groups AB and O have only one. A person with group O is always homozygous recessive.

The notation matters: the gene is written with the letter **I**, and the allele is shown by the superscript. Using A, B and O on their own, or a capital and a small letter, would hide the fact that $\text{I}^\text{A}$ and $\text{I}^\text{B}$ are codominant.

## Predicting blood groups

:::worked-solution[Example 1: group A × group B, both heterozygous]
Parents: blood group A ($\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{o}$) × blood group B ($\text{I}^\text{B}\text{I}^\text{o}$).
1. Gametes: the group A parent gives $\text{I}^\text{A}$ or $\text{I}^\text{o}$; the group B parent gives $\text{I}^\text{B}$ or $\text{I}^\text{o}$.
2. Punnett square:

| Gametes | $\text{I}^\text{B}$ | $\text{I}^\text{o}$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\text{I}^\text{A}$ | $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{B}$ (AB) | $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{o}$ (A) |
| $\text{I}^\text{o}$ | $\text{I}^\text{B}\text{I}^\text{o}$ (B) | $\text{I}^\text{o}\text{I}^\text{o}$ (O) |

3. Offspring: 1 AB : 1 A : 1 B : 1 O.
4. Each child has a $\frac{1}{4}$ chance of each blood group: these parents could have children of all four groups.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: group AB × group O]
Parents: blood group AB ($\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{B}$) × blood group O ($\text{I}^\text{o}\text{I}^\text{o}$).
1. Gametes: $\text{I}^\text{A}$ or $\text{I}^\text{B}$ from the AB parent; only $\text{I}^\text{o}$ from the O parent.
2. Offspring: $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{o}$ and $\text{I}^\text{B}\text{I}^\text{o}$, in equal numbers.
3. Phenotypes: 1 group A : 1 group B.
4. None of the children can be group AB or group O, the groups of their own parents.
:::

### Working backwards

You can also use blood groups to work out genotypes. A child with group O must be $\text{I}^\text{o}\text{I}^\text{o}$, so **each** parent must have passed on $\text{I}^\text{o}$. A parent with group AB has no $\text{I}^\text{o}$ allele, so a group AB parent can never have a group O child.

## Codominance in cattle: the roan coat

In Shorthorn cattle, the alleles for red coat ($\text{C}^\text{R}$) and white coat ($\text{C}^\text{W}$) are codominant. A heterozygous animal, $\text{C}^\text{R}\text{C}^\text{W}$, has a **roan** coat: a mixture of red hairs and white hairs growing side by side. Both alleles show in the phenotype.

:::worked-solution[Example 3: roan × roan]
1. Parents: roan bull ($\text{C}^\text{R}\text{C}^\text{W}$) × roan cow ($\text{C}^\text{R}\text{C}^\text{W}$).
2. Gametes from each parent: $\text{C}^\text{R}$ or $\text{C}^\text{W}$.
3. Offspring genotypes: 1 $\text{C}^\text{R}\text{C}^\text{R}$ : 2 $\text{C}^\text{R}\text{C}^\text{W}$ : 1 $\text{C}^\text{W}\text{C}^\text{W}$.
4. Phenotypes: 1 red : 2 roan : 1 white.
5. With codominance the phenotype ratio is the same as the genotype ratio, because every genotype looks different.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking group O is dominant because it is common"}
$\text{I}^\text{o}$ is **recessive**. Group O is common in many populations because the allele is common, not because it is dominant.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting that groups A and B have two genotypes"}
A person with group A could be $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{A}$ or $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{o}$. Read the question, or the family's blood groups, to decide which.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying an AB parent can have a group O child"}
An AB parent passes on $\text{I}^\text{A}$ or $\text{I}^\text{B}$, never $\text{I}^\text{o}$, so none of their children can be group O.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling a blend codominance"}
In some flowers, red × white gives offspring that are evenly **pink**. That is a blend of the two colours, not both colours showing: biologists call it incomplete dominance. In codominance both features appear, like the red **and** white hairs of a roan cow or the A **and** B antigens of group AB. If a question tells you which pattern to use, follow the question.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write the ABO alleles exactly as $\text{I}^\text{A}$, $\text{I}^\text{B}$ and $\text{I}^\text{o}$, and give a key for any other codominant alleles. Using different letters for alleles of the same gene loses marks.
:::

:::exam-tip
In a genetic diagram, show the gametes with one allele each, and give the offspring's blood groups as phenotypes ("group AB"), not just genotypes.
:::

:::exam-tip
For "explain" questions about blood groups, say which alleles are codominant and which is recessive before you draw the diagram.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- In codominance both alleles of a heterozygote contribute to the phenotype.
- The ABO gene has three alleles: $\text{I}^\text{A}$ and $\text{I}^\text{B}$ are codominant, and both are dominant to $\text{I}^\text{o}$.
- Group A is $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{A}$ or $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{o}$; group B is $\text{I}^\text{B}\text{I}^\text{B}$ or $\text{I}^\text{B}\text{I}^\text{o}$; group AB is $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{B}$; group O is $\text{I}^\text{o}\text{I}^\text{o}$.
- $\text{I}^\text{A}\text{I}^\text{o}$ × $\text{I}^\text{B}\text{I}^\text{o}$ gives 1 A : 1 B : 1 AB : 1 O; AB × O gives 1 A : 1 B.
- Roan cattle ($\text{C}^\text{R}\text{C}^\text{W}$) show codominance: red and white hairs together; roan × roan gives 1 red : 2 roan : 1 white.
:::
