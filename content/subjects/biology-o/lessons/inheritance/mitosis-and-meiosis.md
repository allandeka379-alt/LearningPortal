## Introduction

A graze on your knee heals in a week. A baby who began as a single cell grows into a body of trillions of cells. A farmer plants sweet potato vine cuttings from one plant, and every new plant is identical to the parent. Yet no two brothers or sisters (apart from identical twins) are exactly alike. Two kinds of cell division explain all of this: **mitosis** and **meiosis**.

You should already know that the nucleus contains chromosomes made of DNA, and how gametes and fertilisation are involved in reproduction.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Chromosome"}
A thread-like structure of DNA, found in the nucleus, that carries genes.
:::

:::definition{term="Diploid nucleus"}
A nucleus containing two sets of chromosomes. Human body cells are diploid, with 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs.
:::

:::definition{term="Haploid nucleus"}
A nucleus containing a single set of unpaired chromosomes. Human gametes are haploid, with 23 chromosomes.
:::

:::definition{term="Mitosis"}
Nuclear division giving rise to genetically identical cells.
:::

:::definition{term="Meiosis"}
A reduction division in which the chromosome number is halved from diploid to haploid, resulting in genetically different cells.
:::

:::definition{term="Stem cell"}
An unspecialised cell that divides by mitosis to produce daughter cells that can become specialised for specific functions.
:::

## Chromosomes in human cells

Human body cells have **46 chromosomes in 23 pairs**: one chromosome of each pair came from the mother and one from the father. Gametes (sperm and eggs) have **23 chromosomes**, one from each pair. Different species have different numbers; for example, the body cells of maize have 20 chromosomes, so its gametes have 10.

## Mitosis

1. **Before** mitosis, every chromosome is copied exactly, so each chromosome is made of two identical copies held together.
2. **During** mitosis, the copies separate, and one copy of every chromosome goes to each end of the cell.
3. Two new nuclei form, each with exactly the same chromosomes as the original nucleus: 46 in a human cell.
4. The cytoplasm divides, giving **two genetically identical cells**.

### What mitosis is used for

- **Growth**: a zygote becomes an embryo, a baby and an adult; a seedling becomes a tree.
- **Repair** of damaged tissues: healing a cut or a broken bone.
- **Replacement** of cells that wear out: skin cells, cells lining the gut, and blood cells, which are made in the bone marrow.
- **Asexual reproduction**: potato tubers, sweet potato vine cuttings and sugar cane setts all grow into plants identical to the parent.

:::example{title="Example 1: counting cells"}
A zygote divides by mitosis, and then every new cell divides again, and so on. After 1 round there are 2 cells, after 2 rounds 4 cells, and after 3 rounds $2 \times 2 \times 2 = 2^3 = 8$ cells. Every one of these cells has the same 46 chromosomes as the zygote, because mitosis copies the chromosomes exactly.
:::

### Stem cells

As an embryo develops, its cells **specialise** to become nerve cells, muscle cells, blood cells and so on. **Stem cells** are cells that have not yet specialised. They divide by mitosis to produce daughter cells that can become specialised.

- **Embryonic stem cells**, in a very early embryo, can become almost any type of cell.
- **Adult stem cells**, such as those in the **bone marrow**, produce a limited range of cells: bone marrow stem cells make new blood cells throughout life.

In plants, cells in the **meristems** at the tips of shoots and roots divide by mitosis in a similar way, producing new cells for growth.

## Meiosis

Meiosis happens only in the **reproductive organs**: the **testes** and **ovaries** in humans, and the **anthers** and **ovules** in flowering plants.

1. It is a **reduction division**: a diploid cell divides to form cells with half the number of chromosomes. In humans, 46 becomes 23.
2. The chromosomes of each pair are shared out, so the cells produced are **haploid** (one set) and become **gametes**.
3. The chromosomes are shared out in different combinations, so the cells are **genetically different** from each other and from the parent cell.
4. One cell that starts meiosis divides twice and produces four haploid cells. (In the ovary, only one of the four becomes an egg.)

### Why meiosis is needed

At fertilisation, a haploid sperm (23) fuses with a haploid egg (23) to form a **diploid zygote** (46). Without meiosis, the chromosome number would double in every generation. Meiosis also produces **genetic variation**, because every gamete has a different combination of chromosomes.

:::example{title="Example 2: maize"}
A maize plant has 20 chromosomes in each body cell. Its pollen nuclei and egg cells are made by meiosis, so they each have **10**. When a pollen nucleus fertilises an egg cell, the zygote has $10 + 10 = 20$ chromosomes. As the seed germinates and the plant grows, its new cells are made by mitosis, so each keeps **20**.
:::

## Mitosis and meiosis compared

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where | Throughout the body, wherever cells grow or are replaced | Only in the reproductive organs (testes, ovaries, anthers, ovules) |
| Number of cells produced | 2 | 4 |
| Chromosome number | Kept the same (diploid to diploid; 46 to 46 in humans) | Halved (diploid to haploid; 46 to 23 in humans) |
| Genetic make-up of the new cells | Identical to each other and to the parent cell | Different from each other and from the parent cell |
| Used for | Growth, repair, replacement, asexual reproduction | Making gametes for sexual reproduction |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying human gametes are made by mitosis"}
Gametes are made by **meiosis**, which halves the chromosome number. Mitosis would give gametes with 46 chromosomes.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying chromosomes are copied during meiosis or mitosis"}
Chromosomes are copied **before** the division starts; during the division the copies are separated.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking haploid means half a pair"}
Haploid means **one complete set** of chromosomes: one of each pair, 23 in humans.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking mitosis produces variation"}
Mitosis produces **genetically identical** cells. Variation comes from meiosis and fertilisation.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Learn the two definitions word for word: mitosis gives **genetically identical** cells; meiosis is a **reduction division**, diploid to haploid, giving **genetically different** cells.
:::

:::exam-tip
In chromosome-number questions, ask: was the cell made by mitosis (number kept) or by meiosis (number halved)? A zygote has the full diploid number.
:::

:::exam-tip
For roles of mitosis, give the four headings: growth, repair, replacement of cells and asexual reproduction, each with an example.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Human body cells are diploid (46 chromosomes in 23 pairs); gametes are haploid (23 chromosomes).
- Mitosis: chromosomes are copied first, then the copies separate, giving two genetically identical cells with the same chromosome number.
- Mitosis is used for growth, repair, replacement of cells and asexual reproduction.
- Stem cells are unspecialised cells that divide by mitosis to produce cells that can specialise.
- Meiosis, in the reproductive organs, is a reduction division (diploid to haploid) producing genetically different gametes; fertilisation restores the diploid number.
:::
