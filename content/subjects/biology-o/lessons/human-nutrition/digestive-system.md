## Introduction

Within a day of eating a plate of sadza and beans, the useful parts of the meal are in your blood and being used by your cells, and the rest is on its way out of your body. The journey happens in the **alimentary canal**, a muscular tube several metres long that runs from the mouth to the anus, with glands and organs along the way that add juices to it.

This lesson names the parts of the digestive system, explains what each part does and shows you how to recognise them on a diagram. You should already know that large food molecules such as starch, proteins and fats must be broken down before the body can use them.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Ingestion"}
Taking food and drink into the body through the mouth.
:::

:::definition{term="Digestion"}
The breakdown of large, insoluble food molecules into small, soluble molecules that can be absorbed. It can be mechanical (physical) or chemical (by enzymes).
:::

:::definition{term="Absorption"}
The movement of digested food molecules and water from the intestines into the blood.
:::

:::definition{term="Assimilation"}
The uptake of absorbed nutrients by body cells and their use to build or run the cells.
:::

:::definition{term="Egestion"}
Passing out food that has not been digested or absorbed, as faeces, through the anus.
:::

## The parts of the system

### The alimentary canal, in order

1. **Mouth.** Food is ingested. Teeth chew it into smaller pieces. Saliva from the salivary glands moistens it, and the enzyme amylase in saliva starts to digest starch. The tongue rolls the food into a ball (a bolus) and pushes it back for swallowing.
2. **Oesophagus (gullet).** A muscular tube that carries food from the throat, through the chest and diaphragm, to the stomach. Its job is to move food along, not to digest it.
3. **Stomach.** A muscular bag that holds food for a few hours. Its walls churn the food, mixing it with gastric juice. The juice contains **hydrochloric acid**, which kills many microorganisms, and a **protease** that starts protein digestion. A ring of muscle at the exit lets the liquid food into the small intestine a little at a time.
4. **Small intestine.** The first part, the **duodenum**, receives bile from the liver and pancreatic juice from the pancreas, and digestion continues there. The long, coiled **ileum** completes digestion and **absorbs** the digested food and most of the water into the blood.
5. **Large intestine.** The **colon** absorbs more water (and some mineral ions) from the undigested remains. The **rectum** stores the faeces until they are egested through the **anus**.

### The associated organs

Food never passes through these organs, but they are essential to digestion.

| Organ | What it does for digestion |
| --- | --- |
| Salivary glands | Make saliva, containing amylase and mucus, and release it into the mouth |
| Liver | Makes **bile**; also receives the absorbed nutrients from the intestine and processes them (assimilation) |
| Gall bladder | **Stores** bile and releases it through the bile duct into the duodenum |
| Pancreas | Makes pancreatic juice containing amylase, protease and lipase and releases it into the duodenum |

:::keyfact{title="Liver makes bile, gall bladder stores it"}
The gall bladder does not make bile: it is a storage bag beneath the liver.
:::

### Recognising the organs on a diagram

On a front-view diagram of the digestive system:

- the **oesophagus** is a narrow tube running down the centre of the chest, behind the windpipe;
- the **stomach** is a J-shaped bag on the body's left side, just below the diaphragm;
- the **liver** is the largest organ in the abdomen, on the body's right side just under the diaphragm, partly covering the stomach;
- the **gall bladder** is a small sac tucked under the liver;
- the **pancreas** is a flattened, leaf-shaped gland lying below and behind the stomach, in the curve of the duodenum;
- the **small intestine** forms the many coils filling the lower abdomen;
- the **colon** is the wider tube that frames those coils on three sides, leading to the **rectum** and **anus**.

Remember that a diagram shows the person's left on **your** right, as if they were facing you.

## The five processes

| Process | Where it happens | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ingestion | Mouth | Taking a bite of food |
| Digestion | Mouth, stomach, small intestine | Amylase breaking starch down into maltose |
| Absorption | Small intestine (most); colon (some water) | Glucose passing from the ileum into the blood |
| Assimilation | Body cells | Liver cells storing glucose as glycogen; muscle cells using amino acids to make proteins |
| Egestion | Anus | Faeces leaving the body |

:::example{title="Following a meal of sadza and kapenta"}
- **Mouth:** Tendai chews the sadza and kapenta; amylase in saliva starts digesting the starch in the sadza.
- **Oesophagus:** each swallowed ball of food is carried down to the stomach.
- **Stomach:** the walls churn the food; acid kills bacteria; protease starts digesting the kapenta protein.
- **Duodenum and ileum:** bile and pancreatic juice arrive; starch, protein and fat are fully digested; glucose, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol are absorbed.
- **Liver:** the absorbed nutrients are carried to the liver, then around the body, and are assimilated by cells.
- **Colon and rectum:** water is absorbed from the undigested fibre; the faeces are stored and finally egested.
:::

## Egestion is not excretion

**Egestion** removes food that was never absorbed: it never entered the body's cells. **Excretion** removes waste products made **inside** cells by metabolism, such as carbon dioxide (breathed out) and urea (in urine). Faeces are mostly undigested fibre, bacteria, dead cells from the gut lining, mucus and water.

## Peristalsis (ZIMSEC)

The wall of the alimentary canal contains two layers of muscle: **circular muscles** running round it and **longitudinal muscles** running along it. Waves of contraction called **peristalsis** push food along:

- **behind** the food, the circular muscles contract, so the tube narrows and squeezes the food forward;
- **in front of** the food, the circular muscles relax and the longitudinal muscles contract, so the tube widens and shortens, ready to receive the food.

Mucus lubricates the food. Peristalsis does not depend on gravity, which is why astronauts can swallow in space. Fibre in the diet helps peristalsis, because the muscles have something bulky to push against.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Sending food through the liver or pancreas"}
Food travels only along the alimentary canal. The liver, gall bladder, pancreas and salivary glands add juices to it through ducts.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the gall bladder makes bile"}
The liver makes bile; the gall bladder stores it.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying digested food is absorbed in the stomach or large intestine"}
Nearly all digested food is absorbed in the small intestine. The colon absorbs mainly water and some mineral ions.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling egestion excretion"}
Undigested food in faeces was never part of the body's cells, so passing it out is egestion, not excretion.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When asked to **identify** organs on a diagram, follow the tube from the mouth downwards and name the associated organs separately. Spell **oesophagus** and **duodenum** carefully.
:::

:::exam-tip
"State **one** function" means one precise job: "the gall bladder stores bile" scores; "the gall bladder helps digestion" does not.
:::

:::exam-tip
Learn the five processes in order (ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, egestion) and be ready to give the place where each happens.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Food passes through the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine (duodenum, ileum), large intestine (colon, rectum) and anus.
- The salivary glands, liver, gall bladder and pancreas are associated organs: food does not pass through them.
- The liver makes bile, the gall bladder stores it, and the pancreas makes pancreatic juice; both enter the duodenum.
- Most digestion and nearly all absorption take place in the small intestine; the colon absorbs water.
- Egestion removes undigested food as faeces; excretion removes waste made by cells.
- ZIMSEC: peristalsis is waves of circular and longitudinal muscle contraction that push food along the canal.
:::
