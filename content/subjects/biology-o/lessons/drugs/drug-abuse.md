## Introduction

A school health club in Gweru is planning a drug-awareness week. Its members want to explain, calmly and with facts, why misusing alcohol, tobacco and other drugs harms young people, their families and their communities. In Zimbabwe, recent concerns include the misuse of cough syrups containing codeine and the spread of crystal methamphetamine.

In this lesson you will learn what drug misuse and dependence are, the effects of the main drugs that are misused, and how to avoid drug misuse and where to find help. You should already know that a drug is any substance that changes chemical reactions in the body (see the lesson on medicinal drugs) and how synapses work.

:::note{title="Syllabus"}
This lesson covers ZIMSEC topic 8.21.1: the misuse of drugs such as alcohol, tobacco, cannabis and solvents, and their physical, mental and social effects. The effects of smoking on the lungs and gas exchange are covered in more detail with the gas exchange system.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Drug misuse"}
Taking a drug for a non-medical reason, or taking a medicine in a way, amount or frequency that harms the body, the mind or other people.
:::

:::definition{term="Dependence (addiction)"}
A condition in which a person feels unable to manage without a drug, because their body and brain have adapted to it.
:::

:::definition{term="Withdrawal symptoms"}
Unpleasant physical and mental effects, such as shaking, sweating, sickness, anxiety, sleeplessness and strong cravings, felt when a dependent person stops taking a drug.
:::

:::definition{term="Tolerance"}
The need to take more and more of a drug to get the same effect.
:::

## How misused drugs affect the body

Most misused drugs act on the **brain**, many of them at **synapses**. **Depressants**, such as alcohol, solvents and opioids like codeine, slow down the nervous system, so reactions become slower and judgement poorer. **Stimulants**, such as nicotine and methamphetamine, speed it up for a while. With repeated use the brain adapts: the user develops **tolerance**, needs larger doses and becomes **dependent**, and stopping brings **withdrawal symptoms**.

## Alcohol

Alcohol (ethanol) is found in beer, wine, spirits and traditional brews. It is a **depressant**.

- **Short-term effects**: relaxation at first, then poor judgement, loss of self-control, slurred speech, poor coordination and **slower reactions**. This leads to accidents, **drink-driving** crashes, violence and risky sexual behaviour, which increases the risk of HIV and unplanned pregnancy. Drinking a lot quickly can cause unconsciousness and can stop breathing.
- **Long-term effects**: the **liver**, which breaks down alcohol, is damaged; scar tissue replaces liver cells (**cirrhosis**) and liver cancer becomes more likely. Heavy drinking also damages the brain and memory, raises blood pressure, harms the heart and stomach, and leads to dependence.
- **In pregnancy**: alcohol crosses the placenta and can damage the developing brain of the fetus.

## Tobacco

**Nicotine** in tobacco is a highly addictive **stimulant**, so smokers quickly become dependent and find it hard to stop. Tobacco smoke also contains **tar**, which causes cancers of the lungs, mouth and throat, and **carbon monoxide**, which reduces the oxygen carried by the blood and contributes to heart disease. Smoking costs money every day, and other people breathe in second-hand smoke.

## Cannabis

Cannabis (*mbanje*) changes mood and perception. It impairs **memory**, **concentration** and **coordination** and slows reactions, which makes driving dangerous. Heavy, regular use, especially by young people, increases the risk of **mental illness**: anxiety, paranoia and **psychosis**, in which a person loses touch with reality. Smoking it damages the lungs, and users can become dependent. Possessing cannabis for recreational use is illegal in Zimbabwe.

## Solvents

Glue, petrol, paint thinners and aerosol sprays give off vapours that some people sniff. They are **depressants**, causing dizziness, hallucinations and loss of coordination. They damage the **brain**, liver and kidneys, and they can cause **sudden death**, even the first time, by making the heart beat irregularly or by suffocation.

## Other drugs of current concern

- **Codeine cough syrups**: codeine is an **opioid**. Misused in large amounts for its calming effect, it causes drowsiness, slowed breathing and dependence, with painful withdrawal.
- **Crystal methamphetamine** (known locally by names such as *mutoriro* or *guka*): a powerful **stimulant** and very addictive. It causes sleeplessness, paranoia, aggression and psychosis, and damages the heart and teeth.
- **Injecting drugs**: sharing needles passes **HIV** and hepatitis viruses from one person's blood to another's.

:::example{title="Example 1: sorting the effects of heavy drinking"}
| Physical | Mental | Social |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Liver cirrhosis | Dependence | Fights and domestic violence |
| High blood pressure | Depression and anxiety | Money spent on alcohol, not food or school fees |
| Injuries in accidents | Memory loss | Loss of a job; crime; family breakdown |
:::

## Avoiding drug misuse and getting help

- **Know the facts**, so you are not misled by friends or adverts.
- **Practise saying no** to peer pressure, and choose friends who respect your decision.
- Spend time on **sport, clubs, church or youth groups and hobbies**, and talk about stress instead of trying to escape it with drugs.
- **Getting help**: dependence is a health problem, not a sign of weakness. A person who is struggling should talk to a trusted adult, such as a parent, guardian, teacher or school counsellor, or to a health worker at a clinic. Rehabilitation services can help people stop. Someone who is heavily dependent on alcohol or opioids should stop with **medical help**, because withdrawal can be dangerous.

:::example{title="Example 2: helping a friend"}
Thandeka notices that her friend has started missing lessons, seems drowsy and has been drinking cough syrup every day. Instead of ignoring it or telling everyone, she talks to him privately, tells him she is worried, and encourages him to speak to the school counsellor. She also tells a trusted teacher, because his health may be at risk.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying alcohol is a stimulant"}
People may feel lively at first, but alcohol is a **depressant**: it slows down the nervous system and reactions.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking traditional brews or 'natural' drugs are harmless"}
Traditional brews contain alcohol, and cannabis is a plant, but both can harm the body and mind.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking medicines cannot be misused"}
Medicines such as codeine cough syrup can be misused and can cause dependence.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up tolerance and dependence"}
Tolerance means needing **more** of the drug for the same effect. Dependence means feeling **unable to manage without** it.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Sort effects into **physical**, **mental** and **social**, and give a specific example of each, such as "cirrhosis of the liver", not just "it damages the body".
:::

:::exam-tip
Define dependence and withdrawal precisely; examiners often ask for both together.
:::

:::exam-tip
For "how can drug misuse be avoided", give practical actions with reasons, such as joining a sports team to spend time with friends who do not use drugs.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Drug misuse is taking a drug for non-medical reasons, or in a harmful way or amount; repeated use leads to tolerance, dependence and withdrawal symptoms.
- Alcohol is a depressant: it slows reactions and impairs judgement (accidents, drink-driving) and damages the liver (cirrhosis), brain and heart.
- Nicotine in tobacco is addictive; cannabis impairs memory and increases the risk of mental illness; solvents damage the brain and can kill suddenly.
- Codeine syrups and crystal methamphetamine are current concerns; sharing needles spreads HIV.
- Avoid misuse by knowing the facts and resisting peer pressure; dependent people should get help from trusted adults and health workers.
:::
