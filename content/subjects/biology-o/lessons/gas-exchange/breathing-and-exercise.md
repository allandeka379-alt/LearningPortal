## Introduction

Run up a steep hill in Mutare and within a minute you are breathing faster and more deeply, and your heart is pounding. Your body is responding to the extra work your muscles are doing.

This lesson compares the air you breathe in with the air you breathe out, shows how to investigate the differences, and explains why breathing changes during exercise. You should already know how gas exchange happens in the alveoli and that respiration uses oxygen and produces carbon dioxide.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Inspired air"}
The air breathed in.
:::

:::definition{term="Expired air"}
The air breathed out.
:::

:::definition{term="Breathing rate"}
The number of breaths taken per minute.
:::

:::definition{term="Depth of breathing"}
The volume of air breathed in (or out) in one breath.
:::

## Inspired and expired air compared

These are approximate values for a person at rest.

| | Inspired air | Expired air |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oxygen | 21% | 16% |
| Carbon dioxide | 0.04% | 4% |
| Nitrogen | 78% | 78% |
| Water vapour | Varies, usually less | More (saturated) |
| Temperature (ZIMSEC) | That of the surroundings | Close to body temperature, so usually warmer |

Notice that expired air still contains plenty of oxygen (16%), which is why rescue breaths given to an unconscious person can keep them alive. Most of expired air is still nitrogen; only about 4% is carbon dioxide.

### Explaining the differences (Supplement)

- **Less oxygen:** oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood and is used by cells in **aerobic respiration**.
- **More carbon dioxide:** cells **produce** carbon dioxide in respiration; the blood carries it to the lungs, where it diffuses into the alveoli.
- **More water vapour:** water **evaporates** from the moist lining of the alveoli and airways into the air.
- **Warmer (ZIMSEC):** the air is warmed by the blood as it passes through the airways and lungs.
- **Nitrogen unchanged:** the body does not use nitrogen gas.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: how much of the oxygen is absorbed?]
Using the table, what percentage of the oxygen in inspired air is taken up by the body?
1. Oxygen absorbed $= 21 - 16 = 5$ percentage points.
2. As a percentage of the oxygen breathed in: $\dfrac{5}{21} \times 100 = 23.8\%$.
3. So only about a quarter of the oxygen breathed in is absorbed; the rest is breathed out again.
:::

## Investigating carbon dioxide with limewater

**Limewater** is a clear solution that turns **milky (cloudy)** when carbon dioxide is bubbled through it. A typical set-up uses two boiling tubes with the same volume of limewater, joined by tubes to a single mouthpiece:

- When the student **breathes in**, inspired air is drawn through the limewater in tube A.
- When the student **breathes out**, expired air bubbles through the limewater in tube B.

After a few breaths, tube B turns milky while tube A stays almost clear, because expired air contains much more carbon dioxide. **Hydrogencarbonate indicator** can be used instead: it changes from red (or orange) to yellow as the carbon dioxide concentration rises.

Other differences can be shown too: breathing onto a cold mirror produces condensation (water vapour), and a thermometer held in expired air shows it is warmer than the room (ZIMSEC).

## Breathing during exercise

During exercise both the **rate** and the **depth** of breathing increase. To investigate, count breaths for one minute at rest, then straight after a fixed exercise (such as two minutes of star jumps), and every minute afterwards until breathing returns to normal. A spirometer can measure the depth of each breath. (ZIMSEC: the **pulse rate** also rises during exercise, delivering more oxygen to the muscles.)

The volume of air breathed per minute is:

:::formula{name="Volume of air breathed per minute"}
$$
\text{volume per minute} = \text{breathing rate} \times \text{volume per breath}
$$
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: breathing at rest and during exercise]
At rest Tendai takes 14 breaths a minute of 0.5 dm³ each. During a race he takes 36 breaths a minute of 2.0 dm³ each. Compare the volumes of air he breathes per minute.
1. At rest: $14 \times 0.5 = 7.0\ \text{dm}^3$ per minute.
2. During the race: $36 \times 2.0 = 72\ \text{dm}^3$ per minute.
3. $\dfrac{72}{7.0} = 10.3$, so he breathes about ten times as much air each minute during the race.
:::

### Why breathing increases (Supplement)

1. Working muscles **respire faster**, so they produce more **carbon dioxide**.
2. The concentration of carbon dioxide in the **blood rises**.
3. The **brain detects** this increase.
4. The brain sends more nerve impulses to the **intercostal muscles and diaphragm**, which contract more often and more strongly.
5. Breathing becomes **faster and deeper**, so carbon dioxide is removed more quickly and more oxygen reaches the blood for respiration.

After exercise, breathing stays fast and deep for a while. During hard exercise some energy came from anaerobic respiration, producing lactic acid, and extra oxygen is needed to remove it (you will meet this oxygen debt in the lesson on anaerobic respiration).

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying expired air contains no oxygen"}
Expired air still contains about 16% oxygen; only about a quarter of the oxygen breathed in is absorbed.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying expired air is mostly carbon dioxide"}
Expired air is about 78% nitrogen and only about 4% carbon dioxide.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the brain detects a lack of oxygen during exercise"}
(Supplement) It is the **rise in carbon dioxide** in the blood that the brain detects.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying limewater tests for oxygen"}
Limewater turns milky with **carbon dioxide**.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"**Explain** the difference" needs a reason linked to the body: "less oxygen, **because** it diffuses into the blood and is used in respiration". Stating the numbers alone only **describes**.
:::

:::exam-tip
In practical questions, keep the **volume of limewater** and the **number of breaths** the same in both tubes, and say which tube turns milky **faster**.
:::

:::exam-tip
When you describe exercise data, mention **both** rate and depth, quote values and give the time taken to return to resting values.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Expired air has less oxygen (16% against 21%), more carbon dioxide (4% against 0.04%) and more water vapour than inspired air; ZIMSEC: it is also warmer.
- Limewater turns milky (hydrogencarbonate indicator turns yellow) faster with expired air, showing it contains more carbon dioxide.
- During exercise the rate and depth of breathing increase (and, ZIMSEC, the pulse rate rises).
- Volume of air per minute = breathing rate × volume per breath.
- Supplement: working muscles make more carbon dioxide; the brain detects the rise in the blood and makes the intercostal muscles and diaphragm work faster and harder.
:::
