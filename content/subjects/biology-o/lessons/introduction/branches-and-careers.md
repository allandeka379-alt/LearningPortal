## Introduction

Biology is a very wide subject. It covers everything from the chemicals inside a single cell to the way elephants change the woodland of a whole national park. No one person can be an expert in all of it, so biologists specialise in **branches** of biology.

Biology also matters far beyond the classroom. A nurse at a rural clinic, a veterinary officer vaccinating cattle, an agronomist advising maize and tobacco farmers, an ecologist counting elephants in Hwange National Park and a food technologist checking milk at a dairy all use biology every day. In this lesson you will learn the main branches of biology, what each one studies, and some of the careers that depend on them.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Biology"}
The scientific study of living organisms: their structure, how they work, how they interact with each other and with their surroundings, how they reproduce and how they change over time.
:::

:::definition{term="Branch of biology"}
A specialised area of biology that concentrates on one group of organisms (for example plants) or on one aspect of life (for example inheritance).
:::

## The main branches of biology

| Branch | What it studies | A question it could answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cytology (cell biology) | Cells: their structure and how they work | What structures are found inside a root hair cell? |
| Anatomy | The structure of organisms and their parts, often studied by dissection | Where are the kidneys, and how are they connected to the bladder? |
| Physiology | How the parts of organisms work: their functions and processes | How do the kidneys filter the blood? |
| Ecology | How organisms interact with each other and with their environment | How do elephants affect the msasa woodland in Hwange? |
| Genetics | Inheritance and variation: how characteristics are passed from parents to offspring | How is albinism passed from parents to their children? |
| Biotechnology | Using living organisms, or substances made by them, to make useful products | How can yeast be used to make bread rise? |
| Microbiology | Microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses and microscopic fungi | Which bacterium causes cholera, and how does it spread? |
| Zoology | Animals | How does a tigerfish take in oxygen under water? |
| Botany | Plants | How do maize roots absorb water and mineral ions? |

:::keyfact
Anatomy is about **structure** (what the parts are and where they are). Physiology is about **function** (how the parts work). A dissection of a heart is anatomy; measuring how the heart rate changes during exercise is physiology.
:::

:::note{title="Other branches you may meet"}
Entomology (insects, for example tsetse flies and mosquitoes), parasitology (parasites such as the malaria parasite and bilharzia worms), taxonomy (naming and classifying organisms) and biochemistry (the chemical reactions in living things).
:::

Branches overlap. Finding out how HIV damages white blood cells uses microbiology (the virus), cytology (the cells it attacks) and physiology (the effect on the immune system).

## Careers that use biology

| Career | What the work involves | Branches used most |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doctor | Diagnosing and treating illnesses such as malaria, pneumonia and diabetes | Anatomy, physiology, microbiology |
| Nurse | Caring for patients, giving medicines and vaccinations, and teaching communities about health | Physiology, microbiology |
| Pharmacist | Preparing and supplying medicines and advising people how to use them safely | Physiology, biochemistry |
| Research scientist | Planning and carrying out investigations, for example breeding drought-tolerant maize or testing new malaria treatments | Any branch, often genetics or microbiology |
| Ecologist or conservationist | Monitoring animal and plant populations, managing habitats and protecting endangered species | Ecology, zoology, botany |
| Dietitian | Planning balanced diets for patients (for example people with diabetes) and advising communities on good nutrition | Physiology, biochemistry |
| Agronomist or agricultural extension officer | Advising farmers on crop varieties, fertilisers, soil care, pests and diseases | Botany, genetics, ecology |
| Veterinary surgeon or veterinary officer | Preventing and treating diseases of livestock and pets, for example vaccinating cattle and controlling ticks by dipping | Anatomy, physiology, microbiology, zoology |
| Food technologist | Developing food products and checking that food is safe and of good quality, for example at a dairy or a mill | Microbiology, biotechnology |
| Environmental health officer | Inspecting water supplies, sanitation and places that sell food, to prevent diseases such as cholera | Microbiology, ecology |

## Linking problems to branches and careers

:::example{title="Yellowing maize near Chinhoyi"}
A farmer finds that her maize plants are stunted and their leaves have pale yellow streaks. An **agronomist** or extension officer visits. Using **botany**, they check whether the plants lack a mineral such as nitrogen. Using **microbiology** and **ecology**, they consider whether a virus spread by small insects (leafhoppers) is the cause. Their advice could be to apply fertiliser, to plant a resistant variety or to control the insects.
:::

:::example{title="A cholera outbreak in a town"}
**Environmental health officers** test the water supplies for the cholera bacterium (**microbiology**) and find the source. **Nurses** and **doctors** treat patients with oral rehydration, replacing lost water and salts (**physiology**). Researchers may study how the bacterium spreads and how to prevent future outbreaks.
:::

:::example{title="Elephants in Hwange National Park"}
**Wildlife ecologists** count the elephants, for example from the air, and study how they affect the trees, water holes and other animals (**ecology** and **zoology**). **Veterinary surgeons** treat injured animals, and park rangers protect them from poaching. Together they advise on how the park should be managed.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up anatomy and physiology"}
Anatomy is the structure of the body and its parts; physiology is how those parts work. "The heart has four chambers" is anatomy; "the heart beats faster during exercise" is physiology.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking microbiology means small animals"}
Microbiology is the study of microorganisms (bacteria, viruses, microscopic fungi and protoctists), most of which can only be seen with a microscope.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking biotechnology only means changing genes"}
Biotechnology includes traditional processes such as using yeast to make bread rise and bacteria to make yoghurt, as well as modern genetic modification.
:::

:::mistake{title="Swapping zoology and botany"}
Zoology is the study of animals; botany is the study of plants. A memory aid: a zoo keeps animals.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Name the branch" or "state the branch" needs only the name, spelt correctly: cytology, physiology, microbiology. Do not describe it unless you are asked to.
:::

:::exam-tip
When you are asked to "describe the work" of a career, give two or three specific activities ("vaccinates cattle against anthrax", "advises farmers on fertiliser use"), not a vague phrase such as "works with animals".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Biology is the scientific study of living organisms, divided into specialised branches.
- Cytology studies cells, anatomy studies structure, physiology studies function, ecology studies interactions with the environment, and genetics studies inheritance and variation.
- Biotechnology uses living organisms to make useful products; microbiology studies microorganisms; zoology studies animals; botany studies plants.
- Careers that use biology include medicine, nursing, pharmacy, research, ecology and conservation, dietetics, agronomy, veterinary science and food technology.
- Real problems, such as a disease outbreak or a failing crop, usually need several branches of biology at once.
:::
