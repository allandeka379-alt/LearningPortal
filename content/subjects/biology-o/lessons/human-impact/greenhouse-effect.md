## Introduction

In 2022 the water level of Lake Kariba fell so low, after poor rains and heavy use of its water, that electricity generation at the dam had to be cut sharply, and homes and factories across Zimbabwe faced long power cuts. Farmers across southern Africa know the damage that a drought or a late start to the rains can do to a maize crop.

Scientists expect droughts, heatwaves and floods to become more frequent and more severe in southern Africa as the world warms. The main cause of this warming is an increase in the gases that trap heat in the atmosphere. This lesson explains the greenhouse effect, where the extra gases come from, what their effects may be and what can be done. You should already know the carbon cycle.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Greenhouse gases"}
Gases in the atmosphere, such as carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour, that absorb thermal (infrared) energy radiated from the Earth's surface.
:::

:::definition{term="Greenhouse effect"}
The warming of the Earth's surface and lower atmosphere because greenhouse gases absorb thermal energy radiated from the surface and send some of it back towards the Earth.
:::

:::definition{term="Enhanced greenhouse effect"}
The extra warming caused by the increase in greenhouse gases, mainly carbon dioxide and methane, released by human activities.
:::

:::definition{term="Climate change"}
Long-term changes in the Earth's climate, such as rising temperatures and changing patterns of rainfall and extreme weather.
:::

## The natural greenhouse effect

1. Energy from the Sun passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface.
2. The warm surface gives off **thermal (infrared) energy**.
3. Greenhouse gases in the atmosphere **absorb** much of this thermal energy and **re-emit** some of it back towards the Earth.
4. This keeps the Earth's surface far warmer than it would otherwise be: an average of about 15 °C, instead of about −18 °C with no greenhouse gases.

The natural greenhouse effect is essential: without it most of the Earth would be frozen.

## The enhanced greenhouse effect

Human activities are increasing the concentrations of greenhouse gases. More thermal energy is trapped, so the average temperature of the Earth rises (**global warming**), which leads to **climate change**.

### Sources of carbon dioxide

- **Burning fossil fuels**: coal in power stations such as Hwange, petrol and diesel in vehicles, and fuels in industry.
- **Deforestation**: fewer trees remove carbon dioxide by photosynthesis, and the cut wood is burned or rots.
- **Veld fires**, which burn grassland and woodland.

### Sources of methane

Methane is present in much smaller amounts than carbon dioxide, but each molecule traps far more heat.

- **Cattle and other ruminants**: microorganisms in their stomachs break down grass and release methane, which the animals belch out.
- **Rice paddy fields**: bacteria decomposing plant material in waterlogged, airless soil release methane.
- **Landfill and rubbish dumps**: organic waste decomposes without oxygen, releasing methane.
- **Decomposing organic waste** in sewage works and manure heaps, and leaks from coal mines and gas wells.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: the rise in carbon dioxide]
At an observatory in Hawaii, far from local pollution, the mean concentration of carbon dioxide in the air was about 317 parts per million (ppm) in 1960 and about 414 ppm in 2020.
1. Increase $= 414 - 317 = 97$ ppm.
2. Percentage increase $= \dfrac{97}{317} \times 100 = 30.6\%$.
3. Mean rate of increase $= \dfrac{97}{60} \approx 1.6$ ppm per year, and the yearly rise has itself been getting larger.
:::

## Effects of climate change

The effects are expressed as **risks**: no single drought or flood can be blamed on climate change alone, but warming makes some events more likely.

- **Higher temperatures** and heatwaves stress people, livestock and crops; maize yields fall when it is very hot during flowering.
- **Changing rainfall**: more frequent droughts in southern Africa, and heavier storms and floods when rain does fall, leading to **crop failure**, food shortages and loss of livestock.
- **Water shortages**: lower river flows and dam levels, affecting drinking water, irrigation and hydroelectric power.
- **Loss of habitats and extinction**: species that cannot move or adapt quickly enough to the new conditions may die out.
- **Spread of disease**: as cooler highland areas warm, malaria mosquitoes may be able to survive at higher altitudes.
- **Melting ice and rising sea levels** around the world, flooding low-lying coasts.

## Alternative sources of energy

Replacing fossil fuels and firewood with energy sources that release little or no carbon dioxide reduces the enhanced greenhouse effect.

:::example{title="Example 2: cleaner energy in Zimbabwe"}
- **Solar energy**: Zimbabwe has strong sunshine for most of the year. Solar panels provide electricity for homes, schools, clinics and water pumps, and solar water heaters replace electric geysers. They release no carbon dioxide while working.
- **Hydroelectricity**: the power stations at Kariba generate electricity from the flow of water without burning fuel, although their output falls when droughts lower the lake.
- **Biogas**: in a sealed digester, bacteria break down cattle dung or human waste without oxygen, producing methane that is piped to a stove for cooking. Burning the methane turns it into carbon dioxide, which traps less heat, instead of letting it escape; it replaces firewood, saving trees; and the slurry left over is a good fertiliser.
- **Wind power** and **using energy efficiently** (efficient stoves and lights) also help, and **planting trees** removes carbon dioxide from the air.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the greenhouse effect with the ozone hole"}
They are different problems. The greenhouse effect is about gases trapping thermal energy. The ozone layer absorbs ultraviolet light; damage to it does not cause global warming.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the greenhouse effect is always harmful"}
The natural greenhouse effect is essential for life. The problem is the **enhanced** effect caused by extra gases from human activities.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting methane"}
Carbon dioxide is not the only gas. Methane, from cattle, rice fields and landfill, traps much more heat per molecule.
:::

:::mistake{title="Blaming one event on climate change"}
Say that climate change makes droughts or floods **more likely or more severe**; do not claim that it caused one particular event.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When explaining the enhanced greenhouse effect, use the sequence: more greenhouse gases → more thermal (infrared) energy absorbed and re-emitted towards the Earth → global warming → climate change.
:::

:::exam-tip
Match each gas to its sources: carbon dioxide (combustion of fossil fuels, deforestation); methane (cattle, rice paddies, landfill).
:::

:::exam-tip
For alternative energy, name the source **and** say why it reduces greenhouse gases (no fuel burned, or methane captured, or fewer trees cut).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour) absorb thermal energy from the Earth's surface and re-emit some back, keeping the Earth warm: the natural greenhouse effect.
- Human activities increase carbon dioxide (burning fossil fuels, deforestation, veld fires) and methane (cattle, rice paddies, landfill), causing the enhanced greenhouse effect.
- Global warming leads to climate change, with risks of drought, floods, crop failure, water shortages, loss of habitats and extinctions.
- Solar power, hydroelectricity, biogas, wind, efficient energy use and tree planting reduce greenhouse gas emissions.
:::
