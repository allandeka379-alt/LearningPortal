## Introduction

At planting time, maize farmers across Zimbabwe drop a handful of basal fertiliser such as Compound D into each planting station. About six weeks later, when the maize is knee-high, they apply a top dressing of ammonium nitrate. Where no fertiliser is used on tired, sandy soils, the maize is often short, pale and yellow, and the cobs are small.

Photosynthesis gives a plant its carbohydrates, but carbon, hydrogen and oxygen are not enough to build a living plant. Proteins need nitrogen, and chlorophyll needs magnesium. Plants absorb these elements from the soil as **mineral ions**. In this lesson you will learn why the main mineral ions are needed, what happens when they are missing and how fertilisers replace them.

:::note{title="Syllabus"}
Cambridge IGCSE requires **nitrate** and **magnesium** ions only. The sections on **phosphorus** and **potassium** are for ZIMSEC candidates.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Mineral ion"}
A charged particle (ion) that supplies an element a plant needs, such as a nitrate ion (which supplies nitrogen) or a magnesium ion. Plants absorb mineral ions dissolved in soil water and use them to make important substances. Mineral ions are not food: they provide no energy.
:::

:::definition{term="Deficiency"}
A shortage of a mineral ion, which causes typical symptoms such as stunted growth or yellow leaves.
:::

:::definition{term="Fertiliser"}
A substance added to soil to supply mineral ions, especially nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P) and potassium (K).
:::

## How plants absorb mineral ions

Mineral ions are dissolved in the water between soil particles. **Root hair cells** give the root a very large surface area. The concentration of many ions is higher inside the root hair cells than in the soil water, so the cells absorb them by **active transport**, which uses energy from respiration. The ions then travel up to the leaves in the **xylem**.

:::keyfact{title="Roots need oxygen to take up ions"}
Active transport needs energy from aerobic respiration, which needs oxygen. In waterlogged or compacted soil the roots are short of oxygen, so they absorb fewer mineral ions, even if the soil contains plenty.
:::

## Nitrate and magnesium (both syllabuses)

### Nitrate ions

Plants absorb nitrogen as **nitrate ions**. They combine the nitrogen with sugars from photosynthesis to make **amino acids**, which are joined to make **proteins**. Proteins are needed for growth: every new cell needs proteins, including enzymes. Chlorophyll also contains nitrogen.

**Nitrogen deficiency:** stunted (poor) growth with weak stems, and leaves that turn **yellow**, starting with the older, lower leaves.

### Magnesium ions

**Magnesium** is part of the **chlorophyll** molecule. Without it the plant cannot make enough chlorophyll, absorbs less light and photosynthesises less.

**Magnesium deficiency:** **yellowing between the veins** of the leaves (the veins stay green), starting with the older leaves.

## Phosphorus and potassium (ZIMSEC)

### Phosphorus

Phosphorus is absorbed as phosphate ions. It is used to make **ATP**, the molecule that carries energy in cells, and **DNA and RNA**, so it is needed wherever cells are dividing, especially in growing **roots**.

**Phosphorus deficiency:** poor root growth, stunted plants and dull, **purplish** leaves.

### Potassium

Potassium ions help keep the **ion and water balance** of cells, help guard cells open and close the stomata, and activate enzymes in photosynthesis and respiration. Potassium is important for flowering, fruiting and resistance to disease.

**Potassium deficiency:** **yellow or brown (scorched) leaf margins** and tips, and poor flowering and fruiting.

| Mineral ion | Used for | Deficiency symptoms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nitrate (N) | Amino acids, proteins, chlorophyll | Stunted growth; yellow leaves, older leaves first |
| Magnesium (Mg) | Chlorophyll | Yellowing between the veins |
| Phosphate (P), ZIMSEC | ATP, DNA, RNA; root growth | Poor roots; stunted; purplish leaves |
| Potassium (K), ZIMSEC | Ion and water balance; stomata; enzymes | Yellow or brown leaf margins; poor flowering and fruiting |

## Fertilisers on the farm

Crops remove mineral ions from the soil when they are harvested, and heavy rain washes soluble ions down out of reach of the roots (**leaching**). Farmers replace them with:

- **organic fertilisers** (cattle manure, compost), which release ions slowly and also improve soil structure;
- **inorganic fertilisers**, which supply known amounts of ions quickly. The label gives the N:P:K ratio, the relative amounts of the three main nutrients. Compound D, a common basal fertiliser for maize, is 7:14:7, so it is rich in phosphorus for root growth.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: nitrogen in a bag of top dressing]
A 50 kg bag of ammonium nitrate (AN) is labelled 34.5% N. How much nitrogen does it contain?

1. Write the percentage as a decimal: $34.5\% = 0.345$.
2. Mass of nitrogen $= 50 \times 0.345 = 17.25$ kg.
3. So each bag contains about 17 kg of nitrogen; the rest is mostly the oxygen and hydrogen of the compound.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: how many bags to buy]
An extension officer advises Rudo to apply 300 kg of Compound D per hectare. Her field is 0.6 hectares. Fertiliser is sold in 50 kg bags. How many bags must she buy?

1. Mass needed $= 300 \times 0.6 = 180$ kg.
2. Number of bags $= 180 \div 50 = 3.6$.
3. She cannot buy part of a bag, so she must buy **4 bags** (round up, not down).
:::

:::example{title="Example 3: diagnosing a deficiency"}
Tendai walks through his maize and finds four problems:

- Plants short and pale yellow all over, especially the lower leaves: **nitrogen** deficiency.
- Older leaves with yellow stripes between green veins: **magnesium** deficiency.
- Small plants with purplish leaves and weak roots: **phosphorus** deficiency.
- Leaves with brown, dried edges and small cobs: **potassium** deficiency.

Each symptom points to the job the missing ion does: no nitrate, no proteins for growth; no magnesium, no chlorophyll.
:::

:::example{title="Example 4: a water culture experiment"}
Seedlings are grown with their roots in jars of solution. Jar 1 has a **complete** nutrient solution (the control); the other jars each lack one ion. Distilled water is used so that no extra ions are present, air is bubbled through so the roots can respire, and the jars are covered with black paper so algae cannot grow. After four weeks the seedling lacking nitrate is short with yellow leaves, the one lacking magnesium has leaves yellow between the veins, and the control is tall and green.
:::

:::note{title="Too much fertiliser"}
Over-use wastes money, and nitrate and phosphate leached into rivers and dams cause algal blooms (eutrophication). A very concentrated fertiliser solution next to the roots can even draw water **out** of root cells by osmosis and damage them.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Minerals are plant food"}
Mineral ions supply elements, not energy. A plant's food (carbohydrate) is made by photosynthesis.
:::

:::mistake{title="Plants use nitrogen gas from the air"}
Most plants cannot use nitrogen gas. They absorb nitrogen from the soil as **nitrate ions**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the yellowing"}
Magnesium deficiency causes yellowing **between the veins**; nitrogen deficiency makes leaves yellow all over and the plant stunted.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ions enter roots by osmosis"}
Water enters by osmosis; most mineral ions are absorbed by **active transport**, which needs energy.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Link ion → substance → effect: "magnesium is needed to make **chlorophyll**, so less light is absorbed and photosynthesis slows, giving less glucose for growth."
:::

:::exam-tip
Describe symptoms precisely: "yellowing **between the veins**" earns the mark; "the leaves are unhealthy" does not.
:::

:::exam-tip
In fertiliser calculations, show the percentage as a decimal and round the number of bags **up**.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Root hair cells absorb mineral ions mainly by active transport, which needs oxygen for respiration.
- Nitrate is needed for amino acids and proteins; lack of it causes stunted growth and yellow leaves.
- Magnesium is needed for chlorophyll; lack of it causes yellowing between the veins.
- ZIMSEC: phosphorus for ATP, DNA and roots (purplish leaves, poor roots when short); potassium for ion balance, stomata and flowering (brown leaf margins when short).
- Fertilisers replace ions removed by crops and leaching; N:P:K labels show the main nutrients, and over-use pollutes water.
:::
