## Introduction

Small words cause big errors. *She is an university student*, *I like the football*, *There were less people than last year*: each sentence sounds wrong to a careful reader, and each would lose marks in a composition. Many languages spoken in Zimbabwe, including Shona and Ndebele, do not use articles the way English does, so this is an area where even strong writers slip.

Articles (*a, an, the*) and other **determiners** (*some, many, few, each, neither* ...) sit in front of nouns and tell the reader which one, whose, or how many. In the examination they appear in gap-fill exercises, in error-correction tasks and in rewriting tasks such as *Begin: Neither road ...*.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Determiner"}
A word placed before a noun (and before any adjectives) that shows which one, whose, or how many or how much: ***the** red bus*, ***my** sister*, ***several** goats*, ***little** water*.
:::

:::definition{term="Indefinite article"}
*A* or *an*: used with a singular countable noun when the thing is not a particular one, or is mentioned for the first time.
:::

:::definition{term="Definite article"}
*The*: used when both writer and reader know which particular thing is meant.
:::

:::definition{term="Countable and uncountable nouns"}
Countable nouns have plurals (*a pupil, three pupils*). Uncountable nouns have no plural and are not used with *a/an* (*water, information, advice, furniture*).
:::

## A or an? Listen to the sound

The choice depends on the **first sound** of the next word, not the first letter.

| *an* before a vowel sound | *a* before a consonant sound |
| --- | --- |
| an apple, an egg, an hour, an honest man, an heir | a horse, a hotel, a uniform, a university |
| an MBA, an X-ray, an SMS ('em', 'ex', 'es') | a European, a one-day match, a useful tool |

:::example{title="Listening to the first sound"}
*It took **an** hour to reach **a** university in **a** one-horse town.* The *h* of *hour* is silent; *university* begins with 'yoo'; *one* begins with 'w'.
:::

## When to use the

1. **Second mention**: *We saw **a** jackal. **The** jackal ran into the bush.*
2. **Shared knowledge**: *Please close **the** door.* (the door of this room)
3. **Unique things**: *the sun, the moon, the sky, the President, the equator*.
4. **Superlatives and ordinals**: *the tallest girl, the first prize*.
5. **Some place names**: rivers (*the Zambezi, the Save*), oceans and seas (*the Indian Ocean*), mountain ranges (*the Chimanimani Mountains*), deserts (*the Kalahari*), and countries with plural or 'union' names (*the United Kingdom, the Netherlands*).
6. **Musical instruments** after *play*: *She plays **the** mbira.*

## When to use no article

- Plural and uncountable nouns used **in general**: *Cattle need water. Education is important.*
- Most countries, provinces, towns, single lakes and single mountains: *Zimbabwe, Matabeleland, Mutare, Lake Kariba, Mount Nyangani*.
- Meals, sports and school subjects: *We had lunch. They play football. I enjoy geography.*
- Some places when we mean their main purpose: *go to school, go to church, in hospital, in bed*.

:::example{title="General and particular"}
- *Water is essential for life.* (water in general: no article)
- ***The** water in this borehole is salty.* (particular water: *the*)
- *My brother is in hospital.* (as a patient) / *I visited him at **the** hospital.* (the building)
:::

## Some and any

**Some** is used in positive statements and in offers or requests; **any** in negatives and most questions.

:::example{title="Some and any"}
- *We saw **some** zebras.*
- *We did not see **any** elephants.* (not *did not see no elephants*)
- *Would you like **some** tea?* (an offer)
- *Have you got **any** questions?*
:::

## Quantifiers: much, many, few, little

| With plural countable nouns | With uncountable nouns |
| --- | --- |
| many pupils | much water |
| a few friends (some) | a little sugar (some) |
| few friends (hardly any) | little hope (hardly any) |
| fewer people | less rain |

The difference between *few* and *a few* is about attitude:

:::example{title="Few and a few"}
- *Fortunately, I had **a few** friends who helped me.* (some: positive)
- *Sadly, **few** people came to the meeting.* (hardly any: negative)
- *There was **little** hope of finding the goats, so the boys gave up.* (hardly any)
- *Add **a little** salt.* (a small amount)
:::

In formal writing use **fewer** with countable nouns and **less** with uncountable ones: *fewer people, fewer cars; less rain, less money*.

## Each, every, either, neither, both, all

- **Each** and **every** take a **singular** noun and verb: *Each pupil **has** a locker. Every seat **was** taken.* *Each* can refer to two things and can be followed by *of* (*each of the two boys*); *every* needs three or more and is never followed directly by *of*.
- **Either** (one or the other) and **neither** (not one and not the other) refer to **two** and take a singular noun and verb: *Neither road **leads** to Masvingo.*
- **Both** (two) and **all** (three or more) take plural nouns: *both hands, all the pupils*.

:::example{title="Rewriting with neither"}
*The road through the forest is not safe at night, and the road along the river is not safe either.*

→ ***Neither road is safe at night.*** *Neither* already means *not*, so do not add another negative.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Following the letter instead of the sound"}
*An university* and *a hour* are wrong. Say the word aloud: *a university*, *an hour*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using a or an with uncountable nouns"}
*An information*, *an advice* and *a furniture* are wrong. Use *some information*, *a piece of advice*, *an item of furniture*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adding the to town and country names"}
*I live in the Gweru* and *She visited the Zambia* are wrong. Towns and most countries take no article.
:::

:::mistake{title="Less with countable nouns"}
*There were less pupils this year* should be *fewer pupils*.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In a gap-fill passage, ask two questions for every noun: is it countable or uncountable, and is it a particular one the reader already knows? The answers decide the article or determiner.
:::

:::exam-tip
In rewriting tasks beginning with *Neither* or *Either*, use a singular noun and a singular verb, and never add a second negative.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- *A/an* depends on the first sound: an hour, a university.
- *The* is for things already mentioned, shared knowledge, unique things, superlatives, rivers, oceans, mountain ranges and plural country names.
- No article for general plurals and uncountables, most countries, towns, single lakes and mountains, meals, sports and subjects.
- Some in positive statements and offers; any in negatives and questions.
- Many/few/fewer with countable nouns; much/little/less with uncountable ones. A few and a little are positive; few and little are negative.
- Each, every, either and neither take singular nouns and verbs.
:::
