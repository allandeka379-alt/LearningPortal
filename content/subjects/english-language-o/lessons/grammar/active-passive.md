## Introduction

*Vandals damaged the school fence.* / *The school fence was damaged by vandals.* / *The school fence was damaged.* All three sentences describe the same event, but each puts the spotlight in a different place. The first focuses on the vandals, the second on the fence, and the third leaves the vandals out altogether.

Changing a sentence from the **active** to the **passive** voice is one of the most common language tasks in ZIMSEC Paper 2 and in Cambridge exercises. It also matters in your own writing: reports, minutes, science write-ups and news articles use the passive often, while stories and friendly letters are usually livelier in the active. This lesson shows you how to make the passive in every tense, and when to use it.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Active voice"}
The subject does the action: ***The mechanic** repaired the bus.*
:::

:::definition{term="Passive voice"}
The subject receives the action: ***The bus** was repaired by the mechanic.*
:::

:::definition{term="Agent"}
The doer of the action in a passive sentence, introduced by *by*: *... by the mechanic*. It is often left out.
:::

:::definition{term="Transitive verb"}
A verb that takes an object: *repair the bus*, *write a letter*. Only transitive verbs can be made passive.
:::

## Forming the passive

The passive is made with the correct form of **be** + the **past participle** of the main verb. The tense is carried by *be*.

:::worked-solution[Changing active to passive, step by step]
Active: *The headmaster opened the new library.*
1. Find the object of the active verb: *the new library*. It becomes the new subject.
2. Find the tense of the active verb: *opened* is past simple.
3. Put *be* into that tense to agree with the new subject: *was*.
4. Add the past participle of the main verb: *opened*.
5. Add *by* + the old subject if the doer matters: *by the headmaster*.
Passive: ***The new library was opened by the headmaster.***
:::

## The passive in each tense

| Tense | Active | Passive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present simple | They **clean** the hall every day. | The hall **is cleaned** every day. |
| Present continuous | They **are cleaning** the hall. | The hall **is being cleaned**. |
| Past simple | They **cleaned** the hall. | The hall **was cleaned**. |
| Past continuous | They **were cleaning** the hall. | The hall **was being cleaned**. |
| Present perfect | They **have cleaned** the hall. | The hall **has been cleaned**. |
| Past perfect | They **had cleaned** the hall. | The hall **had been cleaned**. |
| Future simple | They **will clean** the hall. | The hall **will be cleaned**. |
| Modal | They **must clean** the hall. | The hall **must be cleaned**. |
| Modal perfect | They **should have cleaned** the hall. | The hall **should have been cleaned**. |

:::keyfact{title="Keep the tense"}
The passive sentence must be in the same tense as the active one. Only the form of *be* shows the tense; the main verb is always the past participle.
:::

:::example{title="Agreement in the passive"}
Active: *The council repairs the roads.*
Passive: *The roads **are** repaired by the council.* (plural subject *roads*, so *are*)
Active: *Someone has stolen my bicycle.*
Passive: *My bicycle **has** been stolen.* (singular subject, so *has*)
:::

## Pronouns change form

When a pronoun moves from object to subject (or the reverse), it changes case.

:::example{title="Pronoun changes"}
Active: *The teacher praised **us**.* → Passive: ***We** were praised by the teacher.*
Active: ***She** invited **them**.* → Passive: ***They** were invited by **her**.*
:::

## Verbs with two objects

Verbs such as *give, send, offer, show* and *award* have two objects, so they can make two passives. The one beginning with the person is more common.

:::example{title="Two possible passives"}
Active: *The school awarded Tafadzwa a prize.*
Passive 1: *Tafadzwa was awarded a prize (by the school).*
Passive 2: *A prize was awarded to Tafadzwa (by the school).*
:::

## When to keep or drop the agent

Leave out the agent when:

- the doer is unknown: *My phone has been stolen.*
- the doer is obvious: *The thief was arrested* (by the police).
- the doer is unimportant: *Maize is grown in all the provinces.*
- the doer is "people" or "someone": *English is spoken here.*

Keep the agent when it is new or important information: *The bridge was designed by a young engineer from Gwanda.*

## Choosing the voice

:::example{title="Passive in a report"}
*A meeting **was held** in the school hall on 12 March. Several complaints about the tuckshop prices **were raised**, and it **was agreed** that a new price list **would be displayed**.*
The report focuses on what happened, not on who did each thing, so the passive suits it.
:::

:::example{title="Active in a story"}
Weak: *The door was slowly opened and a torch was shone into the room.*
Better: *Someone slowly opened the door and shone a torch into the room.*
In narrative writing the active voice is more direct and dramatic.
:::

## Passive to active

To change back, find the agent (after *by*) and make it the subject. If there is no agent, choose a suitable subject such as *someone*, *people* or *they*.

:::example{title="Passive to active"}
Passive: *The winners will be announced by the judges at noon.*
Active: *The judges will announce the winners at noon.*
Passive: *The windows have been broken.*
Active: *Someone has broken the windows.*
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Changing the tense"}
*They are painting the classroom* → *The classroom was painted* is wrong: the active is present continuous, so the passive must be *The classroom is being painted*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the past simple instead of the participle"}
*The letter was wrote by Rudo* is wrong. Use the past participle: *was written*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Making intransitive verbs passive"}
Verbs without an object cannot be passive: *The accident was happened* is wrong. Write *The accident happened.*
:::

:::mistake{title="Keeping useless agents"}
*The thief was arrested by the police* is acceptable, but *English is spoken by people in many countries* sounds clumsy; drop *by people*.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In a rewrite item with a given beginning, the beginning tells you which word becomes the subject. Copy it exactly, then add the correct form of *be* and the participle.
:::

:::exam-tip
Check three things in every passive answer: the tense is the same as the original, *be* agrees with the new subject, and the main verb is a past participle.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Passive = correct form of *be* + past participle; the object of the active becomes the subject.
- The tense is shown by *be* and must match the active sentence.
- Modals: modal + *be* + participle (*must be done*, *should have been done*).
- Pronouns change case (*us* → *we*, *she* → *by her*).
- Drop the agent when it is unknown, obvious or unimportant; keep it when it is important.
- Use the passive in reports and formal writing; prefer the active in stories.
:::
