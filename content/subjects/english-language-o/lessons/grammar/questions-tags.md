## Introduction

Every sentence has a job. A **statement** gives information, a **question** asks for it, a **command** tells someone to do something and an **exclamation** shows strong feeling. In your own writing you need all four: questions to open a speech (*Have you ever wondered where our rubbish goes?*), commands in a set of instructions, and the odd exclamation in a story.

In ZIMSEC Paper 2 and in Cambridge language work you may be asked to supply a question tag, to turn a statement into a question, to rewrite a blunt question politely, or to punctuate a sentence correctly. Most errors come from word order and from choosing the wrong auxiliary verb, so this lesson focuses on those.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Yes/no question"}
A question that can be answered with *yes* or *no*. It begins with an auxiliary or modal verb: *Is it raining? Can you swim?*
:::

:::definition{term="Wh- question"}
A question that begins with a question word (*who, what, which, where, when, why, whose, how*) and asks for information.
:::

:::definition{term="Question tag"}
A short question added to the end of a statement to check information or ask for agreement: *It's hot today, **isn't it**?*
:::

:::definition{term="Indirect question"}
A question placed inside a longer, more polite sentence, with statement word order: *Could you tell me **where the post office is**?*
:::

## Forming questions

To make a question, put the **auxiliary** (be, have, do or a modal) **before the subject**.

| Statement | Question |
| --- | --- |
| She **is** reading. | **Is** she reading? |
| They **have** left. | **Have** they left? |
| He **can** drive. | **Can** he drive? |
| You **live** in Kadoma. | **Do** you live in Kadoma? |
| She **passed** the test. | **Did** she pass the test? |

When there is no auxiliary in the statement (present or past simple), add **do, does** or **did** and use the **base form** of the main verb.

:::example{title="Do, does and did"}
Correct: ***Does** your sister **work** at the bank? **Did** you **see** the eclipse?*
Incorrect: *Does your sister works at the bank? Did you saw the eclipse?*
The auxiliary carries the tense and the *-s*; the main verb stays in its base form.
:::

### Wh- questions

Put the question word first, then the auxiliary, then the subject: *Where **do** you live? Why **was** the bus late? How many pupils **have** registered?*

:::example{title="Subject questions"}
When *who* or *what* is itself the subject, there is no *do* and no inversion:
*Who **broke** the window?* (not *Who did break the window?*)
*What **happened** at the meeting?* (not *What did happen?*)
Compare with an object question: *Who **did** you **meet** at the market?*
:::

## Indirect (polite) questions

Begin with a polite phrase and then use **statement word order**, without *do/does/did*.

:::example{title="Direct and indirect questions"}
Direct: *What time does the clinic open?*
Indirect: *Could you tell me **what time the clinic opens**?*
Direct: *Is the headmaster in his office?*
Indirect: *Do you know **if the headmaster is** in his office?*
The whole sentence still ends with a question mark when the opening phrase is a question (*Could you tell me ...?*), but with a full stop after a statement (*I would like to know what time the clinic opens.*).
:::

## Question tags

A question tag uses the **same auxiliary** as the statement and a **pronoun** for the subject.

- Positive statement → **negative** tag: *You are coming, **aren't you**?*
- Negative statement → **positive** tag: *You aren't coming, **are you**?*
- No auxiliary → use **do/does/did**: *She sings well, **doesn't she**? They arrived late, **didn't they**?*

:::keyfact{title="Special tags to learn"}
*I am right, **aren't I**?* (not *amn't I*)
*Let's go, **shall we**?*
*Close the door, **will you**?* (after a command)
*Nobody called, **did they**?* (negative words such as *nobody, never, hardly* make the statement negative, so the tag is positive)
*There is a problem, **isn't there**?*
*Everyone is here, **aren't they**?*
:::

:::example{title="Working out a tag"}
*Mr Moyo has already marked the tests, ___?*
1. Auxiliary in the statement: *has*.
2. Statement is positive, so the tag is negative: *hasn't*.
3. Subject *Mr Moyo* becomes the pronoun *he*.
Answer: *Mr Moyo has already marked the tests, **hasn't he**?*
:::

## Commands

Commands (imperatives) use the base form of the verb with no subject: *Switch off the lights. Don't run in the corridor.* Add *please*, or use a question, to make them polite: *Please sign the register. Would you sign the register, please?* Commands end with a full stop, or an exclamation mark if urgent: *Stop!*

## Exclamations

Exclamations show surprise, delight or shock and end with an exclamation mark. Two patterns are common:

- **What** + (a/an) + adjective + noun: ***What** a beautiful sunset! **What** lovely flowers!*
- **How** + adjective or adverb: ***How** tall you have grown! **How** quickly the year has passed!*

:::example{title="What or how?"}
Correct: *What a long queue! How long the queue is!*
Incorrect: *How a long queue!* (*How* is followed by an adjective without a noun.)
Incorrect: *What long queue!* (A singular countable noun needs *a*.)
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the auxiliary"}
*Where you are going?* and *Why she left?* are wrong. Put the auxiliary before the subject: *Where are you going? Why did she leave?*
:::

:::mistake{title="Using 'isn't it?' for every tag"}
*You like football, isn't it?* is a common error. The tag must match the verb and the subject: *You like football, don't you?*
:::

:::mistake{title="Question order inside an indirect question"}
*Can you tell me where is the hall?* should be *Can you tell me where the hall is?*
:::

:::mistake{title="Overusing exclamation marks"}
In formal writing, use exclamation marks rarely and never more than one at a time.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For any question tag item, find the auxiliary first. If there is none, the tag uses *do, does* or *did* in the same tense as the main verb.
:::

:::exam-tip
A rhetorical question in the opening of a speech or article can earn credit for engaging the audience, but make sure it is correctly formed and punctuated.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Questions: auxiliary before the subject; add *do/does/did* when there is no auxiliary, and use the base verb.
- Wh- questions: question word + auxiliary + subject; no *do* when *who/what* is the subject.
- Indirect questions use statement word order.
- Tags repeat the auxiliary with a pronoun: positive statement → negative tag, negative statement → positive tag. Learn *aren't I*, *shall we*, *will you*.
- Exclamations: *What (a) + adjective + noun!* / *How + adjective!*
:::
