## Introduction

Nouns are the naming words of English: they name people (*a nurse, Tsitsi*), places (*a market, Chinhoyi*), things (*a hoe, a bicycle*) and ideas (*patience, freedom*). Almost every sentence you write contains at least one.

Nouns may seem simple, but they cause many of the errors that cost marks in compositions and in the language questions of ZIMSEC Paper 2 and the Cambridge papers: wrong plurals (*sheeps*, *informations*), missing capital letters on proper nouns, misplaced apostrophes and confused forms such as *advise* and *advice*. Examiners also test **noun formation**, where you change a verb or an adjective into a noun to complete a sentence. This lesson covers all of these.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Noun"}
A word that names a person, place, thing, animal, idea or feeling.
:::

:::definition{term="Common noun"}
A general name for any member of a group: *girl, river, town, doctor*.
:::

:::definition{term="Proper noun"}
The name of one particular person, place, organisation, day or month. It always begins with a capital letter: *Kudakwashe, the Save River, Masvingo, Air Zimbabwe, Tuesday, August*.
:::

:::definition{term="Collective noun"}
A noun that names a group as one unit: *class, family, crowd, band, committee*.
:::

:::definition{term="Abstract noun"}
A noun that names something you cannot see, touch, hear, smell or taste: a quality, feeling, state or idea such as *courage, joy, poverty, friendship*.
:::

:::definition{term="Uncountable noun"}
A noun that cannot be counted directly, has no plural form and is not used with *a/an*: *water, rice, money, homework, traffic*.
:::

## Common, proper and concrete nouns

A **concrete** noun names something you can experience with your senses: *a mango*, *thunder*, *smoke*. Most common nouns are concrete.

:::example{title="Capital letters for proper nouns"}
Incorrect: *We visited the great zimbabwe monument on a friday in june.*
Correct: *We visited the **Great Zimbabwe** monument on a **Friday** in **June**.*
Names of places, days and months are proper nouns. The seasons (*summer, winter*) are common nouns and take no capital.
:::

## Collective nouns

Collective nouns name groups of people, animals or things. Some go with particular words:

| Collective noun | Group |
| --- | --- |
| a troop of | baboons, monkeys |
| a pride of | lions |
| a school (or shoal) of | fish |
| a crowd of | people |
| a bunch of | bananas, keys, flowers |
| a gang of | labourers, thieves |
| a panel of | judges, experts |
| a congregation of | worshippers |

:::example{title="Collective nouns in a sentence"}
*A **troop** of baboons raided the maize field at dawn.*
*The **panel** of judges announced the winners of the poetry competition.*
:::

## Abstract nouns and noun formation

Many abstract nouns are formed by adding a suffix to a verb or an adjective. Examiners often ask you to supply the correct noun.

| Suffix | From | Noun |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -ness | kind, dark, lazy | kindness, darkness, laziness |
| -ment | develop, punish, agree | development, punishment, agreement |
| -tion / -sion | inform, explain, permit | information, explanation, permission |
| -ity | able, curious, generous | ability, curiosity, generosity |
| -ance / -ence | appear, depend, patient | appearance, dependence, patience |
| -hood | child, neighbour, mother | childhood, neighbourhood, motherhood |
| -ship | friend, member, leader | friendship, membership, leadership |
| -th | strong, long, deep | strength, length, depth |

:::example{title="Spelling changes"}
Watch the spelling when a suffix is added:
*lazy → laziness* (y becomes i), *explain → explanation* (the *i* is dropped), *generous → generosity* (the *u* is dropped), *strong → strength* (the vowel changes).
:::

:::example{title="Noun formation in context"}
*The (arrive) ___ of the new doctor was welcomed by the whole community.* → ***arrival***
*Her (brave) ___ in the face of danger earned her a medal.* → ***bravery***
After *the* or a possessive such as *her*, and before a verb, you need a noun.
:::

## Countable and uncountable nouns

Countable nouns have a singular and a plural and can follow *a/an* or a number: *a goat, three goats*. Uncountable nouns have no plural, take a singular verb and are used with *some*, *much*, *a little* or a unit word.

:::example{title="Counting uncountable nouns"}
Incorrect: *The teacher gave us many homeworks.*
Correct: *The teacher gave us **a lot of homework**.* or *... **three pieces of homework**.*
Other uncountable nouns: *bread (a loaf of), water (a glass of), money, traffic, machinery, evidence, research, knowledge*.
:::

Some nouns can be both, with a change of meaning: *a light* (a lamp) / *light* (brightness); *a paper* (a newspaper or an exam paper) / *paper* (the material).

## Plurals

| Rule | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Most nouns add -s | goat → goats, road → roads |
| -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z add -es | bus → buses, brush → brushes, box → boxes |
| consonant + y → -ies | city → cities, lady → ladies |
| vowel + y adds -s | day → days, donkey → donkeys |
| some -o nouns add -es | hero → heroes, echo → echoes, mosquito → mosquitoes |
| other -o nouns add -s | radio → radios, piano → pianos, photo → photos |
| many -f/-fe → -ves | loaf → loaves, wolf → wolves, life → lives |
| irregular | goose → geese, louse → lice, person → people |
| same in both | sheep, deer, aircraft, species |

:::example{title="Correcting plural errors"}
Incorrect: *The farmer counted his sheeps and donkies before the storm.*
Correct: *The farmer counted his **sheep** and **donkeys** before the storm.*
*Sheep* does not change; *donkey* ends in vowel + *y*, so it simply adds *-s*.
:::

## Possessive nouns

- Singular noun: add **'s**: *the driver's licence*, *James's pen*.
- Plural noun ending in *s*: add only **'**: *the drivers' union*, *the girls' hostel*.
- Plural noun not ending in *s*: add **'s**: *the women's club*, *the people's choice*.

:::example{title="One apostrophe changes the meaning"}
*The nurse's uniforms* = the uniforms of one nurse.
*The nurses' uniforms* = the uniforms of several nurses.
:::

## Gender of nouns

Some nouns have different masculine and feminine forms: *king/queen, prince/princess, actor/actress, waiter/waitress, landlord/landlady, groom/bride, ram/ewe, stallion/mare*. Many modern words are used for both: *teacher, doctor, pilot, chairperson*.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Making uncountable nouns plural"}
*Equipments, informations, advices, furnitures* and *homeworks* do not exist. Use a unit word: *two items of equipment*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using an apostrophe to make a plural"}
*Fresh tomato's for sale* is wrong. Plurals never need an apostrophe: *Fresh tomatoes for sale.*
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing verbs and nouns that look alike"}
*advise* (verb) / *advice* (noun); *practise* (verb) / *practice* (noun); *licence* (noun) / *license* (verb) in British English.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting capital letters"}
Proper nouns, including the names of schools, rivers, mountains and organisations, need capitals: *Mutare Boys High School*, *the Runde River*.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In noun-formation items, look at the words around the gap. After *a, an, the, his, their* or an adjective, and before a verb, the gap almost always needs a noun. Then check whether it should be singular or plural.
:::

:::exam-tip
When unsure of a plural, say the word in a sentence with *two*: *two loaves*, *two heroes*. If it is uncountable, the phrase will sound wrong (*two advices*), which tells you to use a unit word instead.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Nouns name people, places, things, animals, ideas and feelings.
- Proper nouns need capital letters; collective nouns name groups; abstract nouns name qualities and ideas.
- Uncountable nouns have no plural and no *a/an*; count them with a unit word.
- Learn the plural rules for *-y, -o, -f/-fe* and the irregular plurals.
- Possessives: *'s* for singular and irregular plurals, *s'* for plurals ending in *s*.
- Form nouns with suffixes such as *-ness, -ment, -tion, -ity, -ance, -hood, -ship, -th*.
:::
