## Introduction

Stories come alive through dialogue: *"Run!" shouted Tendai. "The bull has broken through the fence!"* Reports, minutes and news articles, on the other hand, usually tell us what people said without quoting them word for word: *Tendai shouted to us to run because the bull had broken through the fence.*

You need both skills. In compositions, correctly punctuated direct speech shows control of the language. In ZIMSEC Paper 2 and in Cambridge exercises, you may be asked to change a sentence from direct into reported speech, or the reverse, and to write minutes or a report of a meeting from what speakers said. The changes follow clear rules, which this lesson sets out one by one.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Direct speech"}
The speaker's exact words, placed inside quotation marks: *"I am tired," said Rufaro.*
:::

:::definition{term="Reported (indirect) speech"}
The speaker's words retold by someone else, without quotation marks and usually with changes to tense, pronouns and time words: *Rufaro said that she was tired.*
:::

:::definition{term="Reporting verb"}
The verb that introduces the speech: *said, told, asked, explained, ordered, advised, promised, suggested*.
:::

:::definition{term="Back-shift"}
Moving the verb in reported speech one tense into the past when the reporting verb is in the past: *is → was*, *has seen → had seen*.
:::

## Punctuating direct speech

1. Put the spoken words inside quotation marks (single or double, but be consistent).
2. Begin the spoken words with a capital letter.
3. Separate the speech from the reporting clause with a comma, question mark or exclamation mark placed **inside** the closing quotation mark.
4. Start a new paragraph each time a new person speaks.

:::example{title="Correct punctuation"}
*"We will leave at dawn," said the guide.*
*The guide said, "We will leave at dawn."*
*"Have you packed the water?" asked Nyarai.*
*"We will leave at dawn," said the guide, "so get some sleep."* (The speech continues after the interruption, so *so* has no capital letter.)
:::

## From direct to reported speech: statements

Use *said (that)* or *told + person (that)*. Remember: we *say something* but *tell someone*.

:::keyfact{title="Tense back-shift after a past reporting verb"}
present simple → past simple; present continuous → past continuous; present perfect → past perfect; past simple → past perfect; will → would; can → could; may → might; must → had to (or must).
:::

:::example{title="A statement reported"}
Direct: *"I am going to the clinic today," said Mrs Banda.*
Reported: *Mrs Banda said that she **was going** to the clinic **that day**.*
Changes: *I → she*, *am going → was going*, *today → that day*.
:::

### Pronouns, time and place words

Pronouns change to fit the new speaker: *I → he/she*, *we → they*, *my → his/her*, *you → I/me/we* (depending on who is spoken to). Time and place words change because the report is made later and somewhere else:

| Direct | Reported |
| --- | --- |
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| last week | the week before / the previous week |
| next month | the following month |
| ago | before / earlier |
| here | there |
| this / these | that / those |

:::note{title="When the tense need not change"}
If the reporting verb is in the present (*She says ...*), or if the statement is still true (*The teacher said that the earth goes round the sun*), the tense may stay the same. In examination exercises, back-shift unless the instruction or context clearly shows otherwise.
:::

## Reporting questions

Reported questions are **not** questions any more, so:

- use *asked* (or *wanted to know*, *enquired*),
- use statement word order (subject before verb),
- drop the question mark and *do/does/did*,
- use *if* or *whether* for yes/no questions; keep the question word for *wh-* questions.

:::example{title="Two kinds of question"}
Direct: *"Do you live near the school?" the visitor asked me.*
Reported: *The visitor asked me **if I lived** near the school.*
Direct: *"Where have you put the keys?" Father asked Kuda.*
Reported: *Father asked Kuda **where he had put** the keys.*
Incorrect: *Father asked Kuda where had he put the keys?* (question word order and question mark)
:::

## Reporting commands and requests

Use *told, ordered, commanded, asked, begged, advised* + person + **to-infinitive**. For a negative command, use **not to**.

:::example{title="Commands"}
Direct: *"Close the windows," the teacher said to us.*
Reported: *The teacher **told us to close** the windows.*
Direct: *"Please don't make a noise," the nurse said to the visitors.*
Reported: *The nurse **asked the visitors not to make** a noise.*
:::

Choosing a precise reporting verb also captures the speaker's tone: *suggested, insisted, warned, apologised, promised, refused, admitted*.

:::example{title="Using precise reporting verbs"}
*"Let's walk to the dam after lunch," said Simba.* → *Simba **suggested** walking (or that we walk) to the dam after lunch.*
*"I'll pay you back on Friday," Chiedza said.* → *Chiedza **promised** to pay me back on Friday.*
:::

## From reported back to direct speech

Reverse every change: put the words inside quotation marks, restore the original tense, pronouns and time words, and add the correct punctuation.

:::example{title="Reported to direct"}
Reported: *The coach told the players that they had played well the day before.*
Direct: *"You played well yesterday," the coach told the players.*
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Keeping question word order"}
*She asked me where was the library* is wrong. Use statement order: *She asked me where the library was.*
:::

:::mistake{title="Using said with a person"}
*He said me that he was late* is wrong. Use *told me* or *said to me*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting time words"}
*He said that he would come tomorrow* is correct only if the report is made on the same day. In exercises, change *tomorrow* to *the next day*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Punctuation outside the quotation marks"}
*"I'm hungry", said Tino.* The comma belongs inside: *"I'm hungry," said Tino.*
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Work through a reported-speech item as a checklist: reporting verb, *that/if/whether/to*, pronouns, tense, time and place words, word order, final punctuation. Each is a common place to lose a mark.
:::

:::exam-tip
When a beginning is given (for example *She asked ...*), follow it exactly. It tells you the reporting verb and often the type of sentence.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Direct speech uses quotation marks, a capital letter at the start and punctuation inside the closing mark.
- In reported speech, after a past reporting verb, move tenses back (*is → was*, *will → would*, *has → had*).
- Change pronouns and time and place words (*today → that day*, *here → there*).
- Questions: *asked if/whether* or *asked + wh-word*, with statement word order and no question mark.
- Commands: *told/asked/ordered + person + (not) to + verb*.
:::
