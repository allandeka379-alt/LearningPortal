## Introduction

*We discussed about the trip, then arrived to Harare on the evening and went to home.* Every preposition in that sentence is wrong or unnecessary. Prepositions are short words, but they follow patterns that cannot always be worked out by logic, so they must be learned.

**Prepositions** link a noun or pronoun to the rest of the sentence and show relationships of time, place, movement, reason and manner. The examination tests them in gap-fill passages, in error-correction tasks and in rewriting tasks such as *Begin: In spite of ...*. In your own compositions and letters, correct fixed pairs (*interested in, responsible for, complain about*) make your English sound natural and accurate.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Preposition"}
A word placed before a noun, pronoun or -ing form to show its relationship to another part of the sentence: *under the tree*, *at noon*, *with her*, *before leaving*.
:::

:::definition{term="Compound preposition"}
A preposition made of two or more words that act as one: *because of, in spite of, on account of, instead of, in front of*.
:::

:::definition{term="Dependent preposition"}
The fixed preposition that a particular verb, adjective or noun always takes: *depend **on***, *proud **of***, *reason **for***.
:::

## Prepositions of time

| Preposition | Use | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| at | clock times, festivals, *night*, *the weekend* | at 7 a.m., at midnight, at Christmas, at night |
| on | days and dates | on Monday, on 18 April, on Heroes Day |
| in | months, years, seasons, parts of the day, longer periods | in June, in 2009, in the rainy season, in the morning |
| since | the starting point (with the present perfect) | since Tuesday, since 2015 |
| for | a length of time | for three days, for a year |
| during | a period named by a noun | during the holiday, during the night |
| by | not later than | by Friday |

:::example{title="Since, for and during"}
- *Mai Chiedza has been in hospital **since** Tuesday.* (starting point)
- *She has been in hospital **for** three days.* (length of time)
- *It thundered **during** the night.* (period: noun)
- *It thundered **while** we were sleeping.* (*while* is a conjunction: it needs a clause)
:::

## Prepositions of place

- **at** a point: *at the bus stop, at school, at the door*
- **on** a surface or line: *on the table, on the wall, on the Masvingo road*
- **in** an enclosed space or an area: *in the classroom, in the car, in Bulawayo, in Zimbabwe*
- others: *under, above, below, beside* (next to), *between* (two), *among* (more than two), *opposite, behind, in front of, near*

:::example{title="Arrive at or arrive in?"}
- *Our bus arrived **at** the terminus.* (a point or building)
- *We arrived **in** Mutare at midnight.* (a town or country)
- Never *arrive to*.
:::

## Prepositions of movement

*into* (outside → inside), *out of*, *onto*, *over* (from one side to the other, above), *across* (from one side to the other of a surface), *through* (inside something, from one end to the other), *along*, *past*, *towards*, *up*, *down*, *round*.

:::example{title="Movement in a description"}
*The goats jumped **over** the fence, trotted **across** the road, pushed **through** the gap in the hedge and wandered **into** Mrs Dube's vegetable garden.*
:::

## Beside or besides?

- **beside** = next to: *She sat **beside** her mother.*
- **besides** = in addition to: ***Besides** English, Farai speaks three other languages.*

## Fixed combinations

Many verbs, adjectives and nouns are followed by one particular preposition. There is no rule; learn them as pairs.

| Verb + preposition | Adjective + preposition | Noun + preposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| depend on, rely on | interested in | reason for |
| accuse someone of | good at, bad at | cause of |
| apologise for | proud of, afraid of | solution to |
| prevent someone from | similar to | increase in |
| succeed in | different from | demand for |
| object to | absent from | discussion about |
| insist on, congratulate on | responsible for | attitude to/towards |
| complain to someone about | keen on | respect for |

Some verbs take **no** preposition: *discuss the plan* (not *discuss about*), *enter the room* (not *enter into*), *reach Gweru* (not *reach at*), *attend a meeting*, *go home*.

### Prepositions followed by verbs

A verb that follows a preposition takes the **-ing form**:

:::example{title="Preposition + -ing"}
- *She is interested in **becoming** a doctor.*
- *The team succeeded in **winning** the cup.*
- *I look forward to **meeting** you.* (here *to* is a preposition, so not *to meet*)
:::

## Compound prepositions in sentence transformation

*In spite of*, *despite*, *because of* and *on account of* are prepositions, so they are followed by a noun phrase or an -ing form, **not** a full clause.

:::example{title="Rewriting with in spite of"}
*Although it rained heavily, the farmers' show went ahead.*

- Correct: ***In spite of the heavy rain**, the farmers' show went ahead.*
- Correct: ***In spite of the fact that it rained heavily**, ...*
- Incorrect: *In spite of it rained heavily, ...* (a bare clause after a preposition)
- Incorrect: *In spite of the heavy rain, **but** the show went ahead.* (*but* is not needed)
:::

:::example{title="Rewriting with because of"}
*The match was postponed because the pitch was waterlogged.* → *The match was postponed **because of** (or **on account of**) **the waterlogged pitch**.*
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Adding prepositions where none is needed"}
*Discuss about*, *enter into the room*, *reach at*, *go to home*: remove the preposition.
:::

:::mistake{title="Following in spite of with a clause"}
*In spite of she was ill* is wrong. Write *In spite of her illness* or *In spite of being ill*, or use *although she was ill*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using since with a length of time"}
*I have lived here since five years* is wrong. Use *for five years* or *since 2021*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the infinitive after a preposition"}
*I look forward to see you* should be *I look forward to seeing you*.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In a gap-fill passage, first decide the job of the preposition (time, place, movement, or a fixed pair) and then apply the rule. For fixed pairs, read the word before the gap: *interested ___*, *accused ___*.
:::

:::exam-tip
When rewriting with *in spite of*, *despite* or *because of*, turn the clause into a noun phrase (*it rained heavily* → *the heavy rain*) and check that you have not added *but* or *so*.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Time: at (clock times), on (days, dates), in (months, years, seasons); since + starting point, for + length, during + noun.
- Place: at a point, on a surface, in an enclosed space or area; arrive at a building, arrive in a town.
- Movement: into, over, across, through, along, towards.
- Learn fixed pairs: interested in, good at, similar to, depend on, accuse of, succeed in, complain about.
- Some verbs take no preposition: discuss, enter, reach, attend, go home.
- After a preposition, use a noun or an -ing form; in spite of and because of are never followed by a bare clause.
:::
