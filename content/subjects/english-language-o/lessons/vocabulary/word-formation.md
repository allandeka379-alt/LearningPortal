## Introduction

English builds many of its words from smaller parts. From the single root *employ* we get *employer, employee, employment, unemployment, unemployed* and *employable*. If you understand how these parts fit together, you can work out the meaning of words you have never seen and use the right form of a word in your own writing.

Word formation is tested directly in the language section of ZIMSEC Paper 2, where you complete sentences with the correct form of a word given in brackets, and indirectly in every composition and summary: *He was very success* or *the beautify of Nyanga* lose marks for accuracy. Cambridge examiners reward a wide and precise vocabulary, and much of that range comes from knowing word families.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Root (base word)"}
The main part of a word that carries its basic meaning, such as *kind* in *unkindness* or *port* in *transport*.
:::

:::definition{term="Prefix"}
A group of letters added to the **beginning** of a word to change its meaning: *un-* + *happy* = *unhappy*.
:::

:::definition{term="Suffix"}
A group of letters added to the **end** of a word. It usually changes the word class: *happy* (adjective) + *-ness* = *happiness* (noun).
:::

:::definition{term="Word family"}
A group of words built from the same root: *decide, decision, decisive, decisively, indecisive*.
:::

## Prefixes change meaning

A prefix does not normally change the word class: *legal* and *illegal* are both adjectives. It changes the meaning.

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| un-, in-, im-, il-, ir-, dis- | not, opposite of | unfair, incorrect, impossible, illegal, irregular, dishonest |
| mis- | wrongly | misunderstand, misspell, mislead |
| re- | again, back | rebuild, rewrite, repay |
| pre- | before | prepaid, preview, prehistoric |
| over- / under- | too much / too little | overcrowded, undercooked |
| inter- | between | international, interschool |
| sub- | under, below | subway, submarine, subtitle |
| anti- | against | antiseptic, antisocial |
| co- | together | co-operate, co-author |

:::example{title="Choosing the negative prefix"}
*im-* usually comes before *m* and *p*: **im**mature, **im**patient, **im**possible.
*il-* comes before *l*: **il**legal, **il**literate, **il**logical.
*ir-* comes before *r*: **ir**regular, **ir**responsible, **ir**relevant.
Other words take *un-*, *in-* or *dis-*, and these must be learnt: **un**able, **in**visible, **dis**agree.
:::

When a prefix is added, the spelling of the root does not change. That is why *misspell* has two *s*'s (*mis* + *spell*) and *unnecessary* has two *n*'s (*un* + *necessary*).

## Suffixes change word class

The suffix usually tells you what kind of word you have.

| Word class | Common suffixes | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noun | -ness, -ment, -tion/-sion, -ity, -ance/-ence, -er/-or, -ee, -hood, -ship, -ism | kindness, payment, invitation, ability, assistance, farmer, employee, childhood, friendship |
| Verb | -ise, -en, -ify, -ate | modernise, widen, beautify, activate |
| Adjective | -ful, -less, -able/-ible, -ous, -ive, -al, -ic, -y | careful, careless, reliable, dangerous, attractive, national, energetic, dusty |
| Adverb | -ly | quickly, happily, carefully |

:::example{title="One root, four word classes"}
**Noun:** The *beauty* of the Eastern Highlands attracts many visitors.
**Verb:** The council planted trees to *beautify* the main street.
**Adjective:** Mutarazi Falls is a *beautiful* sight in the rainy season.
**Adverb:** The choir sang *beautifully* at prize-giving.
:::

Notice that the spelling of the root sometimes changes when a suffix is added: *beauty* becomes *beauti-* before *-ful*. The main spelling changes are:

- **Final -y after a consonant changes to i:** happy → happiness, happily; beauty → beautiful.
- **Final silent -e is usually dropped before a vowel suffix:** create → creative, use → usable; but kept before a consonant suffix: care → careful, hope → hopeless.
- **Some roots change more:** pronounce → pronunciation, explain → explanation, maintain → maintenance, proud → pride, strong → strength, deep → depth.

## Using the right form in a sentence

In an exam item, the gap tells you which word class you need. Look at the words on either side.

:::example{title="Reading the gap"}
*The (able) of the team was clear from the first match.*
After *The* and before *of*, the gap needs a **noun**: *ability*.

*The nurse spoke (gentle) to the frightened child.*
The gap describes **how** she spoke, so it needs an **adverb**: *gently*.

*Kudzai was (employ) for two years after leaving college.*
The sentence says she had no job, so you need a negative adjective: *unemployed*.
:::

Sometimes you need a prefix **and** a suffix. Read the whole sentence for meaning: a word such as *but*, *although* or *sadly* often signals that the meaning should be negative.

:::example{title="Prefix and suffix together"}
*The bus driver was fined for driving (care) on the Harare–Bulawayo road.*
Being fined suggests bad driving, so the answer is *carelessly*: *care* + *-less* (without) + *-ly* (adverb).
:::

## People nouns: -er, -or, -ee, -ist, -ant

Many nouns name a person who does something. *-er* and *-or* name the person who acts (*employer, inspector*); *-ee* names the person who receives the action (*employee, trainee, interviewee*). *-ist* and *-ant* are also common: *scientist, journalist, assistant, applicant*. Check which person the sentence needs: the employer pays the wages, the employee receives them.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the wrong word class"}
*She answered all the questions correct* is wrong: the word describes how she answered, so it must be the adverb *correctly*. *He showed great confident* is wrong: after *great* you need the noun *confidence*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adding a suffix to the wrong root"}
*Beautifulness*, *successfulness* and *happyness* are not standard. Use *beauty*, *success* and *happiness*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Choosing the wrong negative prefix"}
*Unpossible*, *inlegal* and *unresponsible* do not exist. Learn *impossible*, *illegal* and *irresponsible*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting spelling changes"}
*Happyly*, *carefull* and *arguement* are misspellings: *happily*, *careful* (one *l* in the suffix *-ful*) and *argument* (the *e* of *argue* is dropped).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Before writing anything, decide which **word class** the gap needs (noun, verb, adjective or adverb). Then check the meaning: positive or negative, person or thing, singular or plural.
:::

:::exam-tip
Read your finished sentence aloud in your head. If it does not sound like natural English, try a different suffix.
:::

:::exam-tip
In compositions, varying word forms makes your writing more precise: *The decision was difficult* and *She decided quickly* say different things in different ways.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A **prefix** goes at the beginning and changes meaning; a **suffix** goes at the end and usually changes word class.
- Negative prefixes: *un-, in-, im-* (before m, p), *il-* (before l), *ir-* (before r), *dis-*.
- Noun suffixes: *-ness, -ment, -tion, -ity, -ance/-ence, -er/-or, -ee, -hood, -ship*. Adjective suffixes: *-ful, -less, -able, -ous, -ive, -al*. Verb suffixes: *-ise, -en, -ify*. Adverbs usually end in *-ly*.
- Watch spelling changes: *y → i*, dropping silent *e*, and irregular roots such as *pronunciation* and *strength*.
- In gap-fill items, the words around the gap tell you which word class you need.
:::
