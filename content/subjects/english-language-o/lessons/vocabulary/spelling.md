## Introduction

Spelling errors cost marks in every paper that involves writing. In compositions they lower the language mark; in summaries and comprehension answers, a badly misspelt word can even make the meaning unclear. Examiners notice in particular when a candidate misspells words that were printed correctly in the passage on the question paper.

English spelling has a reputation for being difficult, and it does have exceptions. But most of the words you write follow a small number of rules. If you learn those rules and then a list of the most commonly misspelt words, you can remove the majority of spelling errors from your work.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Vowel"}
The letters *a, e, i, o, u*. (Sometimes *y* works as a vowel, as in *gym* and *happy*.)
:::

:::definition{term="Consonant"}
All the other letters: *b, c, d, f, g* and so on.
:::

:::definition{term="Suffix"}
A letter group added to the end of a word: *-ing, -ed, -er, -ful, -ness, -ly, -able*. A **vowel suffix** begins with a vowel (*-ing, -ed, -able*); a **consonant suffix** begins with a consonant (*-ful, -ness, -ly, -ment*).
:::

## Rule 1: i before e, except after c

When the sound is *ee*, write **ie**, except straight after **c**, where you write **ei**.

:::example{title="ie and ei"}
**ie:** believe, achieve, field, piece, chief, thief, relief
**ei after c:** receive, ceiling, deceive, receipt, conceive
**Exceptions to learn:** seize, weird, protein, either, neither
When the sound is not *ee*, *ei* is common: *eight, weigh, neighbour, their, foreign, height*.
:::

## Rule 2: doubling the final consonant

When you add a **vowel suffix** to a word that ends in **one vowel + one consonant**, double the consonant if the word has **one syllable** or the **stress falls on the last syllable**.

:::example{title="Doubling"}
One syllable: run → **running**, stop → **stopped**, big → **bigger**, sit → **sitting**.
Stress on the last syllable: be-GIN → **beginning**, re-FER → **referred**, oc-CUR → **occurred**, ad-MIT → **admitted**.
Stress **not** on the last syllable, so no doubling: VIS-it → **visited**, OF-fer → **offered**, BUD-get → **budgeted**.
:::

In British English, a final **l** after a single vowel is doubled whatever the stress: *travel → travelled, travelling*; *cancel → cancelled*; *label → labelled*.

Do **not** double when the word ends in two vowels + consonant (*rain → raining*, *cool → cooler*) or two consonants (*help → helping*).

## Rule 3: dropping the silent e

Drop the final silent *e* before a **vowel suffix**; keep it before a **consonant suffix**.

:::example{title="Silent e"}
Drop: hope → **hoping**, write → **writing**, use → **usable**, argue → **arguing**, believe → **believable**.
Keep: hope → **hopeful**, care → **careful**, safe → **safety**, move → **movement**, late → **lately**.
Exceptions: *argue → argument*, *true → truly*, *nine → ninth*, *whole → wholly*. Words ending in *-ce* or *-ge* keep the *e* before *-able* and *-ous* to keep the soft sound: *noticeable, manageable, courageous*.
:::

## Rule 4: y to i

When a word ends in **consonant + y**, change *y* to *i* before most suffixes. Keep the *y* before *-ing*.

:::example{title="y to i"}
happy → happiness, happily; carry → carried, carries; beauty → beautiful; busy → business
Keep y before -ing: carry → carrying, study → studying, cry → crying
Vowel + y keeps the y: play → played, enjoy → enjoyment, obey → obeyed
Learn the exceptions: day → daily, pay → paid, say → said, lay → laid.
:::

## Rule 5: the suffix -ful and adding -ly

The suffix **-ful** always has **one l**: *careful, hopeful, beautiful, grateful*. When *-ly* is added, the two *l*'s meet: *careful + ly = carefully*, *beautiful + ly = beautifully*. In the same way, a word ending in *l* keeps it before *-ly*: *real → really*, *usual → usually*, *final → finally*.

When a prefix is added, nothing is lost either: *dis + appear = disappear* (one *s*), *dis + satisfied = dissatisfied* (two *s*'s), *mis + spell = misspell*, *un + necessary = unnecessary*.

## Rule 6: plurals

| Ending | Rule | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| most nouns | add -s | books, teachers |
| -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x | add -es | buses, classes, brushes, churches, boxes |
| consonant + y | y → ies | city → cities, baby → babies |
| vowel + y | add -s | day → days, boy → boys |
| some -o words | add -es | potatoes, tomatoes, heroes, echoes |
| some -f / -fe words | → -ves | knife → knives, leaf → leaves |

## Commonly misspelt words

Learn these by looking at the tricky part.

| Correct | Tricky part |
| --- | --- |
| accommodation | double c, double m |
| necessary | one c, double s |
| separate | *a rat* in the middle: sep-a-rate |
| definitely | *finite* in the middle |
| embarrass | double r, double s |
| government | the *n* before *ment* |
| environment | the *n* before *ment* |
| February | *bru* in the middle |
| committee | double m, double t, double e |
| occasion | double c, one s |
| privilege | no d: privi-lege |
| rhythm | no vowels after the *rh* except y |
| tomorrow | one m, double r |
| until | one l |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Doubling when you should not"}
*Visitted*, *offerred* and *budgetted* are wrong: the stress is on the first syllable, so write *visited*, *offered*, *budgeted*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Keeping or dropping e in the wrong place"}
*Comeing*, *useing* and *hopeing* keep an *e* that should be dropped. *Carful* and *hopful* drop an *e* that should be kept.
:::

:::mistake{title="-full instead of -ful"}
*Beautifull*, *carefull* and *successfull* are wrong. The suffix is *-ful*; only *full* on its own has two *l*'s.
:::

:::mistake{title="Copying errors from the question paper"}
Words printed in the passage (names, places, technical terms) must be spelt exactly as printed. Check them before you hand in.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Keep a personal list of words you misspell and learn them using **Look, Say, Cover, Write, Check**. Five words a week makes a real difference by the exam.
:::

:::exam-tip
In the last minutes of each paper, read your work **only** for spelling. Look especially at words with suffixes (*-ing, -ed, -ful, -ly*) and at *ie/ei* words.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- *i before e, except after c* when the sound is *ee*: believe, receive. Learn *seize, weird, either, neither*.
- Double the final consonant before a vowel suffix when the word ends vowel + consonant and the stress is on the last (or only) syllable: begin → beginning; visit → visited. British English doubles final *l*: travelled.
- Drop silent *e* before a vowel suffix (hoping); keep it before a consonant suffix (hopeful).
- Consonant + *y* → *i* (happily), but keep *y* before *-ing* (carrying).
- *-ful* has one *l*; *-fully* has two.
:::
