## Introduction

Read this sign outside a shop in Gweru: *Stationary for sale: pens, books and envelopes.* The owner meant *stationery* (writing materials). *Stationary* means *not moving*. The two words sound the same, and a spell-checker will not notice the mistake because both are real words.

Errors like this are among the most common in compositions and letters, and examiners deduct marks for them because they change the meaning. Language items in ZIMSEC Paper 2 and practice exercises for the Cambridge papers often ask you to choose the correct word from a confusable pair. This lesson groups the most important pairs and gives you memory tricks for each.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Homophones"}
Words that **sound** the same but have different spellings and meanings: *there / their / they're*, *weather / whether*.
:::

:::definition{term="Homographs"}
Words that are **spelt** the same but have different meanings and often different pronunciations: *lead* (to guide) and *lead* (a soft metal).
:::

:::definition{term="Homonyms"}
Words with the same spelling **and** sound but different meanings: a river *bank* and a *bank* that keeps money.
:::

:::definition{term="Confusable words"}
Words that are not exact homophones but are close enough in sound or spelling to be mixed up: *accept / except*, *loose / lose*, *quiet / quite*.
:::

## The everyday homophones

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| there | a place; also *there is / there are* | Put the bags over **there**. |
| their | belonging to them | The learners packed **their** bags. |
| they're | they are | **They're** leaving at six. |
| its | belonging to it | The dog wagged **its** tail. |
| it's | it is / it has | **It's** raining in Mutare. |
| your | belonging to you | Is this **your** pen? |
| you're | you are | **You're** late again. |
| to / too / two | towards; also / excessively; the number 2 | We went **to** Hwange **too**, for **two** days. |
| whose / who's | belonging to whom / who is | **Whose** book is this? **Who's** coming? |

:::example{title="The apostrophe test"}
If you can replace the word with *it is* or *it has*, write **it's**. If not, write **its**.
*The school celebrated (its / it's) fiftieth anniversary.* → *The school celebrated it is anniversary* makes no sense, so **its**.
The same test works for *they're* (they are), *you're* (you are) and *who's* (who is).
:::

## Pairs that change meaning

| Pair | How to tell them apart |
| --- | --- |
| stationary / stationery | stationary = not moving (**a** for p**a**rked car); station**e**ry = writing materials (**e** for **e**nvelopes) |
| principal / principle | principal = head of a college, or main; principle = a rule or belief |
| affect / effect | affect is usually the verb (**A** for **A**ction); effect is usually the noun (the result) |
| accept / except | accept = to receive or agree to; except = not including |
| loose / lose | loose = not tight; lose = to misplace or fail to win |
| quiet / quite | quiet = silent; quite = fairly, or completely |
| weather / whether | weather = rain and sun; whether = if |
| desert / dessert | desert = dry land; dessert = pudding (two *s*'s, as in *sweet stuff*) |
| complement / compliment | complement = complete or go well with; compliment = praise |
| council / counsel | council = an elected group; counsel = advice, or to advise |
| breath / breathe | breath = the noun; breathe = the verb |
| passed / past | passed = the verb (went by); past = earlier time, or beyond |

:::example{title="Affect and effect in context"}
*The drought **affected** crops in Matabeleland South.* (verb: it acted on them)
*The **effect** of the drought was a poor harvest.* (noun: the result)
:::

## Noun and verb pairs in British English

In British English, some pairs use **c** for the noun and **s** for the verb.

:::example{title="c for the noun, s for the verb"}
**practice** (noun): *Football practice starts at four.* / **practise** (verb): *You must practise every day.*
**licence** (noun): *He has a driving licence.* / **license** (verb): *The council licenses market stalls.*
**advice** (noun): *She gave me good advice.* / **advise** (verb): *I advise you to revise early.*
A memory aid: *ice* is a noun, so the word with **c** is the noun.
:::

## Homographs

Some words are spelt the same but pronounced differently, depending on meaning.

| Word | Meaning 1 | Meaning 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lead | to guide (rhymes with *need*) | a soft grey metal (rhymes with *bed*) |
| tear | a drop from the eye (rhymes with *here*) | to rip (rhymes with *care*) |
| wind | moving air (rhymes with *pinned*) | to turn or wrap (rhymes with *kind*) |
| bow | to bend forward (rhymes with *cow*) | a knot, or a weapon for arrows (rhymes with *go*) |
| minute | sixty seconds | very small (*my-NEWT*) |

Be careful with **lead** and **led**: the past tense of the verb *lead* is **led**. *The captain led the team onto the field yesterday.* Many writers wrongly write *lead* because the metal is pronounced *led*.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using it's as a possessive"}
*The company announced it's results* is wrong. Possessive pronouns (*its, his, hers, ours, theirs, yours*) never take an apostrophe.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing lose for loose"}
*I don't want to loose the match* is wrong: you **lose** a match or a key; a button is **loose**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing lead for led"}
*She lead the choir last term* should be *She **led** the choir last term.*
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing practice and practise"}
*We need more practise* should be *more **practice*** (noun). *Practice your speech* should be ***Practise** your speech* (verb).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Work out the word class first. If the gap needs a verb, choose *affect, practise, advise, license, breathe*; if it needs a noun, choose *effect, practice, advice, licence, breath*.
:::

:::exam-tip
Proofread your compositions specifically for *their/there/they're*, *its/it's*, *to/too* and *lose/loose*. These are easy marks to save in the last five minutes.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- **Homophones** sound alike; **homographs** are spelt alike; **homonyms** share both sound and spelling.
- Use the substitution test for apostrophe words: *it's = it is*, *they're = they are*, *you're = you are*, *who's = who is*.
- *Affect* is usually a verb, *effect* usually a noun.
- In British English, *practice, licence, advice* are nouns; *practise, license, advise* are verbs.
- The past tense of *lead* is *led*; *stationery* is paper and pens; a *principle* is a rule.
:::
