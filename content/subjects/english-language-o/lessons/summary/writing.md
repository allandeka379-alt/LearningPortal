## Introduction

You have selected your points (see the lesson on selecting points). Now you must write them up. Examiners give marks for **content** (the points) and for **expression**: writing in your own words, concisely, in fluent, connected sentences, within the word limit. ZIMSEC usually asks for a paragraph of a stated length; Cambridge 0500 asks for a selective summary of no more than 120 words in your own words.

A good summary reads like a short, clear paragraph that someone who has not read the passage could understand. A poor one is a list of copied phrases or a long retelling of the passage.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Continuous prose"}
Writing in complete sentences joined into a paragraph, not in notes, bullet points or a numbered list.
:::

:::definition{term="Concise"}
Saying something fully in as few words as possible, with no padding or repetition.
:::

:::definition{term="Word limit"}
The maximum number of words allowed. Points written after the limit is reached may not be credited.
:::

## Step 1: start directly

Your first sentence should already contain a point. Do not waste words on an introduction.

:::example{title="Openings"}
- Weak: *In this passage the writer talks about the many different ways in which families in Bulawayo save water.* (18 words, no point)
- Strong: *Families reuse kitchen water on their gardens and catch the cold water from showers in buckets.* (two points at once)
:::

## Step 2: use your own words

Change the key words of each point (see the lesson on own words). You may keep words that have no simple equivalent (*borehole*, *msasa*) and technical terms that cannot be avoided.

## Step 3: combine points

Combining saves words and makes the writing fluent.

:::example{title="Combining notes into one sentence"}
**Notes:** water early or late · mulch soil · plant drought-resistant indigenous plants

**Sentence:** *Gardeners water in the cool of the morning or evening, cover the soil to hold moisture and grow indigenous plants that need no watering.*

Three points, one sentence, twenty-four words.
:::

Useful ways to combine:

- **Lists**: *They reuse, collect and recycle…*
- **Relative clauses**: *…indigenous plants, which need little water.*
- **Participles**: *Checking pipes monthly, they repair leaks at once.*
- **Linking words**: *also, as well as, in addition, while, so that, because*.

## Step 4: link logically

Group related points (for example everything about the garden together), and use connectives to show relationships: addition (*also*, *furthermore*), cause (*because*), purpose (*so that*), contrast (*although*).

## Step 5: check length and accuracy

- Count your words. Roughly: count the words in three typical lines and multiply by the number of lines.
- If you are over the limit, cut words, not points: remove adjectives you don't need, merge sentences, delete repeated ideas.
- Check that you have not changed the meaning, added opinions or included examples.

## A worked example

:::example{title="From notes to summary"}
**Task:** *Summarise the ways families in Bulawayo save water. Write no more than 80 words.*

**Notes:** reuse kitchen water · catch shower water · bucket not hosepipe for cars · sand bottle in cistern · flush less · fix leaks · water at cool times · mulch · indigenous plants · harvest rainwater

**Summary (79 words):** *Families pour water used in the kitchen onto their gardens, collect the cold water that runs before a shower warms up and clean cars with a bucket instead of a hosepipe. A sand-filled bottle in the cistern reduces each flush, and some households flush less often. Leaks are repaired quickly. Gardeners water in the cool of the day, cover the soil to keep it moist and grow plants that survive on rain, which many homes now store in drums.*

Ten points, own words, one paragraph, under the limit.
:::

## Improving a weak summary

:::example{title="Before and after"}
**Weak draft (45 words):** *The passage tells us about saving water. Families save water. One grandmother in Nkulumane has two drums. These drums carried her vegetable garden through a whole winter. Families also save water in the toilet by putting a two-litre bottle filled with sand in the cistern.*

Problems: an empty opening, a vague point (*families save water*), an anecdote instead of a point, and a lifted sentence.

**Improved (24 words):** *Families store rainwater from their roofs in drums for the dry season and place a sand-filled bottle in the cistern to reduce each flush.*

The improved version is half the length and makes two clear points in the writer's own words.
:::

When you edit, ask of every sentence: *Does this give a point? Is it in my own words? Could it be shorter?*

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Writing notes or bullet points"}
Unless the task asks for notes, a summary must be in continuous prose.
:::

:::mistake{title="Lifting whole sentences"}
Copying loses the own-words marks and usually makes the summary too long.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adding a conclusion or opinion"}
*In conclusion, these are very good ideas that everyone should follow* adds nothing and wastes words.
:::

:::mistake{title="Going far over the limit"}
Points after the limit may not earn marks. Plan to finish a little under.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Draft, then polish"}
If time allows, write a quick draft from your notes, count it, then write the final version.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Stay in the passage's world"}
Use the third person and present the writer's points neutrally: *Families do…*, not *You should…*.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Open with a point, not an introduction.
- Use your own words; keep only unavoidable terms.
- Combine and link points into fluent sentences.
- Leave out examples, repetition, opinions and conclusions.
- Keep within the word limit; cut words, not points, when editing.
:::
