## Introduction

ZIMSEC Paper 1 Section B (guided composition) gives you **material to work from**: a list of notes, a table of figures, a graph described in words or a set of pictures, and asks you to turn it into a letter, report, speech, article or memo. Half the skill is the format; the other half is **using the material well**: every note included, expanded into fluent sentences, and every figure reported accurately.

Candidates lose marks by copying the notes as a list, leaving notes out, misreading figures or inventing details that contradict the material. This lesson shows how to avoid all four.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Notes (pegs)"}
Short phrases given in a task that must be expanded into complete sentences. Each note is a peg on which you hang a developed idea.
:::

:::definition{term="Amplify"}
To expand a note into full, connected sentences by adding grammar, linking words and relevant detail.
:::

:::definition{term="Interpret data"}
To describe what figures show (comparisons, trends, highest and lowest) accurately in words.
:::

:::definition{term="Trend"}
The general direction of change over time: rising, falling, steady or fluctuating.
:::

## Part 1: Amplifying notes

Every note should become **at least one full sentence**, usually two: one to state it and one to explain or develop it. Add:

- **Grammar:** articles, verbs in the right tense, prepositions.
- **Links:** *in addition*, *as a result*, *however*, *because*.
- **Detail** that is reasonable and consistent with the notes (a reason, an example, a consequence), never contradicting them.

:::example{title="Notes amplified into a paragraph"}
Notes (from a task asking for a report on a school trip to Victoria Falls):

- *left 5 a.m.; bus breakdown near Lupane; 2-hour delay*
- *guided walk through rainforest; spray; rainbows*
- *learners' behaviour excellent; thanks to teachers*

Amplified:

The bus left the school at 5 a.m. on Friday. Near Lupane, however, it broke down with a fan-belt problem, and the group waited two hours for a mechanic, arriving at the Falls in the early afternoon. **[Note 1: tense, linking word, reasonable detail (fan belt)]**

Despite the delay, the guided walk through the rainforest was the highlight of the trip. Spray from the Falls soaked everyone within minutes, and rainbows formed over the gorge whenever the sun broke through. **[Note 2: developed with description; linked to note 1 with *Despite*]**

The learners' behaviour throughout the day was excellent, and the club is grateful to the three teachers who supervised the trip. **[Note 3: complete sentence; gratitude made specific]**
:::

## Part 2: Turning data into words

When the task gives a **table** or describes a **graph**, your job is to report what matters, accurately.

1. **Read the title, headings and units** (numbers, percentages, years).
2. **Find the big picture:** the highest and lowest, the main trend, the biggest difference.
3. **Select**: you do not need every number. Choose the figures that support your points.
4. **Compare** using exact figures and approximate expressions: *nearly half*, *more than twice as many*, *a sharp rise*.
5. **Explain or recommend** only in ways the data supports.

:::example{title="Table into report findings"}
*Survey of how 200 learners travel to school*

| Mode of transport | Number of learners |
|---|---|
| Walk | 96 |
| Kombi | 58 |
| Bicycle | 24 |
| Private car | 22 |

Amplified finding:

Walking is by far the most common way of getting to school: 96 of the 200 learners (almost half) walk. **[Highest first, with a fraction]** Kombis are the second most popular choice, used by 58 learners, while bicycles (24) and private cars (22) account for only about a quarter between them. **[Comparison and grouping; accurate arithmetic: 46 of 200 is about a quarter]** Since so many learners walk, the safety of the footpaths and the road crossing near the gate deserves the committee's attention. **[A conclusion the data supports]**
:::

### Language for describing data

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Proportions | *almost half*, *just over a third*, *a quarter*, *the vast majority*, *a small minority* |
| Comparison | *twice as many as*, *slightly more than*, *far fewer*, *the highest/lowest* |
| Trends | *rose steadily*, *increased sharply*, *fell slightly*, *remained steady*, *fluctuated*, *peaked in* |
| Caution | *appears to*, *suggests that*, *according to the survey* |

:::example{title="A line graph described in words"}
*The graph shows the number of library books borrowed each month from January to June.* Borrowing rose steadily from about 120 books in January to a peak of about 310 in May, the month before examinations, and then fell sharply in June. **[Trend, peak and fall, with approximate figures and a reasonable link to exams]**
:::

## Pictures

If the task gives pictures described in words (for example, four scenes of a fire drill), treat each picture as a note: put the scenes in order, describe each in full sentences and link them with time connectives (*first*, *then*, *within minutes*, *finally*).

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Copying notes as notes"}
*"Left 5 a.m. Bus breakdown. 2-hour delay."* is not continuous writing. Every note must become full sentences.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving out a note"}
Each note usually carries marks. Tick them off as you use them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Misreading figures"}
58 out of 200 is not "more than half". Check arithmetic before you write fractions or comparisons.
:::

:::mistake{title="Contradicting the material"}
If the notes say the trip was in July, do not describe it in the rainy season. Added detail must fit the notes.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Number the notes on the question paper and write the number in the margin of your plan beside the paragraph that uses it.
:::

:::exam-tip
Lead with the most important figure, then compare. One accurate comparison is worth more than a list of every number.
:::

## Checklist

- Every note used and expanded into full sentences.
- Linking words join the notes into paragraphs.
- Added detail is reasonable and consistent.
- Figures read and reported accurately, with comparisons and trends.
- Conclusions or recommendations supported by the data.
- The required format (letter, report, speech, article or memo) is followed.

## Summary

:::summary
Writing from notes and data means amplifying every note into full, linked sentences with reasonable detail, and turning tables, graphs and pictures into accurate continuous writing. Read the units, find the big picture, select key figures, compare them with precise language and draw only the conclusions the data supports, all within the format the task requires.
:::
