## Introduction

A review helps readers decide whether to read a book, watch a film, attend an event or visit a place. It does two jobs at once: it **describes** enough for readers to understand what is being reviewed, and it **evaluates**, giving opinions backed by reasons. Cambridge 0510 Exercise 6 regularly sets reviews for a school magazine or website, and ZIMSEC functional writing may ask you to review a school play, a community event or a book you have studied.

Reviews are marked for **content** (description and justified opinion), **register** (lively and engaging, but suitable for the readers) and **organisation** (a clear path to a recommendation).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Review"}
A piece of writing that describes and evaluates a book, film, event, product or place, ending with a recommendation.
:::

:::definition{term="Spoiler"}
A detail that reveals a surprise or the ending, spoiling the experience for readers who have not yet seen or read the work.
:::

:::definition{term="Recommendation"}
The reviewer's final advice: who should read, watch or visit, and why (or why not).
:::

:::definition{term="Balanced judgement"}
An evaluation that mentions both strengths and weaknesses before reaching an overall view.
:::

## Structure

1. **Title** (often with the name of the thing reviewed) and, for some tasks, a star rating.
2. **Introduction:** what it is, where or when, and a hook or first impression.
3. **Description:** the essential facts (plot outline without spoilers, or what the place offers).
4. **Strengths:** with reasons and specific examples.
5. **Weaknesses:** fair criticism, also with reasons.
6. **Conclusion and recommendation:** an overall verdict and who would enjoy it.

## Annotated model review

:::example{title="Model review for a school website"}
**Eating Out: The Mopane Pot, Bulawayo ★★★★☆** **[Title with the name and a rating]**

If you have ever wanted to taste home cooking without doing the washing-up, the Mopane Pot on Fife Street might be your new favourite place. **[Hook addressing the reader]**

The restaurant, which opened last year, serves traditional Zimbabwean dishes in a small, bright room decorated with baskets from Binga. On a Saturday lunchtime it was full of families, a good sign. **[Essential description: what, where, atmosphere]**

The food is the star. My plate of sadza with tender beef stew and a side of fresh covo was generous and full of flavour, and my friend's road-runner chicken was smoky and crisp. Portions are large enough for a hungry teenager, and prices are reasonable for the quality. **[Strengths, with specific examples]**

However, the service was slow. We waited almost forty minutes for our meals, and the single waiter was clearly struggling. The menu also has very few options for vegetarians. **[Fair criticism with reasons]**

Overall, the Mopane Pot is well worth a visit for anyone who loves traditional food, especially families and groups who are not in a hurry. Go early, be patient, and order the stew. **[Verdict and recommendation for a clear audience]**
:::

## Language for reviews

Reviews mix the **present tense** for describing (*The film follows…*, *The restaurant serves…*) with **evaluative language**.

:::example{title="Useful review phrases"}
- Positive: *gripping*, *beautifully filmed*, *well worth a visit*, *a must-read*, *full of flavour*, *the highlight was…*
- Negative: *disappointing*, *predictable*, *overpriced*, *the pace drags in the middle*, *let down by…*
- Balanced: *Although…, …*; *While the acting is strong, the plot…*; *My only criticism is…*
- Recommending: *I would recommend it to anyone who…*; *Give it a miss if…*; *Not to be missed.*
:::

### Opinions need reasons

Weak: *The book is very good and I liked it.*\
Strong: *The book is gripping because each chapter ends on a question, so I read the last hundred pages in one night.*

## Different things to review

| Book or film | Event | Place (restaurant, park, museum) |
|---|---|---|
| genre, setting, main character | what, when, where, who organised | location, atmosphere, facilities |
| plot outline (no spoilers) | highlights of the programme | what it offers (food, exhibits) |
| acting or writing style, themes | organisation, atmosphere | service, prices, access |
| who would enjoy it | was it worth attending? | who should visit and when |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Retelling the whole story"}
A review that summarises the whole plot, including the ending, is a summary with spoilers. Keep the description short and focus on evaluation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Opinions without reasons"}
*"It was nice"* earns little. Explain *why*, with an example.
:::

:::mistake{title="Only praise or only criticism"}
A balanced review is more convincing. Mention at least one weakness, even in a positive review.
:::

:::mistake{title="No recommendation"}
Readers want to know: should they go? End with a clear verdict.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In Cambridge 0510 Exercise 6, write about 120-160 words: one paragraph of description, one or two of evaluation, one of recommendation.
:::

:::exam-tip
Choose a book, film or place you really know, so you have specific examples. Fictional restaurants and events are fine if the task allows.
:::

## Checklist

- Title naming what is reviewed (rating optional).
- Hook and essential facts.
- Short description with no spoilers.
- Strengths and weaknesses, each with reasons and examples.
- Evaluative vocabulary; present tense for describing.
- Clear recommendation for a named audience.

## Summary

:::summary
A review describes briefly and evaluates fully. Give a title, a hook and the essential facts, avoid spoilers, support every opinion with a reason and an example, balance strengths with weaknesses, and finish with a clear recommendation saying who would enjoy it. Use lively evaluative vocabulary in a register that suits the readers.
:::
