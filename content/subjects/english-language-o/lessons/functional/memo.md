## Introduction

A **memorandum** (memo for short; plural *memoranda* or *memos*) is a short message sent **inside** an organisation: from the Head to staff, from a manager to workers, from a club chairperson to members. It is one of the text types in ZIMSEC Paper 1 Section B, where you may be given notes about a staff meeting, a change in timetable or a new safety rule and asked to write a memo.

Memos are marked for **format** (the heading block), **content** (all the notes, in a logical order) and **style** (brief, clear, polite and direct). Memos are short: busy readers must understand them in one reading.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Memo (memorandum)"}
A brief, formal internal message within an organisation, with a To/From/Date/Subject heading and short, direct content.
:::

:::definition{term="Action required"}
The part of a memo that tells readers exactly what they must do, and by when.
:::

:::definition{term="Internal communication"}
Communication between people in the same organisation, such as a school, a company or a club.
:::

## The format

1. The word **MEMORANDUM** (or **MEMO**) at the top, often with the organisation's name.
2. A heading block:
   - **To:** the reader(s), by title or group (*All teaching staff*).
   - **From:** the writer, with title.
   - **Date:**
   - **Subject:** (or *Re:*) a short, specific description.
3. A line across the page (optional) to separate the heading.
4. **Body:** a purpose sentence, then short paragraphs or numbered points.
5. **Action required** with a deadline.
6. The writer's **initials or signature** at the end. There is **no salutation** (*Dear…*) and **no complimentary close** (*Yours faithfully*).

## Annotated model memo

:::example{title="Model memo from a school bursar"}
**MEMORANDUM** **[Text type named]**

**GLEN VIEW HIGH SCHOOL**

**To:** All Form Teachers\
**From:** Mrs P. Murambiwa, Bursar\
**Date:** 9 February 2026\
**Subject:** Collection of sports levy receipts **[Specific subject]**

---

This memo explains the new procedure for recording sports levy payments, which begins on Monday 16 February. **[Purpose in one sentence; no greeting]**

1. Learners who pay at the bank must bring the deposit slip to their form teacher, not to the bursary. **[Numbered points: one instruction each]**
2. Form teachers should record each slip on the class list provided and staple the slips to it.
3. Lists and slips must be handed in to the bursary every Friday by 1 p.m.
4. Learners who pay by mobile money should show the confirmation message; the form teacher writes the reference number on the list.

Please collect your class lists from the bursary by Friday 13 February. **[Action required with a deadline]**

Thank you for your co-operation. **[Brief courteous line]**

*P.M.* **[Initials, not a formal close]**
:::

## Style

- **Brief:** most memos fit on one page; cut every word that does not help.
- **Direct:** *Please hand in…*, *Staff should…*, *The meeting will be held…*.
- **Formal but not stiff:** the reader is a colleague, so courtesy matters, but long polite formulas are unnecessary.
- **Logical order:** purpose → details → action.
- **Numbered points** for instructions or several items.

:::example{title="Wordy versus direct"}
- Wordy: *It has come to my attention that it might perhaps be a good idea if teachers could possibly think about handing in their registers at some point before the end of the week, if that is convenient.*
- Direct: *Please hand in your class registers to the office by Friday 13 March.*

The direct version is shorter, polite (*Please*) and gives a clear deadline. Busy readers act on it at once.
:::

:::example{title="Useful memo phrases"}
- *The purpose of this memo is to inform you that…*
- *Please note that, with effect from 1 March,…*
- *All staff are requested to…*
- *Kindly submit… by…*
- *Should you have any questions, please contact…*
:::

## Memo, letter or notice?

| Memo | Formal letter | Notice |
|---|---|---|
| inside an organisation | to an outside person or body | displayed for everyone to see |
| To/From/Date/Subject | addresses, salutation, close | headline, key details, contact |
| initials at the end | *Yours faithfully* + name | name of the issuer |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Adding Dear and Yours faithfully"}
Memos do not have a salutation or a complimentary close. The heading block replaces them.
:::

:::mistake{title="A vague subject line"}
*Subject: Important* does not help a busy reader. *Subject: Change to Friday sports times* does.
:::

:::mistake{title="No clear action"}
If readers finish the memo not knowing what to do or by when, it has failed. End with the action and the deadline.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing an essay"}
Long paragraphs of explanation bury the key information. Use short points.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Turn each note in the question into one numbered point, and keep them in a sensible order (when, where, who, what to bring, what to do).
:::

:::exam-tip
Write *MEMORANDUM* at the top and fill every line of the heading block: these format marks are easy to earn.
:::

## Checklist

- MEMORANDUM heading and organisation name.
- To, From (with title), Date, Subject lines.
- Purpose stated in the first sentence.
- Short paragraphs or numbered points.
- Action required with a deadline.
- Initials or signature; no *Dear*, no *Yours faithfully*.

## Summary

:::summary
A memo is a brief internal message. It has a heading block (To, From, Date, Subject) instead of a greeting, states its purpose at once, presents details in short numbered points, ends with the action required and a deadline, and is signed with initials. Keep it brief, direct, courteous and logically ordered.
:::
