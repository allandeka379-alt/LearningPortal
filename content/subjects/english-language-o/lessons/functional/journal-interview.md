## Introduction

Two text types ask you to write **in someone's voice**. A **journal** (diary) entry records events and, more importantly, the writer's **thoughts and feelings** about them. An **interview** presents a conversation as questions and answers, so that readers learn about a person's experience and views.

Both appear in Cambridge 0500 (Paper 1 Question 4 often asks for a journal, interview, letter or speech **in role**, based on a reading text) and in ZIMSEC functional and guided writing. Examiners look for a convincing voice, content that uses the details given, and the correct conventions of each form.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Journal (diary) entry"}
A personal, first-person record of events, thoughts and feelings, usually dated and written soon after the events.
:::

:::definition{term="Writing in role"}
Writing as a particular person (a character in a passage, a nurse, a farmer), using their knowledge, feelings and voice.
:::

:::definition{term="Interview"}
A conversation in which an interviewer asks questions and an interviewee answers, written out with the speakers' names or initials.
:::

:::definition{term="Open question"}
A question that invites a full answer (*How did you feel when…?*), unlike a closed question answered *yes* or *no*.
:::

## Part 1: The journal entry

### Layout and features

- **Date** (and sometimes the day and time or place) at the top.
- Often a short **greeting** such as *Dear Diary* (optional; many writers begin straight away).
- **First person** (*I*, *we*) and mostly the **past tense** for events, the **present** for current feelings.
- **Reflection**: why things happened, what the writer felt, what they fear or hope.
- An **informal but controlled** style: questions to oneself, exclamations, honest feelings.
- An ending that looks forward (*Tomorrow I will…*) or sums up the feeling.

:::example{title="Model journal entry, written in role as a Form 3 learner in Tsholotsho"}
**Monday 9 February** **[Date at the top]**

I still can't believe what happened last night. **[First person, immediate feeling]** The rain started at about eight o'clock, heavy and steady, and by midnight the water was over the doorstep. Baba woke us all and we carried the mattresses, the radio and the bag of mealie-meal onto the table. **[Events in order, past tense]**

What frightened me most was the sound: the water gurgling under the door like something alive. **[Sensory detail and reflection]** My little sister held my hand so tightly that it hurt, and I kept telling her it would stop soon, though I didn't believe it myself. **[Honest feeling]**

At first light the neighbours came with buckets and spades. Everyone worked together, even old Gogo Moyo, who directed the digging of a channel like an army general. I laughed for the first time since the storm. **[Shift in mood; character detail]**

My school books are ruined. Will Mr Sibanda believe me? **[Question to self]** I suppose the whole class will have a story like mine.

Tomorrow we dry everything in the sun. Tonight, I'm just grateful we're all safe. **[Forward-looking ending]**
:::

## Part 2: The interview

### Layout and features

- A short **introduction** (in italics or a heading) saying who is being interviewed and why, if the task allows.
- Speakers identified by **name or initials** and a colon, on a new line for each turn. **No inverted commas.**
- **Open questions** that lead to developed answers.
- Questions in a **logical order** (background → experience → views → future).
- Answers in the interviewee's **voice**: personal, with details and feelings.
- A closing **thank you** from the interviewer.

:::example{title="Model interview for a school magazine"}
*Our reporter, Tanatswa Mutero, spoke to Kudzai Nhamo, a Form 5 learner from Murehwa who keeps twelve beehives on her family's plot.* **[Introduction: who and why]**

**Tanatswa:** How did you become interested in bees? **[Open question about background]**

**Kudzai:** My grandfather kept hives when I was small. I used to watch him from a safe distance, and when he gave me my first hive at thirteen, I was terrified and thrilled at the same time. **[Answer with detail and feeling]**

**Tanatswa:** What is the hardest part of beekeeping?

**Kudzai:** Honestly, patience. You cannot rush bees. In a dry year, they produce less honey, and all you can do is plant flowering trees and wait.

**Tanatswa:** Has the business helped your family? **[Follows logically]**

**Kudzai:** Yes. We sell honey at the growth point and to teachers at school. It pays for my uniform and books now.

**Tanatswa:** What advice would you give other young people?

**Kudzai:** Start small, learn from someone experienced and respect the bees. They have more to teach us than we think.

**Tanatswa:** Thank you, Kudzai, and good luck with this season's harvest. **[Closing thanks]**
:::

## Useful phrases

:::example{title="Journal and interview language"}
- Journal: *I can't stop thinking about…*, *Looking back, I realise…*, *What if…?*, *I've never felt so…*
- Interview questions: *What made you decide to…?*, *How did you feel when…?*, *What would you say to someone who…?*, *What are your plans for…?*
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="A journal that only lists events"}
*"I woke up. I ate. I went to school."* Journals are about **reflection**: thoughts, feelings and reasons.
:::

:::mistake{title="Inverted commas in interviews"}
Interviews are set out like a script: name, colon, words. Do not use speech marks.
:::

:::mistake{title="Closed questions"}
*"Do you like bees?" "Yes."* gives nothing. Ask open questions that let the interviewee develop answers.
:::

:::mistake{title="Losing the role"}
If you are writing as a nurse in a passage, use what she knows and feels, not your own life.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In role-based tasks based on a passage, use the details in the text but develop them: explain feelings and reasons the passage only hints at.
:::

:::exam-tip
In an interview, make the answers longer than the questions. The interviewee's voice is where the content marks are.
:::

## Checklist

- Journal: date; first person; events plus feelings and reflection; consistent voice; forward-looking end.
- Interview: introduction; names and colons; no speech marks; open questions in logical order; developed answers in role; closing thanks.
- Every detail the task requires used and developed.

## Summary

:::summary
A journal entry is a dated, first-person record that reflects on events and feelings in a personal but controlled voice. An interview is set out with names and colons, uses open questions in a logical order and gives the interviewee developed answers in a convincing voice. In both, stay in role and use and develop every detail the task provides.
:::
