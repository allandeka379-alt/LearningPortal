## Introduction

Formal letters are among the most frequently set tasks in ZIMSEC Paper 1 Section B. You may be asked to apply for a post as a prefect or a holiday job, complain to a council about uncollected refuse, request sponsorship for a sports tour, or write to the editor of a newspaper about a community problem. Half of the marks depend on **how** you write: correct layout, a clear purpose, a polite but firm tone and accurate language.

The good news is that the layout is the same every time. Learn it once, practise it, and those marks are safe.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Formal letter"}
A letter to a person or organisation you do not know personally, or to someone in an official role, written in a formal register with a fixed layout.
:::

:::definition{term="Salutation"}
The greeting at the start of a letter, such as *Dear Sir* or *Dear Mrs Dube*.
:::

:::definition{term="Subject line"}
A short heading after the salutation, often beginning *RE:*, stating what the letter is about.
:::

:::definition{term="Complimentary close"}
The phrase before the signature: *Yours faithfully* or *Yours sincerely*.
:::

## The layout

1. **Your address** at the top right (no name above it).
2. **The date** under your address, written in full: *14 March 2026*.
3. **The recipient's title and address** on the left, a line lower.
4. **Salutation**: *Dear Sir*, *Dear Madam*, *Dear Sir/Madam* or *Dear Mr Moyo*.
5. **Subject line**, underlined or in bold.
6. **Body**: an opening paragraph stating your purpose, one or two paragraphs of details, a closing paragraph stating the action you want.
7. **Complimentary close**, signature and your full name (and title if relevant).

### Faithfully or sincerely?

| Salutation | Close |
|---|---|
| *Dear Sir*, *Dear Madam*, *Dear Sir/Madam*, *Dear Editor* (name unknown) | **Yours faithfully** |
| *Dear Mr Chirwa*, *Dear Mrs Dube* (name known) | **Yours sincerely** |

Only the first word of the close has a capital letter: *Yours faithfully*, not *Yours Faithfully*.

## Annotated model: a letter of complaint

:::example{title="Model formal letter (complaint to a town council)"}
14 Mopane Road\
Mkoba 6\
Gweru\
3 February 2026

**[Writer's address and date: placed at the top right of the page; no name]**

The Town Clerk\
City of Gweru\
P.O. Box 278\
Gweru\

**[Recipient's title and address on the left]**

Dear Sir/Madam **[Name unknown, so Sir/Madam]**

**RE: UNCOLLECTED REFUSE IN MOPANE ROAD, MKOBA 6** **[Subject line: specific place and problem]**

I am writing to complain about the failure to collect refuse in Mopane Road since the beginning of January. **[Purpose in the first sentence]**

Before December, the council truck came every Wednesday. Over the past five weeks it has not come at all. Residents have kept their bins at the gate as instructed, but the bins are now overflowing, dogs have torn open the bags and rubbish is blowing into the storm drains. **[Facts in time order; no exaggeration]**

The situation is becoming a health risk. Flies have increased sharply, and with the rainy season the blocked drains may cause flooding. Several families with young children are particularly worried about disease. **[Consequences explained calmly]**

I would be grateful if the council could resume weekly collections immediately and clear the drains in our road. I would also appreciate being informed of the reason for the delay, so that residents can plan accordingly. **[Clear, polite request for action]**

I look forward to your prompt reply. **[Courteous closing sentence]**

Yours faithfully **[Matches Dear Sir/Madam]**

*T. Mlambo*

Tendai Mlambo (Mr) **[Signature, then full name]**
:::

## Types of formal letter and their focus

| Type | Opening purpose | Key content | Closing request |
|---|---|---|---|
| Application | *I wish to apply for the post of…* | qualifications, experience, qualities | availability for interview |
| Complaint | *I am writing to complain about…* | what, when, where, effects | specific action, explanation |
| Request | *I am writing to request…* | what is needed, why, how it will be used | a reply or permission by a date |
| Enquiry | *I would like to enquire about…* | precise questions | the information you need |
| Letter to the editor | *I wish to draw your readers' attention to…* | the issue, evidence, proposal | a call to readers or authorities |

## Useful phrases

:::example{title="Formal phrase bank"}
- Opening: *I am writing to…*, *I wish to apply for…*, *I would like to bring to your attention…*
- Linking: *Furthermore*, *In addition*, *As a result*, *However*
- Requesting: *I would be grateful if you could…*, *I would appreciate it if…*
- Closing: *I look forward to hearing from you.* *Thank you for considering my application.*
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Wrong close"}
*Dear Sir … Yours sincerely* is a mismatch. Unknown name: *faithfully*. Known name: *sincerely*.
:::

:::mistake{title="Name above the address"}
Your name goes at the end, under your signature, not above your address.
:::

:::mistake{title="Vague subject line"}
*RE: Complaint* tells the reader nothing. *RE: Uncollected refuse in Mopane Road* does.
:::

:::mistake{title="Rambling openings"}
Do not begin with *I hope this letter finds you well*. Formal letters state their purpose in the first sentence.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Use every note given in the question. Examiners tick each required point; a missing point costs content marks however good the language is.
:::

:::exam-tip
Write a separate paragraph for each main idea and end with a specific request. A letter that complains but asks for nothing is incomplete.
:::

## Checklist

- My address and the date at the top right; recipient on the left.
- Correct salutation and matching close (faithfully or sincerely).
- A specific subject line.
- Purpose stated in the first sentence.
- One idea per paragraph; facts not insults.
- A clear request or action in the final paragraph.
- Signature and full name; no contractions or slang.

## Summary

:::summary
A formal letter has a fixed layout: your address and date, the recipient's address, salutation, subject line, purposeful paragraphs, a matching close and your name. State your purpose at once, give facts in logical order, keep a polite but firm tone and end by asking for specific action. Dear Sir/Madam takes Yours faithfully; a named reader takes Yours sincerely.
:::
