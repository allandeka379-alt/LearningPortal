## Introduction

A report gives a reader who was **not there** the facts they need to make a decision. In ZIMSEC Paper 1 Section B you may be the secretary of a club reporting to the Head on a trip, a prefect reporting on a problem in the hostels, or a member of a committee investigating litter in a market. Cambridge papers also set reports for a teacher or a youth group.

Reports are marked for **format** (headings, logical sections), **content** (every note used, facts separated from recommendations) and **style** (objective, concise, formal). The reader should be able to find any piece of information in seconds.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Report"}
A formal, factual document written for a specific reader that presents findings under headings and ends with conclusions and recommendations.
:::

:::definition{term="Terms of reference"}
The opening statement of what the report was asked to investigate, by whom and why.
:::

:::definition{term="Findings"}
The facts discovered, presented objectively under headings or numbered points.
:::

:::definition{term="Recommendations"}
Suggested actions, based on the findings, for the reader to take.
:::

## The layout

1. **Title** stating the subject.
2. **To / From / Date** (the recipient, the writer with position, the date).
3. **Introduction / Terms of reference**: what was investigated and why.
4. **Procedure / Method** (optional): how the information was gathered.
5. **Findings** under clear sub-headings.
6. **Conclusion**: a short overall judgement.
7. **Recommendations**: numbered actions.
8. **Signature**, name and position.

## Annotated model report

:::example{title="Model report: the Form 3 tree-planting project"}
**REPORT ON THE TREE-PLANTING PROJECT AT CHIREDZI HIGH SCHOOL** **[Title: subject in full]**

**To:** The Head, Chiredzi High School\
**From:** Kudakwashe Hove, Secretary, Environment Club\
**Date:** 18 December 2026 **[Reader, writer with role, date]**

**1. Introduction** **[Terms of reference]**\
At the request of the Head, the Environment Club reviewed the tree-planting project carried out by Form 3 classes during National Tree Planting Day and suggested how it could be improved next year.

**2. Procedure** **[How the facts were gathered]**\
Club members counted the surviving seedlings on 10 December, interviewed the groundsman and the Agriculture teacher, and asked twenty Form 3 learners to complete a short questionnaire.

**3. Findings** **[Facts only, under sub-headings]**

*3.1 Planting.* One hundred and twenty indigenous and fruit seedlings were planted along the boundary fence and near the hostels in early December.

*3.2 Survival.* Seventy-four seedlings were still alive at the time of counting. Most losses were along the fence, where goats from neighbouring plots had entered through gaps.

*3.3 Watering.* No watering roster had been drawn up. The questionnaire showed that learners watered "their" trees only for the first week.

*3.4 Attitudes.* Most learners enjoyed the activity and said they would take part again if each class were responsible for its own section.

**4. Conclusion** **[Brief overall judgement]**\
The project was popular and largely successful, but losses were mainly caused by goats and the lack of a watering plan, both of which can be prevented.

**5. Recommendations** **[Numbered, practical, linked to findings]**
1. The gaps in the boundary fence should be repaired before next year's planting.
2. Each class should adopt a section and keep a weekly watering roster.
3. Seedlings near the fence should be protected with wire or thorn-bush guards.
4. Survival should be checked each term and reported at assembly.

*K. Hove*\
Kudakwashe Hove, Secretary, Environment Club
:::

Notice that the findings are **facts**, without opinion, and every recommendation answers a finding.

## Report style

- **Objective:** *Seventy-four seedlings survived*, not *Sadly, lots of the poor trees died*.
- **Impersonal structures:** the passive (*It was found that…*, *Learners were interviewed*) and *It is recommended that…*.
- **Concise:** short paragraphs, numbered points, no storytelling.
- **Formal register:** no contractions or slang.

:::example{title="Useful report phrases"}
- *The purpose of this report is to…* / *This report examines…*
- *It was found that…* / *The majority of respondents stated that…*
- *It would appear that…* / *This suggests that…*
- *It is recommended that…* / *The committee should consider…*
:::

## Report or article?

| Report | Article |
|---|---|
| for a specific reader who must decide | for the general readers of a magazine or newspaper |
| headings and numbered points | a headline and flowing paragraphs |
| objective, impersonal | lively, can be personal |
| ends with recommendations | ends with a memorable conclusion |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Writing a story"}
*"On Monday we woke up early and…"* is a narrative. A report organises facts under headings, not in the order you experienced them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing findings and opinions"}
Keep what you found (findings) separate from what you think should happen (recommendations).
:::

:::mistake{title="Recommendations that come from nowhere"}
Each recommendation must answer a finding. A recommendation about the tuck shop in a report on tree planting is irrelevant.
:::

:::mistake{title="Missing the To/From/Date"}
Without a recipient and writer, a report has no clear purpose or authority.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Turn each note in the question into a finding under its own sub-heading, then write one recommendation for each problem you have found.
:::

:::exam-tip
Use the role the task gives you in the From line, and sign off with your name and position.
:::

## Checklist

- Title; To, From (with position), Date.
- Introduction stating the purpose (terms of reference).
- Findings under headings, facts only.
- Short conclusion.
- Numbered recommendations linked to findings.
- Objective, concise, formal style; signature and position.

## Summary

:::summary
A report tells a specific reader what was investigated, what was found and what should be done. It has a title; To, From and Date lines; an introduction; findings under headings; a conclusion; and numbered recommendations. Write objectively and concisely, using impersonal phrases such as *It was found that…* and *It is recommended that…*.
:::
