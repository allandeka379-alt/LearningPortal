## Introduction

"Using your own words, explain why the farmers were worried." This instruction appears in ZIMSEC Paper 2 and in Cambridge reading papers, and it is also the heart of summary writing. It tests one thing: **have you understood the passage?** If you copy the writer's words, the examiner cannot tell whether you understood them, so copied (lifted) key words earn no marks.

Writing in your own words is called **paraphrasing**. It does not mean replacing every word, and it does not mean writing something new. It means keeping the writer's **meaning** while changing the **key words** and, where you can, the sentence structure.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Paraphrase"}
A re-expression of an idea in different words that keeps exactly the same meaning.
:::

:::definition{term="Lifting"}
Copying words or phrases directly from the passage. Lifting the key words in an own-words answer earns no credit.
:::

:::definition{term="Key words"}
The words that carry the main meaning of the idea, usually nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs that the question depends on.
:::

## What must change and what can stay

You do not need synonyms for small, common words (*the, and, people, school, water*) or for words with no simple equivalent (*maize, borehole, Harare*). You **must** change the words that carry the idea the question is testing.

:::example{title="Finding the key words"}
**Passage.** *The farmers were anxious because the rains had been erratic and their stores of grain were depleted.*

**Question.** *In your own words, explain why the farmers were worried.*

Key words: **erratic**, **stores of grain**, **depleted**. (*Farmers* and *rains* can stay.)

- Lifted: *The rains had been erratic and their stores of grain were depleted.* (0 marks)
- Own words: *The rain had fallen unpredictably, and they were running out of the food they had saved.* (full marks)
:::

## The four-step method

1. **Underline** the part of the passage that answers the question.
2. **Circle** the key words in it.
3. **Replace** each key word with an accurate synonym or a short explanation. Make sure the replacement fits this context.
4. **Rebuild** the sentence, changing the structure if possible, and **check** that you have not added, lost or changed any meaning.

:::example{title="Changing the structure as well"}
**Passage.** *Despite the heavy rain, the spectators remained in their seats until the final whistle.*

- Words changed, structure kept: *In spite of the downpour, the crowd stayed seated until the end of the match.*
- Structure changed too: *Although it was pouring, the crowd did not leave before the match ended.*

Both keep the meaning. The second shows even more clearly that you understand.
:::

## Accuracy matters more than fancy words

A synonym must keep the exact meaning and strength of the original.

:::example{title="Keeping the strength of the meaning"}
**Passage.** *The pupils were **furious** about the cancelled trip.*

- *The pupils were extremely angry.* Accurate.
- *The pupils were a little annoyed.* Too weak: the meaning has changed.
- *The pupils were violent.* Wrong: adds a meaning that is not there.
:::

Watch for words that change meaning: *some* is not *all*; *often* is not *always*; *may* is not *will*; *nearly* is not *completely*.

## Paraphrasing figurative language

If the passage uses an image, explain the idea plainly.

:::example{title="Explaining an image"}
**Passage.** *News of the closure spread through the township like wildfire.*

Own words: *Very quickly, almost everyone in the township heard that the place was closing.*
:::

## Building your synonym bank

Some words appear again and again in passages. Learn plain equivalents:

| Passage word | Plain own-words version |
| --- | --- |
| deteriorate | get worse |
| abundant | plentiful, more than enough |
| reluctant | unwilling |
| obtain | get, acquire |
| scarce | in short supply |
| vital | extremely important, essential |
| commence | begin |
| inadequate | not enough, not good enough |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Changing small words but keeping the key words"}
*The rains had been really erratic* still lifts *erratic*. The key words are the ones that must change.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using a synonym that does not fit the context"}
*The farmers' grain stores were* ***exhausted*** might work, but *the farmers were exhausted* means they were tired — a different idea.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adding your own opinions"}
*The government should have helped the farmers* is not in the passage. Paraphrase only what the writer says.
:::

:::mistake{title="Writing a vague answer"}
*Things were bad for the farmers* avoids lifting but loses the specific points (erratic rain, low stores).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Know when own words are needed"}
If a question says *in your own words* or *using your own words*, every key word must be changed. If it simply asks *what* or *where*, brief quotation is usually allowed.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Keep the number of points"}
If the passage gives three reasons, your paraphrase must include all three, each in your own words.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Read your answer back"}
Ask: would someone who had not read the passage understand exactly what the writer meant from my answer?
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Own-words answers prove you have understood; lifted key words earn nothing.
- Change the key words; small common words and words with no equivalent can stay.
- Underline, circle key words, replace them accurately, rebuild the sentence and check the meaning.
- Keep the same strength and limits of meaning (some/all, may/will).
- Never add opinions or lose points.
:::
