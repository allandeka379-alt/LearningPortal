## Introduction

Your History teacher sets a project on the Great Zimbabwe monument. Your Biology teacher wants a page on malaria. You need to check the spelling and meaning of *archaeology*. Where do you look, and how do you know what you find is true?

The ZIMSEC syllabus expects you to use reference sources skilfully: to choose the right source, find information quickly inside it, and judge whether it can be trusted. These skills save time in every subject and protect you from the mass of unreliable information online.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Reference source"}
A book, database or website designed to be consulted for particular information rather than read from cover to cover.
:::

:::definition{term="Reliable source"}
A source whose author or publisher is known and qualified, whose information is accurate, up to date and checked, and which has no hidden motive to mislead.
:::

:::definition{term="Plagiarism"}
Presenting someone else's words or ideas as your own without acknowledging them.
:::

## Choosing the right source

| Source | Use it to find |
| --- | --- |
| **Dictionary** | Meaning, spelling, pronunciation, part of speech, word origin |
| **Thesaurus** | Synonyms and antonyms (words with similar or opposite meanings) |
| **Encyclopaedia** | Overview articles on topics: people, places, events, science |
| **Atlas** | Maps, locations, physical and political geography |
| **Library catalogue** | Which books the library holds, by title, author or subject, and where they are shelved |
| **Journal / magazine** | Recent articles and research on specialised topics |
| **Newspaper** | Current events and recent reports |
| **Internet search** | Almost anything, but quality varies widely |

:::example{title="Matching need to source"}
- *I need a word that means "very happy" but isn't "happy".* → **thesaurus** (jubilant, elated, delighted).
- *How do you pronounce "hyperbole"?* → **dictionary**.
- *A general account of the Kingdom of Mutapa.* → **encyclopaedia**.
- *Does our library have any books on beekeeping?* → **library catalogue**.
:::

## Using a dictionary well

Dictionaries list words in **alphabetical order**. **Guide words** at the top of each page show the first and last entries, so you can find a word quickly. A typical entry gives:

- the headword and its pronunciation;
- the part of speech (*n.*, *v.*, *adj.*);
- one or more numbered meanings, often with examples;
- related forms (*decide*, *decision*, *decisive*) and sometimes the word's origin.

When a word has several meanings, choose the one that fits your context.

## The parts of a book

| Part | Where | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Title page** | Front | Title, author, publisher |
| **Imprint / copyright page** | Back of title page | Date of publication, edition, copyright, ISBN |
| **Contents** | Front | List of chapters or sections with page numbers, in order |
| **Preface / foreword / introduction** | Front | Why the book was written, how to use it; a foreword is often by someone other than the author |
| **Body (chapters)** | Middle | The main text |
| **Glossary** | Back | Alphabetical list of difficult or specialised words with meanings |
| **Bibliography / references** | Back | Sources the author used |
| **Appendix (plural appendices)** | Back | Extra material: tables, documents, data |
| **Index** | Very back | Alphabetical list of topics, names and terms with the pages where they appear |

:::example{title="Contents or index?"}
- You want to see the **overall plan** of a geography textbook → **contents** (it follows the order of the book).
- You want every page that mentions **Lake Kariba** → **index** (alphabetical, detailed).
:::

## Judging reliability, especially online

Ask five questions:

1. **Who** wrote it? Is the author or organisation named and qualified?
2. **Why** was it written? To inform, or to sell, persuade or entertain?
3. **When** was it written or updated? Is it current enough for your topic?
4. **Can it be checked?** Do other reliable sources agree? Does it give its own sources?
5. **How** is it presented? Careful writing and references suggest care; wild claims and many errors are warning signs.

:::example{title="Comparing two websites"}
- A page on malaria prevention from a national health ministry, dated last year, citing medical research → **reliable**.
- An anonymous post saying a herbal tea "cures malaria in one day", selling the tea on the same page → **unreliable** (no author, selling a product, unsupported claim).
:::

## Using sources responsibly

- Take notes **in your own words**.
- Put direct quotations in **quotation marks** and say where they came from.
- Record the author, title, publisher or website and date for every source you use, so you can list them at the end of a project.
- Never copy and paste a whole section and hand it in as your own: that is **plagiarism**.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using a dictionary for a topic overview"}
A dictionary gives meanings, not full accounts. Use an encyclopaedia or textbook for a topic.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing glossary and index"}
A glossary explains words; an index tells you which pages they are on.
:::

:::mistake{title="Trusting the first search result"}
The top result is not necessarily the most reliable. Check who wrote it and why.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Learn the order"}
Front: title page, imprint, contents, preface. Back: appendices, glossary, bibliography, index.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Purpose questions"}
If asked "Where would you look to find…?", name the specific source **and** say why it suits that need.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Choose the source that matches your need: dictionary, thesaurus, encyclopaedia, atlas, catalogue, journal, newspaper or internet.
- Use guide words and alphabetical order to search dictionaries quickly.
- Know the parts of a book: contents (order of chapters), index (alphabetical page finder), glossary (meanings), appendix (extra material), preface (purpose).
- Judge reliability: who, why, when, can it be checked, how it is presented.
- Use sources responsibly: own-words notes, quotation marks, records of sources, no plagiarism.
:::
