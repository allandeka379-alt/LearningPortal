## Introduction

The Sun has shone for about 4.6 billion years by **joining** light nuclei together. A nuclear power station, such as Koeberg near Cape Town in South Africa, produces electricity by **splitting** heavy nuclei apart. Both processes release enormous amounts of energy from tiny amounts of fuel. This lesson is Cambridge Supplement content.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Nuclear fission"}
The splitting of a large, heavy nucleus (such as uranium-235) into two smaller nuclei, releasing energy and a few neutrons.
:::

:::definition{term="Nuclear fusion"}
The joining of two light nuclei (such as isotopes of hydrogen) to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy.
:::

## Nuclear fission

A slow-moving neutron is absorbed by a uranium-235 nucleus, which becomes very unstable and splits into two smaller nuclei ("fission fragments"), giving out two or three neutrons and a large amount of energy (as kinetic energy of the fragments and gamma radiation).

:::worked-solution[Checking a fission equation]
$$
{}^{235}_{92}\text{U} + {}^{1}_{0}\text{n} \rightarrow {}^{141}_{56}\text{Ba} + {}^{92}_{36}\text{Kr} + 3\,{}^{1}_{0}\text{n}
$$

1. Nucleon numbers: left $235 + 1 = 236$; right $141 + 92 + 3 \times 1 = 236$. They balance.
2. Proton numbers: left $92 + 0 = 92$; right $56 + 36 + 0 = 92$. They balance.
:::

The neutrons released can cause further fissions, giving a **chain reaction**. In a power station the chain reaction is **controlled**, so that on average one neutron from each fission causes another; the energy heats water to drive turbines and generators.

## Nuclear fusion

Two light nuclei are forced together so closely that they join. Because both are positive, they repel strongly, so fusion needs **extremely high temperatures and pressures**, such as those in the core of the Sun and other stars.

:::worked-solution[Checking a fusion equation]
$$
{}^{2}_{1}\text{H} + {}^{3}_{1}\text{H} \rightarrow {}^{4}_{2}\text{He} + {}^{1}_{0}\text{n}
$$

1. Nucleon numbers: $2 + 3 = 5$ and $4 + 1 = 5$.
2. Proton numbers: $1 + 1 = 2$ and $2 + 0 = 2$.
3. Deuterium and tritium (isotopes of hydrogen) fuse to form helium and a neutron.
:::

## Where does the energy come from?

In both fission and fusion, the total **mass** of the products is very slightly **less** than the total mass of the particles that reacted. This "missing" mass has been converted into **energy**, which is released as the kinetic energy of the products and as radiation. A very small loss of mass gives a very large amount of energy, which is why nuclear fuels are so concentrated: a few kilograms of uranium can release as much energy as thousands of tonnes of coal.

| | Fission | Fusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| what happens | a heavy nucleus splits | light nuclei join |
| typical fuel | uranium-235, plutonium-239 | hydrogen isotopes |
| conditions | absorption of a neutron | very high temperature and pressure |
| where | nuclear power stations, nuclear weapons | the Sun and other stars; experimental reactors |
| mass of products | slightly less than before: energy released | slightly less than before: energy released |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Fission and fusion mixed up"}
**Fission** splits (think "fissure", a crack); **fusion** joins (think "fuse" together).
:::

:::mistake{title="Numbers do not balance"}
In every nuclear equation, the top numbers (nucleon numbers) and the bottom numbers (proton numbers) must each add up to the same total on both sides.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
To find a missing particle in a nuclear equation, balance the nucleon numbers and then the proton numbers; the proton number tells you which element it is.
:::

:::exam-tip
When explaining the energy released, say that the mass of the products is less than the mass before, and the lost mass is released as energy.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Fission: a heavy nucleus absorbs a neutron and splits into two smaller nuclei, releasing neutrons and energy.
- The released neutrons can cause a chain reaction, controlled in a nuclear reactor.
- Fusion: light nuclei join to form a heavier nucleus; it needs very high temperatures and pressures and powers the stars.
- In both, the products have slightly less mass than the reactants; the lost mass is released as energy.
- Nucleon numbers and proton numbers balance in every nuclear equation.
:::
