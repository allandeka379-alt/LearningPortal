## Introduction

Why does a log of msasa wood float down a flooded river while a granite pebble of the same size sinks? Why does cooking oil sit on top of water in a pot? The answer is **density**: how much mass is packed into each unit of volume.

You should already be able to measure mass with a balance and volume with a measuring cylinder.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Density"}
The mass per unit volume of a substance.
:::

:::formula{name="Density"}
$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V}
$$

where $\rho$ (the Greek letter rho) is the density, $m$ the mass and $V$ the volume. Units: $\text{kg/m}^3$ (SI) or $\text{g/cm}^3$.
:::

:::keyfact
$1\ \text{g/cm}^3 = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$. Pure water has a density of about $1.0\ \text{g/cm}^3$ ($1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$).
:::

## Measuring density

### A regular solid

Measure its mass on a balance. Measure its dimensions with a rule (or Vernier callipers) and calculate the volume, for example length × width × height for a cuboid. Then divide.

### A liquid

Find the mass of an empty measuring cylinder, pour in a measured volume of the liquid, and find the mass again. The difference is the mass of the liquid.

:::worked-solution[The density of cooking oil]
1. Mass of the empty measuring cylinder $= 62\ \text{g}$.
2. With $50\ \text{cm}^3$ of oil added, the mass is $108\ \text{g}$.
3. Mass of oil $= 108 - 62 = 46\ \text{g}$.
4. Density $= \dfrac{46\ \text{g}}{50\ \text{cm}^3} = 0.92\ \text{g/cm}^3 = 920\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
:::

### An irregular solid that sinks (displacement)

Part-fill a measuring cylinder with water and note the level. Lower the object in gently on a thread so that no water splashes out. The rise in level is the volume of the object.

![Density by displacement: water in a measuring cylinder reads 50 cm³; with the 156 g stone lowered in on a thread it reads 110 cm³. The volume of the stone is 110 minus 50, which is 60 cm³, so its density is 156 divided by 60, which is 2.6 g/cm³.](media:diagrams/density-displacement-stone.png "Figure: finding the volume of an irregular stone by displacement")

:::worked-solution[The density of a stone]
1. A stone has a mass of $156\ \text{g}$.
2. The water level rises from $50\ \text{cm}^3$ to $110\ \text{cm}^3$ when it is lowered in.
3. Volume of the stone $= 110 - 50 = 60\ \text{cm}^3$.
4. Density $= \dfrac{156}{60} = 2.6\ \text{g/cm}^3 = 2600\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
:::

For a large object, a **displacement (eureka) can** is used instead: the water that overflows into a beaker is measured.

## Floating and sinking

An object **floats** in a liquid if its density is **less** than the liquid's density, and **sinks** if its density is greater. Wood (about $600\ \text{kg/m}^3$) floats on water; stone and iron sink.

Two liquids that do not mix form layers: the **less dense** liquid floats on top. Cooking oil ($920\ \text{kg/m}^3$) floats on water; water floats on mercury ($13\,600\ \text{kg/m}^3$). (For Cambridge, comparing liquids is Supplement content.)

:::example{title="Why a steel ship floats"}
A ship is made of steel ($7800\ \text{kg/m}^3$) but its hull encloses a large volume of air. The average density of the whole ship (steel plus air inside) is less than that of water, so it floats.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing units"}
A mass in grams divided by a volume in cubic metres gives a meaningless number. Use g with cm³ (answer in g/cm³) or kg with m³ (answer in kg/m³).
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the final water level as the volume"}
The volume of the object is the **rise** in level, not the final reading.
:::

:::mistake{title="Heavy things sink"}
Floating depends on density, not on mass: a huge log floats while a tiny nail sinks.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When describing the displacement method, mention lowering the object gently on a thread, reading the bottom of the meniscus at eye level, and subtracting the two readings.
:::

:::exam-tip
To convert g/cm³ to kg/m³, multiply by 1000. A quick check: densities in kg/m³ for solids and liquids are usually hundreds or thousands.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Density is mass per unit volume: $\rho = m/V$, in kg/m³ or g/cm³ ($1\ \text{g/cm}^3 = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$).
- Regular solids: volume from measurements. Liquids: mass by difference. Irregular solids: volume by displacement.
- An object floats if it is less dense than the liquid.
- Liquids that do not mix settle with the least dense on top.
:::
