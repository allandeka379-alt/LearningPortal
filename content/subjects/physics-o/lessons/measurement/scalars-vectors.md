## Introduction

"The kombi travelled 40 km" and "the kombi travelled 40 km north" are different statements. The first gives only a size; the second also gives a direction. In physics many quantities need a direction before they are fully described, and adding them is not the same as adding ordinary numbers.

For Cambridge this lesson is Supplement (Extended) content; ZIMSEC learners meet it in topic 1.2.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Scalar"}
A quantity that has magnitude (size) only, for example mass or temperature.
:::

:::definition{term="Vector"}
A quantity that has magnitude **and** direction, for example force or velocity.
:::

:::definition{term="Resultant"}
The single vector that has the same effect as two or more vectors acting together.
:::

## Scalars and vectors

| Scalars | Vectors |
| --- | --- |
| distance | displacement |
| speed | velocity |
| time | acceleration |
| mass | force (including weight) |
| energy | momentum |
| temperature | electric field strength |
| volume, density | gravitational field strength |

A vector is drawn as an arrow: its length shows the magnitude and its direction shows the direction.

## Adding vectors along one line

If two forces act along the same line, add them when they point the same way and subtract when they point opposite ways. The resultant points in the direction of the larger force.

## Adding two vectors at right angles

Draw the vectors **nose to tail**: start the second arrow where the first one ends. The resultant goes from the start of the first arrow to the end of the second. With a right angle between them, Pythagoras gives the size and trigonometry gives the direction.

:::worked-solution[A boat crossing the Zambezi]
A boat heads straight across a river at $3.0\ \text{m/s}$ relative to the water, and the current flows downstream at $1.6\ \text{m/s}$.

1. The two velocities are at right angles, so the resultant speed is $\sqrt{3.0^2 + 1.6^2} = \sqrt{11.56} = 3.4\ \text{m/s}$.
2. The angle $\theta$ between the resultant and the straight-across direction satisfies $\tan\theta = \dfrac{1.6}{3.0}$.
3. $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.533) = 28^\circ$ downstream of straight across.
:::

:::worked-solution[Two forces on a crate]
A crate is pulled with $9.0\ \text{N}$ due east and $4.0\ \text{N}$ due north.

1. Resultant $= \sqrt{9.0^2 + 4.0^2} = \sqrt{97} = 9.8\ \text{N}$.
2. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{4.0}{9.0}$, so $\theta = 24^\circ$.
3. The resultant is $9.8\ \text{N}$ at $24^\circ$ north of east.
:::

### By scale drawing

1. Choose a scale, for example $1\ \text{cm}$ represents $1\ \text{N}$.
2. Draw the first vector to scale with a ruler and protractor.
3. Draw the second vector from the head of the first.
4. Draw the resultant from the tail of the first to the head of the second, measure its length and convert it with the scale; measure its angle with a protractor.

![Velocity triangle for a boat crossing a river: 3.0 m/s straight across and 1.6 m/s downstream, drawn nose to tail. The resultant is 3.4 m/s (square root of 3.0 squared plus 1.6 squared) at 28 degrees downstream of straight across, since tan of the angle is 1.6 over 3.0.](media:diagrams/vectors-boat-river.png "Figure: adding two velocities at right angles, nose to tail")

A scale drawing is less precise than a calculation but works for any angle, which is why ZIMSEC asks for the graphical method for coplanar vectors.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Adding magnitudes directly"}
$3.0\ \text{m/s}$ across and $1.6\ \text{m/s}$ downstream do **not** make $4.6\ \text{m/s}$. Only vectors along the same line add like ordinary numbers.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving out the direction"}
A vector answer needs a direction: "9.8 N" alone loses the direction mark; "9.8 N at 24° north of east" earns it.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In a scale drawing, state your scale, draw arrows at least $8\ \text{cm}$ long for accuracy, and label the resultant with a double arrowhead.
:::

:::exam-tip
"Distance, speed and mass are scalars; displacement, velocity and weight are vectors" is a favourite one-mark question. Learn the table.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A scalar has magnitude only; a vector has magnitude and direction.
- Displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, weight and momentum are vectors; distance, speed, time, mass, energy and temperature are scalars.
- Vectors along one line add or subtract like signed numbers.
- For two vectors at right angles: resultant $= \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$, direction from $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$.
- A scale drawing (nose to tail) finds the resultant for any angle.
:::
