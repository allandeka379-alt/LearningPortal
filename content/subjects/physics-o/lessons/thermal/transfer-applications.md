## Introduction

Good designs control thermal energy: they keep tea hot, water cold, rooms comfortable and engines from overheating. Each design reduces or uses **conduction**, **convection** and **radiation**. This lesson puts the three together.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Insulation"}
Material or a design feature that reduces the rate of thermal energy transfer, for example trapped air, a vacuum or a shiny surface.
:::

:::definition{term="Sea breeze"}
A daytime wind blowing from the sea (or a large lake) towards the land, caused by convection over the warmer land.
:::

## The vacuum flask

A vacuum flask keeps hot drinks hot and cold drinks cold by reducing all three transfers:

| Feature | Reduces |
| --- | --- |
| **vacuum** between the double walls | conduction and convection (no particles) |
| **silvered** inner surfaces | radiation (shiny surfaces are poor emitters and reflect radiation back) |
| **stopper** of cork or plastic, an insulator | conduction, convection and evaporation through the top |
| insulated supports holding the inner bottle | conduction to the outer case |

## The solar water heater (ZIMSEC)

A solar water heater on a roof heats water with sunlight:

- a **collector panel** of copper pipes painted **dull black** absorbs infrared radiation well;
- a **glass cover** lets sunlight in but traps the warm air (reducing convection losses), and the panel's back is **insulated** to reduce conduction losses;
- heated water becomes less dense and rises by **convection** to an insulated storage tank placed **above** the panel, while cooler water sinks back down to the panel, so the water circulates with no pump.

## Sea and land breezes

Water has a much higher specific heat capacity than land, so the land heats up and cools down faster.

- **By day** the land is warmer than the water. Air over the land is heated, expands and rises; cooler air from over the water moves in to replace it: a **sea breeze** (or lake breeze, by Lake Kariba).
- **At night** the land cools faster and the water stays warmer. Air rises over the water and air flows from the land: a **land breeze**.

## Everyday examples

- **Cooking pans** have copper or aluminium bases (good conductors) and plastic handles (insulators).
- **Room heating**: a heater near the floor warms air that rises and circulates by convection.
- **Clothing and houses**: wool, blankets and thatched roofs trap air to reduce conduction; white-washed walls reflect sunlight to keep houses cool.

## Several transfers at once (Cambridge Supplement)

:::example{title="A fire burning wood or coal"}
The flames and hot coals emit strong infrared **radiation**, which warms people facing the fire. Hot gases above the fire rise by **convection**, carrying smoke up the chimney and drawing in fresh air. A metal poker left in the fire becomes hot along its length by **conduction**.
:::

:::example{title="A car radiator"}
Water pumped through the engine is heated by **conduction** from the hot metal and carries the energy (forced **convection**) to the radiator. The radiator's thin metal fins conduct energy from the water to their large surface, where air flowing past carries it away by **convection**, and a little is emitted by **radiation**.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="The vacuum stops radiation"}
Radiation passes through a vacuum. The **silvering** reduces radiation; the vacuum stops conduction and convection.
:::

:::mistake{title="The storage tank goes below the panel"}
In a solar water heater without a pump, the tank must be **above** the panel so hot water rises into it by convection.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For each design feature, name the method of transfer it reduces **and** explain how: "the vacuum has no particles, so conduction and convection cannot occur".
:::

:::exam-tip
For breezes, link specific heat capacity, the temperature difference, rising warm air and the direction of the wind.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A vacuum flask: vacuum (stops conduction and convection), silvered walls (reduce radiation), insulating stopper.
- A solar water heater: black collector, glass cover, insulation, and a tank above the panel for convection.
- Sea breezes by day and land breezes at night arise because land heats and cools faster than water.
- Pans, clothes and buildings use conductors, insulators and trapped air to control energy transfer.
- Fires and car radiators involve conduction, convection and radiation together.
:::
