## Introduction

On a cold winter morning in Nyanga the grass is covered in frost; by midday it has melted, and the puddles slowly dry up in the sun. Water can be a solid (ice), a liquid and a gas (water vapour), and it changes between these **states of matter** as it is heated or cooled. Every substance can exist in these three states.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Solid"}
A state with a fixed shape and a fixed volume.
:::

:::definition{term="Liquid"}
A state with a fixed volume but no fixed shape: it takes the shape of its container and can flow.
:::

:::definition{term="Gas"}
A state with no fixed shape and no fixed volume: it spreads out to fill any container.
:::

## Properties of the three states

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| shape | fixed | takes the shape of the container | fills the whole container |
| volume | fixed | fixed | fills the whole container |
| can it flow? | no | yes | yes |
| can it be compressed? | hardly at all | hardly at all | easily |
| density | usually high | usually high | low |

:::example{title="Gases are easy to squash"}
A bicycle pump with its outlet blocked can be pushed in a long way when it holds air, but hardly at all when it is filled with water. Gases can be compressed; liquids and solids almost cannot.
:::

## Changes of state

| Change | From → to | Name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| heating | solid → liquid | **melting** |
| heating | liquid → gas | **boiling** or **evaporating** |
| cooling | gas → liquid | **condensing** (condensation) |
| cooling | liquid → solid | **freezing** (solidifying) |

**Evaporation** happens at the surface of a liquid at any temperature (washing dries on the line); **boiling** happens throughout the liquid at one temperature, the boiling point.

ZIMSEC also mentions **sublimation**: a direct change from solid to gas without melting, as happens with iodine crystals or solid carbon dioxide ("dry ice") when warmed. (Cambridge does not require changes between gas and solid.)

:::example{title="Water around us"}
Water from Lake Kariba evaporates in the heat of the day; the vapour rises, cools and condenses into clouds; rain falls back into the catchment. On cold nights water vapour condenses on cool surfaces as dew, or freezes as frost.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Steam and water vapour are visible"}
Water vapour is an invisible gas. The white "steam" above a kettle is tiny droplets of liquid water that have already condensed in the cooler air.
:::

:::mistake{title="Liquids have no fixed volume"}
A liquid keeps its volume when poured into a different container; only its shape changes.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When asked to compare states, use the same properties for each: shape, volume, ability to flow and compressibility.
:::

:::exam-tip
Use the exact names of the changes of state: melting, freezing (solidifying), boiling, evaporating and condensing.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Solids have a fixed shape and volume; liquids a fixed volume but no fixed shape; gases neither.
- Liquids and gases can flow; gases are easily compressed.
- Melting (solid to liquid), boiling and evaporating (liquid to gas), condensing (gas to liquid), freezing (liquid to solid).
- Evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature; boiling happens throughout at the boiling point.
- Sublimation is a direct solid-to-gas change (ZIMSEC).
:::
