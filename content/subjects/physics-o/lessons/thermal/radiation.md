## Introduction

Energy from the Sun crosses 150 million kilometres of empty space to warm the Earth: neither conduction nor convection can do that, because both need particles. The energy travels as **thermal radiation**. Everything around you, including your own body, gives out this radiation all the time.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Thermal radiation"}
The transfer of energy by infrared electromagnetic waves. It needs no medium and can travel through a vacuum.
:::

:::definition{term="Emitter"}
A surface that gives out radiation.
:::

:::definition{term="Absorber"}
A surface that takes in radiation, which warms it.
:::

## Facts about thermal radiation

- It is **infrared radiation**, part of the electromagnetic spectrum.
- **All objects** emit it; hotter objects emit more.
- It travels at the speed of light and needs **no medium**: it crosses a vacuum.

## Surfaces: colour and texture

| Surface | Emitting | Absorbing | Reflecting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dull (matt) black | best emitter | best absorber | worst reflector |
| shiny white or silver | worst emitter | worst absorber | best reflector |

Good absorbers are also good emitters.

:::example{title="Leslie's cube"}
A hollow metal cube has four faces finished differently: dull black, shiny black, dull white and shiny silver. It is filled with hot water, so all faces are at the same temperature. An infrared detector held at the same distance from each face gives the highest reading for the dull black face and the lowest for the shiny silver face: dull black is the best emitter.
:::

:::example{title="Comparing absorbers"}
Two identical metal plates, one dull black and one shiny silver, are placed at equal distances on either side of a radiant heater, each with a coin or drawing pin fixed to its back with wax. The wax on the black plate melts first: dull black is the better absorber.
:::

## Energy balance (Cambridge Supplement)

An object stays at a **constant temperature** when it transfers energy away at the same rate as it receives it. If it receives energy faster than it loses it, its temperature rises; if it loses energy faster, its temperature falls. As an object gets hotter it emits more, until a new balance is reached.

The rate of emission is greater for a higher **surface temperature** and a larger **surface area**.

The Earth's temperature depends on the same balance: incoming radiation from the Sun against infrared emitted from the Earth's surface. Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide absorb some of the emitted infrared and re-emit it, some back towards the ground, which keeps the Earth warmer; increasing them upsets the balance and raises the average temperature.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Radiation needs air"}
Thermal radiation travels best through a vacuum; it does not need particles at all.
:::

:::mistake{title="Shiny surfaces are good emitters"}
Shiny surfaces reflect radiation and are poor emitters and poor absorbers.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When describing a Leslie's cube experiment, say that all faces are at the same temperature and the detector is the same distance from each face; that makes the comparison fair.
:::

:::exam-tip
Use the full phrase "dull black surfaces are good absorbers **and** good emitters of infrared radiation".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Thermal radiation is infrared; all objects emit it, and it needs no medium.
- Dull black surfaces are the best emitters and absorbers; shiny white or silver surfaces are the best reflectors.
- Leslie's cube compares emitters; plates with wax compare absorbers.
- An object's temperature is constant when it loses energy as fast as it gains it.
- Emission increases with surface temperature and surface area.
:::
