## Introduction

On a hot October afternoon, the overhead power lines between pylons sag lower than on a cool morning. Railway lines and bridges have small gaps built into them. A tight metal lid on a glass jar can be loosened by running hot water over it. All of these happen because most materials **expand** when heated and **contract** when cooled.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Thermal expansion"}
The increase in size (length, area or volume) of a material when its temperature rises.
:::

:::definition{term="Bimetallic strip"}
Two strips of different metals joined along their length; when heated it bends, because one metal expands more than the other.
:::

## Solids, liquids and gases

All three expand when heated at constant pressure, but by very different amounts:

- **gases** expand the most;
- **liquids** expand more than solids;
- **solids** expand the least.

### Why (Cambridge Supplement)

When a substance is heated, its particles move faster and, on average, further apart. In a **solid** the strong forces between particles hold them tightly, so the spacing grows only a little. In a **liquid** the forces are weaker, so the particles move apart more. In a **gas** the forces are almost zero, so the gas expands a great deal.

:::example{title="The ball and ring"}
A metal ball just passes through a metal ring when both are cold. After the ball is heated in a Bunsen flame it no longer fits through: it has expanded. When it cools, it fits again.
:::

## Applications of expansion

- **Liquid-in-glass thermometers**: mercury or alcohol expands up a narrow tube as the temperature rises.
- **Bimetallic strip thermostats**: in an electric iron, kettle or fire alarm, a strip of brass and iron bends when hot (brass expands more), breaking or making an electrical contact.
- **Fitting tight parts**: a metal tyre or ring is heated so it expands and slips on, then shrinks tightly as it cools; a stuck jar lid is loosened with hot water.

## Problems caused by expansion

- **Railway lines and bridges** have **expansion gaps** (or sliding joints) so they do not buckle on hot days.
- **Concrete roads** are laid in sections with gaps filled with a soft material.
- **Overhead cables** are hung with some slack so they do not snap when they contract on cold nights.
- **Thick glass** may crack if boiling water is poured in, because the inside expands before the outside.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Particles get bigger"}
In expansion the particles move further apart; they do not swell.
:::

:::mistake{title="Liquids do not expand"}
Liquids do expand, more than solids. A full car fuel tank in the sun can overflow as the petrol warms.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For a bimetallic strip, say which metal expands more (it ends up on the outside of the curve) and how the bending opens or closes a circuit.
:::

:::exam-tip
"Explain why a gap is left" questions need: expansion on heating, the force it would cause, and the damage (buckling or cracking) the gap prevents.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Solids, liquids and gases expand when heated; gases most, then liquids, then solids.
- Particles move faster and further apart; weaker forces between particles allow more expansion.
- Uses: thermometers, bimetallic thermostats, shrink-fitting.
- Problems are avoided with expansion gaps in railways, bridges and roads, and slack in cables.
- Sudden uneven heating can crack thick glass.
:::
