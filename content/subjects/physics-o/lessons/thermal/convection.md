## Introduction

Why is the heating element of an electric kettle at the bottom, while the freezing compartment of an old-style fridge is at the top? Why does smoke rise above a fire? In liquids and gases, energy is mainly carried by the fluid itself moving: this is **convection**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Convection"}
The transfer of thermal energy through a liquid or gas by the movement of the fluid itself, caused by differences in density.
:::

:::definition{term="Convection current"}
The circulating flow set up when warm fluid rises and cooler fluid sinks to replace it.
:::

## How convection works

1. Fluid near a heat source warms up. Its particles move faster and spread out, so the fluid **expands**.
2. The warm fluid becomes **less dense** than the cooler fluid around it, so it **rises**.
3. Cooler, **denser** fluid sinks to take its place and is heated in turn.
4. A continuous circulation, a **convection current**, carries energy through the whole fluid.

Convection cannot happen in solids, because their particles cannot flow.

![Beaker of water with a purple crystal at the bottom near the left side, heated gently under that side: the purple stream rises on the heated side, spreads across the top, sinks on the cool side and returns along the bottom, showing a convection current.](media:diagrams/convection-beaker-crystal.png "Figure: a convection current made visible with a potassium permanganate crystal")

:::example{title="Watching convection in water"}
Drop a single crystal of potassium permanganate down a straw to the bottom of a beaker of water, near one side. Heat that side gently with a small flame. A purple stream rises above the crystal, spreads across the top, sinks down the far side and returns along the bottom: the convection current made visible.
:::

:::example{title="Convection in air"}
In a convection chamber (a box with two glass chimneys), a candle burns under one chimney. Smoke from smouldering paper held over the other chimney is drawn down that chimney, across the box and up and out over the candle, showing the air current.
:::

## Convection in everyday life

- **Kettles and hot-water tanks** are heated at the bottom, so the warmed water rises and the whole volume circulates.
- **Fridges** have the cooling unit at the top; cooled air sinks and the warmer air rises to be cooled.
- **Room heaters** work best near the floor; warm air rises and circulates. **Air conditioners** are mounted high on walls, so cooled air sinks through the room.
- **Hot-air balloons** rise because the heated air inside is less dense than the surrounding air.
- **Chimneys** draw smoke up because the hot gases are less dense.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Heat rises"}
It is warm **fluid** that rises, because it is less dense; energy itself does not "rise". Radiation and conduction can transfer energy downwards.
:::

:::mistake{title="Convection in solids"}
Solids cannot flow, so there is no convection in them, only conduction (and radiation through transparent ones).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Explanations of convection must mention **density**: heated fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises; cooler, denser fluid sinks to replace it.
:::

:::exam-tip
When describing the potassium permanganate experiment, say where the crystal is placed, where it is heated, and the path of the colour.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Convection is energy transfer by the movement of a liquid or gas.
- Heated fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises; cooler, denser fluid sinks.
- The circulation is a convection current.
- Kettles are heated at the bottom; fridges are cooled at the top; heaters go low and air conditioners high.
- Convection does not occur in solids.
:::
