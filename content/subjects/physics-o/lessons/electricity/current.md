## Introduction

When you switch on a light, the lamp glows almost at once, even though the switch may be several metres away. Charge is already present in every part of the wire; the cell or the mains supply makes it all flow together, like water filling a pipe that is already full. That flow of charge is an **electric current**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Electric current, I"}
The rate of flow of electric charge past a point in a circuit. Unit: ampere (A).
:::

:::definition{term="Direct current (d.c.)"}
Current that flows in one direction only, as from a cell or battery.
:::

:::definition{term="Alternating current (a.c.)"}
Current that repeatedly reverses its direction, as from the mains supply.
:::

## Current and charge

:::formula{name="Current"}
$$
I = \frac{Q}{t}
$$
where $I$ is the current (A), $Q$ the charge (C) passing a point and $t$ the time (s). One ampere is a flow of one coulomb per second. (Cambridge Supplement; ZIMSEC 11.3.)
:::

:::worked-solution[Current in a lamp]
A charge of $180\ \text{C}$ flows through a lamp in $2.0$ minutes.

1. Convert the time: $t = 2.0 \times 60 = 120\ \text{s}$.
2. $I = \dfrac{Q}{t} = \dfrac{180}{120} = 1.5\ \text{A}$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Charge from a phone charger]
A phone charger supplies a current of $2.0\ \text{A}$ for 30 minutes.

1. $t = 30 \times 60 = 1800\ \text{s}$.
2. $Q = It = 2.0 \times 1800 = 3600\ \text{C}$.
:::

## How metals conduct

A metal contains **free electrons** that can drift between the fixed positive ions. With no cell connected, they move randomly in all directions and there is no overall flow. When a cell is connected, the electrons drift towards the **positive** terminal; this drift of charge is the current.

### Conventional current and electron flow (Cambridge Supplement)

Before electrons were discovered, scientists chose to say that current flows from the **positive** terminal round the circuit to the **negative** terminal. This **conventional current** direction is still used on circuit diagrams. The electrons in a metal actually move the opposite way, from **negative to positive**.

## Direct and alternating current

A cell drives a d.c.: the current always flows the same way round the circuit. The mains supply is a.c.: the current reverses direction many times each second. In Zimbabwe, as in most of Africa and Europe, the mains frequency is $50\ \text{Hz}$, so the current goes back and forth 50 times every second.

## Measuring current with an ammeter

An **ammeter** measures current in amperes; a milliammeter measures in milliamperes ($1\ \text{mA} = 0.001\ \text{A}$).

- Connect the ammeter **in series**, so that all the current being measured passes through it.
- Connect its **positive** (red) terminal towards the positive terminal of the supply.
- On an **analogue** meter, check the needle reads zero before connecting, read the scale with your eye directly above the needle, and choose a range so the needle moves well across the scale.
- On a **digital** meter, start on the highest range and switch down until the reading has as many digits as possible without going over the range.

:::example{title="Choosing a range"}
A current of about $0.3\ \text{A}$ is expected. On a meter with ranges $0$–$10\ \text{A}$, $0$–$1\ \text{A}$ and $0$–$100\ \text{mA}$, the $0$–$1\ \text{A}$ range is best: the $10\ \text{A}$ range would give a tiny deflection, and $300\ \text{mA}$ would go off the $100\ \text{mA}$ scale.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Current is used up"}
Current is not used up by a lamp. The same current flows into and out of each component in series; it is **energy** that is transferred.
:::

:::mistake{title="Minutes in I = Q/t"}
Time must be in seconds. Convert minutes and hours before calculating.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Rearrange $I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$ as $Q = It$ or $t = \dfrac{Q}{I}$, and always write the unit: A, C or s.
:::

:::exam-tip
In circuit drawings, an ammeter is always in series; a voltmeter is always in parallel.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Current is the rate of flow of charge: $I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$, measured in amperes.
- Metals conduct because free electrons drift through them.
- Conventional current flows from positive to negative; electrons flow from negative to positive.
- D.c. flows one way; a.c. reverses direction (the mains in Zimbabwe is 50 Hz).
- Ammeters are connected in series, with a suitable range.
:::
