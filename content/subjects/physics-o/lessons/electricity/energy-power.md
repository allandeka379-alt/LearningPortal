## Introduction

A geyser heats water for a bath, a stove cooks sadza, a fridge keeps milk cold and a phone charger tops up a battery. Each appliance transfers energy from the mains supply at its own rate, its **power**. Knowing the power and the time an appliance runs lets you work out the energy it uses, and how much that energy costs.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Electrical power, P"}
The rate at which an electrical source or component transfers energy. Unit: watt (W); $1\ \text{W} = 1\ \text{J/s}$.
:::

:::definition{term="Kilowatt-hour (kW h)"}
The energy transferred by a $1\ \text{kW}$ appliance working for one hour: $1\ \text{kW h} = 3.6 \times 10^{6}\ \text{J}$. Electricity companies sell energy in kilowatt-hours ("units").
:::

## Energy transfers in circuits

A cell or the mains supply transfers energy to the charges; the components transfer it from the charges to other stores and then to the surroundings.

| Appliance | Main useful energy transfer |
| --- | --- |
| lamp | electrical energy to light (and thermal energy) |
| kettle, stove, geyser, iron | electrical energy to thermal energy |
| fan, borehole pump, food mixer | electrical energy to kinetic energy (motor) |
| radio, loudspeaker | electrical energy to sound |
| phone charger | electrical energy to chemical energy in the battery |

In every case some energy is wasted, usually as thermal energy in the surroundings.

## Power and energy

:::formula{name="Electrical power and energy"}
$$
P = IV \qquad E = IVt
$$
where $I$ is the current (A), $V$ the p.d. (V), $t$ the time (s), $P$ the power (W) and $E$ the energy (J). Since $E = Pt$, energy can also be found from the power rating.
:::

:::worked-solution[Power of a kettle]
A kettle connected to a $230\ \text{V}$ supply takes a current of $8.0\ \text{A}$.

1. $P = IV = 8.0 \times 230 = 1840\ \text{W}$ (about $1.8\ \text{kW}$).
2. Energy in 3.0 minutes: $E = IVt = 8.0 \times 230 \times 180 = 331\,200\ \text{J} \approx 3.3 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J}$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Current drawn by an appliance]
An electric iron is rated $1000\ \text{W}$, $230\ \text{V}$.

1. $I = \dfrac{P}{V} = \dfrac{1000}{230} = 4.3\ \text{A}$.
:::

## The cost of electricity

To work out a cost:

1. Write the power in **kilowatts** and the time in **hours**.
2. Energy (kW h) $=$ power (kW) $\times$ time (h).
3. Cost $=$ energy (kW h) $\times$ price per kW h.

:::worked-solution[Cooking for a month]
A $1.5\ \text{kW}$ stove plate is used for 40 minutes each day for 30 days. Electricity costs \$0.10 per kW h (an assumed tariff for this example).

1. Time each day $= \dfrac{40}{60} = \dfrac{2}{3}\ \text{h}$, so total time $= 30 \times \dfrac{2}{3} = 20\ \text{h}$.
2. Energy $= 1.5 \times 20 = 30\ \text{kW h}$.
3. Cost $= 30 \times 0.10$ = \$3.00.
:::

Many homes in Zimbabwe use prepaid meters: you buy a number of units (kW h) and the meter counts them down. With an ordinary meter, the energy used is the difference between two readings, for example $45\,326 - 45\,104 = 222\ \text{kW h}$ in a month.

## Using electricity wisely

Appliances that heat (geysers, stoves, kettles, irons, heaters) have the highest power ratings, so they use the most units. Switching geysers off when hot water is not needed, using LED lamps instead of filament lamps, and boiling only the water you need all reduce the bill.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Watts and kilowatt-hours"}
The watt is a unit of **power**; the kilowatt-hour is a unit of **energy**. A bill is for energy (kW h), not power.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing units in cost calculations"}
For kW h, use kilowatts and hours. Using watts and minutes gives an answer that is wrong by a large factor.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In cost questions, show the three steps (kW, hours, kW h × price) and give the unit of money in the answer.
:::

:::exam-tip
To convert kW h to joules, multiply by $3.6 \times 10^{6}$ ($1000\ \text{W} \times 3600\ \text{s}$).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Circuits transfer energy from a source to components and then to the surroundings.
- $P = IV$ (watts) and $E = IVt = Pt$ (joules).
- $1\ \text{kW h} = 3.6 \times 10^{6}\ \text{J}$ is the energy used by a 1 kW appliance in one hour.
- Cost $=$ power (kW) $\times$ time (h) $\times$ price per kW h.
- Heating appliances have the largest power ratings and use the most energy.
:::
