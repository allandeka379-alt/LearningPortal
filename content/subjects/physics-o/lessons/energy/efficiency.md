## Introduction

When a family switches from old filament bulbs to LED bulbs, the house is just as bright but the electricity bill falls. LED bulbs are more **efficient**: more of the energy supplied comes out as useful light, and less is wasted heating the room. Efficiency matters for appliances, vehicles and whole power stations.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Useful energy"}
The energy transferred to the store we want, for example light from a bulb or kinetic energy from a motor.
:::

:::definition{term="Wasted energy"}
Energy transferred to stores we do not want, usually the internal (thermal) store of the surroundings.
:::

:::definition{term="Efficiency"}
The fraction (or percentage) of the energy, or power, supplied that is transferred usefully.
:::

:::formula{name="Efficiency (Cambridge Supplement)"}
$$
\text{efficiency} = \frac{\text{useful energy output}}{\text{total energy input}} \times 100\% = \frac{\text{useful power output}}{\text{total power input}} \times 100\%
$$
:::

## Calculating efficiency

:::worked-solution[An electric motor]
1. A motor is supplied with $400\ \text{J}$ of electrical energy and transfers $300\ \text{J}$ to the kinetic store of the load.
2. Efficiency $= \dfrac{300}{400} \times 100\% = 75\%$.
3. The other $100\ \text{J}$ is wasted, mostly heating the motor and the air.
:::

:::worked-solution[A coal-fired power station]
1. Burning coal supplies $2000\ \text{MW}$ of power; the station delivers $700\ \text{MW}$ of electrical power.
2. Efficiency $= \dfrac{700}{2000} \times 100\% = 35\%$.
3. The remaining $1300\ \text{MW}$ heats the cooling water, the air and the surroundings.
:::

You can also rearrange the equation: useful output = efficiency × input.

## Why nothing is 100% efficient

Every real energy transfer wastes some energy:

- **friction** between moving parts heats them;
- electric currents heat the wires (resistance);
- **sound** is produced;
- in power stations and engines, a large amount of energy is carried away by hot gases and cooling water.

The wasted energy spreads out into the surroundings and warms them slightly. It is not destroyed (energy is conserved), but it is no longer useful because it is so spread out: we say it has been **dissipated**.

## Improving efficiency

- Reduce friction by lubricating moving parts.
- Use thicker wires or lower-resistance materials to reduce electrical heating.
- Insulate hot water tanks and pipes so less energy escapes.
- Choose efficient devices: LED bulbs instead of filament bulbs, modern motors and engines.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Efficiency above 100%"}
If you get more than 100%, you have divided the input by the output. Efficiency is always output ÷ input and never exceeds 100%.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing energy and power"}
Use energies on both the top and bottom, or powers on both; do not mix joules with watts.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Give efficiency as a percentage or a decimal fraction, but say which: 0.75 and 75% are both accepted when written correctly.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked why a device is not 100% efficient, name the wasted forms (heating, sound) and their cause (friction, resistance).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Efficiency compares useful output with total input.
- Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input × 100% (energy or power).
- Useful output + wasted output = input (conservation of energy).
- Wasted energy usually heats the surroundings and is dissipated.
- Lubrication, insulation and better designs improve efficiency; nothing reaches 100%.
:::
