## Introduction

Zimbabwe gets most of its electricity from two very different sources: **coal** burnt at Hwange Power Station and **water** falling through the turbines of Kariba South. When drought lowers Lake Kariba, hydroelectric output drops and load shedding follows; many homes, schools and farms have turned to **solar** panels. Choosing energy resources is a balance of cost, reliability and the environment.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Renewable resource"}
An energy resource that is replaced naturally as fast as we use it, such as sunlight, wind, flowing water or biofuels from replanted crops.
:::

:::definition{term="Non-renewable resource"}
A resource that will run out because it is not replaced, such as coal, oil, natural gas and uranium.
:::

## How most power stations work

In most power stations, a **turbine** is turned and drives a **generator**, which produces electricity:

- In **thermal** power stations (coal, oil, gas, biofuel, nuclear, geothermal), energy heats water into high-pressure **steam**, which spins the turbine.
- In **hydroelectric**, wave, tidal and wind power, moving water or air turns the turbine directly.
- **Solar cells** are different: they transfer light energy directly into electrical energy, with no turbine.

## The resources

| Resource | How it works | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas) | burnt to make steam | reliable; large scale; available on demand | non-renewable; carbon dioxide (global warming); coal gives sulfur dioxide (acid rain) |
| Biofuels (wood, ethanol, biogas) | burnt like fossil fuels | renewable if replanted; can use waste | land for crops; deforestation if trees are not replanted |
| Hydroelectric | water from a dam flows through turbines | renewable; no fuel cost; quick to start; no pollution while running | floods land and moves people; depends on rainfall (droughts) |
| Waves and tides | moving sea water turns turbines | renewable; tides are predictable | only on coasts (not possible in Zimbabwe); costly; small scale |
| Geothermal | hot rocks underground heat water to steam | renewable; reliable | only in a few places with hot rocks near the surface |
| Nuclear (fission) | splitting uranium nuclei releases heat to make steam | very large output from little fuel; no carbon dioxide while running | non-renewable; radioactive waste; risk of accidents; very expensive |
| Wind | wind turns the blades of a turbine | renewable; no pollution while running | unreliable when the wind drops; many turbines needed |
| Solar cells and panels | cells convert light to electricity; panels heat water | renewable; ideal for remote homes and clinics; low running cost | no output at night; less on cloudy days; batteries needed for storage |

:::example{title="Comparing Hwange and Kariba"}
Coal at Hwange can be burnt day and night whatever the weather, so it is **reliable**, but it is **non-renewable** and releases carbon dioxide and other pollutants. Kariba's water is **renewable** and produces no pollution while running, but its output depends on **rainfall** over the Zambezi catchment: in drought years the lake level falls and less electricity can be generated.
:::

## The Sun: the source of most energy (Cambridge Supplement)

Radiation from the Sun is the main source of energy for almost all our resources: it grew the plants that became coal, oil and gas; it drives the winds and the water cycle that fills dams; it grows biofuel crops. The exceptions are **geothermal**, **nuclear** and **tidal** energy. The Sun releases its energy by **nuclear fusion**, joining hydrogen nuclei to form helium. Research is under way to use fusion to generate electricity on a large scale on the Earth.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Renewable means pollution-free"}
Burning biofuels still releases carbon dioxide, and dams flood land. "Renewable" only means the resource is replaced.
:::

:::mistake{title="Solar cells use a turbine"}
Solar cells produce electricity directly from light; they have no moving parts.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Compare" questions want both sides: give an advantage and a disadvantage of each resource, using the words renewability, availability, reliability, scale and environmental impact.
:::

:::exam-tip
For a power station, describe the chain: fuel → heating water → steam → turbine → generator → electricity.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Most power stations use a turbine to drive a generator; thermal stations make steam to turn the turbine.
- Renewable: hydroelectric, wind, solar, waves, tides, geothermal, biofuels. Non-renewable: coal, oil, gas, nuclear fuel.
- Fossil fuels are reliable but pollute and will run out; renewables are clean while running but some are unreliable.
- Hydroelectric output depends on rainfall; solar needs storage for the night.
- Most energy resources come from the Sun, which releases energy by nuclear fusion.
:::
