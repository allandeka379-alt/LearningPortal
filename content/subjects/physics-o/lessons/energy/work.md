## Introduction

In everyday speech "work" means any effort, but in physics **work** has an exact meaning: a force does work when it moves something in the direction of the force. Work is how energy is transferred mechanically: a herd boy pushing a cart, a crane lifting a beam, a ZUPCO bus engine pushing the bus along the road.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Work done"}
The product of a force and the distance moved in the direction of the force. Work done equals the energy transferred.
:::

:::formula{name="Work done"}
$$
W = Fd = \Delta E
$$

where $F$ is the force in newtons and $d$ the distance moved in the direction of the force in metres. The unit is the joule: $1\ \text{J} = 1\ \text{N m}$.
:::

:::keyfact
One joule of work is done when a force of one newton moves its point of application one metre in the direction of the force.
:::

## Calculating work

:::worked-solution[Pushing a wheelbarrow]
1. A gardener pushes a wheelbarrow with a steady horizontal force of $80\ \text{N}$ for $25\ \text{m}$.
2. $W = Fd = 80 \times 25 = 2000\ \text{J}$.
3. $2000\ \text{J}$ of energy is transferred from the gardener's chemical store; at steady speed most of it heats the wheel, ground and air through friction.
:::

:::worked-solution[Lifting a sack onto a truck]
1. A worker lifts a sack weighing $450\ \text{N}$ onto a truck bed $1.2\ \text{m}$ high.
2. The force needed to lift it steadily equals its weight, so $W = 450 \times 1.2 = 540\ \text{J}$.
3. This work increases the sack's gravitational potential energy by $540\ \text{J}$.
:::

## When is no work done?

Work needs **movement in the direction of the force**:

- A person holding a heavy bag **still** does no work on the bag (although their muscles get tired, energy is being wasted inside the body, not transferred to the bag).
- A force at **right angles** to the motion does no work: the upward force of a waiter's hand on a tray carried across a level room does no work on the tray.

## Work and energy

Work done is always equal to energy transferred. When you do work on an object, its energy increases, or the energy goes into heating through friction. When friction does work against a moving object, the object's kinetic energy is transferred to the internal store of the surfaces.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the wrong distance"}
Use the distance moved **in the direction of the force**. For lifting, that is the vertical height, not the length of a sloping path.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using mass instead of force"}
Work = force × distance. Convert a mass to a weight ($W = mg$) before multiplying by a height.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Define work done" scores with "force × distance moved in the direction of the force". Leaving out "in the direction of the force" may lose the mark.
:::

:::exam-tip
Link work and energy in explanations: "the work done by the brakes equals the kinetic energy lost by the car".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Work done = force × distance moved in the direction of the force: $W = Fd$.
- Work done = energy transferred, measured in joules ($1\ \text{J} = 1\ \text{N m}$).
- No work is done if there is no movement in the direction of the force.
- Lifting an object steadily does work equal to its gain in gravitational potential energy.
- Work done against friction transfers energy to internal (thermal) stores.
:::
