## Introduction

A solar panel on a rural clinic charges a battery; at night the battery lights the ward. Maize meal gives a farmer the energy to dig; burning coal at Hwange heats water to drive turbines. Energy is never made or destroyed, only moved from one **store** to another. Physics tracks where it goes.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Energy store"}
A way in which energy is held by an object or system, for example as kinetic energy of a moving bus.
:::

:::definition{term="Energy transfer"}
The movement of energy from one store to another, by a force doing work, an electric current, heating, or waves.
:::

:::definition{term="Principle of conservation of energy"}
Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred from one store to another. The total energy stays the same.
:::

## The energy stores

| Store | Example |
| --- | --- |
| kinetic | a moving taxi, a flowing river |
| gravitational potential | water held high behind Kariba Dam |
| chemical | food, fuels, batteries |
| elastic (strain) | a stretched catapult, a compressed spring |
| nuclear | the nuclei of uranium atoms |
| electrostatic | charges held apart, as in a thundercloud |
| internal (thermal) | a hot pot of sadza |

## How energy is transferred

- **Mechanically**: a force does work, e.g. a hand lifting a bucket.
- **Electrically**: an electric current does work, e.g. a battery driving a motor.
- **By heating**: from a hotter object to a colder one, e.g. a stove heating a pot.
- **By waves**: electromagnetic waves such as light and infrared, and sound waves.

:::worked-solution[Following the energy in a torch]
1. Chemical store in the battery.
2. Transferred electrically (by the current) to the bulb.
3. The bulb transfers energy by light (useful) and by heating the surroundings (wasted).
4. All the energy ends up in the internal store of the surroundings once the light is absorbed.
:::

## Conservation of energy

Because energy is conserved, the total energy **out** of any process equals the total energy **in**. The useful part plus the wasted part equals the input.

:::worked-solution[Where the energy goes]
An electric drill is supplied with $500\ \text{J}$ of energy. It transfers $150\ \text{J}$ usefully to the kinetic store of the drill bit.

1. Energy in = energy out, so wasted energy $= 500 - 150 = 350\ \text{J}$.
2. The wasted energy heats the motor and the surroundings and makes sound.
:::

### Sankey diagrams (Cambridge Supplement)

A **Sankey diagram** shows a transfer with arrows whose widths are proportional to the amounts of energy. The input arrow splits into a useful arrow and one or more wasted arrows, which bend away. The widths of the output arrows add up to the width of the input arrow.

For a multi-stage example such as a hydroelectric station, follow the energy step by step: gravitational potential energy of the water → kinetic energy of the falling water → kinetic energy of the turbine → electrical energy from the generator, with some energy wasted by heating at each stage.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Energy is used up"}
Energy is never destroyed. "Used up" really means transferred to stores, usually the internal store of the surroundings, where it is spread out and no longer useful.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing stores and transfers"}
Light, sound and electricity are ways of **transferring** energy, not stores. The energy ends up in stores such as internal or kinetic.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Describe energy changes as "energy is transferred from the chemical store of the fuel, by heating, to the internal store of the water", naming the stores and the way it moves.
:::

:::exam-tip
In Sankey-diagram questions, check that the outputs add up to the input; a missing amount is often what you are asked to find.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Energy stores: kinetic, gravitational potential, chemical, elastic, nuclear, electrostatic and internal (thermal).
- Energy is transferred by forces doing work, electric currents, heating, and waves (light, infrared, sound).
- Energy is conserved: total energy in = total energy out (useful + wasted).
- Wasted energy usually ends up heating the surroundings.
- Sankey diagrams show transfers with arrow widths proportional to energy.
:::
