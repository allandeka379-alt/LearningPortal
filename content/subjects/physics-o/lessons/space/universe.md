## Introduction

From the southern hemisphere you can see two faint smudges near the south celestial pole: the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds, small galaxies close to our own. Beyond them lie billions more galaxies, and almost all of them are moving away from us. That observation tells us the Universe is **expanding**, and it points back to a beginning: the **Big Bang**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Universe"}
Everything that exists: all matter, energy, space and time, including billions of galaxies.
:::

:::definition{term="Redshift"}
The increase in the observed wavelength of electromagnetic radiation from a star or galaxy that is moving away from the observer.
:::

:::definition{term="Big Bang theory"}
The theory that the Universe began about 14 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense point and has been expanding ever since.
:::

## The scale of the Universe

- The **Milky Way** is one of **billions of galaxies** in the Universe.
- The Milky Way is about **100 000 light-years** across.
- The distances between galaxies are usually millions of light-years.

## Redshift and the expanding Universe

When a source of waves moves away from an observer, the waves reaching the observer are stretched: their **wavelength increases** and their frequency decreases. For light, longer wavelength means a shift towards the **red** end of the spectrum: a **redshift**.

- Light from **distant galaxies** is **redshifted** compared with light from the same elements measured on the Earth: the dark lines in their spectra appear at longer wavelengths.
- The **more distant** a galaxy, the **greater** its redshift, so the faster it is moving away.
- This shows that the Universe is **expanding**, and it supports the **Big Bang theory**: if everything is moving apart now, it must all once have been together.

## The cosmic microwave background radiation (Cambridge Supplement)

**Microwave radiation** of a particular frequency (the cosmic microwave background radiation, CMBR) is detected coming from **every direction** in space, almost equally.

It was produced as very high-energy radiation shortly after the Universe formed. As the Universe has expanded, the radiation has been **stretched** (redshifted) so much that it is now in the **microwave** region. The CMBR is strong evidence for the Big Bang.

## Measuring speeds and distances of galaxies (Cambridge Supplement)

- The **speed** $v$ at which a galaxy is moving away is found from the **redshift** of its light.
- The **distance** $d$ to a far galaxy can be found from the observed brightness of a **supernova** in it, since supernovae of a particular type all have the same peak brightness.

Plotting speed against distance for many galaxies gives a straight line: speed is proportional to distance.

:::formula{name="Hubble constant (Cambridge Supplement)"}
$$
H_0 = \frac{v}{d}
$$
The current estimate is $H_0 = 2.2 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{per second}$.
:::

:::worked-solution[The speed of a distant galaxy]
A galaxy is $5.0 \times 10^{24}\ \text{m}$ away.

1. $v = H_0 d = 2.2 \times 10^{-18} \times 5.0 \times 10^{24}$.
2. $v = 1.1 \times 10^{7}\ \text{m/s}$, about 4% of the speed of light.
:::

## The age of the Universe (Cambridge Supplement)

If a galaxy has travelled at speed $v$ ever since the Big Bang, the time it has taken to reach distance $d$ is $\dfrac{d}{v}$, and this is the same for every galaxy: $\dfrac{d}{v} = \dfrac{1}{H_0}$. This estimates the **age of the Universe** and supports the idea that all the matter began at a single point.

:::worked-solution[Estimating the age of the Universe]
1. $\dfrac{1}{H_0} = \dfrac{1}{2.2 \times 10^{-18}} = 4.5 \times 10^{17}\ \text{s}$.
2. In years: $\dfrac{4.5 \times 10^{17}}{3.15 \times 10^{7}} = 1.4 \times 10^{10}$ years, about 14 billion years.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Redshift means the light turns red"}
Redshift means the wavelengths are **longer** than expected. Light from a galaxy that is already blue can be redshifted and still look blue.
:::

:::mistake{title="Galaxies move away from the Earth because the Earth is special"}
Every galaxy sees the others moving away, because space itself is expanding. The Earth is not at the centre.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Link the evidence to the conclusion: redshift increasing with distance → the Universe is expanding → it began in a Big Bang; the CMBR supports this.
:::

:::exam-tip
In Hubble constant calculations, keep $d$ in metres and $v$ in m/s, so that $H_0$ is in per second.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Milky Way is one of billions of galaxies and is about 100 000 light-years across.
- Redshift is an increase in observed wavelength from receding galaxies; more distant galaxies show more redshift.
- Redshift shows the Universe is expanding and supports the Big Bang theory.
- The CMBR, seen in every direction, is radiation from the early Universe stretched into microwaves (Cambridge Supplement).
- $H_0 = \dfrac{v}{d} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{s}^{-1}$; the age of the Universe is about $\dfrac{1}{H_0}$, roughly 14 billion years (Cambridge Supplement).
:::
