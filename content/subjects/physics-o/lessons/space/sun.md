## Introduction

The Sun warms Zimbabwe's farmland, drives its weather and powers the solar panels on thousands of rooftops. Yet among the stars it is quite ordinary: a **medium-sized star**, one of hundreds of billions in our galaxy. It is simply the star that happens to be close to us.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Star"}
A huge, hot ball of gas, mostly hydrogen and helium, that gives out energy produced by nuclear fusion in its core.
:::

:::definition{term="Nuclear fusion"}
The joining of light nuclei to form heavier ones, releasing energy.
:::

## What the Sun is like

- It is a **medium-sized star**: some stars are hundreds of times wider, and many are much smaller. Its diameter is about 1.4 million kilometres, about 109 times that of the Earth.
- It consists mostly of **hydrogen** (about three-quarters of its mass) and **helium** (about one-quarter), with small amounts of heavier elements.
- Its surface temperature is about 5500 °C; its core is far hotter, about 15 million °C.

## The radiation from the Sun

The Sun radiates energy as electromagnetic waves, mainly in three regions of the spectrum:

- **infrared** radiation, which we feel as heat;
- **visible light**, by which we see;
- **ultraviolet** radiation, which causes sunburn and can damage the eyes.

Smaller amounts are given out at other wavelengths.

## How the Sun is powered (Cambridge Supplement)

In the Sun's core, the temperature and pressure are so high that **hydrogen nuclei fuse to form helium nuclei**. The products have slightly less mass than the hydrogen nuclei that formed them, and this lost mass is released as energy, which slowly works its way out to the surface and is radiated into space.

:::example{title="A stable star in balance"}
In a stable star like the Sun, two effects balance. **Gravity** pulls all the gas inwards, trying to make the star collapse. The energy released by **fusion** heats the gas, creating an outward **pressure**. As long as fusion continues steadily, the two balance and the star keeps the same size. The Sun has been stable for about 4.6 billion years and has enough hydrogen for billions of years more.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="The Sun burns like a fire"}
The Sun does not burn by chemical combustion. Its energy comes from nuclear fusion.
:::

:::mistake{title="The Sun is an unusually large star"}
The Sun is a medium-sized star; it looks far bigger and brighter than other stars only because it is so much closer.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Sun is a medium-sized star made mostly of hydrogen and helium.
- It radiates mainly infrared, visible light and ultraviolet.
- Stable stars, including the Sun, are powered by the nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium (Cambridge Supplement).
- Inward gravity is balanced by outward pressure from the energy released by fusion.
:::
