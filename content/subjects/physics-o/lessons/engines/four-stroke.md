## Introduction

Almost every car, kombi, motorbike, tractor and borehole generator in Zimbabwe is driven by an **internal combustion engine**: fuel is burnt **inside** a cylinder, and the hot gases push a piston. Most of these engines work on a **four-stroke cycle**. This lesson covers ZIMSEC topic 8.1.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Stroke"}
One movement of the piston from one end of the cylinder to the other (up or down).
:::

:::definition{term="Carburettor"}
The device in older petrol engines that mixes petrol with air in the right proportions before it enters the cylinder.
:::

:::definition{term="Crankshaft"}
The shaft that turns the up-and-down motion of the pistons into rotation.
:::

## The parts

A cylinder contains a **piston** connected by a connecting rod to the **crankshaft**. At the top of the cylinder are an **inlet valve**, an **exhaust valve** and, in a petrol engine, a **spark plug**. A heavy **flywheel** on the crankshaft keeps the engine turning smoothly between power strokes.

## The four strokes

| Stroke | Piston | Valves | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Induction (intake) | moves down | inlet open | the fuel-air mixture is drawn into the cylinder |
| 2. Compression | moves up | both closed | the mixture is squashed into a small space and heats up |
| 3. Power | pushed down | both closed | the spark plug ignites the mixture; the burning gas expands and drives the piston down |
| 4. Exhaust | moves up | exhaust open | the burnt gases are pushed out through the exhaust |

Only the **power stroke** does useful work; the other three are driven by the momentum of the flywheel (or by other cylinders). One complete cycle takes **two turns** of the crankshaft.

:::example{title="Energy changes in the engine"}
Chemical energy in the fuel → internal (thermal) energy of the hot gases → kinetic energy of the piston and crankshaft. Much of the energy is wasted, carried away by the hot exhaust and the cooling system: a typical car engine is only about a quarter to a third efficient.
:::

## The carburettor and the choke

The carburettor mixes petrol and air in the correct ratio for complete burning. When the engine is cold, the **choke** reduces the air supply to give a richer (more petrol) mixture that is easier to ignite. Problems with fuel and air supply make an engine run badly: a blocked air filter or an over-used choke limits the air, giving **incomplete combustion**, black **soot** and poisonous **carbon monoxide**; worn jets supply the wrong amount of fuel.

## Several cylinders

Most car engines have four or more cylinders whose power strokes happen at different times. This gives:

- **even firing**: a power stroke more often, so the engine runs more smoothly;
- more power from the engine as a whole;
- a more even flow of power to the wheels.

## Petrol and diesel engines

| | Petrol engine | Diesel engine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawn in on induction | fuel-air mixture | air only |
| Compression | lower | much higher, so the air gets very hot |
| Ignition | spark from a spark plug | diesel injected into the hot air ignites by itself (compression ignition) |
| Efficiency and fuel economy | lower | higher (more kilometres per litre) |
| Engine build | lighter | heavier and stronger, to withstand high pressures |

Diesel engines are used in buses, haulage trucks, tractors and generators because they are economical and durable.

The efficiency of a vehicle's engine is often judged by its **fuel economy**: the number of kilometres travelled per litre of fuel.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Every stroke pushes the car"}
Only the power stroke does work on the piston; the other strokes take energy from the flywheel.
:::

:::mistake{title="Diesel engines have spark plugs"}
They do not: the fuel ignites because the air is compressed so much that it becomes hot enough.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For each stroke, state the direction of the piston, which valves are open or closed, and what happens to the mixture. Draw or describe them in order: induction, compression, power, exhaust.
:::

:::exam-tip
When comparing petrol and diesel engines, mention ignition (spark versus compression), what is drawn in, compression ratio and efficiency.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The four strokes are induction, compression, power and exhaust; only the power stroke does work.
- A spark plug ignites the compressed mixture in a petrol engine.
- The carburettor mixes fuel and air; the choke enriches the mixture for cold starts.
- Several cylinders give smoother running and more power.
- Diesel engines compress air only, ignite by compression and are more economical.
:::
