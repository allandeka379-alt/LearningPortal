## Introduction

At a scrap yard, a crane swings a large disc over a pile of old car parts, lifts a load of iron and drops it neatly into a truck. Inside a phone, a tiny magnet in the speaker turns electrical signals into sound. One job needs a magnet that can be switched on and off; the other needs a magnet that never loses its strength.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Permanent magnet"}
A magnet, usually made of steel or a special alloy, that keeps its magnetism.
:::

:::definition{term="Electromagnet"}
A coil of wire, usually wound on a soft-iron core, that is magnetised only while a current flows through it: a temporary magnet that can be switched on and off.
:::

## Choosing the type of magnet

- A **permanent magnet** needs no power supply and its strength does not change, so it suits jobs where a steady magnetic field is needed all the time.
- An **electromagnet** can be **switched on and off**, and its strength can be changed by changing the current. Its **soft-iron core** makes it much stronger and loses its magnetism as soon as the current is switched off, which is exactly what is needed.

## Uses of permanent magnets

| Use | How the magnet is used |
| --- | --- |
| compass | a magnetised steel needle lines up with the Earth's field |
| loudspeakers and earphones | a magnet's field pushes on a current-carrying coil, making the cone vibrate |
| microphones and bicycle dynamos | a magnet and a coil moving relative to each other generate an electrical signal or current |
| electric motors and generators | magnets provide the field in which the coil turns |
| fridge door seals and cupboard catches | a magnetic strip holds the door closed |
| magnetic stripes on cards and hard disk drives | patterns of magnetised regions store information |

## Uses of electromagnets (temporary magnets)

| Use | Why an electromagnet is needed |
| --- | --- |
| crane at a scrap yard | lifts iron and steel when switched on and releases it when switched off |
| separating metals for recycling | pulls steel cans out of a stream of aluminium cans and other waste |
| electric bell | switches itself on and off repeatedly to make the hammer strike the gong |
| relay | a small current in the coil switches on a separate circuit carrying a large current |
| circuit breaker (trip switch) | a large fault current makes the electromagnet strong enough to open the switch |
| magnetic door locks | hold a door shut while the current flows and release it at the press of a button |

:::example{title="How an electric bell works"}
1. Pressing the bell push completes the circuit, and current flows through the electromagnet's coils.
2. The electromagnet attracts the soft-iron **armature**, and the hammer on the armature strikes the gong.
3. As the armature moves, it pulls away from the **contact screw**, breaking the circuit.
4. The electromagnet loses its magnetism; a spring pulls the armature back, remaking the contact.
5. The cycle repeats many times a second, so the bell rings for as long as the push is pressed.

Soft iron is used for the core and the armature because it magnetises and demagnetises instantly; steel would stay magnetised and the armature would stick.
:::

:::example{title="Why a car uses a relay"}
A car's starter motor needs a current of well over a hundred amperes. Instead of sending this through the key switch on the dashboard, the key switch sends a small current through a relay coil. The relay's electromagnet closes heavy contacts in a separate, short, thick-cabled circuit that carries the large current to the starter motor.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Steel cores in electromagnets"}
An electromagnet needs a **soft-iron** core. A steel core would stay magnetised after the current is switched off, so the crane could not drop its load.
:::

:::mistake{title="Permanent magnets can be switched off"}
Only electromagnets can be switched on and off.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When explaining a use, say which property matters: "an electromagnet is used because it can be switched off to release the load".
:::

:::exam-tip
Describe the electric bell as a sequence of steps, each one causing the next.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Permanent magnets keep their magnetism: compasses, loudspeakers, microphones, motors, generators, door catches.
- Electromagnets are temporary magnets with soft-iron cores that can be switched on and off and varied in strength.
- Uses of electromagnets: scrap-yard cranes, metal sorting, electric bells, relays, circuit breakers, door locks.
- Soft iron is chosen for cores and armatures because it gains and loses magnetism easily.
:::
