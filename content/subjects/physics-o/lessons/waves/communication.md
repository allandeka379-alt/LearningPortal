## Introduction

A family in Harare watches a football match broadcast live from Europe, a farmer in Mutoko checks maize prices on a mobile phone, and a bank sends payment data along fibre-optic cables under the ocean. Modern communication depends on choosing the right electromagnetic wave for each job, and on sending information as **digital** signals. This lesson is Cambridge content (3.3).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Geostationary satellite"}
A satellite that orbits above the equator once every 24 hours, so it stays above the same point on the Earth.
:::

:::definition{term="Analogue signal"}
A signal that varies continuously and can take any value.
:::

:::definition{term="Digital signal"}
A signal that has only a few values, usually two (on and off, 1 and 0).
:::

## Satellites

Communication with artificial satellites is mainly by **microwaves**, which pass straight through the atmosphere.

- **Geostationary satellites** stay above one point, so a dish aerial on a house can point at one fixed position: they carry **direct broadcast satellite television** and some satellite phones.
- **Low-orbit satellites** are much closer to the Earth and move across the sky; networks of them carry some satellite phone and internet services, with shorter signal delays.

## Choosing the wave for the job (Cambridge Supplement)

| Use | Wave | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mobile phones and wireless internet (Wi-Fi) | microwaves | penetrate some walls; need only a short aerial |
| Bluetooth | radio waves (low power) | pass through walls; short range is enough |
| optical fibres (internet, cable TV) | visible light or infrared | travel long distances along glass with little loss; carry huge amounts of data |
| satellite TV and phones | microwaves | pass through the atmosphere |

## Analogue and digital signals

An **analogue** signal, such as the voltage from a microphone, rises and falls smoothly, matching the sound. A **digital** signal is a series of pulses with just two values, 1 and 0. A sound can be sent either way: in digital form it is sampled many times a second and each sample is turned into a binary number.

### The benefits of digital signals

All signals pick up **noise** (random unwanted changes) and get **weaker** as they travel, so they are boosted by **regenerators** (repeaters) along the way.

- When an analogue signal is amplified, the noise is amplified too, and the signal gets worse at every stage.
- A digital signal only needs to be recognised as a 1 or a 0, so a regenerator can rebuild a **perfect, clean** copy of it, removing the noise. Digital signals can therefore travel much **further** with accurate regeneration.
- Digital signals can carry data at a much **higher rate**, and many signals can share one channel.

:::example{title="Why your phone call is clear"}
A voice call from Bulawayo to Mutare passes through many regenerators. Each one reads the digital pulses and sends out fresh, clean 1s and 0s, so the voice arrives as clear as it left, without the hiss that builds up on an analogue line.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Satellites use radio for TV"}
Satellite TV and phones use microwaves, which pass through the atmosphere. Long-wave radio cannot reach satellites in the same way.
:::

:::mistake{title="Digital means no noise"}
Digital signals do pick up noise; the advantage is that it can be removed completely when the signal is regenerated.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When explaining a choice of wave, give a property of the wave (passes through walls, travels through the atmosphere, carries lots of data) as the reason.
:::

:::exam-tip
"Benefits of digital signalling": higher data rate and longer range, because the signal can be regenerated accurately without the noise.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Satellites communicate mainly by microwaves; geostationary satellites stay above one point.
- Mobile phones and Wi-Fi use microwaves; Bluetooth uses low-power radio waves; optical fibres use light or infrared.
- Analogue signals vary continuously; digital signals have two values, 1 and 0.
- Sound can be sent as an analogue or a digital signal.
- Digital signals can be regenerated without noise, giving longer range and higher data rates.
:::
