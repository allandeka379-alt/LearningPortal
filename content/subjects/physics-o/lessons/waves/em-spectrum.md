## Introduction

Your phone, the radio in a commuter omnibus, the microwave oven, a TV remote, sunlight, a hospital X-ray machine: all use **electromagnetic waves**. These waves are all the same kind of wave, travelling at the same speed in a vacuum, but with very different wavelengths and frequencies, and so very different uses and dangers.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Electromagnetic (EM) waves"}
Transverse waves of electric and magnetic fields that can travel through a vacuum, all at the speed of light.
:::

:::definition{term="Electromagnetic spectrum"}
The family of EM waves arranged in order of wavelength or frequency.
:::

## The spectrum in order

In order of **increasing frequency** (and **decreasing wavelength**):

![The electromagnetic spectrum in order of increasing frequency: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays; wavelength decreases the same way, and all travel at 3.0 times 10 to the 8 metres per second in a vacuum.](media:diagrams/em-spectrum-order.png "Figure: the electromagnetic spectrum, from radio waves to gamma rays")

**radio waves → microwaves → infrared → visible light → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays**

A memory aid for the order: **R**ich **M**en **I**n **V**egas **U**se **X**-ray **G**lasses. Radio waves have the longest wavelength (metres to kilometres); gamma rays the shortest (smaller than an atom). (Neither syllabus asks you to recall actual wavelengths or frequencies; you must know the order.)

## Properties shared by all EM waves

- They are **transverse** waves.
- They can travel through a **vacuum**.
- They all travel at the same speed in a vacuum, $3.0 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}$, and at almost the same speed in air (the exact value is Cambridge Supplement).
- They obey $v = f\lambda$, so the higher the frequency, the shorter the wavelength.
- They can be reflected, refracted and diffracted, and they transfer energy.

:::worked-solution[The wavelength of an FM radio station]
An FM station broadcasts at $100\ \text{MHz} = 1.0 \times 10^{8}\ \text{Hz}$.

1. $\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f} = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.0 \times 10^{8}} = 3.0\ \text{m}$.
:::

## Uses and dangers

| Region | Typical uses | Danger of excessive exposure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| radio waves | radio and TV broadcasting, astronomy, RFID tags | little known harm |
| microwaves | satellite TV, mobile phones, microwave ovens | internal heating of body cells |
| infrared | remote controls, heaters, thermal imaging, intruder alarms, optical fibres | skin burns |
| visible light | seeing, photography, illumination, optical fibres | very bright light can damage the eyes |
| ultraviolet | security marking, sterilising water, sun tanning | damage to skin cells and eyes; skin cancer, blindness |
| X-rays | medical imaging, airport security scanners | mutations, cell damage, cancer |
| gamma rays | killing bacteria (sterilising equipment and food), treating cancer | mutations, cell damage, cancer |

The higher the frequency, the more energy the waves carry and generally the more harmful they are. X-rays and gamma rays are **ionising**: they can knock electrons out of atoms and damage the DNA in cells. Ultraviolet also damages skin cells and eyes. Exposure must therefore be limited: radiographers stand behind lead screens when taking X-rays, and people use sunscreen and sunglasses in strong sunlight.

## Why each use works

Each use depends on how that radiation interacts with matter:

- **Microwaves** pass through the atmosphere and clouds, so they carry signals to and from satellites; in an oven they are absorbed by water molecules in food, which heats it.
- **Infrared** is emitted by all warm objects, so thermal-imaging cameras can "see" people and animals at night; a TV remote sends coded pulses of infrared to the receiver.
- **Ultraviolet** makes certain chemicals glow (fluoresce), so security marks on banknotes show up under a UV lamp; it also kills bacteria in drinking water.
- **X-rays** pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by bone and metal, so they produce shadow images of bones and of objects in luggage.
- **Gamma rays** penetrate deeply and kill living cells, so they sterilise medical equipment in sealed packs and destroy cancer cells in radiotherapy.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Different speeds"}
All EM waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum. They differ in frequency and wavelength.
:::

:::mistake{title="Sound is part of the spectrum"}
Sound is a longitudinal mechanical wave that needs a medium; it is not electromagnetic.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Learn the order in both directions: increasing frequency (radio to gamma) means decreasing wavelength.
:::

:::exam-tip
For each region, learn one use and one danger; "describe a use" answers should say what the radiation does, not just name a device.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Order of increasing frequency: radio, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays.
- All EM waves are transverse, travel through a vacuum at $3.0 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}$ and obey $v = f\lambda$.
- Higher frequency means shorter wavelength and more energy.
- Each region has typical uses (communication, heating, imaging, sterilising) and dangers.
- UV, X-rays and gamma rays can damage cells and cause cancer.
:::
