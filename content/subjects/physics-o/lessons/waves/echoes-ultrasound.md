## Introduction

Shout towards a rock face in the Matobo Hills and a moment later you hear your voice come back: an **echo**. Bats, dolphins and fishing boats all use echoes to locate objects, and doctors use very high-frequency sound to see inside the body without surgery.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Echo"}
A sound heard after it has been reflected from a surface.
:::

:::definition{term="Ultrasound"}
Sound with a frequency higher than $20\ \text{kHz}$, above the range of human hearing.
:::

## Using echoes to measure distance

In an echo, the sound travels **to** the reflecting surface **and back**, so it covers **twice** the distance to the surface:

$$
\text{distance to surface} = \frac{\text{speed} \times \text{time for echo}}{2}
$$

:::worked-solution[An echo from a cliff]
A learner claps near a cliff and hears the echo $1.2\ \text{s}$ later. The speed of sound in air is $340\ \text{m/s}$.

1. Total distance travelled by the sound $= 340 \times 1.2 = 408\ \text{m}$.
2. Distance to the cliff $= \dfrac{408}{2} = 204\ \text{m}$.
:::

Echoes can also be used to measure the speed of sound: a learner claps in time with the echoes from a wall a measured distance away, and times many claps.

## Uses of ultrasound (Cambridge Supplement)

### Sonar (echo sounding)

A ship sends pulses of ultrasound down through the water and detects the echoes from the seabed or a shoal of fish. Knowing the speed of sound in water (about $1500\ \text{m/s}$), the depth is calculated from the echo time. Fishing boats use echo sounders to find shoals of fish, and survey boats use them to map the bed of a dam or lake.

:::worked-solution[Depth by sonar]
1. An echo returns $0.80\ \text{s}$ after a pulse is sent; sound travels at $1500\ \text{m/s}$ in the water.
2. Depth $= \dfrac{1500 \times 0.80}{2} = 600\ \text{m}$.
:::

### Medical scanning

Ultrasound pulses sent into the body reflect partly from each boundary between different soft tissues. A computer builds an image from the echo times. Ultrasound is used to check babies before birth and to examine organs, because it is not ionising and is safe for soft tissue (unlike X-rays).

### Non-destructive testing

Ultrasound sent into a metal part (a railway line, an aircraft wing, a weld) reflects from the far side. A crack inside produces an extra echo that arrives early, revealing the hidden flaw without damaging the part.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Forgetting to halve"}
The echo time is for the journey there and back. Divide the total distance by 2 to get the distance to the surface.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ultrasound is not sound"}
Ultrasound is sound: a longitudinal wave in a medium. It is simply too high in frequency for humans to hear.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write "distance = speed × time ÷ 2" and say why you divide by 2 (the sound travels there and back). This explanation often earns a mark on its own.
:::

:::exam-tip
For uses of ultrasound, name the use, what is reflected (seabed, tissue boundary, crack) and what is measured (the time for the echo).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- An echo is reflected sound.
- Distance to a reflecting surface = speed × echo time ÷ 2.
- Ultrasound has a frequency above 20 kHz.
- Ultrasound is used in sonar (depth and fish finding), medical scanning of soft tissue, and finding cracks in metals.
- Medical ultrasound is safe because it is not ionising.
:::
