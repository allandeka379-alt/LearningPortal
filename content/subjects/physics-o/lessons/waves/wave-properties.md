## Introduction

Drop a stone into a still dam and ripples spread out across the water. A leaf floating on the surface bobs up and down as they pass, but it is not carried along with them. A **wave** carries **energy** from place to place, but not the water itself. Sound, light, radio signals and earthquake tremors all travel as waves.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Wave"}
A disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without transferring matter.
:::

:::definition{term="Wavelength, λ"}
The distance between two neighbouring crests (or two neighbouring troughs), or between corresponding points on adjacent cycles. Unit: metre.
:::

:::definition{term="Frequency, f"}
The number of complete waves passing a point (or produced by the source) each second. Unit: hertz, $1\ \text{Hz} = 1$ wave per second.
:::

:::definition{term="Amplitude"}
The maximum displacement of a point on the wave from its rest (undisturbed) position.
:::

:::definition{term="Period, T"}
The time for one complete wave to pass a point: $T = \dfrac{1}{f}$ (ZIMSEC).
:::

:::definition{term="Wavefront"}
A line joining points on a wave that are at the same stage of their vibration, such as all the points along one crest.
:::

## The wave equation

:::formula{name="Wave speed"}
$$
v = f\lambda
$$

where $v$ is the wave speed (m/s), $f$ the frequency (Hz) and $\lambda$ the wavelength (m).
:::

:::worked-solution[Ripples on a dam]
A ripple tank vibrator makes water waves with a frequency of $2.5\ \text{Hz}$ and a wavelength of $0.40\ \text{m}$.

1. $v = f\lambda = 2.5 \times 0.40 = 1.0\ \text{m/s}$.
2. The period is $T = \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{2.5} = 0.40\ \text{s}$.
:::

## Transverse and longitudinal waves

### Transverse waves

The vibrations are at **right angles** to the direction the wave travels. Waves on a rope shaken from side to side, water waves, all **electromagnetic waves** (including light) and seismic **S-waves** (secondary waves) are transverse. They have **crests** and **troughs**.

![Transverse wave drawn as displacement against distance: crests and troughs repeat every 0.40 m, so the wavelength is 0.40 m; the amplitude is the height of a crest above the rest line. With a frequency of 2.5 Hz the wave speed is 2.5 times 0.40, 1.0 m/s.](media:diagrams/wave-transverse-wavelength.png "Figure: wavelength is crest to crest; amplitude is rest position to crest")

### Longitudinal waves

The vibrations are **parallel** to the direction the wave travels. Push and pull one end of a stretched slinky spring: regions where the coils are squashed together (**compressions**) and spread apart (**rarefactions**) travel along it. **Sound** and seismic **P-waves** (primary waves) are longitudinal.

![Longitudinal wave on a spring: the coils are drawn as vertical lines that bunch up at compressions (C) and spread apart at rarefactions (R). The vibration is along the direction the wave travels, and the wavelength is the distance between two neighbouring compressions.](media:diagrams/wave-longitudinal-compressions.png "Figure: a longitudinal wave has compressions and rarefactions")

:::example{title="A slinky shows both"}
Moving one end of a stretched slinky from side to side sends a transverse wave along it. Pushing and pulling the end along its length sends a longitudinal wave. In both cases the coils only vibrate about their rest positions; it is the energy that travels along the spring.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Amplitude is crest to trough"}
Amplitude is measured from the rest position to a crest, which is half the crest-to-trough height.
:::

:::mistake{title="Waves carry the medium"}
A floating leaf bobs up and down as ripples pass; it does not travel with the wave. Waves transfer energy, not matter.
:::

:::mistake{title="Frequency and speed"}
Increasing the frequency of a source does not change the wave speed in the same medium; the wavelength gets shorter instead.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Label diagrams carefully: wavelength from crest to the next crest, amplitude from the rest line to a crest.
:::

:::exam-tip
For "describe the difference" questions, compare the direction of vibration with the direction of energy transfer, and give one example of each type of wave.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Waves transfer energy without transferring matter.
- Wavelength, frequency, amplitude and period describe a wave; $T = 1/f$.
- Wave speed: $v = f\lambda$.
- Transverse waves vibrate at right angles to the direction of travel (light, water waves, S-waves).
- Longitudinal waves vibrate parallel to it, with compressions and rarefactions (sound, P-waves).
:::
