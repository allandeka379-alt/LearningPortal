## Introduction

Place a wire carrying a current between the poles of a magnet and it jumps sideways. This **motor effect** turns electrical energy into motion in every electric motor, from a cellphone's vibration motor to the motors that drive borehole pumps and electric cars.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Motor effect"}
The force on a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field (not parallel to the field).
:::

:::definition{term="Fleming's left-hand rule"}
A rule giving the direction of the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.
:::

## Demonstrating the force

Lay two horizontal copper rails across the gap between the poles of a strong U-shaped magnet and rest a light copper rod across the rails. When a current flows through the rails and the rod, the rod rolls along the rails.

- **Reversing the current** makes the rod roll the other way.
- **Reversing the magnetic field** (turning the magnet over) also reverses the force.
- Reversing **both** gives a force in the original direction.
- A **larger current** or a **stronger magnet** gives a larger force.
- If the current is **parallel** to the field lines, there is **no force**.

The force arises because the circular field around the current interacts with the magnet's field: on one side of the wire the two fields add, on the other they partly cancel, and the wire is pushed from the stronger field towards the weaker one.

## Fleming's left-hand rule

Hold the thumb and first two fingers of your **left** hand at right angles to each other:

- **F**irst finger: **F**ield (from N to S);
- se**C**ond finger: **C**urrent (conventional, + to −);
- th**u**mb: thr**u**st, the force or **motion**.

The force is always at right angles to both the field and the current.

:::worked-solution[Which way does the wire move?]
A horizontal wire lies between a north pole on the left and a south pole on the right. The current in the wire flows into the page (away from you).

1. Field: from N to S, so pointing to the right. Point your first finger to the right.
2. Current: into the page. Point your second finger into the page.
3. Your thumb now points **downwards**: the wire is pushed down.
:::

## Beams of charged particles (Cambridge Supplement)

A moving charged particle is a current, so a beam of particles is also pushed sideways by a magnetic field.

- For **positive** particles, the current is in the direction they move.
- For **electrons** (negative), the conventional current is **opposite** to their direction of motion. Point your second finger against the electrons' motion.

:::worked-solution[Deflecting an electron beam]
A beam of electrons travels from left to right across a magnetic field directed into the page.

1. Conventional current: opposite to the electrons, so from right to left.
2. First finger into the page (field), second finger to the left (current).
3. The thumb points **downwards**: the beam is deflected down.
:::

Because the force is always at right angles to the motion, the particles move along a **curved** (circular) path while in the field.

## Forces between parallel currents (ZIMSEC)

Two long parallel wires carrying currents exert forces on each other, because each wire sits in the magnetic field of the other:

- currents in the **same direction** **attract**: between the wires their fields cancel, and the stronger field outside pushes them together;
- currents in **opposite directions** **repel**: between the wires their fields add, and the strong field between them pushes them apart.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the right hand"}
For the force on a current (motors), use the **left** hand. The right hand is for the grip rule and for generators.
:::

:::mistake{title="Electron beams"}
Point the "current" finger opposite to the direction the electrons move.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
State the result of reversing one quantity (the force reverses) and of reversing both (no change). These are common one-mark questions.
:::

:::exam-tip
Write F, B (field) and I on a sketch and apply the rule slowly; most errors come from pointing the field the wrong way (it goes N to S).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field feels a force at right angles to both the field and the current.
- Reversing the current or the field reverses the force; reversing both leaves it unchanged.
- Fleming's left-hand rule: First finger Field, seCond finger Current, thuMb Motion.
- A beam of charged particles is deflected; for electrons the current is opposite to their motion (Cambridge Supplement).
- Parallel currents in the same direction attract; in opposite directions they repel (ZIMSEC).
:::
