## Introduction

Why does a loaded haulage truck take so long to speed up, while a light motorbike shoots away from the traffic lights? Why do passengers lurch forward when a kombi brakes suddenly? Isaac Newton explained all of this with three laws that connect **forces** to **motion**.

You should already know what acceleration is and how to add forces along a line.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Resultant force"}
The single force that has the same effect as all the forces acting on an object together.
:::

:::definition{term="Inertia"}
The tendency of an object to resist changes to its motion. The greater the mass, the greater the inertia.
:::

:::formula{name="Newton's second law"}
$$
F = ma
$$

where $F$ is the resultant force (N), $m$ the mass (kg) and $a$ the acceleration (m/s²). The acceleration is in the same direction as the resultant force.
:::

## Finding the resultant force

Forces acting along one line add if they point the same way and subtract if they are opposite. If the forces are **balanced**, the resultant is zero.

## Newton's first law

An object **stays at rest**, or **keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed**, unless a resultant force acts on it.

So a resultant force is needed to **change** an object's velocity: to speed it up, slow it down or change its direction. A car cruising at a steady $100\ \text{km/h}$ has **no** resultant force on it: the driving force exactly balances air resistance and friction. A resultant force is not needed to keep something moving.

Passengers lurch forward when a kombi brakes because of their **inertia**: their bodies keep moving forward until the seat belt (a force) stops them.

## Newton's second law

A resultant force makes an object accelerate. The acceleration is larger for a larger force and smaller for a larger mass: $a = F/m$.

:::worked-solution[Accelerating a car]
A car of mass $1200\ \text{kg}$ has a resultant forward force of $3000\ \text{N}$ on it.

1. $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{3000}{1200} = 2.5\ \text{m/s}^2$ forwards.
:::

:::worked-solution[Forces that do not balance]
A boat of mass $900\ \text{kg}$ has an engine thrust of $2400\ \text{N}$ forwards and water resistance of $600\ \text{N}$ backwards.

1. Resultant force $= 2400 - 600 = 1800\ \text{N}$ forwards.
2. $a = \dfrac{1800}{900} = 2.0\ \text{m/s}^2$.
3. The acceleration must use the **resultant** force, not the engine force alone.
:::

## Newton's third law

When object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts an **equal and opposite** force on object A. The two forces are of the same type and act on **different** objects.

:::example{title="Third-law pairs"}
A footballer kicks a ball: her foot pushes the ball forward and the ball pushes her foot backward with an equal force. A swimmer pushes water backwards; the water pushes the swimmer forwards. The Earth pulls you down (your weight); you pull the Earth up with the same size of force.
:::

Third-law pairs never cancel each other out, because they act on different objects.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Moving objects need a resultant force"}
Constant velocity needs **zero** resultant force. A resultant force is needed only to change the velocity.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using one force in F = ma"}
Always find the resultant force first, then use $F = ma$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Weight and the normal force are a third-law pair"}
A book on a table has its weight and the table's upward push acting on the **same** object, so they are balanced forces, not a third-law pair.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "explain the motion" questions, compare the forces: "the forward force is greater than the backward force, so there is a resultant force forwards and the car accelerates".
:::

:::exam-tip
Draw a free-body diagram: one dot for the object and an arrow for each force, labelled with its size.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The resultant force is the single force with the same effect as all the forces together.
- First law: with no resultant force, an object stays at rest or keeps a constant velocity.
- Second law: $F = ma$, with the acceleration in the direction of the resultant force.
- Third law: forces come in equal and opposite pairs acting on different objects.
- Inertia is the resistance of a mass to changes in its motion.
:::
