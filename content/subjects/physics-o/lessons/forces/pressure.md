## Introduction

An elephant in Hwange National Park is enormously heavy, yet it walks over soft ground without sinking far, while a person in thin high heels can dent a wooden floor. A sharp knife cuts tomatoes easily; a blunt one squashes them. The wall of Kariba Dam is far thicker at the bottom than at the top. The idea that links these is **pressure**: how concentrated a force is.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Pressure"}
The force acting per unit area, at right angles to a surface.
:::

:::formula{name="Pressure"}
$$
p = \frac{F}{A}
$$

Unit: the pascal, $1\ \text{Pa} = 1\ \text{N/m}^2$.
:::

## Pressure under solids

For the same force, a **small area** gives a **large pressure** and a large area gives a small pressure.

| Small area: high pressure | Large area: low pressure |
| --- | --- |
| sharp knife blade, needle, nail point | wide tractor tyres on a muddy field |
| drawing pin point | elephant's broad feet |
| high-heeled shoes | foundations of a building |

:::worked-solution[A crate standing on two different faces]
A crate weighs $600\ \text{N}$. Its largest face has an area of $0.20\ \text{m}^2$ and its smallest face $0.050\ \text{m}^2$.

1. Standing on the largest face: $p = \dfrac{600}{0.20} = 3000\ \text{Pa}$.
2. Standing on the smallest face: $p = \dfrac{600}{0.050} = 12\,000\ \text{Pa}$.
3. Same force, one quarter of the area, four times the pressure.
:::

## Pressure in liquids

A liquid presses on every surface it touches, and the pressure at a point acts **equally in all directions**. Beneath the surface:

- pressure **increases with depth**, because there is more liquid above pushing down;
- pressure is **greater in a denser liquid** at the same depth.

That is why dam walls are thicker at the base, and why water spurts furthest from the lowest hole in a tall can full of water.

:::formula{name="Pressure change with depth (Cambridge Supplement; ZIMSEC)"}
$$
\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h
$$

where $\rho$ is the liquid's density (kg/m³), $g$ the gravitational field strength (N/kg) and $\Delta h$ the change in depth (m).
:::

:::worked-solution[Pressure at the bottom of a reservoir]
What is the pressure due to the water $8.0\ \text{m}$ below the surface of a reservoir? Take $\rho = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$ and $g = 9.8\ \text{N/kg}$.

1. $\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h = 1000 \times 9.8 \times 8.0 = 78\,400\ \text{Pa}$.
2. The total pressure there also includes the atmospheric pressure pushing on the surface (about $100\,000\ \text{Pa}$).
:::

The pressure depends only on depth and density, **not** on the shape or width of the container.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Area in cm²"}
For pascals the area must be in m². $1\ \text{cm}^2 = 0.0001\ \text{m}^2$, so a force on a few cm² gives a very large pressure in Pa.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the mass instead of the weight"}
Pressure needs a force. Convert mass to weight first: $W = mg$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Wider containers have more pressure"}
At the same depth in the same liquid, the pressure is the same whether the container is a narrow pipe or a wide dam.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
When explaining an everyday example, link three things: "the area is small, so for the same force the pressure is large, so the blade cuts easily".
:::

:::exam-tip
For $\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h$ questions, check that the depth is in metres and the density in kg/m³ before substituting.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Pressure = force ÷ area, $p = F/A$, measured in pascals (N/m²).
- A smaller area gives a larger pressure for the same force.
- Liquid pressure increases with depth and with density, and acts in all directions.
- $\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h$ gives the pressure change with depth.
- Liquid pressure does not depend on the shape of the container.
:::
