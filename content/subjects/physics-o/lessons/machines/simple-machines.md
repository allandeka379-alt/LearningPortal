## Introduction

A builder in Gweru lifts bags of cement with a pulley block; a farmer levers a boulder out of a field with a long iron bar; a lorry driver rolls a heavy drum up a plank into the truck. Each uses a **machine**: a device that lets a small force (the **effort**) overcome a larger force (the **load**).

Machines do not give you energy for free: what you gain in force you pay for in distance. This ZIMSEC topic (4.1) shows how to measure how good a machine is.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Load"}
The force the machine overcomes (for example the weight lifted).
:::

:::definition{term="Effort"}
The force applied to the machine.
:::

:::definition{term="Mechanical advantage (MA)"}
$\text{MA} = \dfrac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}}$. It has no unit. An MA greater than 1 means the machine multiplies force.
:::

:::definition{term="Velocity ratio (VR)"}
$\text{VR} = \dfrac{\text{distance moved by the effort}}{\text{distance moved by the load}}$. It depends only on the design of the machine.
:::

:::formula{name="Efficiency of a machine"}
$$
\text{efficiency} = \frac{\text{MA}}{\text{VR}} \times 100\% = \frac{\text{useful work output}}{\text{work input}} \times 100\%
$$
:::

## Levers

A lever is a rigid bar that turns about a pivot. A long crowbar with the pivot close to the load lets a small effort far from the pivot produce a large turning effect. Wheelbarrows, bottle openers, scissors and claw hammers are levers. (Classifying levers into types is not required.)

## Pulley systems

In a single-string pulley system, one rope runs round several pulleys. The **velocity ratio equals the number of strands of rope supporting the lower (moving) block**. For example, a system with 4 supporting strands has $\text{VR} = 4$: to lift the load by $1\ \text{m}$, each of the four strands must shorten by $1\ \text{m}$, so the effort pulls in $4\ \text{m}$ of rope.

:::worked-solution[A pulley block on a building site]
A pulley system with 4 supporting strands lifts a load of $1000\ \text{N}$ with an effort of $320\ \text{N}$.

1. $\text{MA} = \dfrac{1000}{320} = 3.125$.
2. $\text{VR} = 4$ (the number of supporting strands).
3. Efficiency $= \dfrac{3.125}{4} \times 100\% = 78\%$.
:::

## Inclined planes

Pushing a load up a slope is easier than lifting it straight up, but it must be pushed further. For a slope of length $L$ rising a height $h$, $\text{VR} = \dfrac{L}{h}$.

:::worked-solution[Loading a drum up a plank]
A drum weighing $600\ \text{N}$ is pushed up a plank $3.0\ \text{m}$ long onto a truck bed $1.2\ \text{m}$ high, with a force of $300\ \text{N}$ along the plank.

1. $\text{VR} = \dfrac{3.0}{1.2} = 2.5$.
2. $\text{MA} = \dfrac{600}{300} = 2.0$.
3. Efficiency $= \dfrac{2.0}{2.5} \times 100\% = 80\%$.
4. Check with work: useful work $= 600 \times 1.2 = 720\ \text{J}$; work input $= 300 \times 3.0 = 900\ \text{J}$; $\dfrac{720}{900} = 80\%$.
:::

## Why machines are never 100% efficient

Some of the work input is wasted:

- against **friction** in the pulleys, bearings or between the load and the slope, which heats the parts;
- in **lifting the moving parts** of the machine itself, such as the lower pulley block and the rope.

So the MA is always less than the VR, and the efficiency is less than $100\%$. For a pulley system the efficiency usually **rises** as the load gets heavier, because the weight of the moving block becomes a smaller fraction of the total.

### Improving efficiency

- **Lubricate** moving parts (oil or grease) to reduce friction.
- Use **ball bearings** in the pulley wheels.
- Make the moving parts **lighter**.
- Keep surfaces **smooth**.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Counting all the pulleys"}
The VR of a pulley system is the number of strands supporting the **moving** block, not always the number of pulley wheels.
:::

:::mistake{title="Efficiency over 100%"}
If your efficiency comes out above 100%, you have divided the wrong way round: efficiency = MA ÷ VR, and MA is always the smaller.
:::

:::mistake{title="Machines save work"}
A machine reduces the force needed, not the work done. The work input is always at least the useful work output.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Show MA, VR and efficiency as separate steps with their formulas; each step usually earns a mark even if a later one goes wrong.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked why the efficiency is less than 100%, give two reasons: friction in the moving parts and the weight of the moving parts (pulley block, rope).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A machine lets a small effort overcome a large load.
- MA = load ÷ effort; VR = distance moved by effort ÷ distance moved by load.
- Efficiency = MA ÷ VR × 100% = useful work out ÷ work in × 100%.
- Pulley system: VR = number of strands supporting the moving block. Inclined plane: VR = length ÷ height.
- Friction and the weight of moving parts make efficiency less than 100%; lubrication, bearings and lighter parts improve it.
:::
