## Introduction

You check your uniform in a mirror before school, a driver watches the road behind in a rear-view mirror, and a still dam reflects the hills around it. All rely on the **reflection** of light, which follows one simple law.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Normal"}
A line drawn at right angles (90°) to a surface at the point where a ray meets it.
:::

:::definition{term="Angle of incidence, i"}
The angle between the incident ray and the normal.
:::

:::definition{term="Angle of reflection, r"}
The angle between the reflected ray and the normal.
:::

:::keyfact
**Law of reflection**: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, $i = r$. The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane.
:::

## Using the law

Angles are always measured from the **normal**, not from the mirror.

:::worked-solution[A ray at a mirror]
A ray of light strikes a plane mirror making an angle of $35^\circ$ with the surface of the mirror.

1. The normal is at $90^\circ$ to the mirror, so the angle of incidence is $90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$.
2. By the law of reflection, the angle of reflection is also $55^\circ$.
3. The ray turns through $180^\circ - 2 \times 55^\circ = 70^\circ$.
:::

### Verifying the law

Place a plane mirror upright along a line on paper. Draw a normal. Shine a narrow ray from a ray box at the mirror along a line at a measured angle of incidence, mark the reflected ray with crosses, and measure the angle of reflection with a protractor. Repeat for several angles: $i$ always equals $r$.

## The image in a plane mirror

Light from an object reflects from the mirror into your eye. Your brain assumes the light travelled in straight lines, so the image appears to be **behind** the mirror. The image in a plane mirror is:

- the **same size** as the object;
- the **same distance behind** the mirror as the object is in front;
- **upright** (the right way up);
- **virtual**: no light actually passes through it, so it cannot be formed on a screen;
- **laterally inverted**: left and right are swapped (which is why AMBULANCE is printed backwards on the front of ambulances).

:::worked-solution[How far away is the image?]
1. A girl stands $1.5\ \text{m}$ in front of a wall mirror.
2. Her image is $1.5\ \text{m}$ behind the mirror.
3. The distance from her to her image is $1.5 + 1.5 = 3.0\ \text{m}$.
:::

### Constructing an image (Cambridge Supplement; ZIMSEC)

To locate the image of a point object, draw the normal from the object to the mirror and extend it the same distance behind the mirror: that point is the image. Then draw reflected rays from the mirror to the eye; when extended backwards (as dashed lines) they meet at the image.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Measuring from the mirror"}
The angles of incidence and reflection are measured from the **normal**. A ray making $35^\circ$ with the mirror has $i = 55^\circ$.
:::

:::mistake{title="The image is on the mirror's surface"}
The image appears as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In ray diagrams, draw the normal as a dashed line, put arrows on rays to show direction, and show virtual rays behind the mirror as dashed lines.
:::

:::exam-tip
Learn the five properties of a plane-mirror image: same size, same distance behind, upright, virtual, laterally inverted.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The normal is at 90° to the surface; angles are measured from the normal.
- Law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
- A plane-mirror image is the same size, the same distance behind the mirror, upright, virtual and laterally inverted.
- A virtual image cannot be formed on a screen.
- Images are located by constructing normals and extending reflected rays backwards.
:::
