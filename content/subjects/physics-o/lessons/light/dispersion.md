## Introduction

After an afternoon thunderstorm in the rainy season, a rainbow arches over the land. Spray at Victoria Falls produces rainbows almost every sunny day. A glass prism on a laboratory bench can make the same band of colours. Each shows that **white light is a mixture of colours**, which can be separated by refraction.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Dispersion"}
The splitting of white light into its separate colours, because each colour is refracted by a different amount.
:::

:::definition{term="Spectrum"}
The band of colours produced when white light is dispersed.
:::

:::definition{term="Monochromatic light (Cambridge Supplement)"}
Light of a single frequency (a single colour).
:::

## Dispersion by a prism

When a narrow beam of white light enters a triangular glass prism, it refracts at the first face and again at the second. Each colour travels at a slightly different speed in glass, so each is refracted by a slightly different amount:

- **red** light is refracted (deviated) the **least**;
- **violet** light is refracted the **most**.

The colours spread out into a spectrum on a screen. A second prism, turned the other way, can recombine the colours into white light, showing the colours were already in the white light: the prism separates them, it does not create them.

## The order of the colours

The traditional seven colours of the visible spectrum, from least refracted to most refracted:

**red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet**

| | Red end | Violet end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wavelength | longest | shortest |
| frequency | lowest | highest |
| refraction in glass | least | most |

:::example{title="How a rainbow forms"}
Sunlight enters raindrops, refracts, reflects off the back of each drop and refracts again as it leaves. The colours are separated by different amounts, so an observer with the Sun behind them sees red on the outside of the bow and violet on the inside.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="The prism adds colour"}
The colours are already present in white light; the prism only separates them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Red has the highest frequency"}
Red has the lowest frequency and longest wavelength of visible light; violet has the highest frequency and shortest wavelength.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In a dispersion diagram, show the beam bending towards the normal on entering the prism and away on leaving, with violet bent most and red least.
:::

:::exam-tip
Learn the order with a memory aid, such as **R**ichard **O**f **Y**ork **G**ave **B**attle **I**n **V**ain, and know which end has the higher frequency.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Dispersion is the splitting of white light into colours by refraction.
- Each colour is refracted by a different amount: violet most, red least.
- The seven colours in order: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
- Red has the longest wavelength and lowest frequency; violet the shortest wavelength and highest frequency.
- Light of a single frequency is monochromatic (Cambridge Supplement).
:::
