## Introduction

Spectacles, cameras, projectors, microscopes and the lens in your own eye all use **lenses**: shaped pieces of glass or plastic that refract light to form images. Knowing a few rules lets you predict exactly where an image will form and what it will look like.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Converging (convex) lens"}
A lens thicker in the middle than at the edges; it bends parallel rays inwards to meet at a point.
:::

:::definition{term="Diverging (concave) lens"}
A lens thinner in the middle than at the edges; it spreads parallel rays outwards.
:::

:::definition{term="Principal axis"}
The line through the centre of the lens at right angles to it.
:::

:::definition{term="Principal focus (focal point), F"}
For a converging lens, the point on the principal axis where rays parallel to the axis meet after passing through the lens.
:::

:::definition{term="Focal length, f"}
The distance from the centre of the lens to the principal focus.
:::

## How lenses act on parallel light

A **converging** lens refracts a parallel beam so that it **converges** to the principal focus; a thicker, more curved lens has a shorter focal length. A **diverging** lens spreads a parallel beam out so it seems to come from a focal point on the same side as the light arrives.

### Measuring the focal length (ZIMSEC)

Point a converging lens at a distant object, such as a tree outside the window, and move a screen behind the lens until a sharp image forms. The distance from the lens to the screen is the focal length, because rays from a very distant object are almost parallel.

## Ray diagrams for a converging lens

Use any two of these three rays from the top of the object:

1. A ray **parallel to the principal axis** is refracted through the principal focus $F$ on the other side.
2. A ray through the **centre** of the lens passes straight through without bending.
3. A ray through the principal focus on the object's side emerges **parallel** to the axis.

Where the rays meet is the top of the image.

![Ray diagram for a converging lens with the object beyond 2F (200 units from the lens, focal length 80): a ray parallel to the axis passes through the far focus and a ray through the centre goes straight on; they meet at a real image 133 units behind the lens, inverted and diminished.](media:diagrams/lens-ray-diagram-beyond-2f.png "Figure: converging lens with the object beyond 2F")

## Describing images

Use three pairs of words: **enlarged / same size / diminished**, **upright / inverted** and **real / virtual**. A **real** image is formed where light rays actually meet and can be shown on a screen; a **virtual** image is where rays only appear to come from, found by tracing diverging rays backwards, and cannot be shown on a screen.

| Object position | Image | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| beyond 2F | real, inverted, diminished, between F and 2F | camera, eye |
| at 2F | real, inverted, same size, at 2F | — |
| between F and 2F | real, inverted, enlarged, beyond 2F | projector |
| inside F | virtual, upright, enlarged, same side as the object | magnifying glass |

:::example{title="A camera"}
A distant tree is far beyond 2F of a camera lens. The image formed on the sensor is real (it can be recorded), inverted and diminished, which is why a whole tree fits on a small sensor.
:::

### The magnifying glass (Cambridge Supplement; ZIMSEC)

When an object is placed **closer to a converging lens than its focal length**, the rays leave the lens diverging. They never meet, but traced backwards they appear to come from a point behind the object: the image is **virtual, upright and enlarged**. This is a magnifying glass. (ZIMSEC describes magnification without calculations and does not require images formed by diverging lenses.)

![Ray diagram for a converging lens used as a magnifying glass: the object is 50 units from the lens, inside the focal length of 80. The rays leave the lens diverging; traced backwards as dashed lines they appear to come from an upright, enlarged, virtual image on the same side as the object.](media:diagrams/lens-ray-diagram-magnifier.png "Figure: a converging lens as a magnifying glass")

## Correcting sight (Cambridge Supplement; ZIMSEC)

- **Short sight**: distant objects are blurred because the eye focuses their light **in front of** the retina. A **diverging** lens spreads the rays slightly so the eye focuses them on the retina.
- **Long sight**: near objects are blurred because the eye would focus their light **behind** the retina. A **converging** lens bends the rays in a little first, so they focus on the retina.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="All lens images are inverted"}
A converging lens gives an upright, virtual image when the object is inside the focal length.
:::

:::mistake{title="Short sight and diverging"}
Short-sighted people need diverging lenses; long-sighted people need converging lenses. Remember: short sight, the image falls short (in front of the retina).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Draw ray diagrams with a ruler, mark F on both sides, put arrows on rays, and draw virtual rays as dashed lines.
:::

:::exam-tip
Always describe an image with all three words: for example "real, inverted and diminished".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Converging lenses bring parallel rays to a principal focus; diverging lenses spread them out.
- Focal length: distance from the lens centre to the principal focus.
- Ray diagrams: parallel ray through F; ray through the centre undeviated; ray through F emerges parallel.
- Object beyond 2F: real, inverted, diminished (camera). Inside F: virtual, upright, enlarged (magnifying glass).
- Diverging lenses correct short sight; converging lenses correct long sight.
:::
