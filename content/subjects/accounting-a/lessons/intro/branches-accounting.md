## Introduction

Gweru Furniture makes wooden stools and wardrobes. The bookkeeper records every sale and purchase. At the year end the accountant prepares statements for the bank and ZIMRA. The factory supervisor needs to know what a stool costs to make. The owner asks whether next year's plan to sell 20% more is affordable. These are four different jobs done with accounting information. They belong to different **branches of accounting**, and the syllabus expects you to know them, who they serve and how they differ.

This is a ZIMSEC Form 5 topic. Cambridge candidates do not need it as a separate topic, but the same divisions appear throughout their course.

:::keyfact{title="Terms in this lesson"}
| ZIMSEC (common usage) | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Financial accounting; bookkeeping | Recording and classifying transactions; the basis of the statements |
| Financial reporting | Preparing and presenting the statements for external users |
| Cost accounting; management accounting | Internal; the Cost and Management Accounting section of the syllabus |

This topic is tested in words: definitions, comparisons and classification of tasks. There are no calculations beyond understanding what a typical output looks like.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Financial accounting"}
The recording, classifying and summarising of the financial transactions of a business, ending with the preparation of financial statements.
:::

:::definition{term="Financial reporting"}
The presentation of financial information in statements for external users, following laws and standards.
:::

:::definition{term="Cost accounting"}
The collection and analysis of costs to find the cost of products, jobs or services and to control costs.
:::

:::definition{term="Management accounting"}
The provision of financial and non-financial information to managers for planning, controlling performance and decision making.
:::

## The branches and related activities

- **Bookkeeping** is the day-to-day recording of transactions. It supplies the data for financial accounting.
- **Financial accounting and reporting** turn the records into the income statement (statement of profit or loss), the statement of financial position and related statements for outsiders.
- **Cost accounting** records and analyses costs: materials, labour and overheads, per unit, job or process.
- **Management accounting** uses financial and cost data for planning and decisions: budgets, variance analysis, break-even, investment appraisal.
- **Auditing** is an independent examination of the accounts by someone outside the preparation, usually a qualified auditor, who gives an opinion on whether they are fair.
- **Taxation** is the calculation of tax due from the accounts and its payment to the tax authority.

Auditing and taxation are related activities; they use the accounts but are not branches of recording.

## Why the division matters

The branches share the same raw data but answer different questions. A bookkeeper who records wages correctly lets the financial accountant report the wages expense; the cost accountant uses the same wages to find the labour cost of a stool; the management accountant compares the wages with the budget and asks why they differ. If the first record is wrong, every branch inherits the error, so accuracy in recording matters to all of them.

The branches also differ in how much freedom the accountant has. The statements for outsiders must follow the law and accounting standards so that readers can compare one business with another. Internal reports have no such rules, so a manager can ask for them in whatever form helps a decision, for instance the profit made by each wardrobe model or the cost of each delivery route.

## Who prepares what

| Branch | Usually prepared by | For whom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bookkeeping | Bookkeeper or clerk | The accountant |
| Financial accounting and reporting | Accountant | Owners, lenders, tax authority |
| Cost accounting | Cost accountant or clerk | Production managers |
| Management accounting | Management accountant | Directors and managers |
| Auditing | An independent auditor | Shareholders (or lenders and the owner) |

In a small business one person often does everything. In a large business they are separate departments.

## Differences

| Feature | Financial accounting and reporting | Cost accounting | Management accounting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main purpose | Record transactions and report profit and position | Find the cost of products, jobs and processes | Help managers plan, control and decide |
| Main users | Owners, lenders, tax authority and other outsiders | Managers and cost clerks | Managers and directors |
| Time | Past | Past and present | Past, present and future |
| Rules | Laws, standards and set formats | Chosen to suit the business | Chosen to suit the decision |
| Typical output | Income statement, statement of financial position | Cost statement, cost per unit | Budget, variance report, break-even and appraisal |

:::worked-solution[Example 1: classifying tasks]
For each task, name the branch or activity.

| Task | Branch |
| --- | --- |
| Recording credit sales in the sales journal | Financial accounting (bookkeeping) |
| Preparing the annual statement of financial position | Financial reporting |
| Working out the cost of making one wardrobe | Cost accounting |
| Comparing actual spending with the budget and explaining the difference | Management accounting |
| Checking the accounts and reporting an opinion to the shareholders | Auditing |
| Calculating the business income tax due | Taxation |

1. The test is the purpose: recording (bookkeeping), reporting to outsiders, finding a cost, helping managers decide, checking independently or calculating tax.
2. The same data (for example wages) may appear in all branches but is used differently.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: one business, three typical outputs]
Gweru Furniture makes a batch of 400 stools. Direct materials cost US\$4,200, direct labour US\$3,100 and production overheads US\$1,500. The batch is sold for US\$14,000. The budgeted cost of the batch was US\$9,200.

**Cost accounting output**

**Cost statement for a batch of 400 stools**

|  | US\$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Direct materials | 4,200 |
| Direct labour | 3,100 |
| Production overheads | 1,500 |
| **Total cost of the batch** | 8,800 |
| **Cost per stool (8,800 ÷ 400)** | 22 |

**Management accounting output**

| Cost of the batch | Budget US\$ | Actual US\$ | Difference US\$ | Effect |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Total cost of 400 stools | 9,200 | 8,800 | 400 | Favourable: cost below budget |

**Financial accounting output**

**Extract from the income statement for the batch**

|  | US\$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Revenue (400 stools sold) | 14,000 |
| Cost of sales | (8,800) |
| **Gross profit** | 5,200 |

1. Cost accounting finds the cost per stool: 8,800 ÷ 400 = 22.00.
2. Management accounting compares the actual cost with the budget; the 400 saving is favourable.
3. Financial accounting reports the profit on the batch in the income statement for outsiders.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: answering a comparison question]
**Question:** Distinguish between financial accounting and management accounting.

1. State the difference of purpose: financial accounting reports past performance to outsiders; management accounting helps managers plan and control.
2. State the difference of rules: financial accounting follows laws and standards in a set format; management accounting has no required format.
3. State the difference of time: financial accounting is about the past; management accounting also looks ahead through budgets.
4. A good answer uses "whereas" or "but" to join each pair, for example "financial accounting is historic whereas management accounting also includes forecasts".
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying management accounting is only for the owner"}
It is for all levels of management, such as production, sales and finance managers, not only the owner.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling auditing a branch of recording"}
An auditor checks the records; the auditor does not prepare them. Independence is the key point.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing features without comparing"}
A comparison needs both sides of each point. Writing three features of financial accounting and three of management accounting separately earns fewer marks.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing cost accounting with costing a single job only"}
Cost accounting also controls costs over time, for example by comparing actual costs with expected costs.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Paper 1 asks short classification questions; Paper 2 may ask you to *distinguish*, *state* or *describe* the branches. For *distinguish*, give pairs of contrasting points. For *describe*, give what the branch does, who uses it and one example of its output.
:::

:::exam-tip
When a question lists tasks of a named business, match each to a branch using the purpose of the task, and give the output (for example a cost per unit) if the question asks for an example.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Bookkeeping records transactions; financial accounting and reporting produce statements for outsiders.
- Cost accounting finds and controls the cost of products, jobs and processes.
- Management accounting gives managers information for planning, control and decisions, including forecasts.
- Auditing is an independent check of the accounts; taxation uses the accounts to calculate tax.
- Financial accounting follows set rules and looks at the past; management accounting is flexible and looks ahead.
:::
