## Introduction

Rudo starts a small egg and broiler project outside Masvingo. In the first month she pays in capital, buys cages, buys feed on credit, sells eggs for cash and on credit, and takes some money home. By the end of March she wants to know three things: how much cash is in the bank, how much Sunrise Stores still owes her and how much she owes the feed supplier. Double entry answers all three because it records **every transaction twice**, once as a debit and once as a credit.

This lesson is the foundation for the whole course. You should already know the basic idea from O-Level Principles of Accounting; here the pace is faster and the exam expects speed and accuracy with accounts for assets, liabilities, capital, income, expenses, returns, carriage and discounts. Books of prime entry and the trial balance are separate lessons.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use the same double-entry rules. The words differ a little:

| ZIMSEC (common usage) | Cambridge 9706 |
| --- | --- |
| Debtors; creditors | Trade receivables; trade payables |
| Returns inwards; returns outwards | Sales returns; purchases returns |
| Income statement | Statement of profit or loss |

Cambridge papers say "trade receivables" and "trade payables" for the customer and supplier accounts. ZIMSEC papers may say "debtors" and "creditors". Treat them as the same thing.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Duality (double entry)"}
Every transaction has two equal effects on the accounts: the amount is debited to one account and credited to another, so total debits always equal total credits.
:::

:::definition{term="Accounting equation"}
Assets = Capital + Liabilities. Capital is what the owner has invested and left in the business; liabilities are what the business owes to others; assets are resources the business controls.
:::

:::definition{term="Ledger account"}
A record of all the transactions affecting one item (for example bank, wages or one customer), kept with the debit side on the left and the credit side on the right in a T account.
:::

:::definition{term="Balancing an account"}
Totalling both sides, finding the difference, entering it on the smaller side as the balance carried down (c/d) and bringing it down (b/d) on the opposite side to start the next period.
:::

## The debit and credit rules

Debit and credit are only the left and right sides of an account. They do not mean "good" or "bad". The side that **increases** an account depends on its type.

| Type of account | Increase | Decrease | Normal balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assets (bank, equipment, trade receivables) | Debit | Credit | Debit |
| Expenses (wages, purchases) | Debit | Credit | Debit |
| Drawings | Debit | Credit | Debit |
| Liabilities (trade payables, loans) | Credit | Debit | Credit |
| Capital | Credit | Debit | Credit |
| Income (sales, commission received) | Credit | Debit | Credit |

:::keyfact{title="One rule to remember"}
Debit what comes in or what is used up (assets, expenses, drawings). Credit what the business owes or has earned (liabilities, capital, income). Whatever increases an account on one side is decreased by entries on the other side.
:::

## Two ways of looking at one transaction

Take the sale of eggs for US\$2,100 paid into the bank. The accounting equation view: assets rise by 2,100 and capital rises by 2,100 (profit belongs to the owner). The ledger view: debit Bank 2,100, credit Sales 2,100. Both views are the same event.

For a transaction on credit, the customer or supplier has an **account of their own** in the sales or purchases ledger. A credit sale debits the customer. A credit purchase credits the supplier. Payment reverses the position: the customer's payment is credited to the customer, and a payment to a supplier is debited to the supplier.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: the accounting equation after each transaction]
Rudo's first month, recorded as ten transactions. Capital changes when the owner invests, when income or expenses arise and when she takes drawings.

| Date | Transaction | Assets US\$ | Liabilities US\$ | Capital US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 Mar | Rudo pays US\$10,000 into the business bank account | 10,000 | 0 | 10,000 |
| 2 Mar | Buys cages and an incubator, paying US\$3,600 by bank | 10,000 | 0 | 10,000 |
| 3 Mar | Buys feed on credit from Gweru Feeds, US\$1,250 (expense: purchases) | 10,000 | 1,250 | 8,750 |
| 5 Mar | Sells eggs for US\$2,100, paid into the bank | 12,100 | 1,250 | 10,850 |
| 8 Mar | Sells eggs on credit to Sunrise Stores, US\$900 | 13,000 | 1,250 | 11,750 |
| 10 Mar | Pays wages of US\$420 by bank | 12,580 | 1,250 | 11,330 |
| 12 Mar | Sunrise Stores returns eggs sold for US\$100 | 12,480 | 1,250 | 11,230 |
| 15 Mar | Pays Gweru Feeds US\$1,000 by bank | 11,480 | 250 | 11,230 |
| 20 Mar | Sunrise Stores pays US\$500 by bank | 11,480 | 250 | 11,230 |
| 25 Mar | Rudo withdraws US\$300 for personal use | 11,180 | 250 | 10,930 |

1. After every line, assets equal capital plus liabilities. If they do not, a transaction has been analysed wrongly.
2. At 31 March Rudo has assets of 11,180 (bank 7,280, equipment 3,600, receivable 300), a payable of 250 and capital of 10,930. The capital of 10,930 is 10,000 invested, plus profit for the month, less drawings of 300.
3. Profit for the month is the increase in capital apart from the capital introduced and the drawings: 10,930 - 10,000 + 300 = 1,230. Check with the trial balance below: sales 3,000 less returns 100, purchases 1,250 and wages 420 leaves 1,230.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: choosing the accounts]
Not every transaction is a simple cash movement. Four more, from Rudo's April.

| Transaction | Debit | Credit | US\$ |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Rudo takes eggs from the farm for the family; cost of the eggs US\$40 | Drawings | Purchases | 40 |
| Sunrise Stores owes US\$400 and settles it with 2.5% cash discount, paying by bank | Bank 390 and Discount allowed 10 | Sunrise Stores | 400 |
| Rudo pays US\$75 carriage on feed delivered to the farm, by bank | Carriage inwards | Bank | 75 |
| Gweru Feeds is sent back feed bought for US\$130 (credit purchase) | Gweru Feeds | Purchases returns | 130 |

1. Drawings of goods: the business gives up inventory, so credit Purchases (it reduces the goods bought) and debit Drawings.
2. Discount allowed is a cost to Rudo, an expense, so it is debited. The customer account is credited with the full 400, because the debt is cleared, and Bank is debited with the 390 actually received. Two debits (390 + 10) match one credit (400).
3. Carriage inwards is part of the cost of goods, so it has its own expense account.
4. Returning goods to a supplier reduces what Rudo owes, so the supplier account is debited.
:::

## Returns, carriage and discounts

- **Sales returns** (returns inwards) reduce sales: debit Sales returns, credit the customer.
- **Purchases returns** (returns outwards) reduce purchases: debit the supplier, credit Purchases returns.
- **Carriage inwards** is delivery of goods into the business; **carriage outwards** is delivery to customers. Both are debited as expenses; inwards is added to the cost of purchases, outwards is a distribution cost.
- **Discount allowed** (to customers) is an expense; **discount received** (from suppliers) is income.
- **Capital** is credited when it is introduced. **Drawings** are debited and are never an expense; they reduce capital.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Treating debit as 'increase'"}
Debit increases assets and expenses but decreases liabilities, capital and income. Learn the type of account first, then the side.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting drawings in expenses"}
Money or goods taken by the owner is not a cost of running the business. Debit Drawings; it is deducted from capital, not from profit.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting to bring the balance down"}
The balance c/d on one side must be brought down (b/d) on the other side. A missing b/d means the opening figure of the next period is lost, and the trial balance will not balance.
:::

:::mistake{title="Posting both sides of a credit sale to the sales account"}
A credit sale is debited to the customer, not to Bank. Cash is only debited when money actually arrives.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Papers 1 to 3 and Cambridge Papers 1 and 2 test double entry in every series, either directly (ledger accounts, journals) or as the engine behind statements. *State* questions on debit and credit need the account name and the side. *Prepare* questions on accounts need dates, details naming the other account, a balance c/d and b/d, and totals level on both sides. Never write "cash" if the transaction goes through the bank.
:::

:::exam-tip
When a question asks you to *explain* why a trial balance can balance and still be wrong, give an error type and an example: an entry omitted completely, posted to the wrong class of account, or reversed. Marks are for the type and the reason, not the word "error".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Duality: each transaction is debited to one account and credited to another for the same amount.
- A = C + L must hold after every transaction; profit and drawings change capital.
- Debit assets, expenses and drawings to increase them; credit liabilities, capital and income to increase them.
- Customers' accounts are debited for sales and credited for receipts and returns; suppliers' accounts are credited for purchases and debited for payments and returns.
- Balance each account by totalling the larger side, inserting the balance c/d on the smaller side and bringing it down on the opposite side.
- A balancing trial balance does not prove the ledger is free of error.
:::
