## Introduction

Chegutu Dairy Ltd needs US\$120,000 for a new cold room. The directors do not want to ask the shareholders for more money and do not want to dilute their control, so they decide to borrow by issuing **loan notes** to investors. The investors lend the money for a fixed number of years and receive a fixed rate of interest, whatever the profit. The company must repay the amount on the due date.

You know that a company can raise capital by issuing shares, and you have calculated gearing. This lesson looks at the accounting for loan notes: how the issue is recorded, how interest is charged and accrued, how the redemption is recorded and, for ZIMSEC, how convertible loan stock is turned into shares. It also looks at the effect on gearing and cash flow.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
| ZIMSEC (syllabus and common usage) | Cambridge 9706 (AS) |
| --- | --- |
| Loan notes; debentures; loan stock | Debentures; loan notes |
| Convertible loan stock | Not examined in Cambridge questions |
| Secured and unsecured loan notes | Secured and unsecured debentures |

:::

Convertible loan stock is **ZIMSEC detail**. The comparison of debentures and shares from the company's and the investor's view is common to both boards.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Loan notes (debentures)"}
A long-term loan to a company, evidenced by a certificate, on which the company pays a fixed rate of interest and which it must repay on a stated date.
:::

:::definition{term="Secured loan notes"}
Loan notes backed by a charge over named assets. If the company cannot pay, the holders can claim the assets. Unsecured loan notes have no such charge.
:::

:::definition{term="Convertible loan stock"}
Loan notes that the holder may exchange for ordinary shares on stated terms (ZIMSEC detail).
:::

:::definition{term="Redemption"}
The repayment of the loan notes to the holders at the end of the term, normally at their nominal value.
:::

## Recording the issue and the interest

When the notes are issued, the bank is debited and the loan notes account is credited. The notes are a **non-current liability** until the last 12 months of their life, when the amount is shown as a current liability. The interest is a **finance cost** in the statement of profit or loss and is charged for the period the notes were in issue, whether paid or not. Interest owing at the year end is an accrual, a current liability.

:::keyfact{title="Interest"}
Annual interest = nominal value × rate. For part of a year, multiply by the fraction of the year. Interest charge for the year = interest paid + interest accrued at the end - interest accrued at the start.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 1: issue, interest paid and interest accrued]
On 1 April 2025 Chegutu Dairy Ltd issued US\$120,000 of 10% loan notes at par, redeemable in 2030. Interest is paid half-yearly on 30 September and 31 March. The year end is 31 December. Prepare the ledger accounts and show the extracts from the financial statements for 2025.

Interest for the year = 120,000 × 10% × 9/12 = US\$9,000. Interest paid on 30 September = 120,000 × 10% × 6/12 = US\$6,000. Interest accrued at 31 December = 9,000 - 6,000 = US\$3,000 (three months).

## Debentures against shares

Why would a company choose loan notes instead of shares? The answer depends on whose view you take.

|  | Loan notes (debentures) | Ordinary shares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Return to the holder | Fixed interest, paid whether or not there is a profit | Dividend, only if the directors declare one |
| Repayment | On a fixed date | Not repaid; sold to another investor |
| Claim if the company closes | Before shareholders; secured loan notes against named assets | Last, after all creditors |
| Voting rights | None | Yes |
| Effect on the company's gearing | Raises gearing | Lowers gearing |
| Tax treatment of the return | Interest is an expense before tax | Dividends are paid from profit after tax |

**The company's view.** Loan notes bring in money without giving up control, interest is deducted before tax, and the cost may be lower than the dividends shareholders expect. However, the interest must be paid even in a loss-making year, the loan must be repaid, gearing rises and security may have to be given. **The investor's view.** The investor gets a safer, fixed income and a better claim if the company fails, but cannot share in the growth of the profits and has no vote. A convertible holder (ZIMSEC) can combine a safe income with the option to share in growth.

## Redemption and conversion

At the end of the term the company pays back the nominal value. In the year before redemption the amount is moved to current liabilities, which worsens the current ratio even though the loan has not changed. The redemption reduces the bank balance, gearing and the interest cost. Convertible loan stock may be exchanged for shares at a stated rate, in which case there is no cash outflow.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: redemption and conversion (conversion is ZIMSEC detail)]
Nkayi Fisheries Ltd has US\$150,000 of 9% loan notes, ordinary shares of US\$400,000, share premium of US\$40,000, retained earnings of US\$210,000 and cash of US\$180,000. On 1 July 2026 the company redeems US\$90,000 of the notes at par out of the bank, and the holders of the other US\$60,000 convert into ordinary shares of US\$1 each at the rate of one share for every US\$1.25 of loan notes. Prepare the journal and show the effect on the position and on gearing.

Shares issued on conversion = 60,000 ÷ 1.25 = 48,000. Share capital rises by 48,000; the balance of 12,000 is credited to share premium.

**Journal**

| Date | Details | Debit US\$ | Credit US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 Jul | 9% loan notes | 90,000 |  |
|  | Bank |  | 90,000 |
|  | *Redemption of US\$90,000 of loan notes at par* |  |  |
| 1 Jul | 9% loan notes | 60,000 |  |
|  | Ordinary share capital |  | 48,000 |
|  | Share premium |  | 12,000 |
|  | *Conversion of US\$60,000 of loan notes into 48,000 ordinary shares of US\$1* |  |  |

**Position before and after (US\$)**

|  | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 180,000 | 90,000 |
| Ordinary share capital | 400,000 | 448,000 |
| Share premium | 40,000 | 52,000 |
| Retained earnings | 210,000 | 210,000 |
| **Total equity** | 650,000 | 710,000 |
| 9% loan notes (non-current) | 150,000 | 0 |
| **Capital employed** | 800,000 | 710,000 |

Gearing before was 18.8% (150,000 ÷ 800,000); after both steps it is 0.0%, because there are no loan notes left. Cash falls by US\$90,000, but the conversion costs no cash. The company saves interest of 9% × 150,000 = US\$13,500 a year (less if the redemption and conversion happen part-way through the year), which improves profit before tax. For the new shareholders, the earnings are shared among more shares: the earnings per share may fall.
:::

## Effects on the statements

- **Statement of profit or loss:** finance cost for the interest; no entry for the principal.
- **Statement of financial position:** loan notes in non-current liabilities, then current liabilities in the last year; accrued interest in current liabilities.
- **Gearing and interest cover:** the issue raises gearing and lowers interest cover; the redemption reverses both.
- **Cash flow:** the issue is a financing inflow; the interest is paid out of operating or financing cash flows depending on the policy; the redemption is a financing outflow.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Charging only the interest paid"}
The finance cost is the interest for the period, paid or not. Add the accrual to the interest paid.
:::

:::mistake{title="Charging the full year's interest on notes issued part-way through the year"}
Interest runs from the date of issue. A nine-month period gives three-quarters of the annual interest.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating the repayment as an expense"}
The redemption repays a liability. Debit the loan notes account; it is not a cost in the statement of profit or loss.
:::

:::mistake{title="Crediting the whole conversion to share capital"}
Share capital is credited at the nominal value of the shares issued. The remainder of the loan notes converted goes to share premium.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Loan notes are tested in ZIMSEC Papers 2 and 3 and in Cambridge Papers 1 and 2 (AS). For *prepare ledger accounts*, show the issue, the interest paid, the accrual and the transfer to profit or loss. For *show extracts*, put the notes in non-current liabilities and the accrued interest in current liabilities. For *explain the effect on gearing*, name the numerator and the denominator.
:::

:::exam-tip
For *evaluate* debentures against shares, give a point for the company and a point for the investor, and compare risk and return. For conversion (ZIMSEC only), state the number of shares from the conversion rate, credit nominal value to share capital and the balance to share premium.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The issue debits bank and credits loan notes; the interest is a finance cost for the period in issue, with unpaid interest accrued.
- Loan notes are non-current liabilities, but become current liabilities in the last year before redemption.
- Redemption repays the nominal value from the bank; it is not an expense and it lowers gearing.
- Debentures give investors fixed interest and security, but no vote; shares give votes and a share in growth.
- ZIMSEC: convertible loan stock is exchanged for shares; credit nominal value to share capital and the rest to share premium.
:::
