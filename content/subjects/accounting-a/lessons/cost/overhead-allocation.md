## Introduction

Chipo Nyathi runs Harare Furniture Works with a cutting shop and an assembly shop. The factory also has a maintenance team and a canteen that serve both shops. Rent, power and insurance are paid for the whole factory. To cost a table fairly, the factory must first decide how much of each overhead belongs to the cutting shop and how much to the assembly shop. This process of sharing overheads among cost centres is the first half of **absorption costing**. The second half, absorbing the overhead into products, is in the next lesson. Overhead allocation is examined in ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Paper 3 and in Cambridge Paper 2 question 4.

You should know the cost classifications and be able to calculate percentages and proportions.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
| ZIMSEC (syllabus and common usage) | Cambridge 9706 |
| --- | --- |
| Allocation, apportionment and allotment of overheads | Allocation, apportionment and reapportionment |
| Overhead analysis sheet | Overhead analysis sheet (overhead apportionment table) |
| Production and service departments | Production and service cost centres or departments |

Both boards examine the overhead analysis sheet. Questions give the bases of apportionment, so you do not need to choose from memory, but you may be asked to suggest or justify one.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Allocation"}
Charging a whole cost to the cost centre that caused it, because the cost can be identified with that centre. Example: the salary of the assembly supervisor to the assembly shop.
:::

:::definition{term="Apportionment"}
Sharing a cost that benefits several cost centres among them on a fair basis, such as floor area for rent or number of employees for the canteen.
:::

:::definition{term="Reapportionment"}
Sharing the total overhead of service departments among the production departments (and sometimes other service departments) that use their services. ZIMSEC uses the term allotment for the whole process.
:::

:::definition{term="Production and service departments"}
A production department works directly on the product (cutting, assembly). A service department supports production but makes no product (maintenance, stores, canteen).
:::

## The three steps of overhead allocation

1. **Allocate** every overhead that belongs wholly to one cost centre.
2. **Apportion** the shared overheads using a suitable basis. Record the result on an **overhead analysis sheet**, with a column for each cost centre and a row for each overhead.
3. **Reapportion** the totals of the service departments to the production departments. Only the production departments are left to absorb overhead into products.

Suitable bases follow the cause of the cost: **floor area** for rent, rates, heating and lighting; **carrying amount (or cost) of machinery** for depreciation and insurance of machinery; **number of employees** for the canteen, personnel and supervision; **machine hours or kilowatt hours** for power; **value of materials issued** for stores. Choose the basis that best reflects benefit received. A basis is a convention, not a fact, so a different basis gives different department costs.

When one service department also serves another, the usual way in these syllabuses is to reapportion in order: first the service department that serves the others, then the next one. The cost of the department already shared out is not apportioned back. A repeated-distribution method also exists, but examinations usually give the order and the percentages.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: overhead analysis sheet and reapportionment]
The overheads of Harare Furniture Works for the year ended 31 December 2025 are shown below. The factory has two production departments (Cutting and Assembly) and two service departments (Maintenance and Canteen).

| Overhead | Basis | Total US\$ | Cutting US\$ | Assembly US\$ | Maintenance US\$ | Canteen US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Rent and rates (floor area 600, 300, 60, 40 m²) | Apportioned | 24,000 | 14,400 | 7,200 | 1,440 | 960 |
| Heating and lighting (floor area) | Apportioned | 4,800 | 2,880 | 1,440 | 288 | 192 |
| Depreciation of machinery (carrying amount 60,000; 30,000; 10,000; nil) | Apportioned | 12,000 | 7,200 | 3,600 | 1,200 | 0 |
| Insurance of machinery (carrying amount) | Apportioned | 3,000 | 1,800 | 900 | 300 | 0 |
| Power (meter readings, 60%, 30%, 10%, nil) | Apportioned | 9,000 | 5,400 | 2,700 | 900 | 0 |
| Supervisors' salaries | Allocated | 11,000 | 6,000 | 5,000 | 0 | 0 |
| Maintenance wages and materials | Allocated | 9,512 | 0 | 0 | 9,512 | 0 |
| Canteen wages and food | Allocated | 5,648 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 5,648 |
| **Total** |  | 78,960 | 37,680 | 20,840 | 13,640 | 6,800 |

The canteen is used by the employees of the other departments (Cutting 6 shares, Assembly 3, Maintenance 1). The maintenance department's time is used by Cutting and Assembly in the ratio 70 : 30. The canteen is reapportioned first.

|  | Cutting US\$ | Assembly US\$ | Maintenance US\$ | Canteen US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Overheads after allocation and apportionment | 37,680 | 20,840 | 13,640 | 6,800 |
| Canteen reapportioned (employees 6 : 3 : 1) | 4,080 | 2,040 | 680 | (6,800) |
| Subtotal | 41,760 | 22,880 | 14,320 | 0 |
| Maintenance reapportioned (hours 70 : 30) | 10,024 | 4,296 | (14,320) |  |
| **Total overhead of production departments** | 51,784 | 27,176 | 0 | 0 |

The total overhead of 78,960 has been shared completely: 51,784 + 27,176 = 78,960. Tafara, the production manager, will now use the totals 51,784 and 27,176 to calculate an absorption rate for each department.
:::

## The effect of the basis

Different bases move cost between departments, so they change the cost of products that pass through each department.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: choosing a basis for power]
Power costs US\$12,000 a year. The Machining department has 200 m² of floor and used 500 machine hours; the Finishing department has 100 m² and used 1,500 hours.

| Basis | Working | Machining US\$ | Finishing US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Floor area (200 m² and 100 m²) | 12,000 × 2/3 and 1/3 | 8,000 | 4,000 |
| Machine hours (500 and 1,500) | 12,000 × 1/4 and 3/4 | 3,000 | 9,000 |

Floor area puts two thirds of the power on Machining (8,000) even though it uses only a quarter of the hours. Machine hours put just 3,000 on Machining. Power is used by machines, so machine hours is the better basis here. Products that spend most of their time in Finishing carry much more cost under the second basis.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Apportioning an overhead that can be allocated"}
If a cost clearly belongs to one department (the supervisor of assembly), allocate it in full. Apportion only costs shared by several departments.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting that service departments do not make products"}
After reapportionment the service departments must show nil. The whole overhead lies in the production departments.
:::

:::mistake{title="Reapportioning in the wrong order"}
Do the service department that serves other service departments first, then the one that serves only production. Do not send cost back to a department that has already been cleared.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using a basis that does not match the cost"}
Floor area for the depreciation of machines or machine hours for rent gives a result examiners will not accept. Name the basis and justify it.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Overhead allocation is tested in ZIMSEC Papers 2 and 3 and in Cambridge Paper 2 question 4, usually as a table to complete followed by an absorption rate. For *prepare* the sheet, show every row total and check that the department columns add up to the total. For *calculate*, show the share (for example 24,000 × 600/1,000) before the answer.
:::

:::exam-tip
For *explain* why a basis is suitable, link the basis to the way the cost is caused. For *evaluate*, say that a basis is an estimate and that a different basis would change the cost of products. Always check that the grand total is unchanged after reapportionment.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Allocate costs that belong wholly to one cost centre; apportion shared costs on a fair basis.
- The overhead analysis sheet has a row for each overhead and a column for each department.
- Reapportion service department costs to production departments, in order, until service departments are nil.
- Choose bases that reflect cause or benefit: floor area, carrying amount, employees, machine hours.
- The total overhead never changes; only its distribution among departments does.
:::
