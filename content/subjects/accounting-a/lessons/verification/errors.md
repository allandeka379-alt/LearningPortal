## Introduction

Chipo's bookkeeper in Masvingo has posted the ledger carefully and the trial balance agrees. A month later an auditor finds that a computer was charged to purchases and that a credit sale was never recorded. The trial balance did not reveal either error. Because errors affect profit and the financial statements, accountants must know which errors a trial balance can detect, how to classify the others, how to correct them and what each one does to profit and working capital.

The topic is examined every year by ZIMSEC (Papers 1, 2 and 3) and Cambridge (Papers 1 and 2). It follows the lesson on the trial balance; the suspense account and corrected trial balances come in the next lesson.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge terms"}
Both boards use the same six types of error. Cambridge uses **profit for the year**, **trade receivables** and **trade payables**; ZIMSEC uses **net profit**, **debtors** and **creditors**. **Working capital** is current assets less current liabilities.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Error of omission"}
A transaction is left out of the books completely, so neither the debit nor the credit entry is made.
:::

:::definition{term="Error of commission"}
An entry is made in the wrong account of the same class (for example, one expense account instead of another, or the wrong customer).
:::

:::definition{term="Error of principle"}
An entry is made in the wrong class of account, breaking an accounting principle (for example a capital item treated as an expense, or private expenses treated as business expenses).
:::

:::definition{term="Error of original entry"}
The wrong amount is used for both the debit and the credit entry.
:::

:::definition{term="Reversal of entries"}
The correct accounts are used, but the debit is made on the credit side and the credit on the debit side.
:::

:::definition{term="Compensating errors"}
Two or more errors that cancel each other out, so the trial balance still agrees.
:::

## Errors the trial balance does not reveal

The six types above leave total debits equal to total credits.

| Type | Example | Correcting journal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Omission | Sale not recorded | Make both entries |
| Commission | Telephone debited to electricity | Debit the right account, credit the wrong one |
| Principle | Computer debited to purchases | Debit equipment, credit purchases |
| Original entry | 530 recorded as 350 | Debit and credit the difference in the right accounts |
| Reversal | Payment recorded on the wrong sides | Entry for twice the amount |
| Compensating | Two equal overcasts | Reverse each error |

## Errors the trial balance does reveal

These upset the totals and are held in a suspense account until found (next lesson):

- a **one-sided entry** (only a debit or only a credit made);
- an entry with **different amounts** on the debit and credit sides;
- an **addition error** in an account or in the trial balance;
- a balance copied to the trial balance wrongly, **omitted**, or put in the wrong column.

## Correcting errors with journal entries

1. Work out what was done (which accounts were debited and credited).
2. Work out what should have been done.
3. Make the entry that removes the wrong part and records the right part. Include a narrative.

For a reversal, the account that should have been debited was credited, so the correction is twice the amount: the original entry has to be cancelled and then made correctly.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: classifying and correcting six errors]
Nyasha finds the following errors in her ledger at the end of the year.

| No. | Error | Type | Trial balance affected? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sale of 640 to Mr Moyo not recorded at all | Omission | No |
| 2 | Telephone expense of 215 debited to electricity | Commission | No |
| 3 | Computer of 1,800 debited to purchases | Principle | No |

## The effect of errors on profit and working capital

Not all errors change profit. Use these rules.

- An error between two **expense** accounts, or two **asset** or **liability** accounts, does not change profit.
- A **capital item treated as an expense** (for example a computer in purchases) understates gross profit or profit, because the expense is too high. Correcting it increases profit (before any depreciation).
- A **revenue item treated as capital** (for example repairs added to premises) overstates profit. Correcting it reduces profit.
- An item in the wrong part of **cost of sales** or the **trading section** changes gross profit but not the profit for the year if both items are expenses.
- **Working capital** changes only if a current asset or a current liability is affected. A change in profit changes capital, which is not part of working capital by itself.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: corrected profit]
Nyasha's draft statement of profit or loss shows gross profit of US\$48,200 and profit for the year of US\$21,600. The errors above, and two more, are corrected.

**Corrected profit at 31 December 2025**

|  | Gross profit US\$ | Profit for the year US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Draft figures | 48,200 | 21,600 |
| Sale of 640 omitted | 640 | 640 |
| Computer of 1,800 debited to purchases (capital item treated as an expense) | 1,800 | 1,800 |
| Carriage inwards of 360 debited to carriage outwards | (360) |  |
| Repairs of 520 debited to premises (revenue item treated as capital) |  | (520) |
| Telephone of 215 debited to electricity |  |  |
| Corrected figures | 50,280 | 23,520 |

The telephone error moves cost between two expense accounts, so profit is unchanged. Carriage inwards belongs in cost of sales, so gross profit falls by 360 but profit for the year is unchanged because it was already an expense. The corrected profit for the year is US\$23,520.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: corrected working capital]
Nyasha's draft statement of financial position shows current assets of 31,500 and current liabilities of 18,900. Two of the errors are current items: the sale of 640 on credit and a further error, a credit purchase of 700 for goods that was omitted; the reversed payment (940) also affects bank and trade payables.

**Corrected working capital**

|  | US\$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Draft current assets | 31,500 |
| Add sale on credit omitted (trade receivables) | 640 |
| Less reversed payment corrected (bank) | (940) |
| Corrected current assets | 31,200 |
|  |  |
| Draft current liabilities | 18,900 |
| Add credit purchase omitted (trade payables) | 700 |
| Less reversed payment corrected (trade payables) | (940) |
| Corrected current liabilities | 18,660 |
|  |  |
| Draft working capital (31,500 − 18,900) | 12,600 |
| Corrected working capital (CA − CL) | 12,540 |

Working capital changes from 12,600 to 12,540, a fall of 60. The reversed payment reduced both a current asset and a current liability by the same amount, so it does not change working capital.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Correcting a reversal with the original amount"}
A reversal needs an entry for twice the amount: once to cancel the wrong entry and once to make the right one.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying every error changes profit"}
Errors between two expense accounts, two asset accounts or two liability accounts do not affect profit.
:::

:::mistake{title="Classifying an error of principle as commission"}
Commission is between accounts of the same class. Principle puts the entry in the wrong class, such as capital expenditure in an expense.
:::

:::mistake{title="Correcting through suspense when the trial balance agreed"}
If the trial balance agreed, no suspense account is needed. Correct the error directly with a journal.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC and Cambridge Papers 1 and 2 ask you to **identify** the type of error, **prepare** a correcting journal with a narrative and **calculate** the effect on profit or working capital. Show the draft figure, each adjustment with its sign and the corrected figure.
:::

:::exam-tip
Say whether each error affects the trial balance. In **explain** questions give the reason: for compensating errors, "two errors of equal and opposite effect".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Six errors leave the trial balance in agreement: omission, commission, principle, original entry, reversal and compensating.
- One-sided entries, unequal amounts, addition errors and wrong balances upset the trial balance and are held in suspense.
- Correct with a journal: cancel what was wrong and record what was right; a reversal needs twice the amount.
- Errors between accounts of the same class do not change profit; capital items in expenses understate profit; revenue items in assets overstate it.
- Working capital changes only when a current asset or current liability is corrected.
:::
