## Introduction

Mussolini came to power in Italy in 1922 and Hitler in Germany in 1933. Their regimes are often placed together as "fascist", and the two men admired one another, yet they were not identical. A question that begins "Compare" or "To what extent were Mussolini and Hitler similar?" tests whether you can hold two cases in your mind at once and reach a judgement. This lesson draws together what you have studied about each ruler and adds the alliance they made and the harm their policies did to the world, including to Africa: Ethiopia, the only African state that had defeated a European army at Adwa in 1896, was invaded by Italy in 1935.

You should know the domestic and foreign policies of Mussolini's Italy and of Hitler's Germany from the earlier lessons.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Fascism"}
A political movement and ideology of the early twentieth century, first created in Italy, that was fiercely nationalist, anti-democratic and anti-communist, glorified the state and violence, and demanded unity under a single leader and party. The word comes from the Latin *fasces*, a bundle of rods carried by Roman officials as a symbol of authority.
:::

:::definition{term="Totalitarianism"}
A system in which the state tries to control every part of public and private life. Both regimes claimed it as an ideal; neither achieved it completely.
:::

:::definition{term="The Axis"}
The alliance of Italy and Germany, which began as a diplomatic understanding in 1936, became a military alliance in the Pact of Steel in 1939, and was widened to include Japan in 1940.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and names to learn"}
- 1922: March on Rome. 1933: Hitler becomes Chancellor.
- October 1935: Italy invades Ethiopia. 25 October 1936: Rome-Berlin Axis agreed. 25 November 1936: Anti-Comintern Pact (Germany and Japan); Italy joins in November 1937.
- 22 May 1939: Pact of Steel. 27 September 1940: Tripartite Pact (Germany, Italy, Japan).
:::

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
This lesson is for ZIMSEC (topic 17 and the fascism essay). Cambridge candidates treat Mussolini and Hitler separately in Paper 4, though a Paper 4 judgement can use comparison.
:::

## Ideology: what they believed

Both movements rejected liberal democracy and communism, glorified the nation and the leader, and saw violence as legitimate. Both won support from people alarmed by the post-war economic crisis and by the fear of revolution. The differences were also important.

**Italian Fascism** emphasised the all-powerful state ("everything within the state, nothing outside the state") and national greatness, drawing on memories of the Roman Empire. It was not at first racist or antisemitic; Jews played a part in Italian life and the Fascist party until the late 1930s, and racial laws were introduced in 1938, partly under German influence.

**Nazism** was built on race. Its central beliefs were that humanity was divided into superior and inferior races, that Germans needed *Lebensraum*, and that the Jews were the enemy. Its leader principle (*Führerprinzip*) gave Hitler an absolute authority in theory.

## Comparing domestic policy

| Feature | Mussolini's Italy | Hitler's Germany |
|---|---|---|
| Route to power | Appointed by the king in October 1922 after the March on Rome | Appointed Chancellor in January 1933 after a deal among conservatives |
| Consolidation | Over several years (1922-26), by laws and violence | Within about 18 months (1933-34) |
| Limits on the ruler | The king and the Church kept some independence; the King dismissed Mussolini in 1943 | After 1934 the army, officials and Church lost any real power to check Hitler |
| Terror | OVRA secret police, confino (internal exile); relatively few executions | SS, Gestapo, camps, mass murder |
| Propaganda | The cult of Il Duce, newsreels and rallies | Goebbels, rallies, mass radio and film |
| Youth | Balilla and other organisations | Hitler Youth and BDM |
| Economy | Battle for Grain, corporatism, autarky | Public works, Four Year Plan, rearmament, autarky |
| Race | Racial laws only in 1938 | Racism central from the start |

A typical comparison reaches this conclusion: the two regimes had many techniques in common, but Hitler's was more thorough, more violent and more ideologically driven, while Mussolini's was less able to dominate the established institutions.

## Comparing foreign policy

Both leaders were expansionist and both used the failure of the League of Nations. Mussolini wished to build a new Roman Empire in the Mediterranean and Africa. He bombarded Corfu in 1923, conquered Ethiopia (October 1935 to May 1936, with the use of poison gas) and occupied Albania in April 1939. Hitler aimed to overturn Versailles and win Lebensraum in the east, and used rearmament and diplomacy to take the Rhineland, Austria and Czechoslovakia before invading Poland. Mussolini's aims were more limited and he lacked the industrial strength to support them. Hitler's aims, in the end, had no limits.

## The Axis

Until 1935 Mussolini distrusted Hitler and was alarmed by German interest in Austria, which he wished to remain independent. The war in Ethiopia changed this. Britain and France condemned Italy at the League and Italy was isolated, so Mussolini turned to Germany, which had not joined the sanctions. The two also intervened in the Spanish Civil War (from July 1936) on the side of General Franco, which drew them together. On 25 October 1936 they agreed on cooperation, and Mussolini spoke of a "Rome-Berlin Axis" around which Europe could turn. In November 1936 Germany and Japan signed the **Anti-Comintern Pact**, against communism, and Italy joined it in November 1937 and left the League the next month. In May 1939 the two signed the **Pact of Steel**, a full military alliance. In September 1940 Germany, Italy and Japan signed the Tripartite Pact. The partners did not coordinate closely: Mussolini kept Italy out of the fighting in September 1939 because it was unready, and invaded Greece in October 1940 without consulting Hitler.

:::example{title="Writing a comparison paragraph"}
"Both regimes were one-party states with a leader cult and a secret police. However, the Nazi regime was more extreme. After 1934 Hitler faced no check from the army, civil service or Church, whereas Mussolini remained dependent on the king, who dismissed him in July 1943. Hitler's regime also made racial persecution central to policy from 1933, while Italian Fascism did not adopt racial laws until 1938. This difference helps to explain why the Nazi regime's actions ended in genocide." The example states a similarity, then a specific difference with evidence, then a short explanation of why it matters.
:::

## Impact on the world

- **War.** The aggression of the two regimes was the main cause of the Second World War in Europe. About the causes, see the lesson ww2.causes.
- **Africa.** Italy's invasion of Ethiopia in 1935 shocked Africans and people of African descent across the world. Emperor Haile Selassie appealed to the League of Nations in Geneva in June 1936; the League's weak sanctions, which did not include oil, did not save his country, and it was under Italian rule until British and Ethiopian forces ended it in 1941. African soldiers were drawn into the war in East and North Africa.
- **Europe.** Occupation, forced labour and the genocide of the Jews were carried out by Nazi Germany and its collaborators.
- **After 1945.** Both regimes were destroyed (Mussolini was killed in April 1945, Hitler took his own life on 30 April 1945), and their defeat led to the Nuremberg trials, the division of Germany and the founding of the United Nations. The memory of Fascism shaped postwar democracies, including the human rights treaties which new African states later signed.

:::example{title="Planning a comparison essay"}
For "How far were Mussolini and Hitler similar?" plan four paragraphs: ideology, methods of control, economy, and foreign policy. In each, give a similarity and a difference with evidence, and in the conclusion say which was stronger. For example, in methods of control, both used secret police, but Hitler's terror was on a far larger scale and he faced no check from a king.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Treating the two regimes as the same"}
Similarities are real but a good comparison also gives differences: race, the limits on the ruler, and the scale of terror.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing each regime in turn and not comparing"}
"Compare" needs linked points in the same paragraph ("Whereas Hitler... Mussolini..."), not two separate summaries.
:::

:::mistake{title="Dating the Axis to 1939"}
The Rome-Berlin Axis was agreed in 1936. The Pact of Steel (1939) turned it into a military alliance.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="ZIMSEC Paper 2"}
Typical questions: "To what extent were the domestic policies of Mussolini and Hitler similar?", "Compare the foreign policies of Mussolini and Hitler", "Assess the impact of Fascism on the world." Use a clear plan: ideology, methods of control, economy, foreign policy; give similarities and differences with evidence; end with a judgement on which was greater. Know the order of the pacts.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Both regimes were one-party dictatorships with leader cults, propaganda, secret police, youth organisations and expansionist aims.
- Nazism was far more racial, more thorough in destroying other institutions and more violent; Mussolini's power was limited by the king and the Church.
- Italy's invasion of Ethiopia and the Spanish Civil War drew Mussolini and Hitler together in the Rome-Berlin Axis (1936).
- The Anti-Comintern Pact, the Pact of Steel (1939) and the Tripartite Pact (1940) formed the Axis alliance.
- Their policies caused war, occupation and genocide, and their defeat shaped the post-war world and African nationalism.
:::
