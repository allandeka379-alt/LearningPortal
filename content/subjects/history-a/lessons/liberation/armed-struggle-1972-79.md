## Introduction

In the early hours of 21 December 1972, ZANLA fighters attacked Altena Farm in the Centenary area in the north-east of Rhodesia. The attack was small, but it began a new and much more effective phase of the war. By 1979 the war covered most of the country, Rhodesia's economy was under strain, and the government had been forced to the conference table at Lancaster House.

This lesson explains how the strategy changed after 1972, the methods of mobilisation, the Rhodesian response, the role of the neighbouring states and the Patriotic Front, and the growing pressure on Rhodesia. Dates and names are given here as they are accepted by most historians; casualty figures are estimates only, and the details of raids on camps are disputed. You should know the failures of 1966-72 from the previous lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Pungwe"}
An all-night meeting in a village, with songs, slogans and speeches, at which ZANLA fighters and political commissars explained the aims of the war and won the support of villagers.
:::

:::definition{term="Protected villages"}
Fenced villages, often called "keeps", into which the Rhodesian government moved rural people to cut them off from guerrillas. Many people lost their homes and fields.
:::

:::definition{term="Mujiba and chimbwido"}
Young men (mujibas) and young women (chimbwidos) who acted as messengers, scouts and food carriers for the guerrillas.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and names to learn"}
- 21 December 1972: Altena Farm attack, Centenary area.
- 1973: Selous Scouts formed. 1974-75: détente and a short ceasefire; 18 March 1975, Herbert Chitepo killed in Lusaka.
- June 1975: Mozambique independent. 1976: Mozambique closes the border to Rhodesia (March) and Nyadzonia raid (9 August).
- October 1976: ZAPU and ZANU form the Patriotic Front.
- November 1977: raids on Chimoio and Tembue camps.
:::

## A new strategy

After the failures of 1966-72, ZANLA's commanders, among them Josiah Tongogara and Herbert Chitepo, changed the strategy. Using FRELIMO's help in Tete province, Mozambique, ZANLA political commissars crossed the border in 1971-72 and spent months in the villages of the north-east, explaining the aims of the war and organising supporters, before any attack was made. Spirit mediums, who spoke for the ancestors, gave the war religious legitimacy and linked it to the First Chimurenga of 1896-97. When the attack at Altena Farm came, the guerrillas already had support among the people. The attacks that followed spread through Mashonaland.

:::example{title="Explaining why the second phase succeeded where the first failed"}
1. In 1966-68 fighters entered in large columns with little support; in 1972 they came in small groups after the villagers had been prepared.
2. Mozambique provided a long, easily crossed border and bases.
3. Pungwe meetings, mediums and the use of mujibas and chimbwidos linked the fighters to the villagers.
4. The Rhodesian forces could not tell guerrillas from villagers and had to guard a very long border.
5. This made the war a contest for the support of the rural people, which the government could not win by force alone.
:::

## The Rhodesian response

- **Collective punishment.** After Altena the government fined and punished villages that were thought to help the guerrillas, and closed schools and shops in the border areas.
- **Protected villages.** From 1973-74 hundreds of thousands of people, according to common estimates, were moved into fenced villages in the war areas. Many lost land, cattle and crops. The guerrillas and many Africans condemned the policy, and it failed to stop the war.
- **Special forces.** The Selous Scouts (1973) were units that sometimes operated disguised as guerrillas; the Special Air Service and the Rhodesian Light Infantry used "fireforce" helicopter attacks.
- **Cross-border raids.** Rhodesian forces attacked bases in Mozambique and Zambia.
- **Propaganda and auxiliaries.** The government held up the "terrorist" threat and, from 1978, armed African auxiliaries in areas under the internal settlement. Opinions differ about their role.

## Détente and Chitepo's death

In 1974-75 South Africa and Zambia pressed for talks, and in December 1974 a ceasefire was announced and leaders were released from detention. The ceasefire did not hold. On 18 March 1975 Herbert Chitepo was killed by a car bomb in Lusaka. Responsibility has been disputed: the Zambian inquiry blamed divisions within ZANU, while others have blamed Rhodesian agents. After the killing Zambia arrested many ZANU officials. Robert Mugabe, who had been released from detention in late 1974 and had crossed into Mozambique, became the main figure in ZANU, working with Tongogara, who had been commander of ZANLA.

## Mozambique's independence and the Patriotic Front

Mozambique became independent in June 1975 under FRELIMO, and the long eastern border became open to ZANLA. In March 1976 Mozambique closed its border to Rhodesia, applied sanctions and allowed ZANLA to use camps. The war spread to Manicaland, Masvingo and the Midlands. ZIPRA, based mainly in Zambia, operated in the west and south-west, and, after 1976, ZAPU built up a stronger army. In October 1976, before the Geneva Conference, ZAPU and ZANU formed the **Patriotic Front** under Joshua Nkomo and Robert Mugabe. It was an alliance rather than a merger, and the two armies remained separate.

## Raids across the border

The Rhodesian forces made attacks on camps in Mozambique. Two are commonly cited.

- **Nyadzonia (9 August 1976).** Selous Scouts, disguised as FRELIMO forces, attacked a ZANLA camp near the Nyadzonia River where many people, including refugees and unarmed trainees, were gathered. Zimbabwean and Rhodesian sources differ on numbers, but all agree that many hundreds were killed, and by many accounts more than a thousand.
- **Chimoio and Tembue (23-25 November 1977).** Rhodesian forces attacked large ZANLA camps. Again the numbers are disputed, ranging from the Rhodesian claim of around 1,200 or more to Zimbabwean estimates of several thousand. Many of the dead were young recruits and civilians.

These raids killed many people and destroyed bases, but did not stop ZANLA, which moved and rebuilt, and they drew international criticism.

## The growing pressure on Rhodesia

By 1978-79 most of the country was affected by the war. Big parts of the countryside could not be reached by the government after dark. The Rhodesian economy was damaged by sanctions, the cost of the war and the withdrawal of workers called up for military service. White emigration increased from 1976. Britain, the United States and the Frontline States (Zambia, Tanzania, Botswana, Mozambique and Angola) pressed for a settlement. The shooting down of two Air Rhodesia Viscount aircraft by ZIPRA in September 1978 and February 1979, in which many civilians died, and Rhodesian raids on neighbouring states, showed the war's reach. Estimates of the total number of deaths in the war range from about 20,000 to 30,000 or more, and most of the dead were civilians. This is an estimate and historians do not agree on exact figures.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using precise casualty figures"}
Death tolls are disputed estimates. Write "estimates range from..." and acknowledge that the figures are uncertain.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling the Patriotic Front a merger"}
It was an alliance. ZIPRA and ZANLA stayed separate and the parties kept their own leaders.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing only the Rhodesian atrocities or only the guerrilla ones"}
Violence by all sides affected civilians. Describe each factually without taking sides.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="ZIMSEC Paper 1"}
Typical questions: "Describe the second phase of the armed struggle", "Why was the war more successful after 1972?", "Assess the effects of the war on Rhodesia". Give dates and names, link the change of strategy to Mozambique and the pungwe, and judge the effects on both sides.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- After 1972 ZANLA prepared the villagers before attacking, using Mozambique and mediums; the Altena Farm attack of 21 December 1972 began the new phase.
- Rhodesia responded with protected villages, Selous Scouts and raids on camps in Mozambique such as Nyadzonia (1976) and Chimoio (1977).
- Mozambique's independence in 1975 opened a long border to ZANLA; ZIPRA operated from Zambia.
- ZAPU and ZANU formed the Patriotic Front in October 1976 as an alliance.
- By 1979 the war, sanctions and pressure from neighbouring states forced Rhodesia to negotiate.
:::
