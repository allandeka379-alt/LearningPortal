## Introduction

When Lenin died on 21 January 1924, no one had been named to succeed him, and the Communist Party had no rules for choosing a new leader. Five years later Joseph Stalin ruled the Soviet Union and every serious rival had been removed from power. In 1924 few expected it: Trotsky was the better-known revolutionary, and Stalin seemed to many a dull official. This lesson explains how the struggle was won, and it asks a question that every examiner likes: was Stalin's victory the result of his own skill, of his rivals' mistakes, or of the nature of the party and state that Lenin had built?

You should know from the previous lessons that the Bolsheviks had banned other parties and, in 1921, banned factions within their own party, and that the NEP had restored the economy.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Lenin's Testament"}
Notes dictated by Lenin in December 1922 and January 1923, when he was ill, giving his views on six leading Bolsheviks. A postscript suggested removing Stalin as General Secretary because he was too rude and had accumulated too much power. The party leaders decided not to publish it.
:::

:::definition{term="General Secretary"}
The head of the party's Secretariat, a post which Stalin held from April 1922. It controlled appointments to party posts, and so gave him a growing network of supporters.
:::

:::definition{term="Socialism in One Country"}
Stalin's argument (1924) that the Soviet Union could build socialism by its own efforts without waiting for revolutions elsewhere. It was opposed by Trotsky's theory of Permanent Revolution, which held that socialism in Russia could survive only if revolutions spread to the advanced countries of Europe.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and names to learn"}
- April 1922: Stalin becomes General Secretary. December 1922 to January 1923: Lenin's Testament. 21 January 1924: Lenin dies.
- 1923-25: Stalin, Zinoviev and Kamenev form a triumvirate against Trotsky; Trotsky loses control of the army in January 1925.
- 1925-26: Zinoviev and Kamenev break with Stalin and join Trotsky in the United Opposition (1926); Stalin allies with Bukharin.
- November 1927: Trotsky and Zinoviev are expelled from the party. January 1928: Trotsky is exiled to Alma-Ata; February 1929: expelled from the USSR.
- 1928-29: Stalin turns against Bukharin and the Right. December 1929: Stalin's fiftieth birthday is celebrated in a public cult.
:::

## The vacuum and the candidates

Lenin's illness after strokes in 1922-23 removed him from decisions, and the Politburo, the party's inner circle, ruled collectively. Lenin's Testament was damaging to several of the men who might have followed him. It called Trotsky the most able man on the Central Committee, but too self-confident, and recalled that Zinoviev and Kamenev had opposed the rising in October 1917. It praised Bukharin as the party's favourite but doubted his grasp of Marxism. It criticised Stalin as too rude. In May 1924 the Central Committee decided not to publish it, partly because Zinoviev and Kamenev spoke up for Stalin.

| Candidate | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| Trotsky | Hero of October 1917 and creator of the Red Army; brilliant speaker and writer | Arrogant; joined the Bolsheviks only in 1917, so suspected as a former Menshevik; no support in the party apparatus; seemed to want to rule alone ("Bonapartism") |
| Zinoviev | Head of the Comintern and of the Leningrad party | Disliked, unreliable in 1917, an inconsistent speaker; shifted his policy |
| Kamenev | Head of the Moscow party | Opposed the October rising; lacked drive |
| Bukharin | Theorist and editor of *Pravda*; popular; the voice of NEP | Not a good organiser; a poor politician; tied to a policy that could be attacked |
| Stalin | General Secretary; controlled appointments; seen as safe and hardworking | Rude, disliked by some; criticised in the Testament; not a famous orator |

## The ideological arguments

The arguments were real, not only personal. The **Right** (Bukharin, Rykov, Tomsky) supported NEP, which meant slow industrial growth paid for by trade with the peasants (an often-quoted slogan urging peasants to "enrich themselves" is attributed to Bukharin, though he later qualified it). The **Left** (Trotsky, then Zinoviev and Kamenev) argued that NEP favoured the richer peasants and the Nepmen and that rapid industrialisation was needed, and Trotsky wanted revolution to be spread abroad. Stalin's slogan of **Socialism in One Country** gave party members a national and practical goal. It appealed to ordinary members tired of waiting for world revolution. Stalin positioned himself in the centre and shifted as needed.

## Stages of the struggle

**1923-25: against Trotsky.** After Lenin's death Stalin, Zinoviev and Kamenev formed a **triumvirate** (the "troika") to keep Trotsky from power. Stalin took the lead in organising Lenin's funeral, presented himself in lectures as Lenin's faithful pupil and enrolled more than 200,000 new members (the "Lenin Enrolment") whose loyalty was to the party leadership. Trotsky was ill at the time of Lenin's funeral and was not present, which hurt his standing. In 1924, in his writings on the lessons of October, Trotsky attacked Zinoviev and Kamenev. The party condemned him, and in January 1925 he lost his post as Commissar for War.

**1925-27: against Zinoviev and Kamenev.** Stalin turned on his former allies, who had begun to criticise NEP and his growing power. With Bukharin and the Right as allies he defeated them at the Fourteenth Party Congress (December 1925), because Stalin's supporters filled the delegations. Zinoviev and Kamenev then joined Trotsky in a **United Opposition** (1926) that was condemned for breaking the ban on factions. Trotsky was expelled from the Politburo in 1926; he and Zinoviev were expelled from the party in November 1927 and Trotsky was exiled to Alma-Ata in January 1928. He was deported from the USSR in 1929 and was murdered in Mexico in 1940 by a Stalinist agent.

**1928-29: against Bukharin and the Right.** The grain-procurement crisis of 1927-28, when peasants withheld grain, led Stalin to abandon NEP and to adopt rapid industrialisation, the policy of the Left that he had just defeated. Bukharin, Rykov and Tomsky protested, were accused of "right-wing deviation" and lost their posts by 1929-30. Their defeat meant that no one in the Politburo could now match Stalin.

:::example{title="Why did Stalin defeat Trotsky?"}
A good paragraph names reasons and ranks them. Stalin's control of party appointments was the greatest advantage, because it filled the congresses with loyal delegates; Trotsky relied on persuasion and his reputation and had no organisation. Trotsky also made mistakes: he was absent from the funeral, he underestimated Stalin, and he was suspect because of his Menshevik past and his arrogance. His rivals were also divided and chose to attack him first. A judgement: the deepest cause was the structure of the party, since the ban on factions meant that Trotsky's criticism could be called disloyalty, and Stalin controlled the machine that enforced it.
:::

## Why did Stalin win? The factors

1. **Control of the party machine.** As General Secretary he appointed secretaries and delegates, who owed their careers to him.
2. **Skilful tactics.** He allied with one group against another and never acted alone: first the Left was used against Trotsky, then the Right against the Left, then the Left's policy against the Right.
3. **Mistakes and weaknesses of rivals.** They underrated him, quarrelled with each other, and accepted the ban on factions that Lenin had imposed and that Stalin later used against them.
4. **Ideas and appeal.** Socialism in One Country appealed to patriotism and to practical members.
5. **Repression and propaganda.** Opponents were labelled factionalists and traitors, the OGPU (secret police) watched them, and a cult of Lenin, in which Stalin was Lenin's heir, spread through the press, schools and the mausoleum in Red Square.

Historians differ on emphasis. Some stress Stalin's cunning and ambition; others, the structure of a one-party state in which control of appointments was decisive; others, the weaknesses of the rivals.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying that Stalin was Lenin's chosen successor"}
Lenin's Testament criticised Stalin. He became the heir by winning the struggle, not by Lenin's wish.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating the struggle as only personal"}
The policies mattered: NEP against rapid industrialisation, and Socialism in One Country against Permanent Revolution. Stalin shifted his position to defeat opponents.
:::

:::mistake{title="Believing that Stalin killed his rivals by 1929"}
Trotsky and others were expelled and exiled; the physical elimination came in the 1930s.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Lenin's death in 1924 left a vacuum; the Testament criticised Stalin and Trotsky among others, and was not published.
- The main candidates were Trotsky, Zinoviev, Kamenev, Bukharin and Stalin, who held the key post of General Secretary.
- The ideological arguments concerned NEP versus rapid industrialisation and Socialism in One Country versus Permanent Revolution.
- Stalin defeated Trotsky with Zinoviev and Kamenev (1923-25), then Zinoviev, Kamenev and Trotsky with Bukharin (1925-27), then Bukharin and the Right (1928-29).
- His success rested on control of appointments, skilful alliances, his rivals' mistakes, appealing slogans, repression and propaganda.
:::
