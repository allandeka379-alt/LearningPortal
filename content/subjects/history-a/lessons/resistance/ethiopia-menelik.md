## Introduction

While Africa was being partitioned, one old state defeated a European army and kept its independence. On 1 March 1896 the army of Emperor Menelik II destroyed an Italian force at Adwa in northern Ethiopia. The victory made Ethiopia a symbol of African pride and hope across the continent, and it is the standard example of successful resistance to colonial conquest.

This lesson asks how Menelik did it. The answer lies in Ethiopia's long history as a Christian kingdom, in Menelik's skilful diplomacy and modernisation, in the mistakes of Italy, and in the unity of Ethiopian nobles and soldiers. You should know the causes of the Scramble and the Berlin Conference from the previous lessons. Do not assume that diplomacy and arms were separate: Menelik used both.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Negus negast"}
"King of Kings", the title of the Ethiopian emperor. Menelik became emperor in 1889.
:::

:::definition{term="Protectorate"}
A state that keeps its own ruler but whose foreign affairs are controlled by a stronger power. Italy claimed that the Treaty of Wuchale made Ethiopia one.
:::

:::definition{term="Askari"}
African soldiers recruited into European colonial forces. Italy's army at Adwa included many askari from Eritrea.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and names to learn"}
- 2 May 1889: Treaty of Wuchale between Menelik and Italy (the Italian representative was Count Antonelli).
- 1890: Italy proclaims the colony of Eritrea.
- 1893: Menelik denounces the treaty.
- 1 March 1896: Battle of Adwa; Italian commander General Baratieri.
- 26 October 1896: Treaty of Addis Ababa; Italy recognises Ethiopian independence.
:::

## Menelik and the unification of Ethiopia

Menelik was king of Shewa from 1865 and became emperor in 1889 after the death of Yohannes IV, who was killed fighting the Mahdist army in March 1889. Ethiopia was an ancient Christian state in the highlands, with a church, a literate tradition and a long habit of defending itself. But power was divided among regional rulers, the *rases*. Menelik won their support through marriage alliances, gifts, and a careful balance between persuasion and force. He also extended Ethiopian rule south and east, adding the lands of the Oromo, Sidama and other peoples. Historians today note that these conquests were part of the creation of modern Ethiopia, and that the peoples concerned often suffered loss of land and autonomy; this is a reminder that the state that resisted Italy was itself an expanding power. In 1886 he founded Addis Ababa as the new capital, and his wife, Empress Taytu, was an influential adviser who opposed Italian demands.

## Modernisation

Menelik understood that survival required modern arms. He bought large numbers of rifles and some artillery from France, Russia and even Italy, and used the revenue from taxes and the ivory and coffee trade to pay for them. He also welcomed advisers, built a telegraph and telephone network, a railway concession from the coast at Djibouti (begun in 1897), later ministries and a bank. These changes strengthened the state and impressed European observers. He used diplomacy as carefully as weapons: he wrote to the powers to announce his claims to ancient Ethiopian borders, set Britain, France and Italy against each other, and stayed on good terms with Russia.

## Italy's ambitions and the Treaty of Wuchale

Italy, newly united in 1861-70 and eager for colonies, occupied the Red Sea port of Massawa in 1885 and pushed inland. A first clash at Dogali in 1887 ended in an Ethiopian victory. In 1889 Menelik, who needed Italian help and weapons against his rivals, signed the **Treaty of Wuchale** (2 May 1889). The trouble lay in **Article 17**. The Amharic text said that Menelik *could* use the Italian government for his dealings with other powers. The Italian text said that he *agreed* to do so. Italy claimed that this made Ethiopia a protectorate and told the European powers so. The Italian government also took more land in the north and in 1890 created the colony of Eritrea.

:::example{title="Explaining the dispute over Article 17"}
1. Menelik signed in 1889 because he wanted arms and peaceful relations with Italy.
2. The Amharic text allowed him to use Italy as an intermediary if he wished.
3. The Italian text made it an obligation, which Italy used to claim a protectorate.
4. When Menelik wrote to the European powers as an independent ruler, Italy objected and the powers, who had been told of the "protectorate", were confused.
5. Menelik discovered the difference, denounced the treaty in 1893, and the road to war was open.
The lesson: a treaty is a source of power only if both sides understand the same text, and wording was used as a weapon of imperialism.
:::

## The war and the Battle of Adwa

Italy invaded in 1895 and defeated early Ethiopian detachments at Amba Alagi and Mekele, but its position grew weaker. Menelik called out the army of the whole country, and Ethiopian nobles from many regions came with their men. Historians estimate that the Ethiopian force at Adwa was several tens of thousands strong and far larger than the Italian army of about 17,000, which included many Eritrean askari. The Italian commander, General Baratieri, had poor maps, divided his columns, and was pressed by his government at home to fight. On 1 March 1896 his forces were separated and attacked by the Ethiopians, who held the high ground. By the end of the day the Italians had lost thousands killed and captured; the figures differ between sources but all agree the defeat was severe. Ras Makonnen, Ras Alula, Empress Taytu and Menelik himself had parts in the victory.

## Why did Ethiopia win?

- **Unity and leadership.** Menelik rallied the rases and the church around the defence of an independent Christian state.
- **Modern weapons.** His army was well armed with rifles and artillery bought from Europe.
- **Diplomacy.** Menelik had cultivated France and Russia and knew that Britain preferred Italy to be restrained.
- **Italian weaknesses.** Bad intelligence and maps, divided columns, supply problems, political pressure to win, and an underestimate of the enemy.
- **Geography and morale.** Ethiopian troops knew the terrain and were defending their country.

## Results

By the **Treaty of Addis Ababa** (26 October 1896), Italy recognised Ethiopian independence and cancelled the Treaty of Wuchale. Italy kept Eritrea. Britain and France, who had colonies on Ethiopia's borders, soon agreed boundaries and diplomatic relations. Ethiopia was one of only two African states, with Liberia, never colonised in the Scramble. Many Africans and people in the African diaspora drew inspiration from Adwa, and it fed Pan-African and Ethiopianist ideas. Italy's humiliation did not disappear: in 1935 Mussolini's army invaded, and Ethiopia was occupied until 1941. That later conquest is outside this lesson.

:::example{title="An essay paragraph on causes of success"}
"The most important reason for the Ethiopian victory at Adwa was Menelik's ability to unite Ethiopia. Without the support of the rases and the church he could not have called out an army of tens of thousands. His modern rifles and artillery mattered, but weapons are of little use without unity. Italian mistakes helped, but they were made worse by a campaign planned from Rome in ignorance of the ground. Unity therefore made the other advantages decisive."
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Ethiopia won only because it was never attacked before"}
Ethiopia had faced Egypt, the Mahdists and Italy at Dogali. Its victory came from preparation as well as from history.
:::

:::mistake{title="Missing the diplomacy"}
Adwa was not only a battle. Menelik's letters to the powers, his play on their rivalries and the Treaty of Addis Ababa were part of the resistance.
:::

:::mistake{title="Turning the story into praise only"}
Menelik also expanded the empire at the expense of other peoples. A balanced answer notes this while explaining why Ethiopia remained independent.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Menelik II united Ethiopia, founded Addis Ababa, bought modern arms and used diplomacy to protect independence.
- The Treaty of Wuchale (1889) was disputed because the Italian and Amharic texts of Article 17 differed; Menelik denounced it in 1893.
- Italy invaded in 1895 and was defeated at Adwa on 1 March 1896.
- Ethiopian victory rested on unity, arms, diplomacy and Italian mistakes.
- The Treaty of Addis Ababa (26 October 1896) recognised independence and made Adwa a symbol for Africans everywhere.
:::
