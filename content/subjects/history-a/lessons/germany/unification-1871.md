## Introduction

On 18 January 1871, in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, the palace of the French kings, the princes of Germany proclaimed the King of Prussia as German Emperor. The choice of place was deliberate: Germany had just defeated France and wished the world to see it. The empire created that day was a new great power in the centre of Europe, and the man who had arranged it, Otto von Bismarck, became its Chancellor. Fourteen years later, in 1884-85, he would host the Berlin Conference at which the European powers drew the rules for the partition of Africa, so the unification of Germany belongs also to the history of Africa and Zimbabwe.

![Timeline of German unification: Bismarck becomes Minister-President in 1862, Prussia wins at Königgrätz on 3 July 1866, the Ems Telegram of 13 July 1870 leads to war and Sedan, and the Empire is proclaimed at Versailles on 18 January 1871.](media:diagrams/german-unification-timeline.png "Figure: timeline of German unification, 1861-1871")

This lesson explains how unification was completed after the victory over Austria in 1866: the difficulty of the south German states, the attitude of Napoleon III, the crises over Luxemburg and Spain, the war of 1870-71, and the constitution of the new empire. You should know the events of 1862-66 and the creation of the North German Confederation.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Zollparlament"}
The Customs Parliament created in 1867 so that the Zollverein states, including the four south German states, sent deputies to a common body. It was meant to build political unity through economic ties.
:::

:::definition{term="Particularism"}
Loyalty to one's own state, such as Bavaria or Württemberg, and opposition to being absorbed in a larger Prussian-led state.
:::

:::definition{term="Ems Telegram"}
A report sent on 13 July 1870 from the resort of Ems to Bismarck describing the meeting of King Wilhelm I with the French ambassador. Bismarck edited it for publication in a way that made it look like an insult.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and names to learn"}
- 1867: Zollparlament; Luxemburg crisis (settled at the London Conference, May). 1868: revolution in Spain; the throne is later offered to Prince Leopold of Hohenzollern.
- 13 July 1870: Ems Telegram. 19 July 1870: France declares war. 1-2 September 1870: Sedan.
- 18 January 1871: German Empire proclaimed. 28 January 1871: armistice. 10 May 1871: Treaty of Frankfurt.
:::

## The problem of the south

After 1866 the states south of the Main (Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden and the southern part of Hesse-Darmstadt) were independent. They had signed secret military alliances with Prussia in 1866 because they feared France, but their peoples differed in religion and tradition. Bavaria and Württemberg were largely Catholic and wary of Protestant Prussia, and many people preferred their own rulers. Nationalists in the National Liberal Party and the Nationalverein wanted to complete unity quickly. Bismarck was in no hurry: he preferred to wait for events rather than force a union that might fail.

The Zollparlament of 1867 was an attempt to use economic ties. In the elections to it in 1868, however, anti-Prussian parties did well in Bavaria and Württemberg, showing how strong particularism still was. Unification by peaceful growth looked unlikely. A shared threat might bring the south into the Prussian camp.

## Napoleon III and the Luxemburg crisis

Napoleon III was disappointed by 1866. He had expected a long war and had hoped to gain territory as the price of neutrality, but Prussia won in seven weeks and he received nothing. He then sought compensation: first the Rhineland and Belgium, which Bismarck refused, and then the purchase of Luxemburg from the Dutch king in 1867. Luxemburg had formerly been a member of the German Confederation and still had a Prussian garrison in its fortress.

Bismarck had hinted that he would not oppose the purchase, but when the news leaked he allowed German nationalist opinion to react with anger, and in the Reichstag of the North German Confederation questions were raised. Historians disagree about his motives: some believe that he wished to tempt Napoleon into a move that would embarrass him, others that he merely wished to avoid war while keeping his options open. The crisis was settled by a conference at London in May 1867, which neutralised Luxemburg and saw the Prussian garrison withdraw. France lost prestige, Prussia appeared to have defended German soil, and relations between the two countries became worse. The southern states drew closer to Prussia.

## The Hohenzollern candidature and the Ems Telegram

In 1868 the Spanish queen Isabella II was deposed, and the Spanish government looked for a new king. In 1870 it offered the throne to Prince Leopold of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen, a distant relative of King Wilhelm I. Bismarck encouraged Leopold to accept, and the king reluctantly agreed. Napoleon III and his foreign minister Gramont were furious, since France would be faced with Hohenzollern rulers on two frontiers. On 12 July 1870 Leopold's father withdrew the candidature after French pressure, which was a diplomatic success for France.

The French government then asked for more: a guarantee that the candidature would never be renewed. Its ambassador Benedetti approached King Wilhelm at Ems on 13 July. The king refused politely, and he sent a telegram to Bismarck describing the meeting. Bismarck shortened the text so that it seemed that the king had rudely dismissed the ambassador, and released it to the press. In Paris crowds demanded war; in Germany people were angry at what looked like a French insult. France declared war on 19 July 1870.

Historians disagree about the extent of Bismarck's planning. Some argue that he provoked the war to unite Germany; others point out that the Hohenzollern affair might not have led to war if French leaders had accepted the withdrawal, and that Napoleon III, under pressure from public opinion and his own court, chose war. Most agree that Bismarck was glad to take the opportunity.

## The Franco-Prussian War, 1870-71

Bismarck was in a strong position. France had no allies: Austria had no wish to avenge Königgrätz so soon, Italy was angry that French troops protected the Pope in Rome, Britain was alienated when Bismarck published, in July 1870, a French draft treaty seeking Belgium, and Russia was friendly to Prussia. France had good rifles, but its army was smaller, slower to mobilise and worse led. The Prussian army under Moltke, with better organisation, railways and Krupp artillery, was ready, and the south German states, bound by their alliances and offended by France's threat, joined Prussia.

The Germans advanced rapidly. At Sedan on 1-2 September 1870 the main French army and Napoleon III himself surrendered, and some 100,000 men were taken prisoner. On 4 September the Second Empire fell in Paris and a republic was proclaimed, which continued the war. Paris was besieged from September and, after hunger and bombardment, capitulated; an armistice was agreed on 28 January 1871. By the **Treaty of Frankfurt** (10 May 1871) France ceded Alsace and part of Lorraine, paid an indemnity of five billion francs and accepted German occupation until it was paid. The annexation was bitterly resented in France and made lasting enmity likely.

## The creation of the Empire

During the war, in November 1870, the south German states agreed to join the North German Confederation. On 18 January 1871 Wilhelm I was proclaimed German Emperor at Versailles, though he disliked giving up the title of King of Prussia. The constitution created a federal state of about 25 states. The Emperor, who was also King of Prussia, controlled foreign policy and the army, and appointed and dismissed the Chancellor, who answered to him and not to parliament. The **Bundesrat** represented the states, and Prussia had enough votes to block any change to the constitution. The **Reichstag** was elected by all men over 25 but could not choose the government, and the army budget was fixed for years. Prussia held about two thirds of the territory and population and dominated the empire.

:::example{title="Weighing views of Bismarck"}
Question: "Bismarck engineered the Franco-Prussian War." For: he encouraged Leopold, edited the Ems Telegram and gained from the war by uniting Germany. Against: Leopold withdrew on 12 July, and France could have accepted this success; Napoleon III and Gramont made fresh demands and declared war. A balanced paragraph might conclude that Bismarck created the conditions and took the opportunity, but that French decisions made war possible, so the claim is partly true but overstated.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the Ems Telegram was forged"}
Bismarck shortened a real telegram and changed its tone, but did not invent a meeting. Explain how editing changed its effect.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring the French side"}
France declared war. Napoleon III's weak position at home, his search for prestige and the demands of his ministers must be part of any explanation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling 1871 the end of the German question"}
Austria's Germans were outside, and the annexation of Alsace-Lorraine created a new quarrel. The Empire was a Kleindeutsch solution dominated by Prussia.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- After 1866 the south German states were independent; the Zollparlament showed that particularism was strong.
- Napoleon III's search for compensation led to the Luxemburg crisis (1867), which hurt France's prestige.
- The Hohenzollern candidature and Bismarck's editing of the Ems Telegram (13 July 1870) led France to declare war on 19 July.
- Prussia was in a strong position: France had no allies, and the Prussian army was better organised; Sedan (1-2 September 1870) decided the war.
- The German Empire was proclaimed on 18 January 1871; the Treaty of Frankfurt gave Germany Alsace and part of Lorraine; the new constitution kept Prussia dominant.
:::
