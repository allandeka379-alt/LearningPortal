## Introduction

In September 1862 King Wilhelm I of Prussia was ready to abdicate. His parliament had refused to approve the money for his army reforms, his ministers could find no way out, and he turned in desperation to a man he distrusted: Otto von Bismarck. Four years later, in July 1866, Prussian armies defeated the Austrian empire at Königgrätz in a war that lasted only seven weeks, and Austria was excluded from German affairs. Bismarck had governed for four years against the majority of the elected parliament, and yet in 1866 many of the liberals who had opposed him voted to forgive him.

This lesson explains how Bismarck came to power, how he used the Schleswig-Holstein question to isolate Austria, how he prepared and won the war of 1866, and what the settlement did. It completes the Austro-Prussian contest of the previous lesson and leads into the final stage of unification. You should know the position of Prussia and Austria in 1862 and the aims of the liberals.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Constitutional crisis"}
The conflict of 1860-66 between the Prussian king and the liberal majority of the Landtag over the cost and organisation of the army, which led to government without an approved budget.
:::

:::definition{term="Gap theory (Lückentheorie)"}
Bismarck's argument that the constitution did not say what to do if king and parliament could not agree on a budget, so the government might continue to collect taxes and spend them.
:::

:::definition{term="Indemnity Bill"}
The law of September 1866 by which the Prussian parliament approved, after the event, the spending carried out since 1862 without an approved budget.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and names to learn"}

:::

## The constitutional crisis and Bismarck's appointment

Wilhelm I became king in January 1861. He and his War Minister, Albrecht von Roon, wanted to increase the size of the regular army by raising recruitment and to reduce the role of the Landwehr (the citizen militia), which the king distrusted and the liberals prized. This cost money, and the liberals in the Landtag, who disliked an army loyal only to the king, refused to vote the funds in full. The **German Progressive Party** was founded in 1861 and won the largest number of seats in elections in 1861 and 1862, on a platform of parliamentary control over the budget.

In September 1862 the Landtag rejected the military budget again. The king believed that a conflict over the army would be a conflict over his own authority. Roon advised him to appoint Bismarck, who was then ambassador in Paris, and on 23 September Bismarck became Minister-President and foreign minister. In a speech to a committee on 30 September he said, in effect, that the great questions of the time would not be settled by speeches and majority votes but by "iron and blood", that is, by power. He then governed on the basis of the gap theory: taxes were collected and the army was reformed without a legal budget. He restricted the press and ignored the Landtag's protests. The liberals protested but did not call for resistance; Prussia was prosperous, and many were soon to be won over by success abroad.

## The Congress of Princes and Schleswig-Holstein

In August 1863 Austria's Emperor Franz Joseph called a Congress of Princes at Frankfurt to reform the Confederation. King Wilhelm wished to attend, but Bismarck persuaded him to stay away, and the plan failed without Prussia. Bismarck also proposed a German parliament elected directly by the people, to show that Prussia was the champion of reform, and as a way to embarrass Austria.

The question of Schleswig and Holstein offered a chance. These duchies were ruled by the King of Denmark but had a large German-speaking population, and Holstein was a member of the Confederation. In November 1863 the new Danish king, Christian IX, signed a constitution that tied Schleswig closely to Denmark. German nationalists were outraged, and many supported the claim of Prince Frederick of Augustenburg to be duke of the duchies. Bismarck did not want an independent state under a small prince. He persuaded Austria to join Prussia, in the name of the old agreement of 1852 (the London Protocol), to demand that Denmark withdraw the constitution; when Denmark refused, Austrian and Prussian troops invaded in February 1864. The Prussians took the fortifications at Düppel on 18 April, and by the Treaty of Vienna (30 October 1864) Denmark gave up both duchies to the two powers jointly.

Bismarck had several reasons for involvement: to prevent a nationalist solution that would create a new small state; to win popular and national support for Prussia; to gain the duchies, which had the port of Kiel; and to bind Austria to a joint arrangement that could be turned into a quarrel. The outcome raised tension. By the **Convention of Gastein** (14 August 1865) Prussia would administer Schleswig and Austria would administer Holstein, with Lauenburg sold to Prussia. This was a temporary arrangement which left Holstein surrounded by Prussian territory, and Bismarck could cause disputes whenever he wished.

## Preparing for war with Austria

Bismarck prepared for war by isolating Austria. In October 1865 he met Napoleon III at Biarritz and, without making a written promise, left him hoping for compensation if France stayed neutral; Napoleon expected a long war that would exhaust both sides. Russia, grateful for Prussia's support in the Polish revolt of 1863, was friendly. In April 1866 Bismarck signed an alliance with Italy, which would attack Austria if war began within three months and receive Venetia. He also proposed that the Confederation should adopt a parliament elected by all men, a clever move to appeal to liberals.

War came in June 1866. Austria complained to the Federal Diet that Prussia had broken the Convention of Gastein, which Prussia said it had not; Prussian troops entered Holstein, and Austria asked the Diet to mobilise the federal army against Prussia. When the Diet voted for this on 14 June, Prussia declared the Confederation at an end and attacked. Most of the middle-sized states, including Bavaria, Saxony and Hanover, sided with Austria.

## The Seven Weeks' War and the settlement

The Prussian army was well prepared. Moltke used railways and the telegraph to bring forces to the front, and the Prussian infantry were armed with the breech-loading needle gun. On 3 July 1866 at Königgrätz (Sadowa) in Bohemia the Prussians defeated the main Austrian army. Italy fought Austria in the south. An armistice came at Nikolsburg on 26 July.

Bismarck insisted on a moderate peace, against the wishes of the king and generals, who wanted to march on Vienna. He wanted Austria humbled but not an enemy for ever, and he feared French or Russian intervention. By the **Treaty of Prague** (23 August 1866) Austria lost no territory to Prussia but agreed to the dissolution of the Confederation and to Prussian leadership north of the River Main. Prussia annexed Hanover, Hesse-Kassel, Nassau, Frankfurt and the two duchies. The states north of the Main formed the **North German Confederation** (1867), led by Prussia, with a Reichstag elected by all adult men. The south German states stayed independent but signed secret military alliances with Prussia. Italy received Venetia.

The **Indemnity Bill** of September 1866 asked the Landtag to approve the earlier spending. The liberals were split: some opposed any compromise, but a majority voted for the Bill, believing that the nation's unity mattered more than the legal point. Many of them founded the National Liberal Party, which supported Bismarck.

:::example{title="Explaining Bismarck's appointment"}
Question: why was Bismarck appointed Minister-President in 1862? The king faced a majority in the Landtag that would not pay for his army and he was close to abdication. Roon, who trusted Bismarck's resolve, advised that only a man willing to govern without a budget could break the deadlock. Bismarck, who had experience as a diplomat and was a conservative royalist, promised to carry through the army reform. In short, the appointment was a response to a crisis and not a step in a plan to unify Germany.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying Bismarck planned each step"}
Historians disagree. Some stress his opportunism and his readiness to change course; others see a consistent aim to make Prussia dominant. State a view and support it, but do not claim he had a detailed plan in 1862.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting why the peace was lenient"}
The mild Treaty of Prague was a deliberate policy: Bismarck wanted a future ally, and feared intervention by France or Russia.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing Denmark's war aims with Prussia's"}
The war of 1864 was fought by Austria and Prussia together against Denmark. The conflict between them came after the war.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Army reform divided king and liberal Landtag; Bismarck was appointed on 23 September 1862 and ruled without a budget using the gap theory.
- Bismarck stopped Wilhelm attending the Congress of Princes (1863), and joined Austria against Denmark in 1864, winning Schleswig and Holstein for the two powers jointly.
- The Convention of Gastein (1865) created a joint arrangement that Bismarck could turn into a quarrel; he isolated Austria with Biarritz and an alliance with Italy.
- Prussia won the Seven Weeks' War, most decisively at Königgrätz on 3 July 1866.
- The Treaty of Prague excluded Austria from Germany but took no Austrian land; the North German Confederation was formed, and the Indemnity Bill won the liberals over.
:::
