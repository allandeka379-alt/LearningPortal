## Introduction

Two huge agrarian empires, run by ancient dynasties, collapsed in the early twentieth century. Within a few decades each was governed by a Communist Party that claimed to follow Marx and Lenin, nationalised industry, remade the countryside and punished its critics. Because the two revolutions happened in different countries and under different leaders, they are a favourite topic for ZIMSEC comparison questions: "Compare the causes of the Russian and Chinese revolutions" or "To what extent were the policies of Stalin and Mao similar?" A comparison is not two stories told one after the other. It is an argument about where the cases match and where they differ, and about which is more important.

This lesson draws together the lessons on Russia (the Tsarist regime, 1905, 1917, the Bolsheviks and Stalin) and on China (the revolutions to 1949 and Mao's rule). It teaches the method as well as the content. It assumes that you have read those lessons.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Comparison"}
An examination of the likenesses and differences between two cases, organised by themes (causes, leaders, policies), with a judgement on which is more significant or on how far they were alike.
:::

:::definition{term="Marxism-Leninism"}
The ideology of the Bolsheviks, which taught that a disciplined vanguard party should lead the working class to power. The Chinese Communists adopted it, but Mao changed its emphasis from the urban workers to the peasants.
:::

:::definition{term="Revolution from above"}
Rapid social and economic change imposed by the state and the party, rather than arising spontaneously from below. Stalin's collectivisation and Mao's Great Leap Forward are examples.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Useful pairs of dates"}
- Russia: 1905 revolution; 1917 revolutions; 1918-21 civil war; 1928-41 Stalin's plans; 1941-45 war. China: 1911 revolution; 1927 split; 1934-35 Long March; 1937-45 war; 1946-49 civil war; 1953-76 Mao's policies.
- Leaders: Lenin and Stalin; Mao. Parties: Bolsheviks (Communist Party of the Soviet Union); CCP (founded 1921, with Comintern help).
:::

## Causes of the revolutions

**Similarities.** Both revolutions began in empires where most people were poor peasants, where land was unequal, and where reform came late and half-heartedly (the Duma and Stolypin; the Hundred Days and the New Policies). Both rulers were weakened by defeat in war and by foreign pressure: Russia by Japan in 1905 and by Germany in 1914-17, China by Britain, Japan and the other powers. In each case the monarchy fell to a revolt that no party planned (February 1917; Wuchang, 1911). And in each case the new government (the Provisional Government; Yuan Shikai's Republic and the Guomindang) failed to solve the problems of land and unity, which left room for a more radical party.

**Differences.** Russia's autocracy was overthrown by a rebellion of the capital's workers and soldiers, in the middle of a world war, in a few days. China's empire collapsed through provincial revolts and the army, and was followed by decades of warlordism. Foreign domination, the "unequal treaties" and Japan's invasion, mattered far more in China, and nationalism was a stronger cause there. Russia was a great power with its own empire; China was semi-colonised. In Russia the Bolsheviks seized power first and fought a civil war afterwards (1918-21); in China the civil war came first and the Communists took power at the end (1946-49).

## Leaders and the party

Both revolutions were led by disciplined Leninist parties. The Bolsheviks under Lenin, and the CCP under Mao, centralised authority, expected obedience and used an army controlled by the party (the Red Army with commissars; the PLA). Both parties used terror against enemies: the Cheka and the Red Terror; land reform and the campaigns of 1950-52. Both leaders created cults of personality.

Differences were also significant. Lenin worked within a Marxist tradition that placed the urban workers first, and the October rising was a rapid seizure of the capital. Mao turned the party towards the peasantry from 1927, and built a rural base (Jiangxi, Yan'an) and a guerrilla army over two decades. Lenin's party was small but won control of the soviets; Mao's army and the countryside were the source of strength, and the CCP grew largely during the war with Japan. Stalin ruled through the party apparatus and secret police, and used the Terror against the Party itself in 1937-38; Mao in 1966 used students and the masses against the Party apparatus in the Cultural Revolution.

## The roles of peasants and workers

| Theme | Russia | China |
|---|---|---|
| Decisive group in taking power | Workers and soldiers in Petrograd and Moscow; peasants seized land in 1917 | Peasants in the base areas; the army recruited from the villages |
| Main appeal | Peace, Land and Bread | Land reform, rent reduction and national resistance to Japan |
| Treatment after victory | Peasants resisted requisitioning; many suffered in collectivisation | Peasants gained from land reform in 1950, then lost land in communes |

## Policies compared

**Land.** In both countries the party first confirmed the peasants' seizure of land (1917; 1950) and then took the land back into collective control (collectivisation, 1929-33; co-operatives and communes, 1953-58). Both policies brought resistance and famine: the famine of 1932-33 (an estimated 5 to 8 million dead) and the famine of 1959-61 (an estimated 15 to 45 million or more). The Chinese collectivisation was faster and was carried out with less open peasant revolt, while the Russian one was more violent in its first phase (dekulakisation).

**Industry.** Stalin's Five Year Plans prioritised heavy industry; China's First Five-Year Plan copied the Soviet model with Soviet advisers. The Great Leap Forward then tried a different path, using mass labour rather than machines, which failed.

**Control and culture.** Both states used secret police, show trials or mass campaigns, censorship and propaganda. Stalin's Terror was organised by the NKVD, while the Cultural Revolution was a mass movement that Mao later restrained with the army. Both attacked religion and traditional culture, and both required art to serve the party (Socialist Realism; Mao's demand that art serve the people and the revolution).

**Foreign policy.** Russia gave early aid to the GMD and the CCP (through the Comintern), but the Soviet-Chinese alliance of 1950 ended in a bitter split by 1960, which was reflected in Africa.

:::example{title="A comparative paragraph"}
Both Stalin and Mao reorganised the countryside with great violence, but the means differed. Stalin collectivised rapidly in 1929-33 with armed force and dekulakisation, and the famine of 1932-33 killed an estimated 5 to 8 million. Mao gave land to the peasants in 1950 to win their loyalty and moved to communes only after 1955, but the Great Leap Forward of 1958 led to a famine of even greater estimated size, 15 to 45 million or more. The similarity is that both policies served the party's need to control food and to feed industry; the difference is that Stalin's came after a decade of NEP and Mao's after a successful land reform. The judgement is that both showed the willingness of communist leaders to accept enormous human cost for their goals.
:::

## How to write a comparison essay

1. **Plan by theme, not by country.** Choose three or four themes (causes, leadership, role of peasants, policies, outcomes).
2. **In each paragraph, make a claim that includes both cases.** For example: "Both revolutions grew from the collapse of weak monarchies, but foreign imperialism was far more important in China."
3. **Use evidence from each side**, with dates and facts, and linking words (whereas, similarly, in contrast, by comparison, however).
4. **Weigh and conclude.** Say which similarities or differences matter most, and answer the question's words (how far, to what extent).
5. **Give estimates as estimates** and avoid slogans.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Writing two separate essays"}
Telling the Russian story and then the Chinese story does not compare. Put both in every paragraph.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing similarities without a judgement"}
A comparison should say which resemblances are deep (party structure, collectivisation) and which are superficial.
:::

:::mistake{title="Assuming China copied Russia exactly"}
China used the Soviet model in some areas, but Mao adapted Marxism-Leninism to a peasant country and later rejected the Soviet path.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Paper 2 comparison questions ask "Compare the causes of the Russian Revolution and the Chinese Revolution", "To what extent did the policies of Lenin and Mao differ?" or "Assess the similarities between Stalin's and Mao's rule". Use a thematic plan with a short introduction defining the argument, four paragraphs that each compare both cases, and a conclusion that gives an overall judgement. Use accurate dates and labelled estimates.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Both revolutions came from weak monarchies in poor agrarian countries defeated by foreign powers, after failed reforms; both were led by Leninist parties.
- In Russia the revolution was urban and rapid (1917), followed by civil war; in China it was rural and long (1927-49), with foreign imperialism and Japan far more important.
- Both parties confirmed the peasants' land and then collectivised it, bringing famine (estimates of 5 to 8 million in 1932-33 and 15 to 45 million or more in 1959-61).
- Both used secret police, terror, propaganda and cults of personality; Stalin's Terror was state-run, while Mao's Cultural Revolution was a mass movement.
- A good comparison is organised by theme, uses evidence from both sides, and ends in a judgement.
:::
