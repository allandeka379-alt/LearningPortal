## Introduction

In 1900 China was the world's oldest continuous state, ruled by a Manchu dynasty, the Qing, and weakened by defeat, foreign concessions and rebellion. In 1949 Mao Zedong proclaimed the People's Republic of China from the gate of the Forbidden City in Beijing. Between those dates China saw the fall of the empire, a failed republic, warlords, a nationalist government, a war with Japan and a civil war. For Zimbabwean students this history has a direct link: ZANU's military wing, ZANLA, was trained partly in China from the 1960s and adapted Mao's ideas of mobilising the peasantry in a guerrilla war. This lesson asks why the Qing fell, why the Republic failed to unite China, and why the Communists defeated the Guomindang.

Compare this lesson with the one on Russia's revolutions; the final lesson of this section draws the two together.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Unequal treaties"}
Agreements forced on China by Britain, France, Japan and other powers after military defeats, which gave them concessions, trading rights and extraterritorial privileges, so that foreigners were not subject to Chinese law.
:::

:::definition{term="Warlord"}
A military commander who ruled a region of China with his own army, from the death of Yuan Shikai (1916) until the late 1920s.
:::

:::definition{term="Guomindang (GMD) and Chinese Communist Party (CCP)"}
The GMD (Nationalist Party), founded by Sun Yat-sen and led after 1925 by Chiang Kai-shek, ruled China from Nanjing from 1928. The CCP was founded in 1921 and, under Mao Zedong's leadership after 1935, turned to peasant revolution.
:::

:::definition{term="The Long March"}
The retreat of the Communist Red Army in 1934-35 from its base in Jiangxi to Shaanxi in the north-west, a journey of several thousand miles in which most of the marchers were lost.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Dates and names to learn"}
- 1899-1901: Boxer Rising. 1911: Wuchang rising, 10 October; the Qing emperor abdicates in February 1912. Sun Yat-sen, Yuan Shikai.
- 4 May 1919: May Fourth Movement. July 1921: CCP founded in Shanghai. April 1927: the Shanghai massacre of Communists.
- October 1934 to October 1935: Long March. 1937: war with Japan. 1946-49: civil war. 1 October 1949: People's Republic proclaimed.
:::

## The decline of the Qing and the Boxer Rising

The Qing faced pressure from outside and rebellion within. Britain defeated China in the Opium Wars (1839-42 and 1856-60), and unequal treaties opened ports and gave foreigners special rights. The Taiping Rebellion (1850-64) was one of the deadliest wars of the nineteenth century, and historians estimate that tens of millions died. Japan's victory in 1894-95 showed that "self-strengthening" had not worked. Powers carved out spheres of influence. In 1898 the young emperor Guangxu tried a programme of reform (the Hundred Days), but the conservative Empress Dowager Cixi had him confined and the reformers punished.

The **Boxer Rising** (1899-1901) was an uprising of peasants and others in northern China, in the Society of Righteous and Harmonious Fists, against foreigners, Christian missions and the Qing's weakness. Cixi gave it support, and in 1900 the Boxers besieged the foreign legations in Beijing. An eight-nation army relieved them in August 1900. The Boxer Protocol (1901) required a huge indemnity, which weakened the dynasty further. After this the court introduced reforms (ending the old civil-service examination in 1905, promising a constitution), but they came too late.

## The 1911 Revolution and the Republic

Discontent with the Qing grew among students abroad, merchants, army officers and secret societies. **Sun Yat-sen**, a doctor educated abroad, led a revolutionary league and put forward the Three Principles of the People (nationalism, democracy and people's livelihood). A dispute over railway nationalisation sparked an army mutiny at Wuchang on 10 October 1911 and other provinces declared independence. Sun was chosen provisional president of a republic in January 1912, but the powerful general **Yuan Shikai** became president in March after the child emperor Puyi abdicated (February 1912). Yuan suppressed the new parliament and the Guomindang and tried to make himself emperor in 1915-16. When he died in June 1916 the country broke into the control of **warlords**, and central government was weak until the 1920s.

## The May Fourth Movement and the two parties

In May 1919 students in Beijing protested when the Paris peace conference gave Germany's rights in Shandong to Japan. The **May Fourth Movement** grew into a wider cultural and political movement, with demands for science, democracy and national dignity. Some intellectuals, disillusioned with the West, turned to Marxism and to the example of Russia. The CCP was founded in Shanghai in July 1921, with Chen Duxiu and Li Dazhao among its leaders and Mao Zedong a member. Sun Yat-sen, who had little success with the warlords, accepted Soviet advice and arms and reorganised the Guomindang on a Leninist model. In the **First United Front** (1923-27) the CCP joined the GMD, and a military academy at Whampoa trained officers under Chiang Kai-shek.

After Sun's death in 1925, Chiang led the Northern Expedition (1926-28) against the warlords. In April 1927 he turned on his Communist allies in Shanghai and killed many thousands in the "White Terror". The Nanjing government (1928) was recognised abroad, but its real control was limited to the lower Yangzi, and it depended on landlords, bankers and warlords.

## The Communists, the Long March and Japan

The CCP's urban rising failed, and from 1927 Mao argued that the party should base itself on the peasants. In Jiangxi he set up a rural "Soviet" (1931) with land redistribution, a disciplined Red Army and guerrilla tactics. After Chiang's fifth encirclement campaign, the Communists broke out in October 1934 on the **Long March**. They crossed rivers and mountains, losing most of their strength: perhaps 80,000 to 100,000 left, and under 10,000 reached the north-west, by estimates. At the Zunyi Conference (January 1935) Mao emerged as the leading figure. The survivors settled at Yan'an in Shaanxi, where they built a base and trained cadres.

Japan seized Manchuria in 1931. Chiang concentrated on the Communists first, until the Xi'an Incident of December 1936, when he was held by a general and forced to agree to a **Second United Front**. Full war began in July 1937 and the Japanese took the capital, Nanjing, in December 1937, where Japanese troops killed very large numbers of civilians (estimates range from over 100,000 to about 300,000). The GMD moved its capital to Chongqing. The war damaged the GMD: its best troops were lost, taxes and inflation rose, and corruption spread. The CCP, by contrast, built bases behind Japanese lines, reduced rents, organised the villages and presented itself as the leading patriotic force. Party membership grew from tens of thousands in 1937 to over a million by 1945 (estimates).

## Civil war and victory, 1946-49

After Japan's defeat in 1945 the American attempt at mediation failed and fighting resumed in 1946. The Nationalists had more troops and American weapons, but they suffered from ruinous inflation, corruption, forced conscription and low morale, and lost the support of townspeople. The Communists carried out land reform in the areas they held, which won many peasants, and the People's Liberation Army (PLA) was disciplined and respected the villagers. In three great campaigns from late 1948 they won Manchuria, the Huai-Hai region and the north. Beijing fell in January 1949, Nanjing in April, and in December Chiang withdrew to Taiwan. On 1 October 1949 Mao proclaimed the People's Republic.

:::example{title="Why did the Communists win?"}
A strong answer groups causes and weighs them. The GMD's weakness: corruption, inflation, loss of its best armies in the war with Japan, and its reliance on landlords. The CCP's strengths: peasant support from land reform, disciplined leadership and tactics, a base at Yan'an, and a patriotic reputation from the war. Historians differ on how much weight to give to peasant support compared with GMD failure and the Japanese war. A balanced conclusion is that the war with Japan was the key condition, because it damaged the GMD and gave the CCP room to grow, but the CCP's reforms and organisation turned the opportunity into victory.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the 1911 Revolution with the 1949 revolution"}
1911 overthrew the Qing and created the Republic. 1949 brought the Communists to power.
:::

:::mistake{title="Assuming the Communists always had peasant support"}
They began as an urban party that turned to the peasants after 1927; support was won by land reform, discipline and the war.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating Chiang Kai-shek's government as only weak"}
It had real achievements and foreign recognition, but it was limited by warlords, war and its reliance on landlords.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The Qing declined because of defeats, unequal treaties, rebellion and failed reform; the Boxer Rising and its indemnity weakened it further.
- The 1911 Revolution ended the empire, but Yuan Shikai and then the warlords prevented a stable Republic.
- The May Fourth Movement and Russian example helped to found the CCP in 1921; the GMD and CCP first co-operated, then split in 1927.
- Mao built a peasant base, survived the Long March and led the CCP from Yan'an; war with Japan weakened the GMD and strengthened the CCP.
- The Communists won the civil war because of GMD weakness and the CCP's land reform, organisation and discipline; the PRC was proclaimed on 1 October 1949.
:::
