## Introduction

The alpha-scattering experiment showed that the atom has a nucleus; later experiments that fired very high-energy electrons at protons showed that the proton itself has structure. The electrons bounced off three point-like objects inside it. These objects are **quarks**, and together with the **leptons** (the electron family) they are the fundamental building blocks of all ordinary matter: the iron in a Zisco steel beam, the water in Lake Kariba and the cells of your body.

This lesson introduces the six quark flavours and their charges, shows how protons, neutrons and other hadrons are built from quarks, separates hadrons into baryons and mesons, names the leptons, and explains what happens to the quarks during beta decay.

You should already know the nuclear model, the charge of the proton and electron, and beta-minus and beta-plus decay.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Fundamental particle"}
A particle that is not made of smaller particles (as far as is known). Quarks and leptons are fundamental; protons and neutrons are not.
:::

:::definition{term="Hadron"}
A particle made of quarks (and/or antiquarks), held together by the strong interaction. Hadrons are either baryons or mesons.
:::

:::definition{term="Baryon"}
A hadron made of three quarks. Protons and neutrons are baryons. (An antibaryon is made of three antiquarks.)
:::

:::definition{term="Meson"}
A hadron made of one quark and one antiquark.
:::

:::definition{term="Lepton"}
A fundamental particle that does not feel the strong interaction, such as the electron, the positron and the neutrinos.
:::

:::keyfact{title="The six quark flavours"}
| Quark | Symbol | Charge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| up | u | $+\tfrac{2}{3}e$ |
| down | d | $-\tfrac{1}{3}e$ |
| strange | s | $-\tfrac{1}{3}e$ |
| charm | c | $+\tfrac{2}{3}e$ |
| top | t | $+\tfrac{2}{3}e$ |
| bottom | b | $-\tfrac{1}{3}e$ |

Each antiquark ($\bar{\text{u}}$, $\bar{\text{d}}$, …) has the same mass as its quark and the **opposite** charge: $\bar{\text{u}}$ is $-\tfrac{2}{3}e$, $\bar{\text{d}}$ is $+\tfrac{1}{3}e$.
:::

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
Cambridge only (AS topic 11, Papers 1 and 2). Only the six flavours, their charges, baryon and meson composition, and the quark changes in beta decay are needed; strangeness and colour are not.
:::

## Building hadrons

Quarks are never found alone; they always combine so that the total charge is a whole number of $e$.

| Particle | Type | Quarks | Charge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| proton | baryon | uud | $+\tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{2}{3} - \tfrac{1}{3} = +1$ |
| neutron | baryon | udd | $+\tfrac{2}{3} - \tfrac{1}{3} - \tfrac{1}{3} = 0$ |
| antiproton | antibaryon | $\bar{\text{u}}\bar{\text{u}}\bar{\text{d}}$ | $-1$ |
| positive pion $\pi^+$ | meson | $\text{u}\bar{\text{d}}$ | $+\tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3} = +1$ |
| negative pion $\pi^-$ | meson | $\text{d}\bar{\text{u}}$ | $-\tfrac{1}{3} - \tfrac{2}{3} = -1$ |

Because protons and neutrons are made of quarks, they are **not** fundamental. A proton's mass is far greater than the sum of the masses of its three quarks; most of it comes from the energy of the strong interaction that binds them.

## Classifying particles

The particles of this course divide neatly:

- **Hadrons** (feel the strong force, made of quarks):
  - **baryons**: three quarks, e.g. proton, neutron;
  - **mesons**: quark + antiquark, e.g. pions.
- **Leptons** (fundamental, no strong force): electron, positron, electron neutrino $\nu_e$, electron antineutrino $\bar{\nu}_e$ (and heavier relatives not needed here).

A useful check: a particle that contains quarks is a hadron; a particle that is itself fundamental and is not a quark is a lepton (in this course).

## Quarks in beta decay

Beta decay is the weak interaction changing the **flavour** of a quark.

**Beta-minus decay** (a neutron becomes a proton): a down quark becomes an up quark.

$$
\text{d} \rightarrow \text{u} + {}^{\;\,0}_{-1}\text{e} + \bar{\nu}_e
$$

Charge: $-\tfrac{1}{3} = +\tfrac{2}{3} + (-1) + 0$. The neutron udd becomes the proton uud.

**Beta-plus decay** (a proton in a nucleus becomes a neutron): an up quark becomes a down quark.

$$
\text{u} \rightarrow \text{d} + {}^{\;\,0}_{+1}\text{e} + \nu_e
$$

Charge: $+\tfrac{2}{3} = -\tfrac{1}{3} + 1 + 0$. A free proton cannot decay this way, because it is lighter than a neutron; inside some nuclei the energy is available.

In both decays the electron or positron and the (anti)neutrino are leptons created in the decay; they were not inside the nucleus beforehand.

## Worked examples

:::worked-solution[Charge of a baryon]
A sigma-minus baryon consists of the quarks dds. Find its charge in coulombs.

1. Charges: $-\tfrac{1}{3} - \tfrac{1}{3} - \tfrac{1}{3} = -1$ (in units of $e$).
2. Charge $= -1.60 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{C}$.
3. It is a baryon because it contains three quarks.
:::

:::worked-solution[Identifying a meson]
A particle consists of a charm quark and an anti-down quark. Classify it and find its charge.

1. One quark and one antiquark: a **meson** (a hadron).
2. Charge: $+\tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3} = +1$, so $+e$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Checking a quark change]
Show that charge is conserved when a neutron decays to a proton.

1. Neutron udd: $\tfrac{2}{3} - \tfrac{1}{3} - \tfrac{1}{3} = 0$.
2. After: proton uud ($+1$), electron ($-1$), antineutrino (0): total $0$.
3. Charge is conserved; at quark level one d has become a u.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Antiquark charges"}
The antiquark has the **opposite** charge: $\bar{\text{d}}$ is $+\tfrac{1}{3}e$, not $-\tfrac{1}{3}e$. Most errors with mesons come from this.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling the proton fundamental"}
Protons and neutrons are made of quarks. Only quarks and leptons are fundamental in this course.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mesons with three quarks"}
Baryons have three quarks; mesons have exactly two (a quark and an antiquark).
:::

:::mistake{title="Electrons in the nucleus"}
The beta particle is created when the quark changes flavour; it was not stored in the nucleus.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers"}
Cambridge Papers 1 and 2 (AS topic 11). Multiple-choice items give a quark composition and ask for the charge or the class; structured parts ask for the quark change in beta decay with a charge check.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State* the quark composition: "uud" for the proton, "udd" for the neutron. *Show that* charge is conserved: write the charge of every particle and add. *Describe* the change in beta-minus decay: "a down quark changes into an up quark". *Classify*: baryon, meson or lepton, with the reason (three quarks, quark-antiquark, fundamental).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Six quark flavours: u, c, t ($+\tfrac{2}{3}e$) and d, s, b ($-\tfrac{1}{3}e$); antiquarks have opposite charges.
- Hadrons are made of quarks: baryons (three quarks; proton uud, neutron udd) and mesons (quark + antiquark).
- Leptons (electron, positron, neutrinos) are fundamental and do not feel the strong interaction.
- Beta-minus: d → u + e⁻ + $\bar{\nu}_e$. Beta-plus: u → d + e⁺ + $\nu_e$.
- Charge is conserved at quark level in every decay.
:::
