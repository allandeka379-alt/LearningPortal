## Introduction

A single morning in Masvingo uses most of the electromagnetic spectrum: sunlight (visible, infrared and ultraviolet) warms a solar geyser, a phone exchanges microwaves with a mast, an FM station broadcasts on radio waves, a clinic takes an X-ray image, and a hospital in Harare may treat a tumour with gamma rays. These very different radiations are all the same kind of wave, differing only in wavelength and frequency.

You need the wave equation $v = f\lambda$, the idea of a transverse wave and standard form. Photon energies ($E = hf$) belong to the quantum lessons; here the spectrum is treated as waves.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Electromagnetic wave"}
A transverse wave consisting of oscillating electric and magnetic fields at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel; it needs no medium.
:::

:::definition{term="Electromagnetic spectrum"}
The continuous range of electromagnetic waves ordered by wavelength (or frequency), from radio waves (longest wavelength, lowest frequency) to gamma rays (shortest wavelength, highest frequency).
:::

:::formula{name="Speed of electromagnetic waves"}
$$
c = f\lambda
$$

$c = 3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ is the speed of every electromagnetic wave in free space (a vacuum, and very nearly in air); $f$ in Hz; $\lambda$ in m.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Values to learn"}
- Speed in free space: $3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ for all regions.
- Visible light: about $400\ \text{nm}$ (violet) to $700\ \text{nm}$ (red).
- Order of increasing wavelength: gamma, X-rays, ultraviolet, visible, infrared, microwaves, radio waves.
:::

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
Both boards require the order, approximate wavelengths, and $c = f\lambda$ (ZIMSEC Form 5; Cambridge AS 7.5). Cambridge asks for orders of magnitude of wavelength for each region and the visible range in nm.
:::

## Properties shared by every region

All electromagnetic waves:

- are **transverse**, so they can be polarised;
- travel at $c = 3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ in free space;
- need no medium and can cross a vacuum;
- show reflection, refraction, diffraction and interference;
- carry energy, which is absorbed by matter they meet.

They differ in wavelength, so they interact with matter differently. Long waves diffract round obstacles and drive currents in aerials; short, high-frequency waves can ionise atoms.

## The regions

| Region | Approximate wavelength range / m | Typical source | Typical detector | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Radio waves | $10^{-1}$ to $10^{4}$ and longer | Oscillating currents in an aerial | Aerial and tuned circuit | Broadcasting, communication |
| Microwaves | $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-1}$ | Magnetron, phone circuits | Aerial, diode probe | Mobile phones, satellite links, radar, cooking |
| Infrared | $7 \times 10^{-7}$ to $10^{-3}$ | Warm and hot objects | Thermopile, blackened thermometer, phototransistor | Remote controls, thermal imaging, optical fibres |
| Visible | $4 \times 10^{-7}$ to $7 \times 10^{-7}$ | Very hot objects, lamps, LEDs, lasers | Eye, photographic film, light-dependent resistor | Seeing, photography |
| Ultraviolet | $10^{-8}$ to $4 \times 10^{-7}$ | The Sun, mercury-vapour lamps | Fluorescent materials, photographic film | Sterilising water, detecting forged notes |
| X-rays | $10^{-13}$ to $10^{-8}$ | Electrons striking a metal target | Photographic plate, digital detector | Medical imaging, airport security |
| Gamma rays | $10^{-16}$ to $10^{-11}$ | Radioactive nuclei | Geiger-Müller tube | Radiotherapy, sterilising equipment |

The boundaries are not sharp: the regions overlap. X-rays and gamma rays of the same wavelength are identical; they are named by their **origin** (X-rays from electrons decelerating or changing energy level, gamma rays from nuclei). Only the orders of magnitude need to be learnt.

Moving down the table, the wavelength falls and the frequency rises. Ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are **ionising**: they can damage living cells, which is why the Sun's ultraviolet causes sunburn and why X-ray workers stand behind shielding.

## Calculations with c = fλ

:::worked-solution[Frequency of a microwave]
A school microwave transmitter emits waves of wavelength $3.0\ \text{cm}$. Find the frequency.

1. Convert: $\lambda = 3.0\ \text{cm} = 0.030\ \text{m}$.
2. $f = \dfrac{c}{\lambda} = \dfrac{3.00 \times 10^{8}}{0.030} = 1.00 \times 10^{10}\ \text{Hz} = 10\ \text{GHz}$.
3. The wavelength lies between $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-1}\ \text{m}$, confirming that these are microwaves.
:::

:::worked-solution[Wavelength of an FM radio station]
A Harare FM station broadcasts at $94.5\ \text{MHz}$. Find the wavelength.

1. $f = 94.5 \times 10^{6}\ \text{Hz}$.
2. $\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f} = \dfrac{3.00 \times 10^{8}}{94.5 \times 10^{6}} = 3.17\ \text{m}$.
3. An aerial for this station is typically about half this length, around $1.6\ \text{m}$, so that it resonates with the wave.
:::

:::worked-solution[Frequency range of visible light]
1. Violet end: $f = \dfrac{3.00 \times 10^{8}}{400 \times 10^{-9}} = 7.50 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$.
2. Red end: $f = \dfrac{3.00 \times 10^{8}}{700 \times 10^{-9}} = 4.29 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$.
3. Visible light occupies less than one factor of two in frequency: a tiny part of the whole spectrum, which spans more than twenty powers of ten.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Different speeds for different regions"}
In a vacuum every electromagnetic wave travels at $3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. Gamma rays are not faster than radio waves.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting prefixes"}
nm is $10^{-9}\ \text{m}$, µm is $10^{-6}\ \text{m}$, MHz is $10^{6}\ \text{Hz}$, GHz is $10^{9}\ \text{Hz}$. Convert before using $c = f\lambda$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling sound or ultrasound electromagnetic"}
Sound and ultrasound are longitudinal mechanical waves; they are not part of the electromagnetic spectrum.
:::

:::mistake{title="Order of the regions"}
Infrared lies between visible and microwaves; ultraviolet lies between visible and X-rays. A reliable check is that ultraviolet is "beyond violet" (shorter wavelength) and infrared is "below red" (lower frequency).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers"}
A common Paper 1 multiple-choice topic for both boards, and the first part of longer questions in ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Cambridge Paper 2.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*State* a property common to all electromagnetic waves: transverse, speed $c$ in a vacuum, no medium needed. *Suggest* a wavelength for a region: one order of magnitude within the table is accepted, e.g. $10^{-10}\ \text{m}$ for X-rays. *Calculate*: convert prefixes first and give the answer in standard form with a unit.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Electromagnetic waves are transverse, need no medium and travel at $3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ in free space.
- Order of increasing wavelength: gamma, X-ray, ultraviolet, visible, infrared, microwave, radio.
- Visible light spans about 400 nm to 700 nm.
- Each region has typical sources, detectors and uses; short-wavelength regions are ionising.
- Use $c = f\lambda$ with SI units after converting prefixes.
:::
