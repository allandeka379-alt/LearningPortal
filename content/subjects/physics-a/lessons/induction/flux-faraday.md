## Introduction

The electricity that lights Harare and Bulawayo is generated by spinning magnets inside coils: water falling through the turbines at Kariba and steam at Hwange turn generators in which a changing magnetic field drives current through kilometres of copper. Michael Faraday discovered the principle in 1831: an e.m.f. is induced whenever the magnetic field "linked" with a circuit changes. Transformers, generators, induction cookers, the pick-ups on an electric guitar and the card readers in shops all depend on it.

This lesson defines **magnetic flux** and **flux linkage**, states **Faraday's law**, and uses it for rods moving through fields and coils whose flux changes. The direction of the induced e.m.f. (Lenz's law) and the main applications follow in the next lesson.

You should already know magnetic flux density $B$ and the tesla, $F = BIL$, and that a current is a flow of charge driven by an e.m.f.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Magnetic flux"}
The magnetic flux through an area is the product of the area and the component of the magnetic flux density perpendicular to that area.
:::

:::definition{term="Weber"}
One weber is the magnetic flux through an area of 1 m² placed at right angles to a uniform magnetic field of flux density 1 T. $1\ \text{Wb} = 1\ \text{T m}^2$.
:::

:::definition{term="Magnetic flux linkage"}
The product of the magnetic flux through a coil and the number of turns of the coil, $N\Phi$.
:::

:::definition{term="Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction"}
The magnitude of the induced e.m.f. in a circuit is equal to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage with the circuit.
:::

:::formula{name="Magnetic flux"}
$$
\Phi = BA\cos\theta
$$

$\Phi$ flux in Wb; $B$ flux density in T; $A$ area in m²; $\theta$ angle between the field and the **normal** to the area. When the field is perpendicular to the area ($\theta = 0$), $\Phi = BA$.
:::

:::formula{name="Faraday's law"}
$$
E = \frac{\Delta(N\Phi)}{\Delta t}
$$

$E$ induced e.m.f. in V; $N\Phi$ flux linkage in Wb (sometimes written Wb-turns); $t$ in s. With Lenz's law the sign is negative: $E = -\Delta(N\Phi)/\Delta t$.
:::

:::formula{name="Rod moving across a field"}
$$
E = BLv
$$

A straight conductor of length $L$ moving at speed $v$ at right angles to both its length and a uniform field $B$.
:::

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
Both boards: ZIMSEC Form 6 (electromagnetic induction) and Cambridge A Level (topic 20, Paper 4). Both need flux, flux linkage, Faraday's law, $E = BLv$ and simple experiments.
:::

## Flux and flux linkage

Think of flux as "how much field passes through" a loop. Field lines crossing the loop at right angles count fully; lines skimming along its plane do not count at all.

- Tilt a coil so that its normal makes an angle $\theta$ with the field: the effective area facing the field is $A\cos\theta$, so $\Phi = BA\cos\theta$.
- With the plane of the coil parallel to the field, $\theta = 90^\circ$ and $\Phi = 0$.
- A coil of $N$ turns has the same flux through each turn, so the flux **linkage** is $N\Phi$. The induced e.m.f. in each turn adds in series, which is why coils have many turns.

## Ways to change the flux linkage

Faraday's law says an e.m.f. is induced whenever $N\Phi = NBA\cos\theta$ changes. So we can:

1. change $B$: move a magnet towards or away from a coil, or switch a current on or off in a nearby coil (a transformer);
2. change $A$: move a rod along rails so the circuit area grows, or push a coil into or out of a field;
3. change $\theta$: rotate a coil in a field (a generator).

### Experiment: factors affecting the induced e.m.f.

Connect a coil to a sensitive centre-zero meter (or a data logger) and move a bar magnet in and out.

- No relative motion: no reading, however strong the magnet.
- Faster motion: larger deflection, for a shorter time.
- Stronger magnet or more turns: larger deflection.
- Reversing the motion or the pole: the deflection reverses.

These show that the e.m.f. depends on the **rate** of change of flux linkage, not on the flux itself.

## The moving rod: deriving E = BLv

A rod of length $L$ slides at speed $v$ along two parallel rails in a field $B$ perpendicular to the plane of the rails. In time $\Delta t$ it moves $v\Delta t$, sweeping out an area $\Delta A = Lv\Delta t$. The change in flux is $\Delta\Phi = B\Delta A = BLv\Delta t$, so

$$
E = \frac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t} = BLv
$$

The same answer follows from the force on the free electrons in the rod: each feels $Bev$ along the rod, pushing charge to one end until the electric field balances it.

## Graph: flux linkage against time

A $500$-turn coil of area $4.0 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2$ sits in a field that rises steadily, stays constant and then falls:

| $t\,/\,\text{s}$ | $N\Phi\,/\,10^{-2}\ \text{Wb}$ |
| --- | --- |
| 0.0 | 0.0 |
| 0.1 | 1.0 |
| 0.2 | 2.0 |
| 0.3 | 2.0 |
| 0.4 | 2.0 |
| 0.5 | 1.0 |
| 0.6 | 0.0 |

The e.m.f. is the **gradient** of the $N\Phi$-$t$ graph. From 0 to 0.2 s the gradient is $0.10\ \text{V}$; from 0.2 to 0.4 s it is zero (large flux, but no e.m.f.); from 0.4 to 0.6 s it is $0.10\ \text{V}$ in the opposite direction. The **area** under an $E$-$t$ graph equals the total change in flux linkage.

## Worked examples

:::worked-solution[Flux and flux linkage at an angle]
A rectangular coil of $200$ turns measures $4.0\ \text{cm}$ by $5.0\ \text{cm}$. Its normal is at $30^\circ$ to a uniform field of $0.12\ \text{T}$. Find the flux through it and the flux linkage.

1. $A = 0.040 \times 0.050 = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2$.
2. $\Phi = BA\cos\theta = 0.12 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-3} \times \cos 30^\circ = 2.08 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{Wb}$.
3. $N\Phi = 200 \times 2.08 \times 10^{-4} = 4.16 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{Wb}$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Rod on rails]
A metal rod $0.50\ \text{m}$ long slides at $4.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ along rails at right angles to a field of $0.060\ \text{T}$. Find the induced e.m.f.

1. $E = BLv = 0.060 \times 0.50 \times 4.0$.
2. $E = 0.12\ \text{V}$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Coil pulled out of a field]
A coil of $150$ turns and area $3.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2$ lies at right angles to a field of $0.40\ \text{T}$. It is pulled completely out of the field in $0.25\ \text{s}$. Find the average induced e.m.f.

1. Initial flux linkage: $N\Phi = 150 \times 0.40 \times 3.0 \times 10^{-3} = 0.180\ \text{Wb}$; final: zero.
2. $E = \dfrac{\Delta(N\Phi)}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{0.180}{0.25}$.
3. $E = 0.720\ \text{V}$ (an average: the actual e.m.f. depends on how the flux changes during the motion).
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Flux versus rate of change"}
A large, steady flux induces nothing. Only a **change** of flux linkage induces an e.m.f.
:::

:::mistake{title="Angle measured from the plane"}
In $\Phi = BA\cos\theta$, $\theta$ is between the field and the **normal**. If an angle is given between the field and the plane of the coil, use $\sin$ of that angle.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting N"}
The e.m.f. in a coil is $N$ times the e.m.f. in one turn: use flux **linkage** $N\Phi$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Units"}
Convert cm² to m² ($1\ \text{cm}^2 = 10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2$) and mT to T before calculating flux.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Which papers"}
ZIMSEC Papers 1-3 (Form 6) and Cambridge Paper 4. Expect definitions (flux, weber, Faraday's law), calculations with $N\Phi$ and $BLv$, and reading e.m.f. from the gradient of a flux-linkage graph.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*Define* magnetic flux: "product of area and the component of flux density perpendicular to the area". *State* Faraday's law: e.m.f. equal (proportional) to the **rate of change** of flux linkage. *Show that* $E = BLv$: area swept per second times $B$. *Describe* an experiment: magnet and coil with a centre-zero meter, varying speed, strength and turns.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Flux $\Phi = BA\cos\theta$ in webers ($1\ \text{Wb} = 1\ \text{T m}^2$); flux linkage is $N\Phi$.
- Faraday's law: induced e.m.f. equals the rate of change of flux linkage, $E = \Delta(N\Phi)/\Delta t$.
- Flux linkage can change through $B$, the area, or the angle.
- A rod moving across a field: $E = BLv$.
- The e.m.f. is the gradient of a flux-linkage against time graph; the area under an $E$-$t$ graph is the change in flux linkage.
:::
