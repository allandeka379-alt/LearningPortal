## Introduction

Groundnuts, sunflower seeds and soya beans are grown in Zimbabwe for their oil; the hump of a camel and the blubber of a whale are stores of fat; every cell is wrapped in a membrane built from phospholipids. Lipids are a varied group, but the two you must know in detail, triglycerides and phospholipids, are built on the same plan: glycerol joined to fatty acids.

You should know the emulsion test for lipids and what condensation and hydrolysis reactions are from the carbohydrates lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Lipid"}
A member of a diverse group of organic molecules containing carbon, hydrogen and oxygen (with relatively little oxygen) that are insoluble in water but soluble in organic solvents such as ethanol.
:::

:::definition{term="Triglyceride"}
A lipid formed from one glycerol molecule and three fatty acids joined by ester bonds.
:::

:::definition{term="Ester bond"}
The covalent bond formed by condensation between the –OH group of glycerol and the –COOH group of a fatty acid.
:::

:::definition{term="Saturated fatty acid"}
A fatty acid whose hydrocarbon chain has only single C–C bonds, so it carries the maximum number of hydrogen atoms. An **unsaturated** fatty acid has one (monounsaturated) or more (polyunsaturated) C=C double bonds.
:::

:::definition{term="Phospholipid"}
A lipid in which one of the three fatty acids of a triglyceride is replaced by a phosphate-containing group, giving a hydrophilic head and two hydrophobic tails.
:::

## Structure of a triglyceride

**Glycerol** is a three-carbon alcohol with an –OH group on each carbon. A **fatty acid** has a carboxyl group (–COOH) at one end of a hydrocarbon chain, typically 14–22 carbons long.

1. The –COOH of a fatty acid reacts with one –OH of glycerol.
2. A molecule of water is removed (condensation) and an **ester bond** forms.
3. This happens three times, so one triglyceride forms three ester bonds and releases three molecules of water.
4. Hydrolysis by lipase enzymes (or by heating with acid or alkali) adds three water molecules and breaks the ester bonds again, releasing glycerol and three fatty acids.

### Saturated and unsaturated fatty acids

A C=C double bond puts a **kink** in the hydrocarbon chain. Kinked chains cannot pack closely together, so triglycerides with many unsaturated fatty acids have lower melting points and are **oils** (liquid at room temperature), as in groundnut and sunflower oil. Saturated chains are straight and pack tightly, so triglycerides rich in them, such as many animal fats, are solid at room temperature.

:::worked-solution[Relative molecular mass of a triglyceride]
1. Glycerol is $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8\text{O}_3$: $M_r = 36 + 8 + 48 = 92$.
2. Stearic acid, a saturated fatty acid, is $\text{C}_{18}\text{H}_{36}\text{O}_2$: $M_r = 216 + 36 + 32 = 284$.
3. Glycerol + three stearic acids $= 92 + 3 \times 284 = 944$.
4. Three ester bonds remove three water molecules: $3 \times 18 = 54$.
5. $M_r$ of the triglyceride $= 944 - 54 = 890$, which matches the formula $\text{C}_{57}\text{H}_{110}\text{O}_6$.
:::

## How triglyceride properties suit their functions

Triglycerides are **non-polar** and **hydrophobic**: their long hydrocarbon chains have no charges and cannot form hydrogen bonds with water.

| Property | Function it suits |
|---|---|
| Very many C–H bonds and little oxygen; about 37–39 kJ/g released in respiration, compared with about 16–17 kJ/g for carbohydrate | Compact **energy store**: more than twice the energy per gram (seeds, adipose tissue) |
| Insoluble in water | Stored without affecting the water potential of cells; form droplets in cytoplasm |
| Poor conductor of heat | **Insulation** under the skin of mammals, especially in cold environments (blubber of whales and seals) |
| Less dense than water | **Buoyancy** in aquatic animals |
| Soft, can absorb impact | **Protection** of delicate organs such as the kidneys |
| Oxidation produces much water | **Metabolic water**: important for desert animals such as camels, and for embryos inside eggs |
| Electrical insulation | The myelin sheath around many nerve axons is rich in lipid |

:::example{title="Comparing energy stores"}
A seed stores 30 g of lipid. Using about 38 kJ/g for lipid and 17 kJ/g for carbohydrate:

- Energy in the lipid $\approx 30 \times 38 = 1140$ kJ.
- Mass of starch needed for the same energy $\approx 1140 \div 17 = 67$ g.

The lipid store weighs less than half as much as the equivalent starch, which helps small, wind-dispersed seeds, and helps animals that must fly or move quickly to carry their reserves. Starch, however, can be hydrolysed more quickly and its glucose can be respired anaerobically, so plants and animals use both.
:::

## Phospholipids

In a phospholipid, glycerol is joined to **two** fatty acids and one **phosphate group** (often with another small polar group attached).

- The **phosphate head** is charged and **hydrophilic**: it is attracted to water.
- The two **fatty acid tails** are non-polar and **hydrophobic**: they are repelled by water.

A molecule with a hydrophilic part and a hydrophobic part is described as amphipathic. In water, phospholipids arrange themselves so that the heads face the water and the tails are hidden from it. On a water surface they form a single layer (a monolayer) with the tails pointing into the air. Surrounded by water, they form a **bilayer**: two layers with heads facing outwards towards the water on each side and tails facing inwards. The hydrophobic core of the bilayer is a barrier to ions and polar molecules, which is the basis of the cell surface membrane (described in the fluid mosaic lesson).

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling triglycerides polymers"}
A triglyceride is not a polymer: it is made of one glycerol and three fatty acids, which are not repeating monomer units.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying lipids 'contain more energy because they are bigger'"}
The reason is chemical: lipids have a higher proportion of C–H bonds and less oxygen per gram, so more energy is released per gram when they are respired.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying phospholipid heads are hydrophobic"}
Heads (phosphate) are hydrophilic; tails (fatty acids) are hydrophobic.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the third water molecule"}
Forming one triglyceride releases three water molecules, one per ester bond.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A triglyceride is glycerol + three fatty acids joined by ester bonds formed in condensation reactions (three water molecules released).
- Saturated fatty acids have no C=C bonds; unsaturated ones have C=C bonds that kink the chain and lower the melting point.
- Triglycerides are non-polar and insoluble, store about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrate, and insulate, protect, give buoyancy and release metabolic water.
- Phospholipids have a hydrophilic phosphate head and two hydrophobic fatty acid tails.
- In water, phospholipids form a bilayer with heads outwards and tails inwards, the basis of cell membranes.
:::
