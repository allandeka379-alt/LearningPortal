## Introduction

Cut a garlic clove or an onion and stand it in water on a windowsill in Bulawayo, and within a few days white roots appear. Each root grows because cells just behind its tip divide again and again by **mitosis**. Squash one of those root tips on a slide, stain it, and you can see chromosomes caught at every stage of division: one of the classic practicals in both the ZIMSEC and Cambridge papers.

Mitosis is nuclear division that produces two nuclei with **identical** chromosomes, each identical to the parent nucleus. You should already know the structure of a chromosome and the phases of the cell cycle (G1, S, G2), because mitosis begins with chromosomes that were replicated in S phase.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Mitosis"}
Nuclear division that produces two genetically identical daughter nuclei, each with the same number of chromosomes as the parent nucleus.
:::

:::definition{term="Spindle"}
A structure of protein microtubules that forms between the poles of a dividing cell, attaches to the centromeres of chromosomes and moves the chromatids apart.
:::

:::definition{term="Cytokinesis"}
Division of the cytoplasm after nuclear division, producing two separate daughter cells.
:::

:::definition{term="Mitotic index"}
The proportion of cells in a sample that are in mitosis (visible prophase to telophase): number of cells in mitosis ÷ total number of cells.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Order of the stages"}
**P**rophase → **M**etaphase → **A**naphase → **T**elophase ("PMAT"), followed by cytokinesis. Mitosis is continuous; the stages are names for convenient points in it.
:::

## The stages of mitosis

The cell enters mitosis with 2n chromosomes, each made of two sister chromatids.

### Prophase

1. Chromosomes **condense** (supercoil), becoming shorter and thicker; each is visibly two chromatids joined at a centromere.
2. The nucleolus disappears.
3. In animal cells the two pairs of **centrioles** (replicated in interphase) move to opposite poles; microtubules radiate from them and the **spindle** begins to form. Plant cells have no centrioles but still form a spindle.
4. At the end of prophase the **nuclear envelope breaks down** into small vesicles.

### Metaphase

1. Spindle microtubules attach to each centromere (at the kinetochores), one from each pole.
2. Chromosomes are moved so their centromeres line up across the **equator** of the spindle (the metaphase plate).

### Anaphase

1. The centromeres divide, separating each pair of sister chromatids.
2. Spindle microtubules shorten, pulling the chromatids (now called chromosomes) to **opposite poles**, centromere first, so the arms trail behind in a V shape.
3. Each pole receives one copy of every chromosome. Anaphase is usually the shortest stage.

### Telophase

1. The chromosomes reach the poles and begin to **uncoil** (decondense).
2. A **nuclear envelope reforms** around each set; nucleoli reappear.
3. The spindle breaks down.

### Cytokinesis

- **Animal cells:** a ring of actin filaments under the cell surface membrane contracts at the equator, forming a **cleavage furrow** that pinches the cell in two.
- **Plant cells:** a rigid wall prevents furrowing. Vesicles from the Golgi body gather at the equator and fuse to form a **cell plate**, which grows outwards to meet the existing walls; new cellulose wall is laid down within it.

| Structure | Prophase | Metaphase | Anaphase | Telophase |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chromosomes | Condense; two chromatids visible | Line up on the equator | Chromatids pulled to poles | Uncoil at the poles |
| Nuclear envelope | Breaks down | Absent | Absent | Reforms around each set |
| Spindle | Forms | Attached to centromeres | Microtubules shorten | Breaks down |
| Centrioles (animals) | Move to the poles | At the poles | At the poles | One pair in each new cell |

## Identifying stages in a root tip squash

The tip of a root (the last 1-2 cm) is treated with warm dilute hydrochloric acid to break down the middle lamella so cells separate, stained with a DNA stain such as acetic orcein or toluidine blue, then squashed under a coverslip so cells spread into a single layer.

What to look for at high power:

- **Interphase:** a clear, round nucleus with a uniform, granular stain and one or more nucleoli; no separate chromosomes.
- **Prophase:** thread-like chromosomes visible inside the nucleus area.
- **Metaphase:** chromosomes in a line across the middle of the cell.
- **Anaphase:** two groups of V-shaped chromosomes moving apart.
- **Telophase:** two dense groups at opposite ends; a cell plate may be visible between them.

Safety: hydrochloric acid is an irritant, so wear eye protection; take care with the hot water bath and with the scalpel. Risk: low.

:::worked-solution[Calculating the mitotic index]
In a field of view of a root tip squash, Farai counted 200 cells: 164 in interphase, 18 in prophase, 7 in metaphase, 4 in anaphase and 7 in telophase.
1. Cells in mitosis $= 18 + 7 + 4 + 7 = 36$.
2. Mitotic index $= 36 \div 200 = 0.18$.
3. As a percentage, $0.18 \times 100 = 18.0\%$.
4. A high mitotic index means a large proportion of cells are dividing: the sample was taken from the zone of cell division just behind the root cap.
:::

:::worked-solution[Estimating the duration of each stage]
Assume the cell cycle in these root tip cells takes 22 hours (1320 minutes) and that cells divide independently.
1. Duration of a stage $= \dfrac{\text{cells in that stage}}{\text{total cells}} \times \text{cycle length}$.
2. Prophase: $\dfrac{18}{200} \times 1320 = 118.8 \approx 119$ minutes.
3. Metaphase and telophase: $\dfrac{7}{200} \times 1320 = 46$ minutes each.
4. Anaphase: $\dfrac{4}{200} \times 1320 = 26$ minutes, the shortest stage.
5. The estimates are only as good as the count: with just 4 cells in anaphase, counting several fields of view would make them more reliable.
:::

:::example{title="Comparing samples"}
A squash of tissue 1 cm behind the root tip had a mitotic index of 0.02. **Explain** the difference from 0.18: cells there have stopped dividing and are elongating and differentiating, so very few are in mitosis.
:::

## Why mitosis matters

Mitosis produces genetically identical cells, which is needed for:

- **Growth** of multicellular organisms from a zygote, by increasing cell number.
- **Replacement** of cells that are lost, such as skin, gut lining and blood cells (from stem cells).
- **Repair** of damaged tissues, for example healing a cut or a broken bone.
- **Asexual reproduction**, for example new potato plants from tubers, budding in yeast, and vegetative propagation of sugar cane and cassava from cuttings, giving clones.
- Producing cells for the **immune response**: a selected lymphocyte divides by mitosis to form a clone of identical cells (clonal expansion).

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
Both boards: the four stages, cytokinesis in animals and plants, identifying stages from photomicrographs and slides, and the significance of mitosis. The root tip squash and the mitotic index are practical favourites for ZIMSEC Paper 4 and Cambridge Paper 3.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying chromosomes 'line up in pairs' at metaphase"}
In mitosis chromosomes line up **individually** on the equator. Homologous chromosomes pair only in meiosis I.
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking DNA replicates during prophase"}
DNA replicated during S phase of interphase. Prophase chromosomes already have two chromatids.
:::

:::mistake{title="Counting interphase as a stage of mitosis"}
Interphase cells are **not** in mitosis. Leave them out of the numerator of the mitotic index, but include them in the total.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving plant cells centrioles or a cleavage furrow"}
Flowering plant cells have no centrioles and divide their cytoplasm with a cell plate.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
In ZIMSEC Papers 1 and 2 and Cambridge Papers 1 and 2, *describe* answers about a stage should mention **chromosomes**, the **spindle** and the **nuclear envelope**. Use "chromatids" before anaphase and "chromosomes" after separation.
:::

:::exam-tip
In ZIMSEC Paper 4 and Cambridge Paper 3, you may be asked to identify a stage from a photomicrograph, give a reason ("chromosomes are lined up on the equator") and calculate a mitotic index. Show the counts in the working.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Mitosis produces two genetically identical nuclei: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase, then cytokinesis.
- Prophase: chromosomes condense, spindle forms, nuclear envelope breaks down. Metaphase: chromosomes on the equator. Anaphase: chromatids pulled to poles. Telophase: envelopes reform.
- Animal cells divide by a cleavage furrow; plant cells form a cell plate.
- Mitotic index = cells in mitosis ÷ total cells; the share of cells in a stage estimates its share of the cycle.
- Mitosis is for growth, replacement, repair, asexual reproduction and clonal expansion of lymphocytes.
:::
