## Introduction

Farmers in the tobacco and cotton fields of Zimbabwe are warned to wear protective clothing when spraying some insecticides, and to keep children and livestock away from treated crops. Many of these chemicals are **organophosphates**, which kill insects by jamming the junctions between their nerve cells, and can poison people in exactly the same way. To understand why, you need to know how a **synapse** works.

A synapse is the junction between two neurones (or between a neurone and an effector such as a muscle). The two cells do not touch: an electrical impulse cannot cross the gap, so the message is carried across by a chemical, a **neurotransmitter**. This lesson describes synapses that use **acetylcholine** (ACh), called **cholinergic synapses**. You should already know how an action potential is produced and how exocytosis works.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Synapse"}
A junction between two neurones, or between a neurone and an effector cell, across which information is passed by a chemical neurotransmitter.
:::

:::definition{term="Neurotransmitter"}
A chemical released from the presynaptic neurone that diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds to specific receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, changing its permeability to ions.
:::

:::definition{term="Cholinergic synapse"}
A synapse at which the neurotransmitter is acetylcholine.
:::

:::definition{term="Acetylcholinesterase"}
An enzyme in the synaptic cleft (on the postsynaptic membrane) that hydrolyses acetylcholine into choline and ethanoic acid (acetate), ending its action.
:::

:::definition{term="Summation"}
The adding together of the effects of several excitatory inputs at a postsynaptic membrane so that the threshold is reached, either from repeated impulses in one neurone (temporal summation) or from impulses arriving from several neurones at once (spatial summation).
:::

:::keyfact{title="The width of the gap"}
The synaptic cleft is about 20 nm wide. Diffusion across it takes a short but measurable time, about 0.5 ms at each synapse (the synaptic delay).
:::

## Structure of a cholinergic synapse

Described from the presynaptic side to the postsynaptic side:

| Structure | Description | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Presynaptic knob (bulb) | Swollen end of the axon | Contains the machinery for releasing transmitter |
| Mitochondria | Many, in the knob | Supply ATP for making acetylcholine and for exocytosis and ion pumping |
| Synaptic vesicles | Small membrane sacs, each containing thousands of ACh molecules | Store ACh and release it by exocytosis |
| Voltage-gated calcium ion channels | In the presynaptic membrane | Open when an action potential arrives, letting Ca²⁺ in |
| Synaptic cleft | Gap of about 20 nm | Separates the two cells; ACh diffuses across it |
| Receptor proteins | In the postsynaptic membrane; each forms a sodium ion channel | Bind ACh, which opens the channel (ligand-gated) |
| Acetylcholinesterase | Enzyme on the postsynaptic membrane, facing the cleft | Hydrolyses ACh |

## How a cholinergic synapse works

1. An **action potential arrives** at the presynaptic knob.
2. Depolarisation opens **voltage-gated calcium ion channels** in the presynaptic membrane.
3. **Calcium ions diffuse in**, because their concentration is much higher in the tissue fluid than in the cytoplasm.
4. Ca²⁺ causes synaptic vesicles to move to and **fuse with the presynaptic membrane**, releasing acetylcholine into the cleft by **exocytosis**.
5. ACh **diffuses across the cleft** and **binds to receptor proteins** on the postsynaptic membrane, whose binding sites are complementary in shape to ACh.
6. Binding **opens sodium ion channels** in the receptors. Na⁺ diffuses into the postsynaptic cell, **depolarising** the postsynaptic membrane.
7. If enough channels open for the depolarisation to reach the **threshold**, an action potential is generated in the postsynaptic neurone.
8. **Acetylcholinesterase** rapidly hydrolyses ACh into **choline and ethanoic acid (acetate)**, so the channels close and the postsynaptic membrane repolarises. This stops continuous stimulation.
9. Choline and ethanoic acid **diffuse back** into the presynaptic knob, where ACh is **resynthesised** using ATP from the mitochondria and packaged into vesicles again.
10. Calcium ions are pumped out of the presynaptic knob by active transport.

:::example{title="Why calcium matters"}
A researcher bathes a synapse in fluid without calcium ions and stimulates the presynaptic neurone. **Predict and explain:** action potentials still reach the knob, but no Ca²⁺ can enter, so vesicles do not fuse with the membrane and no acetylcholine is released. No action potential occurs in the postsynaptic neurone.
:::

## The roles of synapses

1. **One-way transmission.** Vesicles and transmitter are only in the presynaptic knob, and receptors only on the postsynaptic membrane, so impulses can cross in one direction only.
2. **Summation.** One impulse may release too little ACh to reach the threshold. Several impulses in quick succession in one neurone (**temporal summation**), or impulses arriving together from several neurones (**spatial summation**), release enough transmitter between them to reach the threshold. Low-level stimuli are filtered out unless they persist or combine.
3. **Integration.** A postsynaptic neurone may receive inputs from many neurones, some excitatory and some inhibitory (which make the inside more negative). Whether it fires depends on the sum of all of these, so synapses let the nervous system combine and process information.
4. **Divergence and convergence.** One neurone can synapse with many others, spreading a signal; or many neurones can converge on one.
5. **Protection from overstimulation.** If a synapse fires repeatedly, it may run short of transmitter (fatigue), which helps protect effectors.

## Drugs and poisons acting at synapses

Many substances affect cholinergic synapses because they resemble acetylcholine in shape or interfere with the enzyme.

| Substance | Action | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nicotine (in tobacco) | Similar in shape to ACh; binds to some ACh receptors and opens their channels | Mimics ACh, stimulating postsynaptic neurones, for example in parts of the brain |
| Organophosphate insecticides | Inhibit acetylcholinesterase | ACh is not broken down, so channels stay open and postsynaptic cells are continuously stimulated: muscles twitch and go into spasm, and breathing can fail |
| Curare-type blockers | Bind to ACh receptors without opening the channels | Block transmission; muscles are paralysed |

:::worked-solution[Synaptic delay in a reflex]
A reflex pathway includes 3 synapses, each delaying the impulse by 0.5 ms. The nerve fibres are 1.2 m long in total and conduct at 80 m s⁻¹.
1. Conduction time $= \dfrac{1.2}{80} = 0.015\ \text{s} = 15\ \text{ms}$.
2. Total synaptic delay $= 3 \times 0.5 = 1.5\ \text{ms}$.
3. Total time $= 15 + 1.5 = 16.5\ \text{ms}$.
4. Percentage of the time due to synapses $= \dfrac{1.5}{16.5} \times 100 = 9.1\%$ (to 2 s.f.).
:::

:::worked-solution[Temporal summation from data]
A postsynaptic membrane at −70 mV has a threshold of −55 mV. Each impulse arriving at the synapse depolarises it by 4 mV, and the effects add if the impulses arrive close together.
1. Depolarisation needed $= -55 - (-70) = 15$ mV.
2. Impulses needed $= 15 \div 4 = 3.75$, so 4 impulses in rapid succession are needed (3 give only 12 mV).
3. This is temporal summation; if the impulses arrive far apart, each depolarisation fades and threshold is never reached.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the impulse jumps across the synapse"}
The electrical impulse stops at the presynaptic membrane. A chemical, acetylcholine, diffuses across the cleft.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting calcium ions"}
Calcium entry is what triggers exocytosis. Answers that go straight from the action potential to vesicles fusing lose a mark.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying acetylcholine enters the postsynaptic cell"}
ACh binds to receptors on the surface; it is the sodium ions that enter.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying acetylcholinesterase is in the presynaptic knob"}
The enzyme acts in the cleft, on the postsynaptic membrane. The products are taken back into the presynaptic knob.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
*Describe the sequence of events* questions (ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Paper 3 essays, Cambridge Paper 4) reward the steps in order: action potential, Ca²⁺ channels, Ca²⁺ in, vesicles fuse, exocytosis, diffusion, binding, Na⁺ channels open, depolarisation, threshold, hydrolysis, recycling.
:::

:::exam-tip
For *suggest* questions about a drug, decide first whether it mimics ACh, blocks its receptors or blocks the enzyme, then follow the consequence for the postsynaptic membrane.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A cholinergic synapse has a presynaptic knob with vesicles of acetylcholine and mitochondria, a cleft of about 20 nm, and receptors on the postsynaptic membrane.
- An action potential opens voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels; Ca²⁺ entry causes exocytosis of ACh, which diffuses across and binds to receptors, opening Na⁺ channels.
- Acetylcholinesterase hydrolyses ACh; choline and ethanoic acid are recycled to make more ACh.
- Synapses ensure one-way transmission, allow summation and integrate information.
- Nicotine mimics ACh; organophosphates inhibit acetylcholinesterase, causing continuous stimulation.
:::
