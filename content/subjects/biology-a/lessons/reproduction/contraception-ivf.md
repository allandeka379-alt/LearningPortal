## Introduction

Clinics across Zimbabwe offer family planning methods from condoms and pills to injections and implants, and couples hold views, shaped by faith and culture, about which are acceptable. At the other end of the spectrum, some couples who want children cannot conceive, and a small number of clinics offer in vitro fertilisation (IVF). Both depend on the same biology: the hormones of the menstrual cycle, gamete formation and fertilisation.

This lesson explains the biology of each method and of IVF, then sets out the main ethical viewpoints fairly; it does not tell anyone which choices to make. You should already know the menstrual cycle, gametogenesis, fertilisation and implantation.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Contraception"}
The deliberate prevention of pregnancy, by preventing ovulation, fertilisation or implantation.
:::

:::definition{term="In vitro fertilisation (IVF)"}
Fertilisation of eggs by sperm outside the body in a laboratory dish, followed by transfer of one or more resulting embryos into the uterus.
:::

:::definition{term="Abortion"}
The ending of a pregnancy so that the embryo or fetus does not survive. A **spontaneous** abortion (miscarriage) happens naturally; an **induced** abortion is brought about by medical or surgical means.
:::

:::keyfact{title="How effective is a method?"}
Effectiveness is usually given as the number of pregnancies per 100 women using the method for one year. **Typical use** (as people really use it, including missed pills or late injections) gives more pregnancies than **perfect use**. Methods that do not depend on the user remembering, such as implants, intrauterine devices and sterilisation, are the most effective in typical use.
:::

## How methods of contraception work

**1. Barrier methods**

- **Male condom:** a thin latex or polyurethane sheath over the erect penis that collects semen, so sperm cannot enter the vagina. **Female condom:** a sheath placed inside the vagina.
- **Diaphragm or cap:** a dome placed over the cervix, used with a spermicide.
- Condoms are the only methods that also greatly reduce the transmission of **sexually transmitted infections, including HIV**. In typical use they prevent pregnancy less reliably than hormonal methods.

**2. Hormonal methods** contain synthetic oestrogen and/or **progestogens** (synthetic progesterone).

- **Combined pill** (oestrogen + progestogen), taken daily: the hormones **inhibit FSH and LH secretion by negative feedback**, so follicles do not mature and there is **no LH surge**, so **no ovulation**. The progestogen also thickens cervical mucus (sperm cannot pass) and keeps the endometrium thin.
- **Progestogen-only pill:** mainly **thickens cervical mucus**; some types also stop ovulation. It must be taken at the same time each day.
- **Injections** (a progestogen given about every three months) and **implants** (a small rod under the skin of the arm releasing progestogen for three to five years): stop ovulation and thicken mucus. They do not depend on daily action, so they are very effective in typical use.
- **Emergency contraception:** high-dose progestogen pills taken soon after unprotected sex delay or prevent ovulation.

**3. Intrauterine devices**

- **Copper IUD:** a small device placed in the uterus. Copper ions are **toxic to sperm and eggs**, reducing fertilisation, and the device makes the endometrium unsuitable for **implantation**. It can stay in place for many years.
- **Hormonal intrauterine system:** releases progestogen locally, thickening mucus and thinning the endometrium.

**4. Sterilisation** (permanent)

- **Vasectomy:** each **sperm duct (vas deferens)** is cut and tied, so semen contains no sperm. Testosterone secretion and ejaculation are unaffected.
- **Tubal ligation:** each **oviduct** is cut, tied or blocked, so eggs and sperm cannot meet.

**5. Natural methods**

- **Fertility awareness:** avoiding intercourse around ovulation, identified by counting days, by the rise of about 0.2-0.5 °C in body temperature after ovulation (caused by progesterone), and by changes in cervical mucus. Effectiveness depends on regular cycles and careful daily recording.
- **Withdrawal** before ejaculation is unreliable, as sperm may be released earlier.
- **Lactational amenorrhoea:** frequent, exclusive breastfeeding in the first months after birth suppresses ovulation, through hormones that inhibit GnRH.

:::example{title="Comparing effectiveness"}
Hypothetical results from a study following women for one year:

| Method | Women followed for one year | Pregnancies |
|---|---|---|
| Method A (typical use) | 400 | 36 |
| Method B (typical use) | 250 | 1 |

**Calculate and compare:** method A: $\dfrac{36}{400} \times 100 = 9$ pregnancies per 100 women per year; method B: $\dfrac{1}{250} \times 100 = 0.4$. Method B is far more effective. **Suggest:** A is typical of a daily pill (doses can be missed) and B of an implant or IUD (nothing to remember). **Evaluate:** groups of different sizes and ages may differ in fertility, so a larger, matched study would be more reliable.
:::

## In vitro fertilisation (IVF)

IVF may help when the oviducts are blocked, sperm numbers are low or ovulation is irregular.

1. **Stimulating the ovaries:** the woman has daily injections of **FSH** (often after drugs that temporarily switch off her own pituitary hormones) so that **several follicles** mature at once.
2. **Final maturation:** an injection of **hCG** (which acts like LH) triggers the final maturation of the oocytes.
3. **Egg collection:** about a day and a half later, before ovulation would occur, oocytes are collected from the follicles with a fine needle guided by **ultrasound**.
4. **Sperm preparation:** a semen sample is washed and the most active sperm selected (and capacitated in the culture medium).
5. **Fertilisation in vitro:** each oocyte is mixed with sperm in a dish of culture medium, or a single sperm is injected directly into it (**intracytoplasmic sperm injection**, ICSI) when sperm are few or poorly motile.
6. **Culture:** zygotes are kept at body temperature and checked as they divide over 2-5 days.
7. **Embryo transfer:** one or two embryos are placed into the uterus through the cervix using a thin tube. The woman is given **progesterone** to maintain the endometrium. Spare embryos may be frozen.
8. **Pregnancy test** for hCG about two weeks later.

:::worked-solution[Following an IVF cycle]
In one treatment cycle, 12 oocytes were collected, 9 were fertilised, and 6 developed into embryos suitable for transfer.
1. Fertilisation rate $= \dfrac{9}{12} \times 100 = 75\%$.
2. Embryos as a percentage of fertilised oocytes $= \dfrac{6}{9} \times 100 = 66.7\%$ (3 s.f.).
3. Transferring only one embryo avoids the higher risks of twin pregnancy; the other five could be frozen for later use, which raises an ethical question about what happens to them.
:::

## Abortion: the biology

- **Spontaneous abortion (miscarriage)** is common in early pregnancy, often because of chromosomal abnormalities in the embryo.
- **Medical abortion** (early pregnancy) uses drugs: an anti-progesterone (**mifepristone**) blocks progesterone receptors, so the endometrium is no longer maintained and the embryo detaches; a **prostaglandin** (misoprostol) a day or two later makes the uterus contract and the cervix soften, expelling the contents.
- **Surgical abortion** removes the contents of the uterus, for example by **vacuum aspiration**.

Laws on abortion differ between countries and are not covered here. Unsafe abortion, by untrained people or with harmful methods, can cause severe bleeding, infection, infertility and death, and is a major public health concern worldwide.

## Ethical viewpoints

Thoughtful people disagree on these questions; a balanced answer gives the main views and their reasons.

**Contraception**

- Supporters argue that it allows couples to plan and space births, which benefits the health of mothers and children, allows education and work, and (with condoms) reduces HIV transmission.
- The Roman Catholic Church teaches that artificial contraception is not acceptable, while accepting natural methods; many other Christian, Muslim and traditional communities accept some or all methods, especially within marriage. Some cultures value large families and see children as a blessing and security in old age.

**IVF**

- Supporters stress the relief of the distress of infertility and the right to found a family.
- Concerns raised include the cost and unequal access, the health risks of ovarian stimulation and multiple pregnancy, and the moral status of **spare embryos** that are frozen, donated or discarded. Some religious groups object to fertilisation outside the body or to the use of donor gametes.

**Abortion**

- Some people, including many religious groups, hold that human life and moral status begin at fertilisation, so ending a pregnancy is morally equivalent to ending a human life.
- Others argue that moral status develops gradually, give weight to the woman's health, wellbeing and right to decide about her own body, and emphasise the dangers of unsafe abortion where safe services are unavailable.
- Many people hold intermediate positions, for example accepting abortion only when the mother's life is in danger or after rape.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the combined pill kills sperm"}
It mainly **prevents ovulation** by inhibiting FSH and LH; copper ions and spermicides affect sperm.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying vasectomy stops testosterone production or ejaculation"}
Only sperm are blocked; the interstitial cells and the glands that make semen still work.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving only one side in an ethics question"}
Examiners reward a **balanced** discussion with reasons for each view, attributed to groups, not personal opinion stated as fact.
:::

This concept appears on ZIMSEC Papers 1-3 and suits a Paper 3 essay. When asked *how a method works*, name the step it blocks (ovulation, fertilisation or implantation) and the biological mechanism (negative feedback on FSH and LH, barrier, toxic copper ions, thick mucus).
:::

:::exam-tip
In *describe IVF*, give the stages in order with the hormones used and why: FSH to mature several follicles, hCG for final maturation, progesterone to maintain the endometrium.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Barrier methods stop sperm reaching the egg; condoms also reduce transmission of HIV and other infections.
- Hormonal methods inhibit FSH and LH by negative feedback, preventing ovulation, and thicken cervical mucus; implants, injections and IUDs are most effective in typical use.
- Copper IUDs harm sperm and prevent implantation; vasectomy and tubal ligation are permanent; natural methods depend on identifying ovulation.
- IVF: FSH stimulation, hCG trigger, ultrasound-guided egg collection, fertilisation (or ICSI) in a dish, culture, embryo transfer, progesterone support.
- Abortion can be spontaneous, medical (mifepristone then a prostaglandin) or surgical; ethical views differ and should be presented in a balanced way.
:::
