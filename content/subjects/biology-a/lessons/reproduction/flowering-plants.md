## Introduction

Every maize cob harvested in Zimbabwe is the result of hundreds of separate fertilisations: each kernel is a fruit containing one seed, formed after a pollen grain landed on a silk and grew a tube down to an ovule. The starchy endosperm that we grind into mealie-meal for sadza is itself a product of fertilisation, but an unusual one: it is triploid, formed when a second male nucleus fuses with two nuclei in the ovule. This **double fertilisation** is found only in flowering plants.

This lesson follows the formation of the male and female gametes inside the flower, then the events of pollination and fertilisation. You should already know the stages of meiosis and mitosis, the meaning of haploid and diploid, and the main parts of a flower (sepals, petals, stamens with anther and filament, and carpels with stigma, style and ovary).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Pollen grain"}
The male gametophyte of a flowering plant: a haploid structure with a tough wall that contains a tube nucleus and a generative nucleus (or two male gametes).
:::

:::definition{term="Embryo sac"}
The female gametophyte inside the ovule: a haploid structure, usually with seven cells and eight nuclei, including the egg cell and two polar nuclei.
:::

:::definition{term="Pollination"}
The transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma; **self-pollination** is within the same flower or plant, **cross-pollination** is between plants.
:::

:::definition{term="Double fertilisation"}
The fusion of one male nucleus with the egg cell nucleus to form a diploid zygote, and of the second male nucleus with the two polar nuclei to form a triploid endosperm nucleus.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Ploidy in a flowering plant (2n)"}
Microspore and megaspore mother cells: **2n**. Microspores, pollen nuclei, megaspores, embryo sac nuclei, egg and male gametes: **n**. Zygote and embryo: **2n**. Endosperm: **3n**. In maize (2n = 20), the endosperm nuclei have 30 chromosomes.
:::

## Structure of the anther

A young anther, seen in transverse section, has **four pollen sacs** (two in each lobe) joined by a connective containing a vascular bundle. From the outside in, the wall of each pollen sac has:

1. **Epidermis:** a single protective layer of cells.
2. **Fibrous layer (endothecium):** cells with bands of thickening. As the anther dries, unequal shrinkage of these cells pulls the wall open, so the anther splits (**dehisces**) and releases pollen.
3. **Middle layers:** a few layers of thin cells that are crushed as the anther develops.
4. **Tapetum:** the innermost layer of large cells with dense cytoplasm, often with more than one nucleus. It **nourishes** the developing pollen and supplies materials for the pollen grain wall.

Inside each sac are many diploid **pollen mother cells** (microspore mother cells).

## Formation of pollen grains

1. Each **pollen mother cell (2n)** divides by **meiosis** to form a **tetrad** of four haploid **microspores**.
2. The microspores separate. Each develops a thick, resistant outer wall (often sculptured, which helps identify pollen) and a thinner inner wall.
3. The nucleus of each microspore divides by **mitosis** to give two haploid nuclei: a large **tube (vegetative) nucleus** and a smaller **generative nucleus** in its own small cell.
4. The **generative nucleus divides by mitosis** to form **two male gametes (male nuclei)**. In some species this happens before the pollen is released; in many it happens in the pollen tube.

## Structure of the ovule and the embryo sac

An ovule is attached to the ovary wall by a stalk. It has one or two protective layers, the **integuments**, which leave a small opening, the **micropyle**. Inside is a tissue called the **nucellus**, in which one cell enlarges to become the **megaspore mother cell (2n)**.

**Development of the embryo sac**

1. The megaspore mother cell divides by **meiosis** to form four haploid megaspores in a row.
2. **Three degenerate**; one **functional megaspore** survives and enlarges.
3. Its nucleus divides by **mitosis three times** (without cell division at first), giving **eight haploid nuclei**, four at each end.
4. One nucleus from each end moves to the centre: these are the **two polar nuclei** in a large **central cell**.
5. Cell walls form around the remaining six nuclei. At the **micropylar end** are the **egg cell** and two **synergids** (which help guide the pollen tube); at the opposite (chalazal) end are three **antipodal cells**.

The mature embryo sac therefore has **seven cells and eight nuclei**.

## Pollination in outline

Pollen is transferred by insects, birds, wind or other agents. **Insect-pollinated** flowers tend to have large, coloured petals, scent and nectar, sticky or spiky pollen and stigmas inside the flower. **Wind-pollinated** flowers, such as maize and grasses, have small, dull flowers, anthers and feathery stigmas that hang outside the flower, and large amounts of light, smooth pollen. Cross-pollination increases genetic variation; many plants have mechanisms that reduce self-pollination.

## Fertilisation

1. A compatible pollen grain on the stigma absorbs water and sugar and **germinates**: a **pollen tube** grows out through a pore in the wall.
2. The tube grows down through the style, secreting **hydrolytic enzymes** that digest a path, and is guided by chemicals towards an ovule. The tube nucleus is at its tip; the generative nucleus divides to form two male gametes if it has not already done so.
3. The tube enters the ovule through the **micropyle**, enters one of the synergids, and bursts, releasing the two male nuclei.
4. **Double fertilisation:** one male nucleus fuses with the **egg nucleus**, forming a **diploid zygote**, which develops into the **embryo**. The other fuses with the **two polar nuclei**, forming a **triploid primary endosperm nucleus**, which divides repeatedly to form the **endosperm**.
5. The ovule becomes the **seed** (integuments become the seed coat, or testa) and the ovary becomes the **fruit**.

**Significance of double fertilisation**

- The endosperm, a **food store** for the embryo, develops only if fertilisation happens, so the plant does not waste resources on unfertilised ovules.
- The endosperm grows rapidly to supply food to the developing embryo and, in cereals, to the seedling at germination.
- Endosperm from cereals such as maize, wheat and rice is the main source of food energy for much of the world's population.

:::worked-solution[Counting chromosomes and gametes]
Maize has a diploid number of 20.
1. Microspores, the tube and generative nuclei and the male gametes are haploid: **10** chromosomes each.
2. The egg and each polar nucleus are haploid: **10**.
3. Zygote $= 10 + 10 = 20$ (diploid). Endosperm nucleus $= 10 + 10 + 10 = 30$ (triploid).
4. Each pollen mother cell gives 4 pollen grains, so 500 pollen mother cells give $500 \times 4 = 2000$ pollen grains; each megaspore mother cell gives only **1** functional megaspore.
:::

:::example{title="Interpreting a pollen germination investigation"}
Pollen was placed in sucrose solutions and the number of grains that germinated (out of 50) was counted after 2 hours.

| Sucrose concentration / % | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pollen grains germinated (of 50) | 4 | 21 | 33 | 38 | 30 | 12 |
| Germination / % | 8 | 42 | 66 | 76 | 60 | 24 |

**Describe:** germination rose from 8% with no sucrose to a peak of 76% at 15%, then fell to 24% at 30%. **Explain:** sucrose provides a respiratory substrate for tube growth, as the stigma does naturally; at high concentrations the water potential of the solution is lower than that of the pollen, so water leaves by osmosis and germination is inhibited.
:::

## Observing a transverse section of an ovary

In a prepared slide of a transverse section of an ovary (for example from a lily), you can see the **ovary wall**, the **locules** (cavities), and **ovules** attached to the **placenta** by stalks, each with integuments, nucellus and, in some sections, an embryo sac. A **low-power plan diagram** shows only the outlines of these tissues in correct proportion, with clear continuous lines, no shading and ruled, labelled label lines.

:::keyfact{title="ZIMSEC and Cambridge"}
This concept is ZIMSEC only (Form 6). Cambridge does not examine reproduction in flowering plants.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying pollen grains are made by meiosis alone"}
Meiosis forms the microspores; **mitosis** then forms the tube and generative nuclei and the two male gametes.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the tube nucleus fertilises the egg"}
The **male gametes** (from the generative nucleus) fertilise; the tube nucleus only controls tube growth.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the endosperm is diploid"}
The endosperm is **triploid** (one male nucleus + two polar nuclei).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
This topic appears on ZIMSEC Papers 1-4 and suits a Paper 3 essay. *Describe* questions on gamete formation need each division named (**meiosis** or **mitosis**) and the number of cells or nuclei at each stage. Paper 4 may ask for a plan diagram of an ovary or anther section.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked for the *significance* of double fertilisation, link it to the endosperm as a food store that forms only when an embryo will develop.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The anther wall has an epidermis, a fibrous layer (opens the anther), middle layers and a nourishing tapetum around the pollen sacs.
- Pollen mother cells divide by meiosis into tetrads of microspores; mitosis gives tube and generative nuclei, and the generative nucleus divides into two male gametes.
- A megaspore mother cell divides by meiosis; one megaspore survives and three mitotic divisions give an embryo sac with egg, two synergids, three antipodals and two polar nuclei.
- In double fertilisation one male nucleus fuses with the egg (diploid zygote) and the other with both polar nuclei (triploid endosperm).
- The ovule becomes the seed and the ovary the fruit; endosperm stores food for the embryo.
:::
