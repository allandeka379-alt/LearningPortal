## Introduction

Substance abuse among young people is a concern raised by families, schools, churches and health workers across Zimbabwe. Crystal methamphetamine (often called *mutoriro* or *dombo*) and codeine-containing cough syrups (often called *bronco*) are widely reported in towns, alongside the older problems of alcohol, tobacco and cannabis. Behind the headlines is biology: each substance changes how the nervous system, liver, heart or lungs work, and repeated use changes the brain in ways that make stopping difficult.

This lesson describes those effects at the level of well-established medical fact. It is written for understanding, not to frighten or to judge. People who use drugs deserve accurate information and support, and dependence is a health condition that can be treated. You should already know the basic structure of the nervous system and synapses, the role of the liver, and the structure of the gas exchange system.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Drug"}
Any substance taken into the body that changes the way the body or mind works, for example by altering chemical reactions or nerve transmission.
:::

:::definition{term="Medicinal and recreational use"}
**Medicinal** use is taking a drug to prevent, diagnose or treat illness, at a prescribed dose. **Recreational** use is taking a drug for pleasure or to change mood, not for a medical reason.
:::

:::definition{term="Drug abuse (misuse)"}
Use of a drug in a way, amount or situation that harms the user or others, including taking medicines without a prescription or above the recommended dose.
:::

:::definition{term="Tolerance"}
The state in which repeated use of a drug produces a smaller effect, so a larger dose is needed to produce the same effect.
:::

:::definition{term="Dependence (addiction)"}
A state in which a person feels a strong need to keep taking a drug. **Physical dependence** shows as withdrawal symptoms when the drug is stopped; **psychological dependence** is a craving for its effects.
:::

:::definition{term="Withdrawal symptoms"}
Unpleasant physical and mental effects, such as shaking, sweating, anxiety, low mood and insomnia, experienced when a dependent person stops taking the drug.
:::

## How dependence develops

Many drugs of abuse increase the release, or block the removal, of the neurotransmitter **dopamine** in the brain's **reward pathway**, producing pleasure and reinforcing the behaviour. With repeated use, nerve cells adapt: for example, they reduce the number of receptors for the neurotransmitter. The same dose then has less effect (tolerance), and without the drug the brain functions below normal, causing withdrawal symptoms and craving (dependence). Young people are especially vulnerable because the brain is still developing into the early twenties.

## Alcohol (ethanol)

- **Nervous system:** ethanol is a **depressant**. It slows synaptic transmission in the brain, so reaction times increase, coordination and judgement are impaired, speech is slurred and memory is affected. Large amounts can depress breathing and cause unconsciousness and death (alcohol poisoning). Long-term heavy use can damage the brain permanently.
- **Liver:** the liver breaks down ethanol, first to ethanal (acetaldehyde), which is toxic. Heavy drinking causes **fatty liver**, then inflammation (**alcoholic hepatitis**) and finally **cirrhosis**, in which liver cells are replaced by scar tissue and the liver fails. It also increases the risk of liver cancer.
- **Other effects:** high blood pressure, heart muscle damage, inflammation of the stomach and pancreas, and cancers of the mouth, throat and oesophagus.
- **Pregnancy:** ethanol crosses the placenta. **Fetal alcohol syndrome** includes restricted growth, characteristic facial features and permanent damage to the brain, causing learning and behavioural difficulties. No safe level of drinking in pregnancy has been established.

:::worked-solution[How much ethanol is in a drink?]
A 750 cm³ bottle of beer contains 5% ethanol by volume. The density of ethanol is 0.79 g/cm³, and one standard drink contains 10 g of ethanol.
1. Volume of ethanol $= 750 \times \dfrac{5}{100} = 37.5$ cm³.
2. Mass of ethanol $= 37.5 \times 0.79 = 29.6$ g (3 s.f.).
3. Standard drinks $= 29.6 \div 10 \approx 3$.
4. One bottle is about three standard drinks, which the liver takes about three hours or more to break down, so "one drink" can be much more alcohol than people think.
:::

## Nicotine and tobacco smoke

- **Nicotine** is the addictive substance in tobacco. It mimics acetylcholine at some synapses and stimulates dopamine release, giving a short-lived feeling of reward; dependence develops quickly. It stimulates release of adrenaline, raising **heart rate and blood pressure**, and causes **vasoconstriction**. It makes platelets more likely to clump, increasing the risk of blood clots.
- **Carbon monoxide** binds to haemoglobin more strongly than oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin, so the blood carries less oxygen; it also damages the lining of arteries, contributing to **atherosclerosis** and coronary heart disease.
- **Tar** contains **carcinogens** that cause mutations in the cells lining the airways (lung, mouth and throat cancers). It also paralyses and destroys cilia and stimulates mucus secretion, leading to chronic bronchitis, and contributes to **emphysema** (breakdown of alveolar walls).
- In pregnancy, smoking reduces oxygen supply to the fetus and is linked to low birth mass.

## Cannabis

The main active substance is **THC** (tetrahydrocannabinol), which binds to cannabinoid receptors in the brain. Short-term effects include relaxation, altered perception of time, impaired **short-term memory**, attention and coordination (dangerous when driving) and increased heart rate. Regular use, especially starting in adolescence, is associated with dependence, poorer memory and learning, and an increased risk of **psychosis** (losing contact with reality) in people who are vulnerable. Smoked cannabis also damages the airways.

## Methamphetamine

Methamphetamine is a powerful **stimulant**. It causes nerve endings to release large amounts of dopamine and noradrenaline and blocks their reuptake. Users feel intense euphoria, energy and alertness and lose appetite and the need to sleep. It raises heart rate, blood pressure and body temperature, which can cause heart attacks, strokes and dangerous overheating. Repeated use rapidly causes tolerance and **strong dependence**; after the effect wears off there is exhaustion and depression. Long-term use can cause anxiety, aggression, paranoia and **psychosis**, memory problems, severe weight loss, skin sores and tooth decay.

## Misuse of codeine-containing cough syrups

**Codeine** is an **opioid** used in medicine to relieve pain and suppress coughs. The liver converts part of it into **morphine**. Drinking large amounts of cough syrup for its effects causes drowsiness, slowed reactions and euphoria. Opioids **depress the breathing centre** in the brainstem, so high doses can stop breathing, especially if combined with alcohol. Repeated use leads to tolerance and physical dependence, with withdrawal symptoms such as aches, sweating, diarrhoea and anxiety. Other ingredients in the syrup add their own effects, and large volumes of sugary syrup harm teeth.

:::example{title="Interpreting data on alcohol and reaction time"}
Hypothetical results from volunteers in a supervised study:

| Ethanol drunk / standard drinks | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean reaction time / s | 0.24 | 0.27 | 0.33 | 0.41 |

**Describe:** reaction time increases with the amount of ethanol, from 0.24 s with none to 0.41 s after 6 drinks, an increase of about 71%; the increase gets larger with each extra 2 drinks. **Explain:** ethanol is a depressant that slows synaptic transmission, so signals take longer to pass through the nervous system. **Apply:** a slower reaction means a driver travels further before braking.
:::

## Social and economic consequences

- **Individuals and families:** poor school performance, loss of jobs, debt, family conflict, neglect of children, and violence; injecting or sharing equipment risks HIV and hepatitis; impaired judgement increases unprotected sex.
- **Communities:** road traffic accidents, crime to pay for drugs, and strain on hospitals and mental health services.
- **Economic:** lost productivity, the cost of health care and policing, and money diverted from food and school fees.

## Support and treatment

Dependence is treatable. Help includes **counselling** and talking therapies, **support groups**, medically supervised **withdrawal** (detoxification) where withdrawal is dangerous, treatment of mental health conditions, and help with education, work and family relationships. Stigma makes people less likely to seek help, so a supportive, non-judgemental approach from families, schools and clinics matters. Prevention relies on accurate education, keeping young people engaged in school, sport and community activities, and controlling the sale of alcohol, tobacco and medicines.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling alcohol a stimulant"}
People may feel more confident, but ethanol **depresses** the nervous system.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing tolerance and dependence"}
Tolerance is needing a bigger dose for the same effect; dependence is needing the drug to feel normal, with withdrawal symptoms when it stops.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying nicotine causes lung cancer"}
Nicotine causes addiction and affects the heart and blood vessels; the **carcinogens in tar** cause cancer.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A drug changes how the body or mind works; abuse is use that harms the user or others.
- Repeated use causes tolerance and dependence through changes at synapses, with withdrawal symptoms on stopping.
- Alcohol is a depressant that damages the liver (fatty liver, hepatitis, cirrhosis) and causes fetal alcohol syndrome.
- Nicotine is addictive and raises heart rate and blood pressure; carbon monoxide reduces oxygen transport; tar is carcinogenic.
- Cannabis impairs memory and coordination; methamphetamine is a strong stimulant causing dependence and psychosis; misused codeine can depress breathing; support and treatment are effective.
:::
