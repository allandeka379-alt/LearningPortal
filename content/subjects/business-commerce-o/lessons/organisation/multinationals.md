## Introduction

Many familiar products in Zimbabwean shops, from soft drinks to mobile phones, are made or sold by businesses that operate in dozens of countries. Some foreign companies run mines, factories, banks or hotels in Zimbabwe, and some Zimbabwean businesses have opened branches in neighbouring countries. Businesses like these, with operations in more than one country, are **multinational companies** (MNCs), also called multinational corporations or transnational companies.

ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 3) asks you to describe their features and assess their impact on the **host** country and the **parent (home)** country. Cambridge 0264 (topic 6.2) asks why businesses become multinational and the benefits and drawbacks for the countries where they operate. Examiners almost always set a case and ask you to **evaluate** whether a government should welcome an MNC.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Multinational company (MNC)"}
A business that produces goods or services (not just sells them) in more than one country.
:::

:::definition{term="Host country"}
A country in which a multinational company sets up operations.
:::

:::definition{term="Home (parent) country"}
The country where a multinational company has its headquarters.
:::

:::definition{term="Repatriation of profits"}
Sending profits earned in the host country back to the multinational's home country.
:::

:::definition{term="Foreign direct investment (FDI)"}
Investment by a business in one country in factories, mines, offices or other productive assets in another country.
:::

## Features of multinational companies

- **Headquarters** in one country (the home country), with factories, mines, farms or offices in other countries.
- **Very large size:** large capital, many employees, high sales; some earn more revenue than the output of small countries.
- Usually **public limited companies**, with shares traded on stock exchanges.
- Produce **globally recognised brands**.
- Use **advanced technology** and specialist managers.
- Large **spending on advertising** and research and development.
- Decisions taken at headquarters affect workers and customers in many countries.

## Why businesses become multinational

1. **Lower costs:** cheaper labour, land or energy in other countries.
2. **Nearness to raw materials:** for example mining where the minerals are found.
3. **New markets:** sell to customers in other countries and grow sales when the home market is saturated.
4. **Avoid trade barriers:** producing inside a country or trading bloc avoids import tariffs and quotas.
5. **Lower transport costs:** producing close to customers.
6. **Spread risk:** a fall in demand in one country can be offset elsewhere.
7. **Government incentives** such as tax breaks or grants offered by host countries.

## Impact on the host country

### Advantages

- **Jobs** for local people, often with training in new skills.
- **Investment** (FDI) in factories, mines and infrastructure such as roads, power lines and housing.
- **Technology transfer:** local firms and workers learn new methods.
- **Exports** increase, earning foreign currency; some imports are replaced by local production.
- **Tax revenue** for the government from company profits and workers' incomes.
- **Wider choice** and often better quality for consumers.
- Business for **local suppliers**, such as transport firms and caterers.

### Disadvantages

- **Competition** can force small local firms out of business.
- **Repatriation of profits** to the home country, so much of the gain leaves the host country.
- **Exploitation** of workers (low wages, poor conditions) and of **non-renewable resources**.
- **Environmental damage:** pollution, land degradation, deforestation.
- **Skilled and senior jobs** may be given to foreign staff; local jobs may be low-skilled.
- **Footloose:** an MNC can close and move to a cheaper country, causing sudden unemployment.
- **Political influence:** a very large MNC may pressure the government over taxes or regulation.
- **Lower tax than expected:** an MNC may arrange its internal prices so that profits appear in countries with lower taxes.

:::example{title="Weighing up a new mine"}
A fictional foreign mining company plans a lithium mine near Masvingo. It promises 600 jobs, a new road and a clinic, and will export most of its output. Benefits: jobs and wages for local people, infrastructure, foreign currency from exports and tax for the government. Costs: lithium is non-renewable, land and water may be polluted, profits will largely be repatriated and senior posts may go to foreign engineers. The government might welcome the mine but require local training, environmental controls and a share of processing in the country, so that more value is added locally.
:::

## Impact on the home country

**Advantages:** profits flow back to the home country; the MNC grows, strengthening its brand; it can import cheaper products from its overseas plants.

**Disadvantages:** jobs may be lost when production moves abroad; less tax may be paid at home if production and profits move; the home economy may become dependent on imports.

## How host governments respond

Host governments try to gain the benefits while limiting the costs:

- Offer incentives such as tax holidays to attract MNCs to areas that need jobs.
- Set rules on **local employment and training**, **environmental protection** and **local processing** of raw materials.
- Encourage **joint ventures** with local businesses.
- Tax profits fairly and check the prices MNC branches charge each other.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling any exporter a multinational"}
A business that only **exports** to other countries is not an MNC. It must **produce** in more than one country.
:::

:::mistake{title="One-sided evaluations"}
Evaluate questions need benefits, drawbacks and a conclusion that depends on the case, for example "welcome the MNC if it trains local staff and controls pollution".
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up host and home countries"}
The host country **receives** the MNC's operations; the home country is where its headquarters are.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Name the stakeholder"}
Say who gains or loses: workers, local firms, consumers, the government, the environment. This turns a list into analysis.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Use 'it depends on'"}
Good conclusions identify what the judgement depends on: how many local jobs, how much profit is repatriated, whether the environment is protected.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 3) wants features, advantages and disadvantages and the impact on host and parent countries. Cambridge 0264 (topic 6.2) adds why a business becomes multinational. Business Enterprise and Skills does not examine this concept directly.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A multinational company produces in more than one country, with headquarters in its home country.
- Businesses go multinational for lower costs, raw materials, new markets, to avoid trade barriers and to spread risk.
- Host countries gain jobs, investment, technology, exports, tax and choice.
- Host countries can suffer from competition with local firms, repatriated profits, exploitation of workers and resources, pollution and footloose plants.
- Governments use incentives and rules to gain the benefits while limiting the costs.
:::
