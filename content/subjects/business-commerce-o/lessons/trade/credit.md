## Introduction

Tatenda wants a fridge for his tuck shop so that he can sell cold drinks, but he does not have the full price in cash. A furniture and appliance shop in Gweru offers him the fridge now if he pays a deposit and then a fixed amount every month. Should he accept? What happens if he cannot pay? Who owns the fridge in the meantime?

These questions are what this Form 2 Commerce topic is about. Examiners expect you to describe hire purchase and deferred payment, compare them, calculate the extra cost of buying on credit, and discuss the advantages and disadvantages for buyers and sellers.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Credit"}
An arrangement in which a buyer receives goods or services now and pays for them later.
:::

:::definition{term="Hire purchase (HP)"}
An agreement in which the buyer pays a deposit, takes the goods and pays the rest in regular instalments. The goods remain the property of the seller (or finance company) until the last instalment is paid.
:::

:::definition{term="Deferred payment (credit sale)"}
An agreement in which the buyer pays for goods in instalments, but ownership passes to the buyer as soon as the agreement is signed.
:::

:::definition{term="Credit card"}
A card that allows the holder to buy goods and services up to a credit limit and pay the card company later, with interest charged on any amount not paid by the due date.
:::

:::definition{term="Informal credit"}
Credit given without a written agreement, for example a tuck shop owner letting regular customers pay at month end.
:::

## Hire purchase

Main features:

- the buyer pays a **deposit** (often a percentage of the cash price);
- the balance is paid in **regular instalments** over an agreed period;
- the total HP price is **higher** than the cash price because it includes **interest** (a charge for credit);
- the seller (or finance house) **owns** the goods until the final instalment;
- if the buyer **defaults** (fails to pay), the seller can **repossess** the goods, subject to the law and the agreement;
- the buyer cannot sell the goods before the last instalment because they do not own them.

:::formula{name="Cost of buying on hire purchase"}
Total HP price = deposit + (instalment × number of instalments)

Extra cost (interest) = total HP price − cash price
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 1: the cost of a television on hire purchase]
A television has a cash price of US\$450. On hire purchase the buyer pays a 20% deposit and 12 monthly instalments of US\$36.

1. Deposit: 20% of US\$450 = US\$90.
2. Instalments: 12 × US\$36 = US\$432.
3. Total HP price: US\$90 + US\$432 = US\$522.
4. Extra cost of buying on HP: US\$522 − US\$450 = US\$72.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: hire purchase or credit sale for a fridge]
A fridge has a cash price of US\$600. The shop offers two credit terms:

- **Hire purchase:** 15% deposit and 10 monthly instalments of US\$58.
- **Credit sale:** no deposit and 6 monthly instalments of US\$105.

1. HP deposit: 15% of US\$600 = US\$90; instalments: 10 × US\$58 = US\$580.
2. Total HP price: US\$90 + US\$580 = US\$670, which is US\$70 more than the cash price.
3. Total credit-sale price: 6 × US\$105 = US\$630, which is US\$30 more than the cash price.
4. The credit sale costs US\$40 less and the buyer owns the fridge at once, but the monthly payments are higher (US\$105 instead of US\$58).
:::

## Deferred payment (credit sale)

Main features:

- payment is spread over a period, often shorter than HP;
- a deposit may or may not be required;
- **ownership passes immediately** to the buyer;
- if the buyer stops paying, the seller **cannot repossess** the goods but must sue for the money owed as a debt;
- usually used for lower-priced goods or for customers the seller trusts.

## Comparing hire purchase and deferred payment

| Point | Hire purchase | Deferred payment (credit sale) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership | Passes after the last instalment | Passes when the agreement is made |
| If the buyer defaults | Seller can repossess the goods | Seller must sue for the debt |
| Can the buyer sell the goods before paying in full? | No | Yes |
| Deposit | Usually required | Sometimes required |
| Typical goods | Expensive durable goods: vehicles, furniture, machinery | Cheaper goods, clothes, small appliances |

## Advantages and disadvantages

**To the buyer:**

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| Enjoys the goods before saving the full price | Pays more than the cash price (interest) |
| Payments are spread over time | Risk of losing goods (HP) if unable to pay |
| A business can buy equipment that earns money to pay the instalments | Temptation to buy more than can be afforded |
| | Long-term commitment that reduces future income |

**To the seller:**

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| More sales, as more customers can afford goods | Risk of bad debts |
| Extra income from interest | Repossessed goods are second-hand and may be damaged |
| Can repossess goods under HP | More administration: records, collecting instalments |
| | Money tied up until instalments are paid |

## How hire purchase is financed

A small retailer cannot wait a year for its money. Often a **finance house** (a finance company) pays the retailer the cash price straight away. The buyer then pays the instalments to the finance house, which owns the goods until the agreement ends and earns the interest. Banks and large retailers with their own capital may also finance HP agreements.

## Credit cards and informal credit

**Credit cards** are issued by banks. The holder buys goods up to a limit and receives a monthly statement. If the whole balance is paid by the due date, no interest is charged; otherwise interest is charged on the unpaid balance. Retailers pay a commission to the card company on card sales but gain from more customers and less cash handling.

**Informal credit** is common in small shops and markets. A tuck shop owner may keep a notebook of regular customers who pay at month end. It builds loyalty but carries a high risk of bad debts, as there is no written agreement or security.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up ownership"}
Under HP the buyer owns the goods only after the **last** instalment. Under a credit sale the buyer owns them **immediately**.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the deposit"}
The total HP price is deposit **plus** all instalments. Leaving out the deposit understates the cost.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calculating the deposit on the HP price"}
A percentage deposit is normally calculated on the **cash price** unless the question says otherwise.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Show every step"}
In calculations write the deposit, the total of instalments, the total HP price and the extra cost as separate lines. Method marks are awarded for each step.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Compare, do not describe twice"}
"Distinguish between hire purchase and deferred payment" is best answered with a point-by-point comparison: ownership, repossession, resale.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board coverage"}
This topic is in ZIMSEC Commerce. Cambridge 0264 mentions hire purchase only as a source of finance for a business.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Credit lets a buyer have goods now and pay later; it usually costs more than paying cash.
- Under hire purchase the seller owns the goods until the last instalment and can repossess them.
- Under deferred payment ownership passes at once and the seller must sue for unpaid debts.
- Total HP price = deposit + all instalments; extra cost = total HP price − cash price.
- Finance houses often fund HP; credit cards and informal credit are other common forms of credit.
:::
