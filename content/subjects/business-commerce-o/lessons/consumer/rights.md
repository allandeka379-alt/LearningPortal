## Introduction

Tendai buys a 2 kg packet of sugar at a tuck shop and finds at home that it weighs much less. Rudo buys a cheap phone charger at a market stall; it overheats on the first night. A shop in town sells tins of fish past their expiry date at the normal price. Each of these consumers has been **exploited**: they paid for something they did not get.

A **consumer** is anyone who buys or uses goods and services for their own use. Producers and traders usually know far more about their goods than consumers do, and many consumers have little time, money or knowledge to check what they buy. That is why consumers need protection.

This lesson covers ZIMSEC Commerce Form 1: the reasons for consumer protection, consumer rights and responsible consumer behaviour. Later lessons cover consumer protection bodies and the legal methods of protection.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Consumer"}
A person who buys or uses goods and services to satisfy his or her own wants, not to resell them.
:::

:::definition{term="Consumer protection"}
The measures taken by government, organisations and consumers themselves to make sure that consumers are not cheated, harmed or treated unfairly when they buy goods and services.
:::

:::definition{term="Exploitation of consumers"}
Unfair treatment of consumers by sellers, for example by giving short measures, describing goods falsely, selling unsafe goods or charging unfair prices.
:::

:::definition{term="Consumer rights"}
What every consumer is entitled to when buying goods and services, such as the right to safe goods, to accurate information and to redress.
:::

## Why consumers need protection

- **Unequal knowledge**: sellers know the quality, contents and origin of goods; consumers often cannot test them, especially technical goods such as electrical items and medicines.
- **Unequal power**: a large business or a monopoly can set prices and terms; one consumer has little bargaining power.
- **Persuasive advertising**: some advertisements exaggerate or mislead, leading people to buy goods they do not need or that do not work as claimed.
- **Health and safety**: unsafe food, medicine, electrical goods or toys can injure or kill.
- **Some consumers are vulnerable**: children, the elderly, people who cannot read labels and people in remote areas with few shops to choose from.
- **Complex contracts**: credit and hire purchase agreements contain small print that consumers may not understand.

## Ways in which consumers can be exploited

| Practice | What happens | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short weights and measures | Less quantity than paid for | A "5 kg" bag of potatoes weighs 4 kg; a tampered scale |
| False or misleading descriptions | Goods are not what the label or seller claims | "Pure honey" mixed with syrup; "genuine leather" that is plastic |
| Unsafe goods | Goods that may cause harm | Faulty electrical goods, toys with sharp parts |
| Expired or poor-quality goods | Goods past their use-by date or substandard | Expired medicines, sour milk sold as fresh |
| Overpricing | Unfair prices, hidden charges, prices raised during shortages | Charging far above the displayed price |
| Misleading advertising | Claims that are false or create a false impression | "Cures all illnesses"; "half price" when the price was never higher |
| Counterfeit goods | Fake copies of branded goods | Fake branded batteries that leak |
| Unfair credit terms | Hidden interest and charges; goods repossessed unfairly | Hire purchase with charges not explained |
| Refusal of redress | Seller refuses to replace faulty goods or refund | "No refunds, no exchanges" on faulty goods |

## Consumer rights

Consumer protection bodies and laws around the world recognise rights such as these:

- **The right to safety**: protection from goods and services that are dangerous to health or life.
- **The right to be informed**: accurate information about price, quantity, ingredients, use and risks, so that consumers can make informed choices.
- **The right to choose**: access to a variety of goods at competitive prices.
- **The right to be heard**: consumers' interests considered when government and business make decisions.
- **The right to redress**: a fair settlement of genuine complaints (repair, replacement, refund or compensation).
- **The right to consumer education**: to learn the knowledge and skills needed to be a confident consumer.
- **The right to basic needs**: access to essential goods and services such as food, water, shelter, health care and education.
- **The right to a healthy environment**: to live and work in an environment that is not harmful.

:::example{title="Applying the rights"}
Rudo's charger overheated on the first night. Her **right to safety** was broken because the product was dangerous, and her **right to redress** means she should get a refund or a safe replacement. If the charger had no maker's name or instructions, her **right to be informed** was also broken.
:::

## Responsible consumer behaviour

Rights come with responsibilities. A responsible consumer:

- **checks goods before buying**: expiry dates, weights, seals, labels and the condition of the goods;
- **compares prices and quality** in several shops before buying;
- **reads and understands** labels, instructions, guarantees and credit agreements before signing;
- **keeps receipts** and guarantee cards as proof of purchase;
- **uses goods as instructed** and does not claim for damage they caused;
- **complains in the right way**: first to the seller, calmly and with evidence, then to a consumer protection body if necessary;
- **reports** unfair or dangerous practices so other consumers are protected;
- **buys only what is needed** and avoids impulse buying caused by advertising;
- **avoids counterfeit and smuggled goods**, which may be unsafe and harm local producers;
- **cares for the environment**: disposes of packaging properly and avoids waste.

:::example{title="A responsible complaint"}
Chipo's new kettle stops working after a week. She takes it back to the shop with her receipt and the guarantee card, explains the fault and asks for a replacement. The shop refuses. She writes down the date and the name of the person she spoke to, and takes her complaint to a consumer protection body with copies of her receipt and guarantee.
:::

## Benefits of consumer protection to business

Consumer protection is not only good for consumers. Honest businesses benefit when dishonest competitors are stopped, customers are confident to buy, and trust in the market grows.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling a trader who resells goods a consumer"}
A tuck-shop owner buying stock to resell is a trader, not a consumer of that stock. A consumer buys for his or her own use.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing rights without responsibilities"}
Questions on "responsible consumer behaviour" want what the consumer must **do**: check, compare, keep receipts, complain properly. Do not answer with a list of rights.
:::

:::mistake{title="Vague exploitation points"}
"Cheating customers" is too vague. Name the practice (short measures, false description, expired goods) and give an example.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Use examples"}
Each way of exploiting consumers earns more when you add a short example. Everyday Zimbabwean examples (tuck shops, market stalls, buses, mobile phone accessories) are ideal.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
*Identify* or *state* a right: a phrase. *Explain* a right: what it means and why it matters. *Discuss* the need for protection: reasons, examples and a conclusion.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Consumers need protection because sellers have more knowledge and power, advertising can mislead and some goods are unsafe.
- Consumers are exploited through short measures, false descriptions, unsafe or expired goods, overpricing, counterfeits and unfair credit.
- Rights include safety, information, choice, being heard, redress, education, basic needs and a healthy environment.
- Responsible consumers check goods, compare prices, read labels and contracts, keep receipts and complain properly.
- Protection also helps honest businesses by building trust.
:::
