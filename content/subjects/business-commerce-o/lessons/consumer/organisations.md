## Introduction

When one consumer complains about a faulty fridge, a shop may ignore her. When a consumer organisation takes up hundreds of similar complaints, publishes the results of tests and talks to government, businesses listen. Consumer protection bodies give individual consumers a stronger voice.

In ZIMSEC Commerce Form 2 you study **consumer protection boards** such as the **Consumer Council of Zimbabwe (CCZ)** and the **Standards Association of Zimbabwe (SAZ)**: what they do and how consumers use them. Questions usually ask you to identify the bodies, explain their roles and describe how a consumer would use them.

:::note{title="Names change"}
Laws and institutions are reorganised from time to time. This lesson describes the roles of the bodies named in the syllabus. Check the current name and contact details of the national consumer protection body with your teacher before you use them in real life.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Consumer protection body"}
An organisation whose purpose is to protect and promote the interests of consumers, for example by educating them, handling complaints and influencing laws.
:::

:::definition{term="Consumer Council of Zimbabwe (CCZ)"}
The national body named in the syllabus that represents consumers: it educates them, handles complaints, monitors prices and quality, and speaks for consumers to government and business.
:::

:::definition{term="Standards Association of Zimbabwe (SAZ)"}
The national standards body: it develops standards (agreed specifications for products and services), tests products and certifies those that meet a standard.
:::

:::definition{term="Standard"}
An agreed set of requirements that a product, service or process must meet, for example for safety, quality, size or composition.
:::

## Roles of a national consumer council

- **Consumer education**: teaching consumers their rights and responsibilities through radio, newspapers, social media, school clubs and workshops.
- **Handling complaints**: receiving complaints from consumers, investigating them and negotiating with the business for a refund, repair or replacement.
- **Price and quality monitoring**: carrying out surveys of the prices of basic goods in shops and publishing them, so consumers can compare.
- **Research and testing**: comparing products and publishing findings on value, quality and safety.
- **Representing consumers**: speaking for consumers to government, for example when laws, prices of essential services or standards are being decided.
- **Lobbying for laws**: pressing for new or stronger consumer protection laws and better enforcement.
- **Warning the public**: alerting consumers to dangerous products, scams and unfair practices.
- **Working with other bodies**: referring cases to the police, health inspectors, standards bodies or courts where necessary.

:::example{title="Using the council to get redress"}
Farai buys a second-hand fridge with a three-month guarantee from a dealer in Gweru. It stops working after five weeks and the dealer refuses to repair it. Farai takes his receipt and guarantee to the consumer council's office. An officer contacts the dealer and reminds him of the guarantee. The dealer repairs the fridge. Farai did not need a lawyer or a court case.
:::

## Roles of the Standards Association of Zimbabwe

- **Developing standards** for products and services (for example for building materials, food, electrical goods and packaging), together with manufacturers, government and consumer representatives.
- **Testing products** in its laboratories to check whether they meet the standard.
- **Certification**: allowing products that meet a standard to carry a **mark of conformity** (the standards mark), which tells consumers that the product has been tested.
- **Certifying management systems**, for example quality management systems in factories.
- **Training and advice** to businesses on how to meet standards.
- **Supporting trade**: products that meet recognised standards are easier to sell, including in export markets.

:::example{title="The standards mark on a product"}
Nyasha is choosing between two brands of electric cable for wiring a house. One carries a standards mark showing it has been tested; the other has no mark and an unfamiliar name. The mark gives her evidence that the cable meets safety requirements, so she can choose it with confidence.
:::

## Other bodies that protect consumers

- **Local authorities**: health inspectors check food premises; officials check scales and measures.
- **Government ministries and regulators**: for example, regulators of medicines, energy and telecommunications protect consumers of those services.
- **The police and courts**: deal with fraud and enforce consumer laws.
- **Trade and professional associations**: set codes of conduct for their members.
- **The media**: newspapers, radio and social media expose bad practices.

## How consumers use protection bodies

1. **Try the seller first**: return the goods with the receipt and explain the problem.
2. **Gather evidence**: receipt, guarantee, photographs, the faulty goods, dates and names.
3. **Contact the body**: visit its office, phone, email or use its social media pages; fill in a complaint form.
4. **Follow up**: the body investigates and negotiates; serious cases may be referred to other authorities or the courts.
5. **Use its information**: read price surveys, warnings and advice before buying; look for standards marks.

## Limitations of consumer protection bodies

- Many consumers, especially in rural areas, **do not know** the bodies exist or how to contact them.
- Offices are mainly in **towns**, so travel is costly for rural consumers.
- Limited **funding and staff** slow down investigations.
- Some bodies can **advise and negotiate** but cannot force a business to pay without going to court.
- Informal traders without fixed premises or receipts are **hard to trace**.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the standards body handles complaints about shops"}
SAZ develops standards and tests and certifies products. Complaints about a trader's treatment of a customer go to the consumer council (or the relevant authority).
:::

:::mistake{title="Vague roles"}
"It helps consumers" earns nothing. Name the role and say how it helps: "It publishes price surveys, so consumers can compare prices and avoid overcharging."
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Two bodies, two sets of roles"}
Learn four roles each for the consumer council and the standards association. Explaining a role means saying what the body does **and** how this protects the consumer.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Procedure questions"}
"Describe how a consumer could get help from the consumer council" wants steps in order, beginning with going back to the seller and gathering evidence.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Consumer protection bodies give consumers a stronger, collective voice.
- A national consumer council educates consumers, handles complaints, monitors prices and quality, warns the public and represents consumers to government.
- The Standards Association of Zimbabwe develops standards, tests products and certifies them with a standards mark.
- Local authorities, regulators, the police and courts, trade associations and the media also protect consumers.
- Limits: low awareness, urban offices, limited resources and limited power to enforce.
:::
