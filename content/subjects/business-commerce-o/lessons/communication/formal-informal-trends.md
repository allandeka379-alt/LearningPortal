## Introduction

At a furniture factory in Chitungwiza, the manager sends a memo announcing that the factory will close for two days for maintenance. Before the memo reaches the workshop floor, workers are already discussing it in the canteen, and the story has grown: some say the factory is closing for good. Both messages are communication, but one followed the official route and one did not.

Every organisation uses **formal** and **informal** communication. ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 3) asks you to explain both, to draft a business letter and to describe trends in business communication and the impact of ICT. Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 3) covers the importance of business communication and the impact of ICTs. The methods of communication and barriers to communication are covered in separate lessons.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Formal communication"}
Communication that follows the official channels and rules of an organisation, such as letters, memoranda, reports, notices and minutes of meetings.
:::

:::definition{term="Informal communication"}
Communication outside the official channels, such as conversations between colleagues, rumours and casual messages; often called the grapevine.
:::

:::definition{term="Grapevine"}
The unofficial network through which information and rumours spread among staff.
:::

:::definition{term="Memorandum (memo)"}
A short written message sent within an organisation, from one person or department to another.
:::

:::definition{term="Report"}
A formal document that sets out facts, findings and recommendations on a subject, usually for managers to make a decision.
:::

## Formal communication

Formal communication follows the **chain of command**: instructions pass down, reports and requests pass up, and departments exchange information through official channels.

| Formal document | Purpose | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business letter | Communicating with people **outside** the business: customers, suppliers, banks, government | Letterhead, references, date, inside address, salutation, subject heading, body, complimentary close, signature |
| Memorandum | Communicating **inside** the business | To, From, Date, Subject; short; no salutation or complimentary close |
| Report | Presenting findings and recommendations | Title, terms of reference, findings, conclusions, recommendations |
| Notice | Informing many staff at once | Displayed on a notice board or sent to all |
| Minutes | Recording what was decided at a meeting | Date, attendance, decisions, who will act |

**Advantages**: messages are official and accurate; there is a written record; responsibility is clear; confidential information is controlled.
**Disadvantages**: can be slow, especially through many levels; can feel impersonal; messages may be distorted as they pass down a long chain.

## Informal communication

Informal communication happens through conversations at break time, chats in corridors, messages between friends at work and the grapevine.

**Advantages**: fast; helps staff build relationships and team spirit; managers can learn how staff feel; it can fill gaps when formal messages are slow.
**Disadvantages**: rumours spread, information becomes inaccurate or exaggerated, confidential matters may leak, and there is no record or responsibility.

Managers cannot stop the grapevine, but they can reduce harmful rumours by communicating **quickly, clearly and honestly** through formal channels, and by listening to what the grapevine reveals.

## Drafting a business letter

A business letter follows a standard **layout** (the fully blocked style, with every line starting at the left margin, is common):

1. **Letterhead**: business name, address, telephone, email.
2. **References**: "Our ref" and "Your ref", so letters can be filed and matched.
3. **Date**: for example 7 October 2026.
4. **Inside address**: name, title and address of the receiver.
5. **Salutation**: "Dear Sir or Madam" (name unknown) or "Dear Mr Moyo" (name known).
6. **Subject heading**: for example "Order for school desks".
7. **Body**: an opening paragraph (why you are writing), the main details, and a closing paragraph (what action you expect).
8. **Complimentary close**: "Yours faithfully" after "Dear Sir or Madam"; "Yours sincerely" after a name.
9. **Signature, name and position** of the writer.
10. **Enclosures** ("Enc.") if documents are attached.

:::example{title="The body of a letter of enquiry"}
Dear Sir or Madam

**Enquiry for school uniforms**

We are a uniform retailer in Masvingo and wish to stock blazers for the coming school year. Please send us your current price list, details of trade and cash discounts and your delivery times for orders of 200 blazers or more.

We look forward to hearing from you.

Yours faithfully

T. Chikore (Mrs), Purchasing Manager
:::

A good business letter is **clear, polite, concise and correct**: one subject, short paragraphs, no slang and accurate figures.

## Trends in business communication

ICT has transformed how businesses communicate:

| Trend | Impact on business |
| --- | --- |
| Email | Fast, cheap written messages with attachments and a record; has largely replaced letters for routine matters |
| Instant messaging and voice notes | Very fast contact with staff, suppliers and customer groups; quick photos of goods and documents |
| Video calls and video conferencing | Meetings without travel; interviews and training at a distance |
| Social media | Two-way contact with customers; advertising; customer service; quick feedback, both good and bad |
| Mobile phones and smartphones | Staff reachable anywhere; mobile payments and ordering |
| Cloud storage and shared documents | Several people work on the same document from different places |
| Websites and online chat | Customers find information and ask questions at any time |

**Benefits of ICT for communication**: speed, lower costs, wider reach, easier record keeping and storage, flexible working from different places, better customer service.

**Problems**: costs of equipment, data and training; dependence on power and networks; security risks (hacking, scams, leaks of confidential data); information overload; messages that are too informal for business; less face-to-face contact; and the grapevine now spreads rumours through social media much faster.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the wrong complimentary close"}
When the receiver's name is not used, close with "Yours faithfully". "Yours sincerely" follows a named salutation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling every email informal"}
An email from a manager to a supplier confirming an order is formal communication. What matters is whether the message follows official channels, not the medium.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying the grapevine has no benefits"}
The grapevine spreads rumours, but it also builds relationships and can tell managers how staff feel.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Drafting a letter"}
Marks are given for layout (letterhead, date, inside address, salutation, subject, matching close) as well as content. Keep the body to three short paragraphs and end with the action you want.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Impact of ICT"}
For "discuss the impact of ICT on business communication", give benefits and problems, apply them to a business, and conclude. Business Enterprise and Skills questions often ask how an enterprise can use ICTs; Commerce questions often ask about trends.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Formal communication follows official channels (letters, memos, reports, notices, minutes): accurate and recorded but can be slow.
- Informal communication (the grapevine) is fast and builds relationships but spreads rumours and has no record.
- A business letter has a letterhead, references, date, inside address, salutation, subject, body, matching close and signature.
- Trends: email, instant messaging, video calls, social media, smartphones, cloud documents and websites.
- ICT makes communication faster, cheaper and wider but brings costs, security risks, overload and dependence on networks.
:::
