## Introduction

Rudo runs a small business in Gweru selling school textbooks to schools in rural districts. Some schools order by email, but they still need the books delivered, invoices sent and payment collected. Some schools have no street address at all, only a box number at the post office. To serve them, Rudo uses **postal and courier services**.

Even in the age of email and messaging, physical goods and some original documents (signed contracts, certificates, cheques, official letters) must still be carried from place to place. ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 2) asks you to describe the agents of postal services, the services they offer to businesses, and the importance and drawbacks of postal services.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Postal service"}
The collection, sorting, transport and delivery of letters, documents and parcels, together with related services such as post boxes and money transfer.
:::

:::definition{term="Courier service"}
A private business that collects and delivers documents and parcels quickly, usually door to door, often with tracking and proof of delivery.
:::

:::definition{term="Registered mail"}
A service in which the post office records the item at every stage, the receiver signs on delivery and compensation is paid if it is lost.
:::

:::definition{term="Cash on delivery (COD)"}
A service in which the postal or courier agent collects payment from the receiver when the goods are delivered and passes it to the seller.
:::

## Agents of postal services

**The national postal service** has post offices in cities, towns and many rural service centres. It offers letter and parcel post, post boxes and private bags, and a range of business services. Its strength is its **wide network**, including rural areas where private firms do not operate, and its relatively low charges.

**Courier companies** are private businesses. They collect from the sender's premises and deliver door to door, often within a day between major towns, and offer **tracking** and **proof of delivery**. They are faster and more convenient but **more expensive**, and some do not cover remote rural areas. Many online sellers and bus companies also run parcel services between towns.

## Services offered to businesses

| Service | How it works | Why a business uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary letter and parcel post | Items posted with stamps, delivered to an address or box | Cheap way to send non-urgent documents and goods |
| Post office box (PO Box) and private bag | A locked box or bag at the post office where mail is collected | A secure, permanent address; useful where there is no street delivery |
| Registered mail | Item recorded at each stage, signed for, compensation if lost | Valuable items, certificates, contracts |
| Recorded delivery | Proof of posting and a signature on delivery (little or no compensation) | Proof that important letters (such as legal notices) were delivered |
| Cash on delivery | Payment collected from the buyer on delivery | Selling to new customers by post or online; seller is sure of payment |
| Poste restante | Mail is kept at a named post office until the receiver collects it | Travellers and people without a fixed address, such as traders who move between towns |
| Business reply service | Customers return a reply card or envelope without paying postage; the business pays | Encourages customers to reply to surveys and order forms |
| Freepost | Customers write to a special address without a stamp; the business pays the postage | Encourages orders and enquiries |
| Franking machine | A machine prints the postage on envelopes; the business pays the post office in advance | Saves buying and sticking stamps; controls postage costs for large mailings |
| Express (speed) mail | Faster, priority delivery for an extra charge | Urgent documents and parcels |
| Parcel post | Larger items sent by post | Sending goods to customers |
| Money transfer (postal orders, money orders) | Money sent through the post office | Paying small amounts safely without sending cash |

:::example{title="Choosing postal services"}
Rudo sends her monthly price lists by ordinary post to schools' PO Box addresses, because they are not urgent. When a new school orders books for the first time, she sends them **cash on delivery**, so she is sure of payment. Signed supply contracts go by **registered mail**, so there is a record and compensation if they are lost. An urgent order for exam booklets goes by **courier**, door to door the next day.
:::

## Importance of postal services to business and trade

- **Delivery of goods and documents** that cannot be sent electronically: parcels, original contracts, certificates.
- **Wide coverage**, including rural areas, so businesses can reach customers throughout the country.
- **Security and evidence**: registered and recorded services give proof of posting and delivery.
- **Payment services**: cash on delivery and money transfer support trade with distant customers.
- **Low cost** for non-urgent items compared with travelling or using a courier.
- **Support for online selling**: goods ordered online must still be delivered.
- **Encouraging customer responses** through business reply and freepost services.

## Drawbacks of postal services

- **Slower** than electronic communication, and sometimes slow even compared with couriers.
- **Risk of loss, delay or damage**, especially for ordinary (unregistered) items.
- **No immediate feedback**: the sender must wait days for a reply.
- **Limited delivery to homes** in some areas, so receivers must collect from the post office.
- **Competition from email and messaging** has reduced letter volumes, which can lead to fewer post offices and services.
- **Courier services are costly** for small businesses.

## Post office or courier?

The choice depends on the item and the customer. A courier is worth its higher charge for **urgent**, **valuable** or **time-sensitive** items between towns, such as tender documents due tomorrow or spare parts for a broken machine, because it collects from the sender, delivers to the door and can show exactly where the item is. The national postal service is the sensible choice for **non-urgent**, **low-value** items and for customers in **remote areas** that couriers do not reach. Many businesses use both: ordinary post for routine statements and price lists, and a courier when speed matters.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up registered mail and recorded delivery"}
Both prove delivery. Registered mail also tracks the item at each stage and pays compensation if it is lost, so it is used for valuable items.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing freepost with business reply service"}
Both let customers reply without paying. Business reply uses a pre-printed card or envelope supplied by the business; freepost uses a special address the customer writes on any envelope.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying email has made postal services useless"}
Goods, original documents and deliveries to people without internet still need postal or courier services.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Describe a service"}
For "describe" questions, say what the service is, how it works and why a business would use it. One sentence for each earns more marks than a long list of names.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Post office or courier?"}
When asked to advise a business, compare speed, cost, security, tracking and coverage for the item being sent. Urgent and valuable items in towns suit couriers; cheap, non-urgent items and remote areas suit the post office.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Agents of postal services: the national postal service (wide, low-cost network) and courier companies (fast, door to door, tracked, more expensive).
- Business services include PO boxes, registered mail, recorded delivery, cash on delivery, poste restante, business reply, freepost, franking machines, express mail, parcel post and money transfer.
- Postal services deliver goods and original documents, reach rural areas, give proof of delivery and support payment and online selling.
- Drawbacks: slower than electronic methods, risk of loss or delay, no instant feedback, limited home delivery and courier costs.
:::
