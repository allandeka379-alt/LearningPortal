## Introduction

Every business depends on communication. A supermarket manager in Gweru tells staff about a new price promotion. A transport company sends a quotation to a customer in Bulawayo. A manufacturer emails an order to a supplier in South Africa. A hair salon posts its new opening hours on social media. If the message is late, unclear or reaches the wrong person, orders are lost, mistakes are made and customers go elsewhere.

Communication appears in all three syllabuses. ZIMSEC Commerce studies its importance in Form 1 and the choice of method in Form 3; Business Enterprise and Skills covers methods, media and the impact of ICT in Form 3; Cambridge 0264 covers why communication is important in topic 2.3. Examiners ask you to compare methods, explain their advantages and disadvantages and, very often, to **recommend** a method for a given situation.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Communication"}
The transfer of information, ideas or instructions from a sender to a receiver, who understands it and may respond (feedback).
:::

:::definition{term="Internal communication"}
Communication between people within the same business, such as managers and employees.
:::

:::definition{term="External communication"}
Communication between the business and people or organisations outside it, such as customers, suppliers, banks and government.
:::

:::definition{term="Feedback"}
The response of the receiver, which shows the sender whether the message was received and understood.
:::

## The communication process

1. The **sender** decides on the message.
2. The message is put into a form (words, numbers, pictures) and sent through a **medium** or **channel** (meeting, letter, email).
3. The **receiver** gets and interprets the message.
4. The receiver gives **feedback**.

Communication is **one-way** when no feedback is expected (a notice on a board) and **two-way** when the receiver responds (a meeting or phone call).

## Why communication is important

- **Gives instructions** so that employees know what to do and work is coordinated.
- **Supports decision-making**: managers need accurate, timely information.
- **Motivates employees**, who feel valued when they are kept informed and listened to.
- **Builds relationships** with customers and suppliers: answering enquiries, confirming orders, handling complaints.
- **Promotes products** to customers.
- **Keeps records** of agreements and transactions (letters, invoices, emails).
- **Helps trade**: in Commerce, communication is an **aid to trade** because it links buyers and sellers who may be far apart.

## Methods of communication

### Oral (spoken) communication

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Face-to-face conversation | Immediate feedback; body language helps understanding; personal | No permanent record; may be forgotten; only possible if people are together |
| Meetings | Several people at once; ideas can be discussed; decisions made together | Time-consuming; some people may not speak; may be costly to bring people together |
| Telephone calls | Fast; two-way over long distances | No record unless noted; no body language; network problems |

### Written communication

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Letters | Formal; permanent record; can be detailed; suitable for legal or official matters | Slow by post; no immediate feedback |
| Memos (memoranda) | Short written messages within a business; a record | Internal only; may be ignored |
| Notices | Reach many staff at once cheaply | No feedback; not everyone reads them |
| Reports | Detailed information for decisions | Take time to write and read |
| Business documents (orders, invoices, receipts) | Accurate record of transactions | Need care to avoid errors |

### Visual communication

Charts, graphs, diagrams, pictures, signs and posters. They make data and ideas easy to understand quickly and help where language is a barrier, but they may be misread and cannot carry detailed explanations on their own.

### Electronic communication

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email | Fast and cheap; can attach documents; reaches many people; a record | Needs internet access; may be ignored or lost among many emails; risk of viruses or hacking |
| Text messages and instant messaging (including messaging apps) | Very fast; cheap; most people have mobile phones; groups can be reached at once | Short messages; informal; can be misunderstood; may be missed |
| Social media | Reaches many customers cheaply; two-way; good for promotion | Negative comments are public; needs constant updating |
| Video calls and conferencing | Face-to-face contact over distance; saves travel | Needs reliable internet and power |
| Websites | Information available to customers at any time | Cost of setting up and updating |

## ZIMSEC Business Enterprise and Skills: the impact of ICT

BES asks how information and communication technology has changed business communication: faster and cheaper communication, mobile money and electronic payments, online marketing, easier contact with suppliers and customers in other countries, and record keeping on computers. Problems include the cost of equipment and data, power cuts and network failures, cyber-crime and the need for staff training.

:::example{title="Communication in a small enterprise"}
Farai runs a welding workshop in Kwekwe with four workers. He gives daily instructions **face to face**, uses a **messaging app** to receive orders and send photographs of finished gates to customers, writes **quotations and receipts** for every job so there is a record, and pins a **notice** about safety rules on the workshop wall. He uses a **phone call** when a supplier is late. Each method is chosen for a reason: speed, cost, the need for a record or the need for discussion.
:::

## Choosing a method

| Factor | Question to ask | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed / urgency | How quickly must the message arrive? | An urgent breakdown: phone call |
| Cost | Is the cost justified by the importance of the message? | A small enquiry: text message rather than a visit |
| Need for a record | Will the message be needed as evidence later? | A contract or complaint: letter or email |
| Need for feedback | Must the receiver respond or discuss? | A disagreement: face-to-face meeting |
| Number of receivers | One person or many? | All staff: notice, group message or meeting |
| Distance | Is the receiver nearby or far away? | A supplier in another country: email or phone |
| Confidentiality | Is the message private? | A disciplinary matter: private meeting and letter |
| Complexity | Is the message detailed or technical? | Sales figures: written report with charts |
| Receiver's access | Does the receiver have phone, internet or literacy? | Rural customers: phone or face-to-face |

:::example{title="Recommending a method"}
Rudo, the manager of a bakery in Masvingo, must tell her 25 staff that the start of the morning shift will move one hour earlier from next month. She should use a **staff meeting**, so that she can explain the reason and answer questions, followed by a **written notice** or group message so there is a record and staff who were absent are informed. A notice alone would give no chance for questions; a meeting alone leaves no record.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Recommending a method without a reason"}
"Use email" earns little. Give the factor: "email, because the supplier is in another country and a written record of the order is needed".
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying one method is always best"}
Every method has advantages and disadvantages. The best method depends on the message, the receiver and the situation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up internal and external communication"}
A memo to staff is internal; a letter to a supplier is external. The same method (email) can be used for both.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Recommend with two reasons and a rejection"}
Strong answers choose a method, give two reasons linked to the case and explain why an alternative is less suitable.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Use the factors"}
Speed, cost, record, feedback, number of receivers, distance and confidentiality give you ready-made reasons.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
ZIMSEC Commerce treats communication as an aid to trade and asks for the choice of mode. Business Enterprise and Skills adds the impact of ICT on enterprises. Cambridge 0264 (2.3.1) expects the importance of communication and the advantages and disadvantages of methods, with a recommendation for a situation.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Communication transfers information from sender to receiver, with feedback in two-way communication.
- It is important for instructions, decisions, motivation, relationships, promotion, records and trade.
- Methods are oral, written, visual and electronic; each has advantages and disadvantages.
- ICT has made communication faster and cheaper but brings costs, network problems and security risks.
- Choose a method by considering speed, cost, need for a record, feedback, number of receivers, distance, confidentiality and complexity.
:::
