## Introduction

Sipho runs a small printing business in Bulawayo. Some things he can control: whom he employs, which machines he buys, how he treats customers. Other things he cannot: power cuts, a rise in the price of paper, a new law on business licences, or a competitor opening across the road. Everything that affects a business, inside or outside it, makes up its **business environment**.

ZIMSEC Commerce studies the internal and external factors affecting a business in Form 4. Business Enterprise and Skills studies the internal and external environment and SWOT analysis in Form 2 and business constraints in Form 3. Examiners ask you to classify factors, explain how a factor affects a named business, carry out a SWOT analysis from a case and suggest how a business should respond.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Business environment"}
All the internal and external factors that affect how a business operates and how well it performs.
:::

:::definition{term="Internal environment"}
Factors inside the business that it can control: owners, employees, management, resources, systems and culture.
:::

:::definition{term="External environment"}
Factors outside the business that it cannot control but must respond to: customers, suppliers, competitors and wider political, economic, social, technological, legal and ecological forces.
:::

:::definition{term="Business constraint"}
Anything that limits what a business can do, such as a shortage of finance, a law, or the state of the economy.
:::

:::definition{term="SWOT analysis"}
A summary of a business's internal Strengths and Weaknesses and external Opportunities and Threats.
:::

## The internal environment

| Internal factor | How it affects the business |
| --- | --- |
| Owners | Provide capital, set objectives and take the main decisions |
| Employees | Their skills, attitude and motivation affect quality and productivity |
| Management | Good planning and leadership make the business efficient; poor management leads to waste and failure |
| Resources | Finance, buildings, machinery, materials and technology limit what the business can produce |
| Culture | The shared values and way of working (for example, a focus on customer service) |
| Systems and records | Good records and procedures help control costs and stock |

## The external environment

### The immediate (task) environment

- **Customers**: their needs, tastes and incomes decide demand.
- **Suppliers**: their prices, reliability and credit terms affect costs and production.
- **Competitors**: their prices and products affect the business's sales and the prices it can charge.

### The wider environment: PESTLE factors

| Factor | Examples | Possible effect on a business |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P**olitical | Government stability, policy on imports, support for SMEs, indigenisation rules | Stable government encourages investment; changes in import policy alter costs |
| **E**conomic | Inflation, interest rates, exchange rates, unemployment, recession or boom, taxes | Inflation raises costs; a recession reduces demand; higher interest raises loan costs |
| **S**ocial | Population size and age, culture and religion, lifestyles, education, urbanisation | A growing young urban population increases demand for fast food and mobile data |
| **T**echnological | Mobile money, the internet, automation, new machines | New technology can cut costs and open new markets, but old equipment becomes outdated |
| **L**egal | Licences, health and safety, employment law, consumer protection, tax law | Compliance costs money; breaking laws leads to fines or closure |
| **E**cological (environmental) | Drought, climate change, pollution control, waste disposal rules | Drought cuts farm output and raises raw-material prices; anti-pollution rules raise costs |

:::example{title="PESTLE factors for a bakery in Mutare"}
- **Economic:** the price of flour rises, so costs rise and the bakery may have to raise prices.
- **Social:** more customers want healthier brown bread, so the bakery changes its product range.
- **Technological:** customers want to pay by mobile money, so the bakery accepts electronic payments.
- **Legal:** new food-hygiene inspections require the bakery to improve its premises.
- **Ecological:** a drought reduces the local wheat harvest, increasing reliance on imported flour.
- **Political:** a change in import rules affects the cost of imported flour and baking equipment.
:::

## Business constraints

A constraint limits the choices of a business. ZIMSEC BES groups the external constraints as:

- **Economic constraints**: shortage of foreign currency, inflation, high interest rates, low demand in a recession.
- **Ecological constraints**: climate, drought, rules to protect land, water and wildlife.
- **Social constraints**: customs, beliefs and attitudes of the community (for example, which products are acceptable), population changes.
- **Legal constraints**: licences, registration, tax, labour and consumer laws.

Internal constraints include a lack of finance, a shortage of skilled staff, limited space or outdated equipment.

## SWOT analysis

A SWOT analysis helps a business understand its position before making a plan.

| | Helpful | Harmful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Internal** (can be controlled) | **Strengths**, e.g. skilled staff, good location, loyal customers | **Weaknesses**, e.g. old equipment, poor records, little finance |
| **External** (cannot be controlled) | **Opportunities**, e.g. a growing market, new technology, a new road, government support | **Threats**, e.g. new competitors, rising costs, new laws, recession |

:::example{title="SWOT for Sipho's printing business"}
- **Strengths:** fast service; a skilled designer; a central location in Bulawayo.
- **Weaknesses:** one old printing machine that breaks down; no online presence.
- **Opportunities:** schools need printed exam papers and reports; small businesses want cheap flyers.
- **Threats:** power cuts; rising paper prices; customers printing at home.
- **Possible plan:** use the strengths to win school contracts, buy a reliable second-hand machine to remove a weakness, and install a generator or solar system to reduce the threat of power cuts.
:::

The point of SWOT is to **act**: build on strengths, correct weaknesses, take opportunities and reduce threats.

## How a business responds to its environment

- **Monitor** changes (read news, talk to customers and suppliers).
- **Adapt products and prices**: new products for changing tastes, price changes when costs rise.
- **Plan** for risks: cash reserves, insurance, alternative suppliers.
- **Use technology**: mobile money, social media marketing.
- **Comply with laws** and keep good relations with government and the community.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Putting strengths and opportunities the wrong way round"}
Strengths and weaknesses are **internal**. Opportunities and threats are **external**. "A new shopping centre opening nearby" is an opportunity, not a strength.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing PESTLE factors without effects"}
"Inflation" alone earns little. Explain the effect on the business in the question: "inflation raises the price of flour, squeezing the bakery's profit margin".
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying a business can control external factors"}
A business cannot stop a drought or a recession; it can only prepare and respond.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Apply every factor"}
Always connect the factor to the business in the case: name it, explain the effect and, if asked, suggest a response.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="SWOT questions"}
Give at least one point in each box, taken from the case. Then show how the business could use the analysis.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 4) asks for the internal and external factors affecting a business. Business Enterprise and Skills adds SWOT analysis (Form 2) and economic, ecological, social and legal constraints (Form 3).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The business environment is everything inside and outside a business that affects it.
- Internal factors (owners, employees, management, resources, culture) can be controlled.
- External factors (customers, suppliers, competitors and PESTLE forces) cannot be controlled, only responded to.
- Constraints limit what a business can do: economic, ecological, social, legal and internal.
- SWOT analysis sums up internal strengths and weaknesses and external opportunities and threats, as a basis for action.
:::
