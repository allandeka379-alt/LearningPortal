## Introduction

Two friends in Masvingo each want to start a market-gardening enterprise. Farai has a plot of land with a borehole but knows little about growing vegetables. Chipo has studied horticulture and has worked on a farm for three years, but owns no land. Each has some of what an enterprise needs and lacks the rest. Together, they might succeed.

Before starting or growing an enterprise, an enterpriser must ask two questions: **what do I have?** (resources) and **what can I do well with it?** (capabilities). Business Enterprise and Skills studies the need for resources and capabilities in Form 1 and their acquisition, ownership and sustainable use in Form 2. Examiners ask you to classify resources, explain why they must be analysed, and suggest how a named enterprise can acquire the resources it lacks.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Resources"}
Everything an enterprise owns or can use to produce goods and services: natural resources, buildings, equipment, money, people and knowledge.
:::

:::definition{term="Tangible resources"}
Physical resources that can be seen and touched, such as land, water, forests, minerals, buildings, machinery, vehicles, stock and money.
:::

:::definition{term="Intangible resources"}
Non-physical resources, such as knowledge, skills, expertise, experience, reputation, brand names, customer goodwill and contacts.
:::

:::definition{term="Capability"}
The ability of an enterprise to combine and use its resources to carry out an activity well, for example to produce good-quality goods on time or to give excellent customer service.
:::

## Tangible and intangible resources

| Tangible resources | Intangible resources |
| --- | --- |
| Land and the minerals in it | Knowledge and technical skills of the owner and workers |
| Water (rivers, dams, boreholes) | Expertise and experience |
| Forests and wildlife | Reputation and goodwill with customers |
| Buildings and premises | Brand names, logos and other intellectual property |
| Machinery, tools and vehicles | Contacts and relationships with suppliers and customers |
| Capital (money) and stock | Ideas, creativity and the ability to solve problems |

Resources are often grouped as the **factors of production**: land (natural resources), labour (people), capital (man-made resources and money) and enterprise (the enterpriser who brings them together).

:::example{title="Resources of a carpentry workshop in Mutare"}
- **Tangible:** a rented workshop, saws, planes and a lathe, a stock of timber, a delivery bicycle, US\$800 in the bank.
- **Intangible:** the owner's fifteen years of carpentry skill, a reputation for strong school desks, good relations with a timber merchant, and the trust of three schools that order every year.
:::

## Capabilities

Resources alone do not make an enterprise successful. What matters is what the enterprise can **do** with them.

| Resources | Capability they create |
| --- | --- |
| Skilled tailors + sewing machines + good fabric suppliers | Producing well-fitting uniforms quickly for schools |
| A borehole + irrigation pipes + horticulture knowledge | Growing vegetables all year round, including in the dry season |
| A trusted name + delivery van + mobile phone orders | Delivering groceries to customers' homes on the same day |

A capability that rivals find hard to copy (such as the carpentry workshop's reputation with schools) gives the enterprise a **competitive advantage**.

## Why resources and capabilities are analysed

An enterpriser analyses resources and capabilities to:

- **Identify what is available** and what is missing before starting a project.
- **Choose suitable opportunities**: an enterprise should do what its resources and skills allow it to do well.
- **Plan acquisition** of missing resources (finance, equipment, skills) and estimate the cost.
- **Use resources efficiently**, avoiding waste and idle equipment.
- **Identify strengths and weaknesses** for a SWOT analysis and business plan.
- **Persuade lenders and partners**, who want to know what the enterprise already has.

:::example{title="Matching an opportunity to resources"}
Thandeka has a large plot near Chinhoyi with a reliable stream, and she has completed a course in poultry keeping. A poultry project suits her resources (land, water) and capabilities (poultry skills) better than opening a hair salon, for which she has neither premises in town nor hairdressing skills.
:::

## Acquiring resources

| Method | How it works | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buying | Pay for the resource and own it outright | Buying a sewing machine with savings |
| Renting or leasing | Pay regularly to use a resource owned by someone else | Renting a market stall or leasing a vehicle |
| Hire purchase | Pay a deposit and instalments, owning the resource after the last payment | A grinding mill on hire purchase |
| Borrowing / loans | Borrow money to buy resources | A bank or microfinance loan for equipment |
| Partnership or joint venture | Combine resources with others | Farai's land and Chipo's skills in one enterprise |
| Inheritance or gifts | Receive resources from family | Inheriting a family plot |
| Government allocation and grants | Land allocated by authorities or grants for projects | Land allocated for a co-operative project |
| Training and recruitment | Acquire intangible skills | Attending a course or employing a skilled worker |

## Ownership and sustainable use of resources

Resources may be owned **individually** (a sole trader's tools), **jointly** (partners or co-operative members), by the **community** (communal grazing land, a village dam) or by the **state** (national parks, some minerals). Ownership affects who may use the resource and who must look after it.

**Sustainable use** means using resources in a way that meets today's needs without destroying them for the future:

- Replant trees when timber is cut and avoid cutting forests faster than they grow.
- Use water carefully (drip irrigation) and protect rivers from pollution.
- Prevent soil erosion with contour ridges and crop rotation.
- Recycle and reduce waste.
- Keep equipment maintained so that it lasts longer.

An enterprise that destroys its own natural resources will eventually lose its means of production.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling skills and reputation tangible"}
If you cannot touch it, it is intangible. Skills, knowledge and reputation are intangible resources even though they are very valuable.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing resources and capabilities"}
A sewing machine is a resource. The ability to make well-fitting uniforms on time is a capability: it comes from combining resources and skills.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the cost of acquiring resources"}
Renting, hire purchase and loans all have costs. When recommending a method, consider whether the enterprise can afford the payments.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Classify with examples from the case"}
"Tangible: the borehole and irrigation pipes. Intangible: Chipo's knowledge of horticulture." Use the enterprise's own resources.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Recommend a method of acquisition"}
Match the method to the situation: renting suits an enterprise with little capital that is testing a market; buying suits a resource used for many years.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Business Enterprise and Skills only"}
This topic is in the ZIMSEC Business Enterprise and Skills syllabus (Forms 1 and 2). It links with the factors of production, SWOT analysis and sustainable resource use.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Resources are what an enterprise owns or can use; they are tangible (land, water, buildings, equipment, capital) or intangible (knowledge, skills, expertise, reputation).
- Capabilities are what the enterprise can do well by combining its resources.
- Analysing resources and capabilities helps choose opportunities, plan, use resources efficiently and persuade lenders.
- Resources can be bought, rented, leased, bought on hire purchase, borrowed for, shared, inherited, allocated or developed through training.
- Resources may be owned individually, jointly, communally or by the state, and should be used sustainably.
:::
