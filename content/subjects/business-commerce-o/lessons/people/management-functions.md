## Introduction

Nyasha owns a busy hardware shop in Gweru with eleven workers. Every morning she decides what stock to order, who will serve customers, who will make deliveries and how much cash the shop must bank. At the end of the week she checks sales against her targets. Nyasha is doing the work of a **manager**. She does not lay bricks or sell every bag of cement herself: she makes sure that other people's work gets done well.

All three syllabi ask about the functions of management. ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 2) and Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 2) list the functions; Cambridge 0264 (topic 2.2.2) adds the importance of each function and the advantages and disadvantages of **delegation**. Questions range from "state two functions of management" to "explain how planning helps a business" and "discuss whether the owner should delegate more tasks".

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Management"}
Getting things done through other people by planning, organising, commanding (directing), coordinating and controlling the resources of a business.
:::

:::definition{term="Planning"}
Setting objectives and deciding in advance how, when and by whom they will be achieved.
:::

:::definition{term="Organising"}
Arranging people, materials, equipment and money so that the plan can be carried out.
:::

:::definition{term="Commanding (directing)"}
Instructing, guiding, leading and motivating employees so that they carry out their tasks.
:::

:::definition{term="Coordinating"}
Making sure that the different people and departments of a business work together towards the same objectives.
:::

:::definition{term="Controlling"}
Measuring actual performance, comparing it with the plan and taking corrective action when results fall short.
:::

:::definition{term="Delegation"}
Giving a subordinate the authority to carry out a task, while the manager remains responsible for the result.
:::

## The five functions of management

The five functions are often remembered as **POCCC** (some textbooks call commanding "leading" or "directing"; Business Enterprise and Skills lists planning, leading, organising and controlling).

| Function | What the manager does | Example at a bakery in Bulawayo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planning | Sets objectives and decides how to reach them | Aims to supply 20 more tuck shops next year and plans a second oven |
| Organising | Arranges staff, materials, equipment and finance | Arranges a night shift, buys flour in bulk and allocates delivery routes |
| Commanding | Gives instructions, leads and motivates | Briefs the shift supervisors and praises the team when targets are met |
| Coordinating | Makes departments work together | Makes sure production bakes enough bread for the orders the sales team takes |
| Controlling | Checks results against the plan and corrects | Compares daily output with the target and investigates wasted dough |

### Why each function matters

- **Planning** gives the business a sense of direction. Without objectives, workers do not know what they are aiming for, and money may be wasted on unnecessary activities. Plans also help when asking a bank for a loan.
- **Organising** makes sure resources are in the right place at the right time. A plan to deliver more bread is useless if there are no drivers or vehicles.
- **Commanding** turns plans into action. Clear instructions reduce mistakes, and good leadership motivates workers to work hard.
- **Coordinating** prevents departments working against each other. If the marketing department runs a promotion but production has not made enough stock, customers are disappointed.
- **Controlling** shows whether the plan is working. Comparing actual results with targets allows problems, such as rising costs or falling sales, to be spotted early and corrected.

:::example{title="The functions working together"}
Farai manages a small poultry co-operative near Chinhoyi. He **plans** to raise 2,000 broilers for the festive season. He **organises** the purchase of day-old chicks and feed and draws up a duty roster. He **commands** by explaining the feeding and cleaning routine to each worker. He **coordinates** the work of the feeding team with the members who are arranging buyers, so that birds are ready when the buyers want them. Finally, he **controls** by recording weight gain every week; when one house shows slow growth, he checks the feed and the ventilation.
:::

## Delegation

As a business grows, a manager cannot do everything. Nyasha cannot order stock, serve every customer and keep the accounts on her own. She can **delegate**: give her senior shop assistant the authority to order stock up to an agreed value. Nyasha remains **responsible** if the orders are wrong, so she must choose the right person and check the work.

### Advantages of delegation

| To the manager | To the subordinate | To the business |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More time for important tasks such as planning | Work is more interesting and challenging | Decisions are made faster by people closer to the problem |
| Less stress and a lighter workload | Develops skills and prepares for promotion | Employees become more motivated (links to Herzberg's motivators) |
| Uses the skills of specialists | Feels trusted and valued | Future managers are trained within the business |

### Disadvantages of delegation

- The manager is still responsible: if the subordinate makes a mistake, the manager must answer for it.
- The subordinate may lack the skill or experience, so the task may be done badly.
- Time is needed to train the subordinate and to check the work.
- Some managers feel they are losing control, and some subordinates see extra tasks as extra work without extra pay.

:::example{title="Should the owner delegate?"}
Chipo runs a catering business in Mutare. She wants to delegate the weekly purchase of ingredients to Sipho, who has worked for her for three years and knows the suppliers. Delegation would free Chipo to look for new wedding and conference contracts. The risk is that Sipho buys poor quality or overpays. Chipo can reduce the risk by setting a spending limit, agreeing a list of approved suppliers and checking the receipts each week. With these controls, delegation is likely to benefit the business.
:::

### What makes delegation work

- Choose a subordinate with the right skills, or train them first.
- Give clear instructions and enough authority (for example a spending limit).
- Agree how and when results will be checked (this is the controlling function).
- Do not delegate tasks that only the manager should do, such as setting the main objectives or disciplining senior staff.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing delegation with giving away responsibility"}
Authority can be delegated; responsibility cannot. The manager remains accountable for the result of a delegated task.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up coordinating and controlling"}
Coordinating makes departments work together. Controlling compares actual results with the plan. "Checking that output met the target" is controlling, not coordinating.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing functions without explaining them"}
"Planning, organising, commanding" earns only the identification marks. For "explain", say what the function involves and why it helps the business, ideally using the business in the question.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Command words"}
"State" or "identify" needs only the name of the function. "Explain" needs the function, what it involves and its benefit. "Discuss whether a manager should delegate" needs advantages, disadvantages and a justified conclusion that refers to the case (the skill of the employee, the size of the business, the type of task).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
ZIMSEC Commerce and Business Enterprise and Skills mainly ask you to state and explain the functions. Cambridge 0264 also expects you to explain the importance of each function and to weigh up delegation in a case; link delegation to motivation and leadership style for higher marks.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Management means getting work done through other people.
- The functions are planning, organising, commanding (directing or leading), coordinating and controlling.
- Each function matters: direction, resources in place, action, teamwork and early correction of problems.
- Delegation passes authority to a subordinate, but the manager keeps responsibility.
- Delegation frees managers, speeds decisions and motivates staff, but needs trust, training and checking.
:::
