## Introduction

Rudo and Tatenda work side by side in a food-processing factory in Chitungwiza, packing peanut butter. They earn the same wage. Rudo arrives early, suggests ways to reduce spillage and has not missed a day this year. Tatenda arrives late, takes long breaks and is looking for another job. Why do two people in the same job behave so differently? The answer lies in **motivation**: the reasons that make people want to work hard.

Motivation is one of the most examined topics in people management. Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 3) names Maslow, Taylor, Herzberg and McGregor. Cambridge 0264 (topic 2.1) asks why people work, the benefits of a well-motivated workforce and the theories of Maslow, Taylor and Herzberg. You must be able to **apply** each theory to a business, not just describe it.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Motivation"}
The reasons that cause a person to behave in a certain way; at work, the factors that make employees want to work well and achieve the business's objectives.
:::

:::definition{term="Labour turnover"}
The rate at which employees leave a business and have to be replaced.
:::

:::definition{term="Absenteeism"}
Employees being absent from work without a good reason.
:::

:::definition{term="Productivity"}
The output produced per worker in a given time.
:::

## Why people work

People work to satisfy **needs** and **wants**:

- **Money** to buy food, shelter, clothing, school fees and other goods.
- **Security**: a regular income and a job they can rely on.
- **Social needs**: friendship and belonging to a team.
- **Esteem**: respect, status and recognition for their work.
- **Self-fulfilment**: the satisfaction of doing interesting work and using their talents.

## Why a well-motivated workforce matters

| Benefit | How it helps the business |
| --- | --- |
| Higher productivity | More output per worker lowers the cost per unit |
| Lower absenteeism | Fewer delays and less need for cover staff |
| Lower labour turnover | Lower recruitment and training costs; experienced staff are kept |
| Better quality and customer service | Fewer complaints and more repeat customers |
| Acceptance of change | Motivated employees cooperate with new methods and machines |
| Better reputation as an employer | Easier to recruit good workers |

## Maslow's hierarchy of needs

Abraham Maslow arranged human needs in five levels, usually drawn as a pyramid. A person tries to satisfy the lowest unmet level first; once a level is satisfied, the next level up motivates.

| Level (from the bottom) | Need | How a business can meet it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Physiological | Food, water, shelter | A wage high enough to live on; rest breaks |
| 2 Safety (security) | Safe conditions, job security | Contracts of employment, safety equipment, pension schemes |
| 3 Social (love and belonging) | Friendship, being part of a team | Team working, staff events, a canteen |
| 4 Esteem | Status, recognition, respect | Praise, job titles, promotion, employee of the month |
| 5 Self-actualisation | Reaching full potential | Challenging work, training, chances to use creativity |

:::example{title="Applying Maslow at a security company"}
Guards at a security company in Bulawayo are paid a fair wage and given uniforms and torches (physiological and safety needs). The manager now arranges guards in teams that share a duty room (social), and introduces a "guard of the month" award (esteem). A guard who wants to study for a supervisor's certificate is helped with fees (self-actualisation).
:::

The practical lesson for managers is to find out which needs are not yet met. Offering praise to a worker who cannot afford rent will not motivate; raising the pay of a well-paid worker who feels ignored may not either.

**Limitations:** not everyone follows the same order; some people value status before security; it is hard to know which level each employee has reached.

## Taylor's scientific management

F. W. Taylor believed that workers are motivated mainly by **money**. He studied each task to find the fastest way of doing it, trained workers to follow it exactly, and paid them by results (**piece rate**), so that the more they produced, the more they earned.

- **Use in business:** piece rate in clothing factories or on farms where output can be counted; breaking jobs into simple, repeated tasks.
- **Limitations:** ignores social and esteem needs; repetitive work can be boring; workers may rush and lower quality; output of many jobs (nurses, teachers, office staff) cannot easily be counted.

## Herzberg's two-factor theory

Frederick Herzberg divided the factors at work into two groups.

| Hygiene factors (maintenance factors) | Motivators |
| --- | --- |
| Pay, working conditions, company rules, supervision, relationships with colleagues, job security | Achievement, recognition, responsibility, interesting work, promotion, personal growth |
| If poor, they cause **dissatisfaction**. If good, they prevent dissatisfaction but **do not motivate** | If present, they **motivate** employees to work harder |

The practical lesson: a business must first get the hygiene factors right (fair pay, safe conditions), then motivate through **job enrichment**: giving workers more responsibility, more challenging tasks and recognition.

:::example{title="Herzberg in a bank"}
A bank raises tellers' pay to the industry level and improves air-conditioning, but productivity does not rise: these were hygiene factors, so they removed dissatisfaction only. When senior tellers are made responsible for training new staff and solving customer problems, they become more motivated: responsibility is a motivator.
:::

## ZIMSEC Business Enterprise and Skills: McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y

Douglas McGregor described two views that managers hold of workers.

| Theory X manager believes workers… | Theory Y manager believes workers… |
| --- | --- |
| Dislike work and avoid it if they can | Enjoy work and find it natural, like rest or play |
| Must be controlled, threatened and closely supervised | Can direct themselves if committed to objectives |
| Avoid responsibility and want to be told what to do | Accept and seek responsibility |
| Are motivated only by money | Are motivated by achievement, recognition and creativity |

Theory X managers tend to use an **autocratic** style and close supervision; Theory Y managers tend to be **democratic** and delegate. McGregor argued that Theory Y is a more accurate view of most workers.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying pay is a Herzberg motivator"}
In Herzberg's theory, pay is a **hygiene factor**: low pay causes dissatisfaction, but a pay rise does not motivate in the long run.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing the theory without applying it"}
"Maslow has five levels" earns little. Say which level the case's workers have reached and what the business should do next.
:::

:::mistake{title="Putting the levels of Maslow's pyramid in the wrong order"}
From the bottom: physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Applying theories"}
Name the theory, state the idea briefly, then link it to the case: "The machinists are paid only a basic wage, so according to Taylor a piece-rate system would raise output." Then add a limitation that matters in that case.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
Cambridge 0264 assesses Maslow, Taylor and Herzberg. ZIMSEC Business Enterprise and Skills adds McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y. Both reward linking motivation to productivity, absenteeism and labour turnover.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- People work for money, security, friendship, status and self-fulfilment.
- Motivated workers give higher productivity, lower absenteeism and labour turnover and accept change.
- Maslow: five levels of needs met in order, from physiological to self-actualisation.
- Taylor: workers are motivated by money; use piece rate and efficient methods.
- Herzberg: hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators (responsibility, recognition) motivate.
- McGregor (ZIMSEC): Theory X workers need control; Theory Y workers seek responsibility.
:::
