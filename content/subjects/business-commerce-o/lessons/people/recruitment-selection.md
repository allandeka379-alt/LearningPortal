## Introduction

A pharmaceutical wholesaler in Bulawayo needs a new warehouse supervisor. If it appoints the wrong person, stock may be lost, staff may be badly managed and the cost of advertising, interviewing and training will have been wasted. Finding the right person is the job of **recruitment and selection**, usually carried out by the human resources department.

Cambridge 0264 (topic 2.3.1) examines the whole process: why a business needs to recruit, the documents it uses, where it advertises, how it selects, and how to recommend which candidate to appoint from data such as a table of applicants. ZIMSEC learners meet the same ideas when studying people in business, but this topic is examined mainly by Cambridge.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Recruitment"}
The process of identifying the need for a new employee, defining the job and the person needed, and attracting suitable applicants.
:::

:::definition{term="Selection"}
The process of choosing the most suitable applicant for the job from those who have applied.
:::

:::definition{term="Job description"}
A document that sets out the title, duties, responsibilities and working conditions of a job.
:::

:::definition{term="Person specification"}
A document that sets out the qualifications, skills, experience and personal qualities needed by the person who will do the job.
:::

:::definition{term="Internal recruitment"}
Filling a vacancy with someone who already works for the business.
:::

:::definition{term="External recruitment"}
Filling a vacancy with someone from outside the business.
:::

## Why businesses recruit

- An employee leaves, retires or is promoted.
- The business is growing or opening a new branch.
- New skills are needed, for example to run new technology.

## The recruitment process

1. **Identify the vacancy**: is a new person really needed, or can the work be shared or done by machines?
2. **Job analysis**: decide exactly what the job involves.
3. **Write the job description** (the job) and the **person specification** (the person).
4. **Advertise** the vacancy internally or externally.
5. **Shortlist**: compare applicants' forms or CVs with the person specification and choose the best few.
6. **Select**: interview, test and check references.
7. **Appoint**: offer the job and agree a contract of employment.

| Job description | Person specification |
| --- | --- |
| Job title | Qualifications required (e.g. a diploma in logistics) |
| Department and to whom the job holder reports | Experience required (e.g. two years supervising staff) |
| Main duties and responsibilities | Skills (e.g. computer stock systems, driving licence) |
| Working hours, place and conditions | Personal qualities (e.g. honest, organised, good communicator) |
| Pay and benefits (sometimes) | Essential and desirable criteria |

The job description helps applicants decide whether to apply and later becomes part of the contract. The person specification helps managers shortlist fairly by comparing every applicant against the same criteria.

## Internal and external recruitment

| | Internal | External |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advantages | Cheaper and quicker; the person already knows the business; the business knows the person's ability; promotion motivates staff | Brings new ideas and skills; a wider choice of applicants; avoids jealousy among existing staff |
| Disadvantages | No new ideas; creates another vacancy to fill; other staff may be jealous; limited choice | More expensive (adverts, interviews); takes longer; the new person needs induction; risk that the person does not fit in |

### Where to advertise externally

- **Newspapers and magazines** (national for senior or specialist jobs; local for others).
- **Online recruitment**: the business's website, job websites and social media; cheap and reaches many people quickly, but may attract too many unsuitable applicants.
- **Employment agencies**: find and shortlist candidates for a fee; save the business time.
- **Notice boards, radio and recruitment fairs** at colleges and universities.

### What a good job advertisement contains

A job advertisement should attract suitable applicants and discourage unsuitable ones. It usually states the job title, the main duties, the essential qualifications and experience, the place of work, the pay or pay range (or "competitive salary"), how to apply (application form, CV, email address) and the closing date. A short advert in the wrong place, or one that leaves out the essential requirements, produces either too few applicants or hundreds of unsuitable ones, which wastes managers' time in shortlisting.

### The cost of getting it wrong

Recruiting the wrong person is expensive. The business pays for the advert and the interviews, then for induction and training, and may lose output and customers while the new employee makes mistakes. If the person leaves or must be dismissed, the whole process starts again. Careful recruitment and selection are therefore a way of controlling costs as well as finding talent.

## Selection methods

| Method | What it shows | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application form | Standard information from every applicant, easy to compare | Applicants may exaggerate |
| Curriculum vitae (CV) | The applicant's own summary of education, experience and skills | Every CV is laid out differently |
| References | A previous employer's or teacher's view of honesty and reliability | Referees may be too kind |
| Interview | Communication skills, personality, answers to questions | Some good people are nervous; interviewers may be biased |
| Tests (skills, aptitude, personality) | Actual ability, e.g. typing speed, driving, numeracy | Cost and time; may not show how the person works in a team |

:::example{title="Choosing a candidate"}
A hotel in Victoria Falls is appointing a front-office supervisor. The person specification requires a hospitality diploma (essential), two years' supervisory experience (essential) and a second language (desirable).

| Candidate | Diploma | Supervisory experience | Second language | Interview score (out of 10) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rudo (internal) | Yes | 3 years | No | 7 |
| Farai (external) | Yes | 1 year | Yes | 9 |
| Chipo (external) | Yes | 4 years | Yes | 6 |

Farai does not meet the essential experience requirement, so he should not be appointed despite the best interview. Rudo and Chipo both meet the essential criteria. Chipo has more experience and the desirable language, but Rudo scored higher at interview and already knows the hotel, so she would be cheaper and quicker to start and her promotion would motivate other staff. A good answer chooses one and justifies it: for example Rudo, because her stronger interview and knowledge of the hotel outweigh the missing desirable criterion.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the two documents"}
The job description describes the **job** (duties). The person specification describes the **person** (qualifications, skills, qualities).
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing recruitment with selection"}
Recruitment attracts applicants; selection chooses between them.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring essential criteria"}
When recommending a candidate from a table, first remove anyone who fails an essential requirement, then compare the rest.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Recommend whom to employ"}
Use every column of the data. Eliminate candidates who fail essential criteria, compare the remaining candidates on two or three factors, consider internal versus external, and give a justified choice.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
This concept is in Cambridge 0264 (topic 2.3.1), which expects you to know the documents, advertising methods and selection methods and to apply them to a case.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Recruitment attracts applicants; selection chooses the best one.
- The job description sets out the job; the person specification sets out the person needed.
- Internal recruitment is cheaper and motivating; external brings new skills and wider choice.
- Vacancies are advertised in newspapers, online, through agencies and at recruitment fairs.
- Selection uses application forms, CVs, references, tests and interviews.
- Recommend a candidate by checking essential criteria first, then comparing the rest.
:::
