## Introduction

When Tatenda was offered a job as a forklift driver at a warehouse in Harare, his new employer gave him a document to sign. It stated his job title, his hours, his pay, his leave and how much notice either side must give to end the job. Two years later, when a new manager tried to cut his hours without warning, Tatenda could point to that document. It was his **contract of employment**.

Governments also pass laws that protect workers, whatever their contract says: laws against unfair dismissal and discrimination, laws on health and safety and, in many countries, minimum wages. Cambridge 0264 (topic 2.3.2) asks what a contract contains, why it benefits both sides, and how legal controls affect employers and employees. Answers should explain effects on **both** groups.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Contract of employment"}
A legal agreement between an employer and an employee that sets out the terms and conditions of the job.
:::

:::definition{term="Dismissal"}
Ending an employee's contract because of the employee's conduct or ability, for example theft or repeated lateness after warnings.
:::

:::definition{term="Unfair dismissal"}
Ending an employee's contract without a fair reason or without following a fair procedure.
:::

:::definition{term="Discrimination"}
Treating a person less favourably because of, for example, gender, race, religion, age or disability.
:::

:::definition{term="Minimum wage"}
The lowest wage rate that employers are allowed by law to pay workers.
:::

## Contents of a contract of employment

| Content | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Names of employer and employee | Kopje Logistics (Pvt) Ltd and Tatenda Moyo |
| Job title and main duties | Forklift driver: loading and unloading trucks |
| Start date and place of work | 1 March, Workington warehouse |
| Hours of work | 07:30 to 16:30, Monday to Friday |
| Pay and how often it is paid | Monthly wage, paid on the last working day |
| Holiday and sick-leave entitlement | Days of paid leave per year |
| Notice period | How much warning either side must give to end the contract |
| Disciplinary and grievance procedures | Warnings before dismissal; how to make a complaint |
| Benefits | Medical aid, pension, protective clothing |

### Benefits of a contract

**To the employee:**
- Knows the pay, hours and duties, so cannot be made to work unfair hours or cut pay without agreement.
- Has evidence if the employer breaks the agreement.
- Gains security from the notice period.

**To the employer:**
- The employee's duties and hours are clear, so the employer can expect them to be done.
- Disputes are reduced because both sides know the terms.
- The notice period gives time to find a replacement if an employee resigns.

## Legal controls over employment

### Unfair dismissal

The law protects employees from being dismissed without a fair reason and a fair procedure (for example, warnings and a hearing). Fair reasons usually include serious misconduct (theft, violence), repeated poor performance after warnings, or redundancy.

- **Effect on employees:** greater job security; they can challenge unfair treatment.
- **Effect on employers:** must keep records and follow procedures; may have to pay compensation or reinstate a worker if they act unfairly.

### Discrimination

Laws require equal treatment in recruitment, pay, training and promotion regardless of gender, race, religion, disability and similar characteristics.

- **Effect on employees:** fair chances of jobs and promotion; equal pay for equal work.
- **Effect on employers:** must use fair recruitment methods; recruitment is from a wider pool of talent; breaking the law risks fines, compensation and a damaged reputation.

### Health and safety

Laws require employers to provide safe working conditions: protective equipment, safe machinery, training, first aid, fire exits and safe hours.

- **Effect on employees:** fewer accidents and illnesses.
- **Effect on employers:** costs of equipment, training and inspections; but fewer days lost to injury, fewer compensation claims and better motivation.

:::example{title="Safety at a quarry"}
A quarry near Kwekwe must provide helmets, dust masks and ear protection, train workers to use blasting equipment safely and keep a first-aid kit on site. This adds to its costs, but it reduces injuries, lost working days and the risk of the quarry being closed by inspectors after an accident.
:::

### Minimum wage

Where a minimum wage applies, employers cannot pay less than the legal rate.

- **Effect on employees:** low-paid workers earn more, can afford basic needs and may be more motivated.
- **Effect on employers:** higher wage costs, which may lead to higher prices, lower profit, fewer workers employed or more use of machines.

:::example{title="A restaurant facing a higher minimum wage"}
Rudo's restaurant in Gweru employs eight waiters on low wages. If the minimum wage rises, her costs rise. She could raise menu prices, reduce staff hours, or accept a lower profit. Higher pay may also reduce labour turnover, saving recruitment and training costs.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Only giving the effect on one side"}
Questions usually say "employers and employees". Every law brings benefits and costs to each side; cover both.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing dismissal with redundancy"}
Dismissal is because of the employee's conduct or ability. Redundancy is because the job is no longer needed. (See the lesson on downsizing and redundancy.)
:::

:::mistake{title="Stating real legal rates"}
Do not quote figures for minimum wages or notice periods unless the question gives them. Explain the effect instead.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Effects questions"}
For "explain the effect of a minimum wage on a business", give the cost effect, then a consequence (higher prices, fewer workers, lower profit), then a possible benefit (more motivated workers). Apply it to the business in the case.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A contract of employment sets out job title, duties, hours, pay, leave, notice and procedures.
- Contracts give employees security and employers clear expectations; both sides avoid disputes.
- Laws against unfair dismissal and discrimination protect workers but require employers to follow fair procedures.
- Health and safety laws reduce accidents but add costs.
- A minimum wage raises low pay but increases employers' costs, which may affect prices and jobs.
:::
