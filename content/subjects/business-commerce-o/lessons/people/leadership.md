## Introduction

Two welding workshops in Mutare make the same scotch carts. In the first, the owner shouts orders and checks every weld himself; the workers do exactly what they are told and no more. In the second, the owner discusses each order with her team, and a young welder suggested a jig that halved the time to make a cart frame. The skills of the workers are similar. The difference is **leadership**.

Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 3) asks for the qualities of a leader and the main leadership styles. Cambridge 0264 (topic 2.2.3) asks you to explain the styles and, most importantly, to **recommend** a style for a given situation. Expect "explain one advantage of democratic leadership" and "which leadership style should the manager use? Justify your answer."

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Leadership"}
Influencing, guiding and inspiring other people so that they work willingly towards the objectives of the business.
:::

:::definition{term="Autocratic leadership"}
A style in which the leader makes all the decisions alone and gives orders, with little or no consultation with employees.
:::

:::definition{term="Democratic leadership"}
A style in which the leader involves employees in decision-making, discusses ideas with them and takes their views into account.
:::

:::definition{term="Laissez-faire leadership"}
A style in which the leader gives broad objectives and leaves employees to make most decisions and organise their own work.
:::

## Qualities of a good leader

| Quality | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Clear vision and decisiveness | Employees know where the business is going and decisions are not delayed |
| Good communication | Instructions and objectives are understood; employees feel listened to |
| Self-confidence | Others trust a leader who is sure of the direction |
| Honesty and integrity | Employees respect and follow a leader who keeps promises |
| Knowledge of the work | The leader can solve problems and earn respect |
| Ability to motivate | Workers give extra effort |
| Willingness to take risks | New opportunities are not missed |
| Fairness and empathy | Employees feel valued, which lowers conflict |

A manager is appointed to a position; a leader is followed because of how he or she behaves. The best managers are also good leaders.

## The three leadership styles

### Autocratic leadership

The leader decides alone and tells employees what to do. Communication is mainly one-way, from the top down.

- **Advantages:** quick decisions; clear instructions; works well in emergencies, with new or unskilled staff, and where safety rules must be followed exactly.
- **Disadvantages:** employees' ideas are not used; workers may feel undervalued, so motivation falls and labour turnover rises; the business depends heavily on one person.

### Democratic leadership

The leader shares information, asks for views and may let the group help make the decision. Communication is two-way.

- **Advantages:** employees feel valued, so motivation and commitment rise; more ideas are considered, so decisions may be better; employees accept change more easily because they helped shape it.
- **Disadvantages:** decisions take longer; not suitable in a crisis; the leader may still have to overrule the group, causing disappointment.

### Laissez-faire leadership

The leader sets broad objectives and leaves employees to decide how to reach them. ("Laissez-faire" is French for "let them do".)

- **Advantages:** suits highly skilled, self-motivated staff, such as designers or researchers; encourages creativity and independence.
- **Disadvantages:** weak coordination; some employees lack direction and become less productive; mistakes may go unnoticed.

| | Autocratic | Democratic | Laissez-faire |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who decides | Leader alone | Leader with employees | Mainly employees |
| Communication | One-way, downwards | Two-way | Mainly between employees |
| Speed of decisions | Fast | Slower | Varies |
| Best for | Emergencies, unskilled staff, safety-critical work | Skilled staff, changes that need acceptance | Highly skilled, creative, self-motivated staff |

## Choosing a style

No style is always best. To recommend a style, look at:

1. **The employees**: their skill, experience and motivation.
2. **The task**: is it routine, creative or dangerous?
3. **Time**: is a decision needed immediately?
4. **The situation**: a crisis, a major change, or normal running.

:::example{title="A fire at a timber yard"}
A fire breaks out at a timber yard in Mutare. The yard manager must give clear orders immediately: who calls the fire brigade, who moves the vehicles, where workers gather. An **autocratic** style is right here, because there is no time to consult and safety comes first.
:::

:::example{title="Introducing a new computer system"}
A medical-aid office in Harare is introducing a new claims system. The staff are experienced clerks who will have to use it every day. A **democratic** style would let them suggest how the system should be set up and how training should be organised, so they are more likely to accept the change and use the system properly.
:::

:::example{title="A design studio"}
Three graphic designers in Bulawayo create adverts for clients. They are skilled and motivated. The owner agrees deadlines and budgets with each client, then lets the designers decide how to do the work: a **laissez-faire** style that encourages creativity.
:::

A good leader may change style with the situation: autocratic during an emergency, democratic when planning a change.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying democratic leadership is always best"}
Democratic leadership motivates, but it is slow. In an emergency or with untrained workers an autocratic style may be better. Examiners reward answers that link the style to the situation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing laissez-faire as no leadership at all"}
The laissez-faire leader still sets objectives and is available for advice; it is the method of work that is left to employees.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing qualities without explanation"}
"Good communicator" is only identification. Explain why it helps: employees understand what is expected, so fewer mistakes are made.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Recommend questions"}
Name the style, then give two or three reasons drawn from the case (the employees' skills, the urgency, the type of work), mention a drawback of your choice and say why it matters less here. Finish with a clear judgement.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 3) asks for qualities of a leader and the styles. Cambridge 0264 asks you to analyse the styles and recommend one for a situation. Link leadership to motivation theories (Theory X managers tend to be autocratic; Theory Y managers democratic) for extra analysis.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Leadership means influencing and inspiring others to work towards objectives.
- Good leaders have vision, communicate well, are honest, knowledgeable and able to motivate.
- Autocratic: leader decides alone; quick, but can demotivate.
- Democratic: employees involved; motivating and better decisions, but slower.
- Laissez-faire: employees decide; suits skilled, creative staff but risks lack of direction.
- The right style depends on the employees, the task, the time available and the situation.
:::
