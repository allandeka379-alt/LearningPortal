## Introduction

Walk into any supermarket in Harare and you will find the same brands of cooking oil, sugar and soap in large stacks, bought by almost every household. Nearby, a small shop sells gluten-free flour, baby-led weaning foods and organic honey to a small group of customers who will pay more for exactly what they need. The first products are sold in a **mass market**; the second in **niche markets**.

ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 2, marketing approaches) and Cambridge 0264 (topic 3.1.3) both ask what mass and niche marketing are, and their benefits and limitations. Questions often ask you to recommend which approach suits a particular business, usually a small enterprise deciding whether to compete with large firms or to specialise.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Mass market"}
A very large market in which most customers buy similar, standardised products, such as basic foods, soap or mobile airtime.
:::

:::definition{term="Mass marketing"}
Selling the same product, with the same marketing mix, to the whole market rather than to a particular group of customers.
:::

:::definition{term="Niche market"}
A small, specialised part (segment) of a larger market, in which customers have particular needs or wants.
:::

:::definition{term="Niche marketing"}
Identifying the needs of a small, specific group of customers and developing products and a marketing mix designed for that group.
:::

## Mass marketing

A business using mass marketing aims at as many customers as possible. Products are standardised and produced in large quantities, prices are usually low, and promotion uses national media.

### Advantages of mass marketing

- **Large sales volume**: a huge number of potential customers.
- **Economies of scale**: producing and buying in bulk lowers the average cost per unit, so prices can be low and profit per unit still acceptable.
- **Lower risk** than relying on one small group: if some customers stop buying, many others remain.
- **Strong brand recognition** from wide advertising and distribution.

### Disadvantages of mass marketing

- **Intense competition**, often on price, so profit margins per unit may be small.
- **High costs** of production capacity, national advertising and wide distribution, which small firms cannot afford.
- **Customers' particular needs are not met**, so some may switch to specialised products.
- A standard product may be seen as ordinary, making loyalty harder to build.

:::example{title="A mass-market product"}
A fictional maize-meal mill produces one standard 10 kg bag sold in supermarkets, wholesalers and rural stores across Zimbabwe. It buys grain in very large quantities and runs its mill day and night, so its cost per bag is low. It advertises on national radio. Its price must stay close to rivals', because customers see maize meal as much the same whichever brand they choose.
:::

## Niche marketing

A business using niche marketing serves a small group of customers with specific needs. Products are specialised, prices are often higher, and promotion is targeted at the group, for example through specialist shops, social-media groups or magazines.

### Advantages of niche marketing

- **Less competition**, because large firms often ignore small markets.
- **Higher prices** can be charged, because customers value products made for them, so profit margins per unit can be high.
- **Customer loyalty**: customers who find a product that meets their exact needs tend to stay.
- **Suits small businesses**, which cannot compete with large firms on cost in mass markets.
- **Targeted promotion** costs less because it reaches only the people who are likely to buy.

### Disadvantages of niche marketing

- **Small sales volume**, so few economies of scale and higher costs per unit.
- **Higher risk**: if the niche's tastes change or the group shrinks, sales collapse, because the business depends on one market.
- **Attracts competition**: if the niche proves profitable, larger firms may enter it.
- **Limited growth**: the business may outgrow its niche.

:::example{title="A niche-market business"}
Thandeka runs a small bakery in Bulawayo making sugar-free cakes and biscuits for people with diabetes. Supermarkets stock few such products. She sells through a pharmacy, a clinic noticeboard and a social-media group for people managing diabetes. She charges more than ordinary cakes, and many customers return every week. Her risk is that a large bakery may notice her success and launch its own sugar-free range.
:::

## Comparing the two approaches

| | Mass marketing | Niche marketing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size of market | Very large | Small, specialised |
| Product | Standardised | Specialised for the group |
| Price | Usually low | Usually higher |
| Competition | Strong | Less, at first |
| Costs per unit | Low (economies of scale) | Higher |
| Risk | Spread over many customers | Concentrated in one group |
| Typical business | Large firms | Small firms, or large firms adding a specialist range |

Large firms often use **both**: a mass-market main brand and smaller niche brands, for example a premium or health version of the same product.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling a niche market a small business"}
A niche is a small **market** segment. A large company can serve a niche, and a small business can sell to a mass market (for example a vendor selling airtime).
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying niche markets have no competition"}
They have less competition at first, but a profitable niche attracts rivals.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting economies of scale"}
The main cost advantage of mass marketing is economies of scale. Use the term in explanations.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Recommend an approach"}
Look at the size and resources of the business, the competition in the market, the customers' needs and the price they will pay. A small business with limited capital usually suits a niche; a business with large capacity and low costs may suit the mass market. Give a drawback of your choice and conclude.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 2) calls these "marketing approaches". Cambridge 0264 (topic 3.1.3) asks for the benefits and limitations of each approach. Niche marketing links closely to market segmentation.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A mass market is very large, with similar products bought by most customers.
- Mass marketing: high volume, economies of scale, low prices, but intense competition and high costs of entry.
- A niche market is a small, specialised segment with particular needs.
- Niche marketing: less competition, higher prices, loyalty, suits small firms, but small volume and high risk.
- The right approach depends on the business's size, costs and customers.
:::
