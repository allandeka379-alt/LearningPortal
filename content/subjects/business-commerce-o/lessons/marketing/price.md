## Introduction

Sipho makes wooden stools in a home-industry workshop in Bulawayo. If he charges too much, customers buy from the carpenter next door. If he charges too little, he works hard and makes no profit. Every business faces this question: **what price should we charge?**

Price is the only element of the marketing mix that brings in revenue; the others cost money. All three syllabuses cover pricing: ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 3, with mark-up calculations), Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 4) and Cambridge 0264 (topic 3.3.2), which names cost-plus, competitive, penetration, skimming and dynamic pricing and asks you to recommend a method.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Price"}
The amount of money a customer pays to buy a good or service.
:::

:::definition{term="Cost-plus pricing"}
Setting the price by adding a percentage mark-up to the cost of making or buying each unit.
:::

:::definition{term="Competitive pricing"}
Setting a price similar to, or slightly below, the prices charged by competitors.
:::

:::definition{term="Penetration pricing"}
Setting a low price when a product is launched in order to attract customers quickly and win market share, then raising it later.
:::

:::definition{term="Price skimming"}
Setting a high price when a new, unique product is launched, to earn high profit from customers keen to buy it first, then lowering it later.
:::

:::definition{term="Dynamic pricing"}
Changing the price frequently according to demand at the time, often automatically online (for example, higher bus fares at peak times).
:::

## Cost-plus pricing

The business calculates the **cost per unit** and adds a **mark-up** (a percentage of cost) for profit:

$$\text{Price} = \text{cost per unit} + (\text{cost per unit} \times \text{mark-up \%})$$

The cost per unit is the **average cost**: total cost ÷ output, where total cost = fixed costs + variable costs.

:::worked-solution[Cost-plus price from total costs]
Sipho expects to make 600 stools a month. Fixed costs are US\$1,800 a month and the variable cost (wood, nails, varnish) is US\$4.50 a stool. He adds a 40% mark-up.
1. Total variable cost = US\$4.50 × 600 = US\$2,700.
2. Total cost = US\$1,800 + US\$2,700 = US\$4,500.
3. Average cost per stool = US\$4,500 ÷ 600 = US\$7.50.
4. Mark-up = 40% × US\$7.50 = US\$3.00.
5. Price = US\$7.50 + US\$3.00 = **US\$10.50**.
:::

:::worked-solution[Mark-up and margin (ZIMSEC Commerce)]
A retailer buys a pair of shoes for US\$12 and adds a mark-up of 25%.
1. Mark-up = 25% × US\$12 = US\$3, so the selling price = US\$15.
2. The gross profit of US\$3 as a percentage of the **selling price** is the margin: 3 ÷ 15 × 100 = **20%**.
3. A 25% mark-up on cost is the same as a 20% margin on selling price; do not confuse the two.
:::

**Advantages**: simple; every unit sold covers its cost and earns a profit (if sales are as expected).
**Disadvantages**: ignores competitors' prices and what customers will pay; if fewer units are sold than planned, the average cost is higher and the profit smaller.

## Competitive pricing

The business looks at rivals' prices and charges about the same. This is common where many firms sell similar products: bread, cooking oil, kombi fares, airtime.

**Advantages**: avoids losing customers to cheaper rivals; easy where prices are well known.
**Disadvantages**: the price may not cover the business's own costs; the business must compete on other things such as quality, service or location.

## Penetration pricing

A **low launch price** attracts customers away from established brands and builds market share quickly. Once customers are loyal, the price is raised.

**Advantages**: quick growth in sales and market share; high sales volume may bring lower average costs.
**Disadvantages**: low profit (or a loss) at first; customers may not accept later price rises; it can start a price war.

## Price skimming

A **high launch price** for a new, unique or high-technology product, aimed at customers who want to be first. The price is lowered as competitors arrive.

**Advantages**: high profit per unit helps recover research and development costs; a high price supports a quality image.
**Disadvantages**: only works if the product is unique or strongly branded; high profits attract competitors; some customers are put off.

:::example{title="Skimming or penetration?"}
A Harare firm develops a new solar-powered irrigation pump that no rival sells. It could **skim**: charge a high price to commercial farmers who value the pump most, and lower it as rivals appear. A firm launching another brand of washing powder into a crowded market would more likely use **penetration** or **competitive** pricing, because customers can easily choose familiar brands.
:::

## Dynamic pricing

Prices change with demand, often many times a day, usually through online systems. Airlines, bus companies, hotels and ride-hailing apps charge more when demand is high (holidays, rush hour) and less when it is low.

**Advantages**: earns more revenue when demand is high; fills empty seats or rooms when demand is low.
**Disadvantages**: customers may feel it is unfair; needs technology and data; prices are hard for customers to plan for.

## Choosing a pricing method

Ask about the case:

1. **Is the product new? Unique?** Unique → skimming; new in a crowded market → penetration.
2. **How many competitors, and how similar are their products?** Many similar rivals → competitive pricing.
3. **What are the costs?** The price must cover costs in the long run → cost-plus as a check.
4. **What do the target customers earn and value?** Low-income customers are price-sensitive.
5. **Does demand change sharply by time?** Yes → dynamic pricing may suit.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calculating the mark-up on the selling price"}
In cost-plus pricing the mark-up is a percentage of **cost**, not of the selling price.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using skimming for an ordinary product"}
Skimming only works if customers cannot easily buy a similar product elsewhere. For a product with many rivals it will lose sales.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing penetration with competitive pricing"}
Penetration pricing is a **temporary** low price for a **new** product. Competitive pricing matches rivals' prices for as long as the market requires.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Recommend a method"}
Identify the method, apply it to the product and market in the case (how new, how many rivals, what customers can afford), state one drawback, and say why it is still the best choice.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
Cambridge 0264 names dynamic pricing and asks for cost-plus calculations using its cost formulas. ZIMSEC Commerce may combine pricing with mark-up and margin calculations: show the cost, the mark-up and the selling price on separate lines.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Cost-plus: price = cost per unit + mark-up % of cost; simple but ignores competitors and demand.
- Competitive: price close to rivals' prices; avoids losing customers but may not cover costs.
- Penetration: low launch price to win market share; low early profit and later rises may be resisted.
- Skimming: high launch price for a unique product; high profit but attracts rivals.
- Dynamic: prices change with demand; raises revenue but may seem unfair. Choose by product, rivals, costs and customers.
:::
