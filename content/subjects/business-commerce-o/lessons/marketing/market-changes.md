## Introduction

Ten years ago most people in Gweru bought airtime as scratch cards from vendors on street corners. Today many top up through mobile money or a banking app. Vendors who sold only scratch cards have had to sell other goods or close. Markets are always changing: what customers buy, how they buy it and how many businesses compete to sell it.

Cambridge 0264 (topic 3.1.2) asks why spending patterns change, why markets become more competitive, how businesses can respond, and how to calculate **market share**. Market share is one of the printed formulas, so expect a calculation followed by an explanation of what the result shows.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Spending patterns"}
What consumers choose to buy, how much they buy and how they buy it.
:::

:::definition{term="Market size"}
The total sales of all businesses in a market over a period, measured in value (US\$) or in volume (units).
:::

:::definition{term="Market share"}
The percentage of total market sales achieved by one business or brand.
:::

:::formula{name="Market share"}
Market share (%) = sales of the business ÷ total sales in the market × 100
:::

## Why consumer spending patterns change

| Reason | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Changes in income | When incomes rise, people buy more meat, appliances and branded goods; when incomes fall, they buy smaller packs and cheaper brands |
| Changes in tastes and fashion | Growing interest in healthy eating raises demand for wholegrain and low-sugar products |
| New technology | Smartphones led to mobile money, online shopping and app-based taxis |
| Changes in population | A growing number of young people in towns raises demand for data bundles and fast food |
| Prices of other goods | A rise in fuel prices may reduce spending on travel |
| Social and environmental concerns | Some customers choose products with less plastic packaging |

## Why markets become more competitive

- **New businesses enter** the market, attracted by profits.
- **Globalisation and imports** bring foreign products into local shops.
- **E-commerce** lets customers compare prices instantly and buy from sellers far away.
- **Lower barriers**: cheaper technology makes it easier to start a business (for example, selling through social media).
- **Customers are better informed** and more willing to switch.

Stronger competition is good for customers, who get lower prices, better quality and more choice. For businesses it means pressure on prices and profit margins, and the need to keep improving. Firms that do not respond lose customers and market share, even if they were once the market leader.

## How businesses respond to changing markets

1. **Market research** to keep up with changing needs.
2. **Develop new products** or improve existing ones.
3. **Improve quality and customer service** to build loyalty.
4. **Cut costs** so prices can be competitive (for example, more efficient production).
5. **Change promotion**, such as moving advertising onto social media.
6. **Use new channels of distribution**, such as selling online or through mobile apps.
7. **Find new markets**, such as new regions or customer groups.

:::example{title="A bakery responds to change"}
A bakery in Masvingo finds that customers are buying fewer large white loaves and more small, healthy rolls, and that two new supermarkets now bake their own bread. It researches what customers want, introduces wholemeal rolls in small packs, takes orders by mobile messaging and delivers to offices. Its sales of large loaves fall, but the new products help it hold its position in a more competitive market.
:::

## Market share

Market share shows how successful a business is **compared with its competitors**, not just whether its own sales are rising.

:::worked-solution[Calculating market share]
Chipo's dairy sold US\$45,000 of yoghurt last year. Total yoghurt sales in the town were US\$300,000.
1. Market share = sales of the business ÷ total market sales × 100.
2. Market share = 45,000 ÷ 300,000 × 100.
3. Market share = **15%**.
:::

:::worked-solution[Sales rise but market share falls]
| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Business sales US\$ | 30,000 | 36,000 |
| Total market sales US\$ | 250,000 | 400,000 |

1. Year 1 market share = 30,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = 12%.
2. Year 2 market share = 36,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 = 9%.
3. The business's sales rose by 6,000 ÷ 30,000 × 100 = 20%, but the whole market grew by 150,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = 60%.
4. So its market share **fell from 12% to 9%**: competitors grew faster. Rising sales do not always mean a business is doing well against its rivals.
:::

Market share can also be measured by **volume**, for example the number of bags or units sold, when the question gives quantities instead of values. The method is the same: the business's units ÷ the market's units × 100.

### Why market share matters

- A high market share can make a business the **market leader**, which helps it get shelf space from retailers and lets customers recognise its brand.
- It may gain **economies of scale** (lower average costs) from producing more.
- A falling market share is a warning that competitors are taking customers, even if sales are still rising.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Dividing the wrong way round"}
Divide the business's sales by the **total market** sales, not the other way. A market share above 100% is impossible.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing sales growth with market share"}
A business's sales can rise while its market share falls, if the market grows faster.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing value and volume"}
Use the same measure for both figures: sales value ÷ market value, or units ÷ units.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Spending patterns change with income, tastes, technology, population and prices.
- Markets become more competitive through new entrants, imports, e-commerce and informed customers.
- Businesses respond with research, new products, better service, lower costs, new promotion and new channels.
- Market share (%) = sales of the business ÷ total market sales × 100.
- Sales can rise while market share falls if the market grows faster.
:::
