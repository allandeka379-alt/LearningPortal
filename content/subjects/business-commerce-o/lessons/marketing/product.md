## Introduction

Walk through Mbare Musika and you will see two sellers offering peanut butter. One scoops it from a large tub into a plastic bag. The other sells it in a clean jar with a printed label, a name, a picture of a farmer and a "best before" date, at a higher price. Many customers still choose the jar. Why? Because customers do not buy just the peanut butter: they buy a **product**, which includes its brand, its packaging and what it promises.

Product is the first of the **four Ps** of the marketing mix (product, price, place and promotion). ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 3) and Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 4) both cover the product element; Cambridge 0264 (topic 3.3.1) asks about brand image, the roles of packaging and the costs and benefits of developing new products. The product life cycle is covered in its own lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Product"}
A good or service offered to customers to satisfy their needs and wants, together with features such as its design, quality, brand and packaging.
:::

:::definition{term="Brand"}
A name, symbol, logo or design that identifies a product and makes it different from competitors' products.
:::

:::definition{term="Brand image"}
The ideas and feelings customers have about a brand, such as quality, reliability, value or luxury.
:::

:::definition{term="Brand loyalty"}
When customers keep buying the same brand rather than switching to rivals.
:::

:::definition{term="Packaging"}
The physical container or wrapping of a product, including the label and printed information.
:::

## What makes a good product?

A product must **meet customer needs** at a price customers will pay and at a cost that lets the business make a profit. Customers judge products on:

- **Function and quality**: does it do the job and last?
- **Design and appearance**: size, colour, style.
- **Brand and reputation**: can I trust it?
- **After-sales service and guarantees**, especially for expensive goods.

Goods are physical (a bag of maize meal, a solar lamp). **Services** are not physical (a haircut, a bus journey, insurance cover), so their "product" is the quality of the service, the skill of the staff and the customer's experience.

## Brands and brand image

A strong brand helps a business in several ways:

- Customers **recognise** the product quickly on a crowded shelf.
- A good brand image allows a **higher price**, because customers trust the quality.
- It builds **brand loyalty**, so repeat sales are more likely and the business is less affected by rivals' price cuts.
- New products launched under the same brand are trusted more quickly.

Building a brand is costly: it needs consistent quality, advertising and time. One scandal (a contaminated batch, a fake product) can damage a brand image built over many years. **Own-label** brands sold by large retailers compete on lower prices.

:::example{title="Building a brand image"}
Thandeka makes body lotion from marula oil in Bulawayo. She chooses the brand name "Marula Glow", uses the same green label on every jar and stresses on social media that the oil is collected by rural women's groups. Customers come to see the brand as natural and local, so she can charge more than unbranded lotions sold at the market.
:::

## The roles of packaging

| Role | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Protects the product | Prevents breakage, spoiling, leaks and contamination during transport and storage |
| Gives information | Ingredients, instructions, weight, price, expiry date, the manufacturer's address, warnings |
| Attracts attention | Colours, shapes and pictures make the product stand out on the shelf |
| Builds the brand | Logo, colours and design make the product recognisable |
| Makes the product convenient | Easy to carry, open, store and reuse; right size for the customer |
| Helps sales promotion | Space for special offers, competitions and coupons |

Packaging adds to **costs**, and too much packaging creates **waste**, which some customers dislike. Packaging that is reusable, recyclable or made from less material can improve a firm's image.

:::example{title="Packaging for different customers"}
A maize-meal mill packs its product in 10 kg bags for families and 2 kg bags for students and people buying daily from tuck shops. The smaller bag costs more per kilogram to pack, but it makes the product affordable for customers who cannot pay for 10 kg at once.
:::

## Developing new products

Businesses develop new products to replace products in decline, to attract new customers, to keep up with competitors and to spread risk across several products. The usual stages are:

1. **Generating ideas**: from customers, staff, competitors and research.
2. **Screening**: rejecting ideas that are too costly, unprofitable or do not fit the business.
3. **Developing and testing a prototype**: a trial version is made and tested.
4. **Test marketing**: selling in one area to see how customers react.
5. **Launch**: full production, distribution and promotion.

| Advantages of developing new products | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| New sources of revenue and profit | Research and development are expensive |
| Attracts new customers and markets | Many new products fail, so money is lost |
| Keeps the business ahead of competitors | Takes time before any revenue is earned |
| Spreads risk over a wider range of products | May need new machinery and staff training |
| Can improve the business's image as innovative | Can take resources away from existing products |

## ZIMSEC Commerce: the product in the marketing mix

Commerce learners study the product together with price, place and promotion. Be ready to explain how a trader's choice of products (range, quality, brands stocked) affects sales, and why manufacturers brand and package goods for retailers.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying a brand is just a name"}
A brand includes the name, logo, design and the image customers have of it. Explain what the brand makes customers think or feel.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing packaging roles without applying them"}
"It protects the product" earns little on its own. Say what it protects against for **this** product, such as milk spoiling or eggs breaking.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring the costs of new products"}
New products are not automatically good. Always weigh the cost and risk of failure against the possible revenue.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Product questions in cases"}
Use the case's product. For a perishable food, protection and information (expiry dates) matter most; for a luxury product, attractive packaging and brand image matter most.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
Cambridge 0264 often asks you to explain the importance of brand image or to evaluate developing a new product. ZIMSEC questions on the marketing mix may ask you to describe the product element with branding and packaging, then discuss it with the other three Ps.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Product is the first P of the marketing mix: the good or service plus its quality, design, brand and packaging.
- A strong brand image builds recognition and loyalty and allows a higher price, but is costly to build and easy to damage.
- Packaging protects, informs, attracts, builds the brand and adds convenience, but adds cost and waste.
- New products are developed through ideas, screening, prototypes, test marketing and launch.
- New products bring revenue and spread risk but are costly and often fail.
:::
