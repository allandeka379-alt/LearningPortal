## Introduction

A farmer in Nyanga grows apples. A family in Bulawayo, on the other side of the country, wants to buy them. How do the apples get from the orchard to the family's kitchen? They might pass through a wholesaler in Harare, then a supermarket in Bulawayo. Or the farmer might sell some directly from a stall at the farm gate, or online with home delivery. Each route is a **channel of distribution**.

"Place" is the element of the marketing mix that makes sure the product is available **where and when** customers want to buy it. ZIMSEC Commerce calls this the **chain of distribution** (Form 2); Business Enterprise and Skills covers it in the marketing mix (Form 4); Cambridge 0264 (topic 3.3.3) asks about the advantages and disadvantages of different channels, including selling online. You must be able to recommend a channel for a business in a case.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Channel of distribution (chain of distribution)"}
The route a product takes from the producer to the final consumer, and the intermediaries it passes through.
:::

:::definition{term="Intermediary (middleman)"}
A business between the producer and the consumer, such as a wholesaler, retailer or agent, that helps the product reach the consumer.
:::

:::definition{term="Wholesaler"}
A business that buys goods in bulk from producers and sells them in smaller quantities to retailers.
:::

:::definition{term="Retailer"}
A business that sells goods in small quantities to the final consumer.
:::

:::definition{term="Agent"}
A person or business that arranges sales on behalf of a producer, usually for a commission, without owning the goods; often used for selling in other countries.
:::

## The main channels

These are usually drawn as arrows; here they are written out as chains.

![Four channels of distribution: producer to consumer directly; producer, retailer, consumer; producer, wholesaler, retailer, consumer; and producer, agent, wholesaler or retailer, consumer. More intermediaries means more mark-ups but wider reach.](media:diagrams/channels-of-distribution.png "Figure: the four main channels of distribution")

| Channel | Route | Typical examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Direct | Producer → consumer | Farm-gate sales, a tailor making clothes to order, online sales with delivery, a hairdresser |
| 2. Through a retailer | Producer → retailer → consumer | A bakery supplying tuck shops; a car maker supplying dealers |
| 3. Through a wholesaler and retailer | Producer → wholesaler → retailer → consumer | Soap, sugar, cooking oil and other groceries sold in small shops across the country |
| 4. Through an agent | Producer → agent → (wholesaler / retailer) → consumer | Exporters using an agent in another country; agents for some imported goods |

## Advantages and disadvantages of each channel

**Direct channel**

- Advantages: the producer keeps all the profit (no intermediaries' mark-ups); close contact with customers and quick feedback; control over how the product is sold.
- Disadvantages: the producer must do all the storage, transport, selling and promotion; it is hard to reach customers far away; it suits small volumes or specialised products.

**Through retailers**

- Advantages: products reach many customers in convenient locations; retailers display and promote the products; the producer deals with fewer, larger customers.
- Disadvantages: retailers take a mark-up, so either the price is higher or the producer earns less; the producer has less control over display and price.

**Through wholesalers and retailers**

- Advantages: wholesalers **break bulk**, store goods, and deliver to thousands of small retailers that a producer could not reach; producers sell large quantities in a few orders and receive payment sooner.
- Disadvantages: two mark-ups raise the final price; slower route, which matters for perishable goods; less contact with final customers.

**Through agents**

- Advantages: the agent knows the local market, language and laws, which helps a business selling abroad; agents are paid commission only on sales made.
- Disadvantages: commission reduces profit; the producer depends on the agent's honesty and effort.

:::example{title="Different channels for one business"}
Farai bakes bread and buns in Kwekwe. He sells fresh bread **directly** from his bakery shop, delivers bread every morning to 40 tuck shops (**through retailers**), and sells his longer-lasting rusks to a **wholesaler**, who supplies shops in rural areas he could never reach himself. Using several channels lets him reach more customers.
:::

## Selling online

Many businesses now sell online through their own websites, social media pages and messaging apps, or through online marketplaces. Online selling is usually a **direct** channel.

- Advantages: reaches customers anywhere; open all the time; lower costs than a shop; payment by mobile money or card.
- Disadvantages: delivery costs and delays; customers cannot see or touch the goods; risk of fraud; customers need internet access.

## Choosing a channel

| Factor | Effect on the choice |
| --- | --- |
| Type of product | Perishable goods (bread, milk, vegetables) need short channels; technical or expensive goods often need direct sales or specialist retailers who can explain them |
| Number and location of customers | Many customers spread across the country → wholesalers and retailers; few customers or one town → direct |
| Size and resources of the producer | A small producer may lack vehicles and staff, so uses intermediaries; a large firm can afford its own outlets and delivery |
| Cost and price | Each intermediary adds a mark-up; a direct channel can lower the price or raise the producer's profit |
| Control | Direct channels keep control of display, price and service |
| Where competitors sell | Customers expect to find the product where rivals' products are |
| Selling abroad | Agents help with unfamiliar markets |

:::example{title="Recommending a channel"}
Rudo makes expensive handmade leather bags in Harare. Her customers are tourists and wealthy buyers. A long channel through wholesalers would add mark-ups and put her bags beside cheap ones. She should sell **directly** online and from her workshop, and through a few high-quality gift shops at Victoria Falls, keeping control of her brand image.
:::

## ZIMSEC Commerce: eliminating the wholesaler

Commerce questions often ask whether the wholesaler can be **eliminated**. Producers sell directly to retailers or consumers when they have their own transport and warehouses, when goods are perishable or made to order, when large supermarket chains buy in bulk directly, and when online selling allows direct delivery. The wholesaler's **functions** (breaking bulk, storage, transport, credit, information) still have to be carried out by someone, so costs do not simply disappear.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying intermediaries only add cost"}
Intermediaries add a mark-up, but they also carry out work (storage, transport, display, credit) the producer would otherwise have to pay for.
:::

:::mistake{title="Choosing a long channel for perishable goods"}
Each extra intermediary adds time. Fresh milk, bread and vegetables need short channels.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting online selling is still a channel"}
Selling online is usually a direct channel, but it still needs delivery and payment arrangements.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Recommend a channel"}
Name the channel as a chain (producer → retailer → consumer), apply at least two factors from the case (product, customers, size of the business), give one drawback and conclude.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
ZIMSEC Commerce uses "chain of distribution" and may ask about the role and elimination of the wholesaler. Cambridge 0264 uses "channels of distribution" and expects you to discuss online selling and to recommend a channel using evidence from the case.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Place makes products available where and when customers want them, through channels of distribution.
- Channels: direct; through retailers; through wholesalers and retailers; through agents.
- Short channels give control and higher profit per unit; long channels reach many customers but add mark-ups and time.
- Online selling is usually a direct channel with wide reach but delivery and trust problems.
- Choose by product type, customers, the producer's size and resources, cost, control, competitors and whether selling abroad.
:::
