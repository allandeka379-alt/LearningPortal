## Introduction

Two young women in Mutare start making fruit juice. Chipo makes the juice she likes best, in large bottles, and then tries to persuade shops to stock it. Nyasha first visits tuck shops and schools, asks what children and parents want, and discovers that small, cheap sachets with less sugar would sell well. She makes those. A year later, Nyasha's business is growing, and Chipo has a store room full of unsold bottles. The difference is **marketing**.

Marketing is examined in all three syllabi: ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 1: the need for marketing and the marketing concepts), Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 1: the need for marketing) and Cambridge 0264 (topic 3.1.1: the role of marketing). It is the foundation for every later marketing topic: market research, segmentation and the marketing mix.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Marketing"}
The management process of identifying, anticipating and satisfying customers' needs and wants profitably.
:::

:::definition{term="Customer needs and wants"}
Needs are the things customers must have, such as food and shelter; wants are the particular goods and services they would like, such as a certain brand or flavour.
:::

:::definition{term="Customer loyalty"}
When customers keep buying from the same business instead of switching to competitors.
:::

:::definition{term="Market orientation"}
An approach in which a business finds out what customers want before it develops and sells a product.
:::

:::definition{term="Product orientation"}
An approach in which a business makes the product it is good at making or believes is best, and then looks for customers.
:::

## The role of marketing

Marketing is much more than advertising. Its main roles are to:

1. **Identify customer needs**: find out through market research what customers want, at what price and where they buy.
2. **Satisfy customer needs**: offer the right product, at the right price, in the right place, with the right promotion.
3. **Maintain customer loyalty**: keep customers coming back through good quality, service and after-sales care.
4. **Build relationships with customers**: communicate with them (for example through social media or loyalty cards) so that the business understands them and they trust the business.
5. **Anticipate changes in customer needs**: spot trends early, such as healthier eating or mobile payments, so the business can adapt before rivals do.

### The objectives of marketing

Marketing helps a business to:

- raise sales revenue and profit;
- increase **market share**;
- create awareness of a new product or business;
- improve the image of the business and its brands;
- enter new markets.

:::example{title="Marketing at a hardware store"}
A hardware store in Kwekwe notices that many customers are installing solar panels. It researches which panels and batteries they want (identifying needs), stocks them at competitive prices and offers installation advice (satisfying needs), gives a discount card to builders who buy regularly (loyalty), sends messages about new stock to customers who sign up (relationships) and starts to stock solar water heaters as interest grows (anticipating change).
:::

## The four marketing concepts (business orientations)

Over time, businesses have taken four different approaches to selling. ZIMSEC Commerce names them the production, product, selling and marketing concepts.

| Concept | Main belief | Focus | Implication for the business |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Production concept | Customers want products that are cheap and easy to find | Producing large quantities efficiently at low cost | Works when demand exceeds supply; risks ignoring what customers actually want |
| Product concept | Customers want the highest quality and most features | Improving the product itself | Good products may be too expensive or not what customers need |
| Selling concept | Customers will not buy enough unless persuaded | Aggressive advertising and sales techniques | Short-term sales, but customers may feel pressured and not return |
| Marketing concept | Success comes from finding and satisfying customer needs better than competitors | The customer: research first, then produce | Higher customer satisfaction and loyalty; needs spending on research |

:::example{title="The four concepts at a bakery"}
- **Production concept:** a bakery bakes thousands of identical white loaves as cheaply as possible.
- **Product concept:** it develops an expensive seeded loaf that its bakers think is the best bread in the city.
- **Selling concept:** it hires sales staff to push surplus loaves into shops with heavy discounts.
- **Marketing concept:** it asks customers what they want and finds demand for smaller loaves for single-person households and wholemeal bread for health-conscious buyers, then bakes those.
:::

### Why most businesses now follow the marketing concept

- **Competition** is stronger, so customers can easily switch to a rival who meets their needs better.
- **Customers are better informed** through the internet and social media.
- **Tastes change quickly**, so businesses that do not listen are left with unsold stock.
- Satisfied customers return and recommend the business, which is cheaper than finding new customers.

The marketing concept does have **costs**: market research, product changes and time. A small business may also lack the money to change its product for every customer group.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking marketing means only advertising"}
Advertising is one part of promotion, which is one part of the marketing mix. Marketing starts with identifying customer needs.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the product concept with the marketing concept"}
The product concept focuses on making the best product as the business sees it. The marketing concept starts from what **customers** want.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing the selling concept with the marketing concept"}
The selling concept persuades customers to buy what has already been made. The marketing concept makes what customers want, so less persuasion is needed.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Explain the role of marketing"}
Name a role (for example, building customer loyalty), explain how marketing achieves it, and say why it helps the business (repeat sales, higher revenue). Apply it to the business in the case.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 1) expects the four concepts by name and their meaning. Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 1) asks why an enterprise needs marketing. Cambridge 0264 (3.1.1) asks for the role of marketing: identifying and satisfying needs, loyalty, relationships and anticipating change.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Marketing identifies, anticipates and satisfies customer needs profitably.
- Its roles: identify needs, satisfy needs, keep loyalty, build relationships, anticipate change.
- Production concept: cheap mass output. Product concept: the best product. Selling concept: persuade customers.
- Marketing concept: find out what customers want first, then supply it better than rivals.
- Most businesses now follow the marketing concept because of competition and changing tastes.
:::
