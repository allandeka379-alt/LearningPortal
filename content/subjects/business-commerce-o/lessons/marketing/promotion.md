## Introduction

Nyasha has opened a small hair salon in Chinhoyi. Her skills are excellent and her prices are fair, but in the first month few customers come in, because few people know she exists. She needs **promotion**: communicating with customers to tell them about her salon and persuade them to try it.

Promotion is the fourth P of the marketing mix. ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 3) studies advertising and sales promotion in detail; Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 4) includes promotion in the marketing mix and may ask you to design an advertisement; Cambridge 0264 (topic 3.3.4) covers the aims of promotion, advertising media, sales promotion and choosing the right methods, including digital and targeted promotion.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Promotion"}
Communicating with customers to inform them about a product and persuade them to buy it.
:::

:::definition{term="Advertising"}
Paid-for communication through media such as radio, television, newspapers, billboards and social media, to inform or persuade customers.
:::

:::definition{term="Informative advertising"}
Advertising that gives facts about a product: what it is, what it does, its price and where to buy it.
:::

:::definition{term="Persuasive advertising"}
Advertising that tries to convince customers to buy, often by creating a desire for the product or linking it to a lifestyle.
:::

:::definition{term="Sales promotion"}
Short-term incentives to encourage customers to buy, such as special offers, coupons, free samples, competitions and loyalty schemes.
:::

## Why businesses promote products

- To **inform** customers about a new product or changes (new price, new branch).
- To **persuade** customers to buy, or to switch from competitors.
- To **remind** customers about established products and keep the brand in their minds.
- To **build a brand image** and loyalty.
- To **increase sales** and market share, for example to clear old stock or fill quiet periods.

## Advertising media

| Medium | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Radio | Reaches people across the country, including rural areas and people who cannot read; cheaper than television | No pictures; message easily forgotten |
| Television | Sound, pictures and movement; reaches a large audience | Very expensive to make and broadcast; viewers may switch channel |
| Newspapers and magazines | Detailed information; can be kept; magazines target special interests | Not read by everyone; short life for newspapers; limited colour in some |
| Billboards and posters | Seen repeatedly by people passing; good for brand reminders | Only short messages; limited to one place |
| Leaflets and flyers | Cheap; target a local area; can carry coupons | Often thrown away unread |
| Social media and websites | Cheap or free to start; two-way contact with customers; can be shared widely | Negative comments spread quickly; needs regular updating |
| Targeted digital messages (SMS, messaging apps, online adverts aimed at chosen groups) | Reach the customers most likely to buy; results can be measured | Customers may see them as spam; data privacy rules must be followed |
| Vehicles, uniforms, sponsorship | Repeated exposure; builds image | Little detail; sponsorship can be costly |

:::example{title="Targeted digital promotion"}
A driving school in Gweru pays for online adverts shown only to people aged 17-25 who live in the city. Instead of paying to reach everyone, as with a newspaper advert, it reaches mostly people likely to need driving lessons, and it can count how many people clicked the advert.
:::

## Sales promotion methods

| Method | How it works | Main purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price reductions and special offers | "20% off", "buy one, get one free" | Quick increase in sales; clear stock |
| Coupons and vouchers | A voucher gives money off the next purchase | Encourage repeat buying |
| Free samples and tasting | Customers try the product free | Encourage trial of a new product |
| Competitions | Buy to enter; win a prize | Excitement; extra sales |
| Loyalty and reward schemes | Points for each purchase, exchanged for gifts or discounts | Repeat purchases and loyalty |
| Gifts with purchase | A free item with the product | Make the product more attractive than rivals' |
| Point-of-sale displays | Eye-catching displays near the till | Impulse buying |

Sales promotion works **quickly** but usually **temporarily**: sales may fall when the offer ends, and too many price offers can make a brand look cheap.

## ZIMSEC Commerce: advertising in more detail

Commerce learners also study:

- **Types of advertising**: informative and persuasive; **competitive** advertising (comparing with rivals) and **generic** or **collective** advertising (a whole industry promoting its product, such as "eat more beef").
- **Advantages of advertising to consumers** (information, choice, lower prices if mass sales lower costs) and **disadvantages** (persuades people to buy what they do not need, cost is added to prices, may mislead).
- **Control of advertising**: advertisements must not mislead; consumer protection laws and the Consumer Council of Zimbabwe help protect consumers against false claims.

## Choosing promotion methods

| Factor | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Budget | How much can the business afford? A small business may rely on social media, leaflets and word of mouth |
| Target market | Who are the customers and which media do they use? Rural farmers may hear radio; young people use social media |
| Type of product | Does it need explaining (informative) or does it compete on image (persuasive)? |
| Stage of the life cycle | Introduction: inform; growth: persuade; maturity: remind and use sales promotion |
| Competitors' promotion | Where do rivals advertise? Can the business be different? |
| Size of market | Local market → leaflets, local radio; national market → national media |

### Was the promotion worth it?

A business should check whether a promotion paid for itself. It can compare sales before, during and after a campaign, count responses (calls, clicks, coupons returned) and ask new customers how they heard about the business. If the extra gross profit from the extra sales is greater than the cost of the promotion, it added to profit. If sales fell back as soon as an offer ended, the promotion may only have moved sales forward in time rather than winning new, loyal customers.

## Designing an advertisement

A good advertisement uses the **AIDA** idea:

1. **Attention**: a strong headline or picture.
2. **Interest**: what the product does for the customer.
3. **Desire**: benefits, offer or image that make the customer want it.
4. **Action**: how and where to buy (address, phone number, opening times, price).

:::example{title="An advertisement for a salon"}
Headline: "New look for the new term!" Picture of a neat hairstyle. Text: "Nyasha's Salon, opposite the bus terminus, Chinhoyi. Braids, cuts and treatments by trained stylists. Students: 10% off every Tuesday. Open 08:00-18:00, Monday to Saturday. Book on WhatsApp: (number)." It attracts attention, explains the service, gives a reason to come (the student offer) and says how to act.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up advertising and sales promotion"}
Advertising communicates through media. Sales promotion gives a short-term incentive (a discount, sample or gift). A radio advert announcing a discount uses both.
:::

:::mistake{title="Recommending television for a small business"}
Television is too expensive for most small businesses. Match the medium to the budget and the target market.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the cost"}
Every promotion costs money. A good answer asks whether the extra sales will be worth the cost.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Choose and justify"}
When asked to recommend promotion, name a specific method or medium, link it to the target customers and budget in the case, mention one drawback and conclude. "Use social media" scores more when you say who will see it and why.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
ZIMSEC Commerce may ask for the advantages and disadvantages of advertising to consumers and producers, or about generic and competitive advertising. Business Enterprise and Skills may ask you to design an advertisement. Cambridge 0264 expects you to recommend promotion methods, including targeted digital promotion, for the business in the case.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Promotion informs, persuades and reminds customers, builds brand image and raises sales.
- Advertising uses paid media: radio, television, print, billboards, leaflets, social media and targeted digital messages.
- Sales promotion gives short-term incentives: offers, coupons, samples, competitions, loyalty schemes and displays.
- Choose methods by budget, target market, product, life-cycle stage, competitors and size of market.
- A good advertisement gets Attention, Interest, Desire and Action (AIDA).
:::
