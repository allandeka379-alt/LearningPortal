## Introduction

Thandeka has just bought a minibus to run a route between Masvingo and the surrounding villages. Before she can carry passengers she needs insurance. Where does she start? What forms does she fill in? What proves she is covered while the insurer prepares the policy? And if the minibus is damaged, how does she claim?

In ZIMSEC Commerce Form 2 you learn the **procedure for taking out a policy**, the **procedure for making a claim**, the **documents** used at each stage and the **people** involved. Examiners ask you to put steps in order, state the purpose of a document, or explain the role of a broker, underwriter, actuary or assessor.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Prospectus"}
A booklet or leaflet (printed or online) from an insurer describing its policies, the cover they give, the conditions and sometimes premium rates.
:::

:::definition{term="Proposal form"}
The application form for insurance, completed by the person wanting cover (the proposer). It asks for all material facts about the risk and is the basis of the contract, so it must be completed in utmost good faith.
:::

:::definition{term="Cover note"}
A temporary document issued by the insurer after the proposal is accepted and the first premium paid, giving cover until the policy itself is issued.
:::

:::definition{term="Policy"}
The formal written contract of insurance, stating the risks covered, the sum insured, the premium, the period of cover, the conditions and the exclusions.
:::

:::definition{term="Claim form"}
The form the insured completes after a loss, giving details of what happened and the value of the loss.
:::

## Taking out an insurance policy

1. **Find information**: the proposer reads insurers' **prospectuses**, visits or contacts insurers, or consults a **broker** for advice on the best cover.
2. **Complete the proposal form**: give full and honest details of the property or person and the risk (utmost good faith), and sign the declaration.
3. **The insurer assesses the risk**: an **underwriter** studies the proposal, may inspect the property, decides whether to accept the risk and sets the **premium** using statistics prepared by **actuaries**.
4. **Pay the premium**: the proposer pays the first premium (or the full premium).
5. **Cover note issued**: gives immediate temporary cover while the policy is prepared.
6. **Policy issued**: the formal contract is sent to the insured. The insured should read it carefully, especially the exclusions.
7. **Renewal**: before the policy ends, the insurer sends a **renewal notice**; paying the renewal premium continues the cover.

:::example{title="Thandeka insures her minibus"}
Thandeka's broker compares prospectuses from three insurers and recommends a comprehensive motor policy. She completes the proposal form, disclosing that the minibus carries passengers for hire. The underwriter accepts the risk and quotes a premium, which she pays. She receives a cover note at once and can start operating; the policy document arrives two weeks later. A certificate of insurance shows the authorities that the vehicle is insured.
:::

## Making a claim

1. **Prevent further loss** and, where required, **report to the police** (theft, accidents) or other authorities.
2. **Notify the insurer** quickly, as the policy requires.
3. **Complete a claim form**, giving details of the event and the loss, with supporting evidence (police report, receipts, photographs, quotations for repairs).
4. **Assessment**: for large or doubtful claims an expert inspects the damage, finds the cause and values the loss. The insurer appoints a **loss adjuster**; the insured may hire a **loss assessor** to prepare the claim and negotiate for them. Many textbooks use "assessor" for both.
5. **Settlement**: the insurer pays cash, repairs or replaces the item, according to the principle of indemnity. A **discharge form** (settlement form) may be signed by the insured to accept the payment in full settlement.
6. **Subrogation**: any salvage passes to the insurer.

| Document | Stage | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prospectus | Before applying | Information about policies and terms |
| Proposal form | Applying | Gives the insurer the material facts; basis of the contract |
| Cover note | After acceptance | Temporary proof of cover |
| Policy | After acceptance | The full contract: risks, sum insured, premium, conditions |
| Certificate of insurance | Motor insurance | Proof to authorities that a vehicle is insured |
| Renewal notice | End of period | Reminds the insured to pay to continue cover |
| Claim form | After a loss | Details of the loss and the amount claimed |
| Discharge (settlement) form | Settlement | Insured accepts the payment in full settlement |

## People involved in insurance

| Person | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Insured (policyholder) | Pays the premium and receives compensation after a covered loss |
| Insurer | The insurance company that accepts the risk, collects premiums and pays claims |
| Insurance broker | An independent expert who advises clients on the best cover and insurer, arranges policies and helps with claims; paid commission by insurers |
| Insurance agent | Works for one insurer, selling its policies and collecting premiums, usually for commission |
| Underwriter | Works for the insurer: assesses each risk, decides whether to accept it and on what terms, and fixes the premium |
| Actuary | A mathematician and statistician who analyses data on losses to calculate probabilities and set premium rates |
| Loss adjuster and loss assessor | Investigate a claim, inspect the damage, find the cause and value the loss: the adjuster acts for the insurer, the assessor for the insured |

:::example{title="A claim after a burglary"}
Burglars break into Sipho's electrical shop in Kwekwe. He reports the break-in to the police and gets a reference number, then phones his insurer. He completes a claim form listing the stolen goods, attaching invoices that show their cost. A loss adjuster sent by the insurer visits, confirms the break-in and agrees the value of the stolen stock. The insurer pays Sipho the agreed amount, and he signs a discharge form.
:::

## Why brokers are useful

- They know the market and can find cover that suits the client at a good price.
- They save the client time by comparing many insurers.
- They explain complicated policy terms and exclusions.
- They help with claims and negotiations with the insurer.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing the cover note with the policy"}
The cover note is temporary; the policy is the full contract. A cover note is issued first so that the insured is protected immediately.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the underwriter and the actuary"}
The actuary works out probabilities and premium **rates** from statistics; the underwriter uses them to decide on each **individual** risk and its premium.
:::

:::mistake{title="Brokers working for one company"}
A broker is independent and can place business with many insurers; an agent represents one insurer.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Procedures in order"}
"Outline the procedure" wants steps in the right order, each with its document: prospectus, proposal form, premium, cover note, policy. For a claim: report, claim form, assessment, settlement.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Purpose of a document"}
State what the document is **and** why it is needed: "The cover note gives temporary cover so the insured is protected until the policy is issued."
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Taking out cover: prospectus, proposal form (utmost good faith), underwriting and premium, cover note, policy, renewal.
- Making a claim: prevent further loss and report, notify the insurer, claim form, loss assessment, settlement (cash, repair or replacement).
- Brokers advise and arrange cover with many insurers; agents sell for one insurer.
- Underwriters accept risks and set premiums; actuaries calculate premium rates from statistics; loss adjusters (for the insurer) and assessors (for the insured) investigate and value claims.
- Each document has a stage and a purpose: learn both.
:::
