## Introduction

Tendai is a 30-year-old teacher in Gweru with two young children. If he dies, who will pay the school fees? When he retires, what will he live on? If he falls ill, how will he afford hospital bills? Assurance and social security answer these questions. And for businesses that sell abroad, a different kind of cover answers another worry: what happens if a foreign buyer never pays?

ZIMSEC Commerce Form 3 covers **types of assurance policy**, **medical aid**, the **benefits of assurance**, the **National Social Security Authority (NSSA)** and the **Export Credit Guarantee Corporation (ECGC)**. Questions usually ask you to describe policies, distinguish between them, or explain the purpose of NSSA and export credit insurance.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Life assurance"}
A contract in which the assured pays premiums and the assurer pays an agreed sum (the sum assured) on the death of the life assured, or at the end of an agreed period.
:::

:::definition{term="Beneficiary"}
The person named in a policy to receive the sum assured, for example the spouse or children of the assured.
:::

:::definition{term="Surrender value"}
The amount paid to a policyholder who cancels a whole life or endowment policy before it matures; it is usually less than the premiums paid.
:::

:::definition{term="Social security"}
A national system, usually run by the state, that protects workers and their families against loss of income from old age, injury at work, invalidity or death.
:::

:::definition{term="Export credit guarantee insurance"}
Insurance that protects an exporter against the risk that a foreign buyer does not pay for goods supplied on credit.
:::

## Types of assurance policy

### Whole life assurance

Premiums are paid for life (or until a stated age), and the sum assured is paid to the beneficiaries **when the assured dies**, whenever that is. It provides for dependants; it also has a surrender value.

### Endowment assurance

Premiums are paid for a fixed period (for example 15 or 20 years). The sum assured is paid **at the end of the period** to the assured, or earlier to the beneficiaries **if the assured dies** before the end. It combines protection with **saving** and is often used to save for school fees, a house or retirement. A **with-profits** endowment also shares in the assurer's investment profits.

### Term assurance

Covers the assured's life for a **fixed term only** (for example 10 years). If the assured dies during the term, the sum assured is paid; if the assured survives, **nothing is paid**. Because there is no saving element, premiums are the cheapest. It is often used to cover a loan, so that the debt is repaid if the borrower dies.

### Funeral assurance

Pays a fixed amount, or provides funeral services (coffin, transport, tent, catering), on the death of the assured or a covered family member. Premiums are small and paid monthly. It is a formal version of the burial society.

### Medical aid

Members pay monthly contributions to a medical aid society, which pays all or part of their **medical bills** (doctors, hospitals, medicines) according to the plan chosen.

| Policy | When it pays | Saving element | Relative premium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whole life | On death, whenever it happens | Yes (surrender value) | Medium |
| Endowment | At the end of the term, or on earlier death | Yes, strong | Highest |
| Term | Only on death within the term | No | Lowest |
| Funeral | On death of a covered person | No | Low |
| Medical aid | When medical treatment is needed | No | Depends on the plan |

:::example{title="Choosing a policy"}
Tendai wants three things: money for his children if he dies, a lump sum in 15 years to pay for their university education, and a cheap cover for his 10-year bank loan. A **whole life** policy protects his family; an **endowment** policy maturing in 15 years saves for university fees and also pays if he dies sooner; a **term** policy for 10 years repays the loan if he dies before it is cleared.
:::

## Benefits of assurance

- **Protects dependants** from poverty after the death of a breadwinner.
- **Encourages saving**: endowment and whole life policies build a sum for the future.
- **Security for loans**: a policy can be pledged (ceded) to a lender, and some policies allow loans against their value.
- **Retirement income**: matured policies and pensions provide income in old age.
- **Covers funeral and medical costs** without borrowing.
- **Investment in the economy**: assurers invest premiums in shares, property and government securities, providing capital for businesses.

## The National Social Security Authority (NSSA)

NSSA was formed by an Act of Parliament to provide **social security for workers in Zimbabwe**. Before it, many workers who were injured, retired or died left their families with no income unless their employer ran a private pension scheme.

**Why it was formed**

- to give **all employees**, not only those with private pension schemes, protection in old age;
- to compensate workers **injured at work** and the families of workers killed at work;
- to reduce poverty among retired, disabled and bereaved families;
- to promote **safety and health** at work.

**How it works**: employers and employees make **compulsory contributions** from wages to NSSA, which invests the funds and pays benefits.

**What it provides** (through its pension scheme and its workers' compensation scheme):

- **retirement (old age) pension** when a worker reaches retirement age;
- **invalidity pension** for workers who can no longer work because of illness or disability;
- **survivors' benefits** to the spouse and children of a member who dies;
- **funeral grant** towards funeral costs;
- **workers' compensation**: medical costs and payments for injury or disease caused by work, and pensions for families of workers killed at work;
- **accident prevention**: safety inspections, training and awareness campaigns in workplaces.

## Export credit guarantee insurance (ECGC)

An exporter often sells on credit to a buyer in another country, and payment may take months. Ordinary insurers rarely cover the risk that the buyer never pays. The **Export Credit Guarantee Corporation** provides this cover.

It insures exporters against:

- **commercial risks**: the buyer becomes insolvent, refuses to accept the goods or simply fails to pay;
- **political risks**: war, government action in the buyer's country, or restrictions that stop money being sent to Zimbabwe.

**Purposes and benefits**

- Exporters can offer **credit terms** to foreign buyers, which helps them win orders.
- Banks are more willing to give **export finance** when the exporter's payments are insured.
- Exporters can enter **new and riskier markets**.
- More exports earn **foreign currency** for the country and create jobs.

:::example{title="An exporter protected"}
Nyasha's company exports furniture to a retailer in a neighbouring country on 90 days' credit. She insures the sale with export credit insurance. The retailer goes out of business before paying. The insurer pays Nyasha most of the value of the goods, so her business survives and she continues exporting.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Term assurance pays at the end"}
Term assurance pays **only if the assured dies during the term**. Endowment pays at the end of the term.
:::

:::mistake{title="NSSA as an insurance company"}
NSSA is a state social security authority funded by compulsory contributions from employers and employees, not a private insurer that people choose to join.
:::

:::mistake{title="Export credit insurance and marine insurance"}
Marine cargo insurance covers **goods** lost or damaged on the voyage. Export credit insurance covers **non-payment** by the buyer.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Distinguish between policies"}
Compare **when it pays** and **whether it has a saving element**. A two-column answer (whole life versus endowment) earns marks quickly.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="NSSA: why and what"}
"Why was NSSA formed?" wants reasons (protection for all workers, injury compensation, reducing poverty). "What does NSSA provide?" wants benefits (pensions, survivors' benefits, funeral grant, workers' compensation).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Whole life pays on death; endowment pays at the end of a term or on earlier death; term pays only on death within the term; funeral assurance covers funeral costs; medical aid pays medical bills.
- Assurance protects dependants, encourages saving, secures loans and provides capital for investment.
- NSSA was formed to give all workers social security; employers and employees contribute.
- NSSA provides retirement, invalidity and survivors' pensions, funeral grants, workers' compensation and accident prevention.
- Export credit guarantee insurance protects exporters against commercial and political risks of non-payment.
:::
