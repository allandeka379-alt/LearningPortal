## Introduction

Not long ago, insuring a car meant visiting an insurer's office in town, filling in a long paper proposal form, paying in cash and waiting days for a cover note. Today a motorist in Harare can compare quotations on a website, pay the premium by mobile money and receive the policy by email within minutes. A funeral policy can be bought and paid for on a basic phone. A farmer who has a claim can send photographs of the damage from the field.

This is **e-insurance** and **e-assurance**: the use of information and communication technology (ICT) to sell, manage and service insurance and assurance. ZIMSEC Commerce covers the impact of ICT on insurance and assurance in Form 4. Expect questions asking you to describe how ICT is used and to discuss its advantages and disadvantages for insurers and their clients.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="E-insurance (e-assurance)"}
Buying, paying for and managing insurance (or assurance) electronically, through websites, mobile apps, mobile phone menus, email and electronic payment systems.
:::

:::definition{term="Online quotation"}
A premium calculated instantly by an insurer's website or app from details the client enters.
:::

:::definition{term="E-policy"}
A policy document issued and stored electronically, sent by email or available in an app or online account.
:::

:::definition{term="Database"}
An organised electronic store of records, such as an insurer's records of clients, policies, premiums and claims.
:::

## How ICT is used in insurance and assurance

### Selling and quotations

- Insurers' **websites and apps** show their products (an online prospectus) and give instant quotations.
- **Comparison websites** let clients compare policies and premiums from several insurers.
- **Social media** and **text messages** are used to advertise and to answer questions.

### Applying and issuing policies

- **Online proposal forms** are completed and submitted electronically; supporting documents and photographs are uploaded.
- **E-policies and electronic cover notes** are issued by email or in an app, often immediately.

### Paying premiums

- Premiums are paid by **mobile money**, **internet banking**, **debit orders** (automatic monthly payments from a bank account) or **cards**.
- **Automatic reminders** by text or email reduce missed payments and lapsed policies.

### Making claims

- Claims are reported by **phone, app or website**, with photographs and documents attached.
- Claims are tracked online, and settlements are paid by **electronic transfer**.

### Running the insurer

- **Databases** hold client, policy and claims records.
- **Actuaries** use computers and software to analyse large amounts of data and set premium rates more accurately.
- **Vehicle tracking** and other data help insurers judge risks and reward careful clients with lower premiums.
- Software helps detect **fraudulent claims** by spotting unusual patterns.

:::example{title="A funeral policy on a basic phone"}
Chipo lives in a rural area of Masvingo province, far from any insurer's office. She dials a mobile phone menu code, chooses a funeral plan for her family, and confirms a small monthly premium deducted from her mobile money wallet. She receives a text message confirming her policy number. When her uncle dies the following year, her cousin reports the claim by phone and sends the death certificate as a photo, and the benefit is paid to Chipo's mobile wallet.
:::

## Advantages of ICT in insurance

**For clients**

- **Convenience**: buy cover, pay and claim at any time, from anywhere, without travelling.
- **Speed**: instant quotations, immediate cover notes, faster claim settlement.
- **Easy comparison** of policies and premiums, which encourages competition and lower prices.
- **Access for rural and remote clients** through mobile phones.
- **Records available** online: policy documents cannot be lost.

**For insurers**

- **Lower costs**: fewer branches and less paperwork, so cheaper administration.
- **Wider market**: reach many more clients, including those far from towns.
- **Better risk assessment** and pricing using data analysis.
- **Faster, accurate records** and easier communication with clients.
- **Fraud detection** using data.
- **Fewer lapsed policies** through automatic payments and reminders.

## Disadvantages of ICT in insurance

**For clients**

- **Needs access** to a phone, internet, data and electronic money; some people lack these or the skills to use them.
- **Little personal advice**: clients may buy unsuitable cover without fully understanding the terms and exclusions.
- **Cyber fraud**: fake insurance websites, phishing messages and theft of personal data.
- **Network failures** can stop payments, so a policy might lapse.

**For insurers**

- **High set-up and maintenance costs** for systems, software and security.
- **Cyber attacks and data breaches** can expose clients' private information and damage the insurer's reputation.
- **System breakdowns** stop business.
- **Fewer jobs** for agents and clerks, which may be resisted by staff.
- **Online fraud** by clients, such as false documents and photographs.

| Use of ICT | Main benefit | Main drawback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Online quotation and proposal | fast, convenient | little advice; risk of misunderstanding |
| E-policy and e-cover note | immediate cover; no lost papers | needs email or app access |
| Mobile money and debit order premiums | easy, regular payments | network failure; fraud |
| Online and app claims | faster settlement | false photos and documents |
| Databases and data analysis | accurate premiums; fraud detection | cost; data protection risks |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Only listing advantages"}
"Discuss the impact" wants advantages **and** disadvantages, preferably for both insurers and clients, and a conclusion.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying ICT removes the need for principles"}
Online proposal forms must still be completed in utmost good faith, and claims are still settled by indemnity. ICT changes how insurance is done, not its principles.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Name the technology"}
Say which ICT tool is used: website, app, mobile phone menu, email, mobile money, database. "Insurers use computers" is too vague for full marks.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Whose point of view?"}
Questions may ask about the impact on the insurer, on clients, or both. Check the question and organise your answer under those headings.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- E-insurance uses websites, apps, mobile phone menus, email and electronic payments to sell and service insurance.
- ICT is used for quotations, proposal forms, e-policies, premium payments, claims, databases and data analysis.
- Clients gain convenience, speed, comparison and access; insurers gain lower costs, a wider market and better risk assessment.
- Drawbacks: access and skills gaps, little personal advice, cyber fraud, data breaches, system costs and failures, fewer jobs.
- The principles of insurance still apply to electronic contracts.
:::
