## Introduction

Every country with a modern banking system has one bank that stands above the others: the **central bank**. In Zimbabwe this is the **Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ)** in Harare. Ordinary people do not open accounts there, yet its decisions affect the interest rate on a trader's overdraft, the safety of a family's savings and the supply of notes and coins in every shop.

This Form 3 ZIMSEC Commerce topic asks you to state and explain the functions of a central bank and of the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe, and to distinguish a central bank from a commercial bank.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Central bank"}
The bank at the head of a country's banking system, owned by or answerable to the state, which issues currency, acts as banker to the government and to other banks, controls the supply of money and credit, and supervises the banking system.
:::

:::definition{term="Monetary policy"}
Action by the central bank to influence the amount of money and credit in the economy and the level of interest rates, in order to keep prices stable and support economic growth.
:::

:::definition{term="Lender of last resort"}
The central bank's role of lending to commercial banks that are short of cash when no other lender will, to prevent bank failures.
:::

:::definition{term="Foreign (external) reserves"}
Holdings of foreign currencies, gold and other assets kept by the central bank to pay for imports and debts and to support the value of the currency.
:::

## Functions of the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe

### 1. Issuing notes and coins

The central bank is the **sole issuer** of the national currency. It decides how many notes and coins to put into circulation, replaces worn notes and protects against forgery with security features. Controlling the issue of currency helps to prevent too much money causing inflation.

### 2. Banker to the government

The central bank:

- keeps the **government's accounts** and receives tax revenue paid into them;
- makes payments on behalf of government ministries;
- **lends** to the government for short periods and manages government borrowing, for example by issuing **treasury bills** and bonds;
- **advises** the government on financial and economic matters.

### 3. Banker to the commercial banks

Commercial banks keep accounts at the central bank. Through these accounts:

- banks **settle** what they owe one another at the end of each day (clearing), including payments made through the RTGS system;
- banks hold part of their deposits as **reserves** with the central bank.

### 4. Lender of last resort

If a commercial bank runs short of cash, for example when many depositors withdraw at once, the central bank can lend to it. This protects depositors and prevents the failure of one bank from spreading panic through the whole system.

### 5. Monetary policy

The central bank influences the supply of money and credit to keep prices stable. Its main tools are:

| Tool | How it works | Effect when used to reduce inflation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank (policy) rate | The interest rate at which the central bank lends to banks | A higher rate makes all borrowing dearer, so borrowing and spending fall |
| Reserve requirements | The share of deposits banks must keep as reserves | Higher requirements leave banks less to lend |
| Open market operations | Buying or selling government securities such as treasury bills | Selling securities takes money out of the banks |
| Directives and moral suasion | Instructions or persuasion on lending | Banks are told or asked to limit lending to some sectors |

To encourage growth when the economy is slow, it does the opposite: lowers rates, reduces requirements or buys securities.

### 6. Supervising and regulating banks

The central bank **licenses** banks and other deposit-taking institutions, sets rules on how much capital they must hold, **inspects** their records, and can take action against a bank that is badly managed. This protects depositors and maintains confidence in the banking system.

### 7. Managing foreign reserves and exchange

The central bank holds and manages the country's **foreign currency reserves** and gold, uses them to pay for essential imports and government debts abroad, and manages exchange rate policy and the rules for foreign currency transactions.

### Other functions

It also manages the **national payment systems** (such as RTGS and clearing), collects and publishes **economic and financial data**, and represents the country in dealings with international financial institutions.

:::example{title="How the bank rate reaches a small business"}
Suppose the central bank raises its policy rate to slow down rising prices. Commercial banks now pay more to borrow, so they raise the interest on overdrafts and loans. Tatenda, who runs a carpentry workshop in Masvingo, finds his overdraft dearer and postpones buying a new machine. Many businesses and households react in the same way, so total spending falls and price rises slow down.
:::

### Why stable prices matter to business

Stable prices let businesses plan, set prices with confidence and sign long-term contracts. When inflation is high and unpredictable, firms struggle to price goods, workers demand frequent wage rises, and savers and lenders lose confidence. By keeping inflation low, the central bank supports investment, trade and employment.

## Central bank and commercial banks compared

| Central bank | Commercial bank |
| --- | --- |
| One in the country; owned by or answerable to the state | Many; usually owned by private shareholders |
| Main aim: stability of prices and of the banking system | Main aim: profit |
| Customers: government and banks | Customers: the public and businesses |
| Issues notes and coins | Cannot issue currency |
| Controls and supervises banks | Is controlled and supervised |
| Lender of last resort | Lends to customers |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying the Reserve Bank takes deposits from the public"}
Individuals and businesses bank with commercial banks. The central bank's customers are the government and the banks.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing functions without explaining them"}
"Lender of last resort" earns more when you add what it means and why it matters: it lends to banks short of cash, so depositors do not panic.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing ZIMRA and the Reserve Bank"}
ZIMRA collects taxes. The Reserve Bank keeps the government's accounts and manages money and banks.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="State and explain"}
Most questions are "state and explain" functions. For each: name the function, then explain how it works and why it matters.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Link monetary policy to inflation"}
When explaining how the central bank controls inflation, follow the chain: higher rate → dearer loans → less borrowing and spending → slower price rises.
:::

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The central bank is at the head of the banking system and aims at stable prices and a sound banking system, not profit.
- The Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe issues notes and coins and is banker to the government and to the commercial banks.
- It is lender of last resort to banks short of cash.
- It carries out monetary policy using the bank rate, reserve requirements, open market operations and directives.
- It licenses and supervises banks and manages foreign reserves, exchange policy and payment systems.
:::
