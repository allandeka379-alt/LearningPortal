## Introduction

When Chipo buys a bag of cement at a hardware shop in Gweru, part of the price she pays is value added tax. When her brother receives his salary, income tax has already been deducted. When a cross-border trader brings goods in at Beitbridge, customs duty may be charged. All of this money goes to the government, which uses it to run schools, clinics, roads and the police.

This Form 2 ZIMSEC Commerce topic asks why governments tax, the difference between direct and indirect taxes, examples of each, the duty of individuals and businesses to pay tax, and the work of the **Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (ZIMRA)**. You may also be asked simple tax calculations, always using rates that the question tells you to assume.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Tax"}
A compulsory payment to the government by individuals and businesses, for which no direct service is given in return.
:::

:::definition{term="Direct tax"}
A tax on income, profit or wealth, paid directly to the tax authority by the person or business on whom it falls.
:::

:::definition{term="Indirect tax"}
A tax on spending (goods and services), collected by sellers or importers and passed on to consumers in the price.
:::

:::definition{term="Value added tax (VAT)"}
An indirect tax charged as a percentage of the value of most goods and services at each stage of production and distribution.
:::

:::definition{term="Tax evasion"}
Illegally avoiding paying tax that is due, for example by hiding income or smuggling goods.
:::

## Why governments tax

- **To raise revenue** for public services: education, health, roads, defence, police and the civil service.
- **To redistribute income**: higher earners pay more, and revenue funds support for the poor.
- **To discourage harmful goods**: high excise duty on tobacco and alcohol reduces consumption.
- **To protect local industries**: customs duty makes imports dearer, so local goods can compete.
- **To manage the economy**: raising taxes reduces spending and can slow inflation; cutting taxes encourages spending and investment.
- **To correct the balance of payments**: duties on imports reduce spending on foreign goods.

## Direct and indirect taxes

| Feature | Direct taxes | Indirect taxes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charged on | Income, profit, wealth | Spending on goods and services |
| Who pays the tax authority | The taxpayer directly (often through the employer) | The seller or importer, who passes it on |
| Examples | Personal income tax (often deducted by employers as Pay As You Earn), corporate (company) tax, capital gains tax | VAT, customs duty, excise duty |
| Advantages | Can be progressive (higher earners pay a larger share); cost of collection is low; revenue is predictable | Hard to evade because it is in the price; can target harmful goods; everyone who spends contributes |
| Disadvantages | May discourage work and enterprise; can be evaded by hiding income | Regressive: the poor pay a larger share of their income; raises prices and may cause inflation |

**Customs duty** is charged on imported goods (and sometimes exports). **Excise duty** is charged on certain locally produced goods, such as fuel, alcohol and tobacco.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: VAT on a purchase]
A mobile phone costs US\$240 before VAT. Assume a VAT rate of 15%.

1. VAT = 15% of US\$240 = 0.15 × 240 = US\$36.
2. Price including VAT = US\$240 + US\$36 = US\$276.
3. The shop collects the US\$36 and pays it to the tax authority; the buyer bears the tax.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: income tax with a tax-free allowance]
Rudo earns US\$700 a month. Assume the first US\$100 is tax-free and the rest is taxed at 20%.

1. Taxable income = US\$700 − US\$100 = US\$600.
2. Income tax = 20% of US\$600 = US\$120.
3. Take-home pay (ignoring other deductions) = US\$700 − US\$120 = US\$580.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: customs duty]
A trader imports goods valued at US\$2,000. Assume customs duty of 25% of the value.

1. Duty = 25% of US\$2,000 = US\$500.
2. The trader adds this cost when setting selling prices, so consumers pay more for the imported goods.
:::

## Qualities (canons) of a good tax system

- **Equity (fairness):** people pay according to their ability to pay.
- **Certainty:** taxpayers know how much, when and how to pay.
- **Convenience:** tax is collected at a convenient time and in a convenient way (for example, Pay As You Earn from wages).
- **Economy:** the cost of collecting the tax is small compared with the revenue.
- **Flexibility:** rates can be changed when the economy's needs change.

## The legal obligation to pay tax

Paying tax is required by law. Businesses must register with the tax authority, keep proper records, file returns on time, charge and pay over VAT where they are registered, and deduct employees' income tax. Individuals must declare taxable income.

**Consequences of not paying:** fines and penalties, interest on unpaid tax, seizure of goods (for example smuggled imports), closure of the business and prosecution. Tax evasion also harms everyone, because the government has less money for services, and honest businesses face unfair competition.

## The Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (ZIMRA)

ZIMRA is the government body responsible for collecting taxes and duties in Zimbabwe. Its main roles are to:

- **assess and collect** taxes such as income tax, corporate tax and VAT;
- **collect customs and excise duties** at border posts, airports and inland ports;
- **control the movement of goods** across the borders and prevent **smuggling**;
- **register taxpayers** and issue tax numbers and clearance certificates;
- **audit** businesses to check that the correct tax has been paid, and enforce penalties;
- **advise** the Ministry responsible for finance on tax matters and **educate** taxpayers about their obligations.

:::example{title="A cross-border trader at the border"}
Farai imports clothes from South Africa to sell at a market in Harare. At the border he declares the goods to the customs officers, who check the value and calculate the duty payable. Paying the duty is part of his cost of sales. If he hid the goods to avoid duty, they could be seized and he could be fined.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Calling VAT a direct tax"}
VAT is an **indirect** tax: it is charged on spending and collected by the seller, even though the consumer bears it.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calculating tax on the wrong amount"}
Income tax is calculated on **taxable** income (after any tax-free amount). VAT is calculated on the price **before** VAT.
:::

:::mistake{title="Stating real tax rates as fact"}
Rates change with each budget. In exam answers say "assume a rate of …" or use the rate the question gives.
:::

:::

:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Tax is a compulsory payment to the government that funds public services, redistributes income, discourages harmful goods and protects industries.
- Direct taxes fall on income, profit and wealth (income tax, corporate tax); indirect taxes fall on spending (VAT, customs and excise duties).
- A good tax is fair, certain, convenient, economical to collect and flexible.
- Paying tax is a legal duty; evasion leads to penalties and harms public services.
- ZIMRA assesses and collects taxes and duties, controls goods at borders, registers taxpayers and enforces the law.
:::
