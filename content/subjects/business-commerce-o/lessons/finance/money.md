## Introduction

At a rural market near Masvingo, a farmer with a bag of groundnuts wants a new hoe. The blacksmith does not want groundnuts: he wants maize meal. Before money existed, the farmer would have to find someone who wanted groundnuts **and** had a hoe to spare, or trade through a chain of swaps. Money solves this problem, which is why it sits at the start of the Form 1 Commerce topic on finance.

Examiners regularly ask you to explain the problems of barter, list and explain the qualities (characteristics) and functions of money, and discuss the causes and effects of inflation. Questions are short and direct, so learn the terms exactly and practise giving an example for each point.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Barter"}
The direct exchange of goods or services for other goods or services, without using money.
:::

:::definition{term="Money"}
Anything that is generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services and in settlement of debts.
:::

:::definition{term="Double coincidence of wants"}
The situation barter needs: each trader must have exactly what the other wants, and want exactly what the other has.
:::

:::definition{term="Inflation"}
A continuing rise in the general level of prices over a period of time, so that each unit of money buys less.
:::

:::definition{term="Purchasing power"}
The quantity of goods and services that a given amount of money can buy.
:::

## From barter to money

Early communities were largely self-sufficient: families grew their own food and made their own tools. As people began to specialise, they exchanged surpluses through barter. Barter works in a small village but breaks down as trade grows.

**Problems of barter:**

- **Double coincidence of wants** is needed, so finding a trading partner takes time.
- **No common measure of value**: how many chickens equal one goat? Every pair of goods needs its own rate of exchange.
- **Indivisibility**: a cow cannot be divided to buy a few cups of salt without destroying its value.
- **Difficulty of storing wealth**: crops rot and animals die, so saving is hard.
- **No standard for deferred payment**: borrowing "two goats, to be repaid next year" causes arguments about the size and quality of the goats.
- **Transport problems**: carrying goods to market to exchange them is costly.

People therefore began to use commodities that everybody wanted, such as cattle, salt, cloth, beads and metals, as **commodity money**. Metals were later made into **coins**; goldsmiths' receipts and bank notes followed, then **bank deposits** moved by cheque, card and electronic transfer. Today much money exists only as entries in bank and mobile money accounts.

## Qualities (characteristics) of good money

| Quality | Meaning | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acceptability | Everyone is willing to take it in payment | The essential quality: without it, it is not money |
| Durability | It lasts and does not wear out quickly | Can be used many times and stored |
| Portability | Easy to carry | Large values can be moved cheaply |
| Divisibility | Can be split into smaller units | Small purchases and exact change are possible |
| Homogeneity (uniformity) | Every unit of the same value is identical | No arguments over quality |
| Scarcity (limited supply) | Its supply is controlled | Keeps its value; too much money causes inflation |
| Stability of value | Its value does not change much | People trust it as a store of value |
| Recognisability and difficulty to forge | Easy to identify, hard to copy | Protects against counterfeits |

:::example{title="Why cattle were poor money"}
Cattle were widely accepted, so they had one important quality. But they were not portable, not divisible, not uniform (no two cows are identical) and not durable (they get sick and die). This is why cattle gave way to coins and notes for everyday trade, although they remain an important store of wealth in many rural families.
:::

## Functions of money

1. **Medium of exchange:** money is accepted in payment, so the double coincidence of wants is no longer needed. This is the main function.
2. **Measure (unit) of value:** prices of all goods are stated in money, so values can be compared easily.
3. **Store of value:** money can be saved and used later, because it does not rot (as long as inflation is low).
4. **Standard of deferred payment:** debts and future payments, such as loans, hire purchase instalments and salaries, can be stated in money.

Some textbooks add that money encourages **specialisation** and trade, because people can sell their output for money and buy everything else they need.

## Inflation

Inflation means a general and continuing rise in prices, not a rise in the price of one product. Inflation is usually measured by comparing the cost of a fixed "basket" of goods and services that households buy.

:::formula{name="Rate of inflation from a basket of goods"}
Rate of inflation = (cost of basket this year − cost of basket last year) ÷ cost of basket last year × 100
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 1: calculating a rate of inflation]
A household basket of maize meal, cooking oil, sugar, soap and bread cost US\$80 last year. The same basket costs US\$86 this year.

1. Increase in cost: US\$86 − US\$80 = US\$6.
2. Rate of inflation: 6 ÷ 80 × 100 = 7.5%.
3. Prices in general rose by 7.5% over the year.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: the effect on purchasing power]
Rudo earns US\$300 a month. Over a year, prices rise by 20%, but her wage does not change.

1. Goods that cost US\$100 last year now cost US\$120.
2. Her US\$300 now buys what US\$300 ÷ 1.2 = US\$250 bought last year.
3. Her **money income** is unchanged, but her **real income** (purchasing power) has fallen by one sixth.
:::

### Causes of inflation

- **Demand-pull inflation:** total demand for goods grows faster than the supply of goods ("too much money chasing too few goods"), for example when the government prints money or when wages rise without more output.
- **Cost-push inflation:** costs of production rise, such as fuel, electricity, imported raw materials and wages, and firms pass the higher costs on in higher prices.
- **Imported inflation:** the prices of imported goods rise, for example when the local currency loses value against foreign currencies.
- **Expectations:** when people expect prices to rise, workers demand higher wages and traders raise prices early, so inflation feeds itself.

### Effects of inflation

| Group | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Workers on fixed incomes and pensioners | Lose purchasing power; standard of living falls |
| Savers | The real value of savings falls if interest is lower than inflation |
| Borrowers | Gain: they repay with money worth less |
| Lenders | Lose: they are repaid with money worth less, so they raise interest rates |
| Businesses | Rising costs, uncertainty, difficulty in pricing and planning; customers buy less |
| Exporters | Goods become dearer abroad, so exports may fall |
| The economy | People switch to foreign currency or goods as stores of value; investment falls |

Very high inflation, called **hyperinflation**, destroys money's function as a store of value and can even stop it being accepted as a medium of exchange, so people return to barter or use foreign currency.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing qualities with functions"}
Durability and portability are **qualities** (what money is like). Medium of exchange and store of value are **functions** (what money does). Read the question carefully.
:::

:::mistake{title="Defining inflation as a rise in one price"}
A rise in the price of bread alone is not inflation. Inflation is a rise in the **general** level of prices that continues over time.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying everyone loses from inflation"}
Borrowers can gain, because they repay debts with money that buys less. Say who loses and who gains.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Explain, do not just list"}
For "explain two problems of barter" give the problem and then why it makes trade difficult, with an example: "Indivisibility: a goat cannot be split to buy a small item such as a packet of salt."
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Calculations"}
In an inflation calculation write the formula, the increase and the answer with a % sign. Always divide by the **earlier** (old) cost.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board coverage"}
Money and inflation belong to ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 1). Cambridge 0264 and Business Enterprise and Skills do not examine this concept directly, although inflation appears as an external influence on business.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Barter needs a double coincidence of wants and suffers from no common measure of value, indivisibility, poor storage and no standard for deferred payment.
- Good money is acceptable, durable, portable, divisible, uniform, limited in supply, stable in value and hard to forge.
- Money is a medium of exchange, a measure of value, a store of value and a standard of deferred payment.
- Inflation is a continuing rise in the general price level; it is caused by excess demand, rising costs, imported prices and expectations.
- Inflation hurts people on fixed incomes, savers and lenders, helps borrowers, and creates uncertainty for businesses.
:::
