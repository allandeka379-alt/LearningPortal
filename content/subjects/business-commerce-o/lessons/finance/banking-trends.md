## Introduction

Twenty years ago, paying a supplier in another town usually meant queuing in a banking hall. Today Sipho, who sells phone accessories at a market stall in Bulawayo, receives payments on his phone, pays his rent with a few key presses and sends money to his mother in a village near Gwanda, who collects it from a small shop that acts as an agent. Technology has changed how people bank, and who can bank at all.

This Form 4 ZIMSEC Commerce topic asks you to describe telebanking, internet banking, mobile banking, mobile money, agency banking and money transfer agents, and to discuss their impact on customers, banks and the economy.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Telebanking"}
Carrying out banking transactions by telephone, either by speaking to a call-centre agent or by using an automated phone menu.
:::

:::definition{term="Internet (online) banking"}
Using a bank's secure website or application on a computer or phone to check balances, transfer money and pay bills.
:::

:::definition{term="Mobile banking"}
Accessing a bank account through a mobile phone, using a banking app or a short code menu.
:::

:::definition{term="Mobile money"}
An electronic wallet linked to a mobile phone number, provided by a mobile network operator, that lets users store, send and receive money and pay for goods without a bank account.
:::

:::definition{term="Agency banking"}
An arrangement in which a bank uses shops, supermarkets, filling stations and other businesses as agents to offer basic services such as deposits, withdrawals and account opening.
:::

:::definition{term="Financial inclusion"}
Giving individuals and small businesses access to useful and affordable financial services: payments, savings, credit and insurance.
:::

## The main trends

### Telebanking

Customers phone the bank to check balances, report a lost card, stop a payment or ask about products. Automated menus work 24 hours a day. It saves visits to a branch but is limited to simple tasks.

### Internet banking

Through a secure login, customers view statements, transfer money between accounts and to other banks (including RTGS payments), pay bills and suppliers, and download statements. Businesses use it to pay salaries and suppliers in bulk.

### Mobile banking

Bank customers use an app or a **USSD short code** (a menu that works even on basic phones without internet) to check balances, transfer funds, buy airtime and pay bills. Alerts by SMS tell customers about each transaction.

### Mobile money

Mobile network operators offer electronic wallets. Users register with their identity document, deposit cash with an agent ("cash in"), send money to other users, pay merchants and bills, and withdraw cash ("cash out"). No bank account is needed, so mobile money reaches people far from bank branches. Many wallets are linked to bank accounts so that money can move between them.

### Agency banking

Banks appoint shops and other outlets as **agents**. Customers can deposit, withdraw, pay bills and sometimes open accounts there. The bank pays the agent a commission. This brings banking to places where opening a full branch would be too costly.

### Money transfer agents

Money transfer agents send money within the country and across borders. The sender pays the amount plus a fee at an agent's outlet; the receiver collects it at another outlet, often within minutes, using a reference number and identity document. Cross-border workers use them to send remittances home.

### Card and point-of-sale payments

Debit cards, point-of-sale (POS) machines in shops and contactless payments let customers pay without cash. For the shop, takings go straight into the bank account, reducing the risk of theft and the time spent counting and depositing cash. For the customer, there is no need to carry large sums. Both, however, depend on electricity and network connections, so many traders keep a small cash float as a back-up.

## Impact of the trends

| On customers | On banks | On the economy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Convenience: banking at any time and place | Lower costs: fewer branches and tellers needed | Financial inclusion of rural and low-income people |
| Less time and money spent travelling and queuing | Reach new customers without building branches | Faster payments speed up trade |
| Safer than carrying cash | Income from transaction fees | Less cash in circulation; easier to trace transactions and collect tax |
| Instant records and alerts | Need to invest in technology and security | Remittances support families and spending |
| **But:** charges on each transaction, network failures, fraud and scams, need for a phone and some literacy | **But:** cyber-attacks, system failures, competition from mobile operators | **But:** job losses in branches; people without phones or networks left behind |

:::example{title="A tuck shop that became an agent"}
Thandeka runs a tuck shop at a growth point in Masvingo province. She becomes an agent for a bank and a mobile money service. Villagers now deposit and withdraw cash at her shop instead of travelling to town. Thandeka earns commission on each transaction, and more people come into the shop, so her sales of groceries rise too. She must, however, keep enough cash and electronic float to serve customers, and she has to protect her shop against theft.
:::

## Risks and how to reduce them

- **Fraud and scams:** never share a PIN or one-time password; check the name of the receiver before confirming.
- **Network or system failures:** keep a small amount of cash or another payment method.
- **Charges:** compare fees; for large regular payments a bank transfer may be cheaper than many small mobile transactions.
- **Lost or stolen phones:** use PINs and report the loss quickly so the account can be blocked.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Treating mobile money as the same as mobile banking"}
Mobile banking uses a phone to reach a **bank account**. Mobile money is an **electronic wallet** run by a mobile network operator and needs no bank account.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving only advantages"}
"Discuss the impact" questions need disadvantages too: charges, fraud, network failures and exclusion of people without phones.
:::

:::mistake{title="Naming specific companies"}
Describe services generally ("a mobile network operator's wallet"). Names and owners of services change.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Telebanking, internet banking and mobile banking let customers reach their bank accounts without visiting a branch.
- Mobile money is an electronic wallet on a phone, provided by a mobile network operator, needing no bank account.
- Agency banking uses shops as bank agents; money transfer agents send money locally and across borders.
- Benefits: convenience, lower costs, safety, faster payments and financial inclusion.
- Drawbacks: charges, fraud, network failures, job losses and exclusion of people without phones or skills.
:::
