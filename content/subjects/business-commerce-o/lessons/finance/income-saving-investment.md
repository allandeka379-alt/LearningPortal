## Introduction

Farai is a carpenter in Chinhoyi. He earns money from making furniture, his wife earns a salary as a nurse, and the family rents out a room. At the end of each month a little money is left over. Should they keep it at home, put it in a bank, buy shares, or buy a few goats? This lesson explains where personal income comes from, why people save, the ways they can save and invest, and how saving differs from investment.

The topic is examined in ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 1, personal finance) and in Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 1, investment and saving). Expect "state", "explain" and "distinguish" questions, and short calculations of interest and returns.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Income"}
Money received by a person or household over a period, for example wages, profit, rent, interest or dividends.
:::

:::definition{term="Saving"}
The part of income that is not spent on consumption now, kept for future use.
:::

:::definition{term="Investment (personal)"}
Using savings to buy an asset that is expected to earn an income or rise in value, such as shares, property or livestock.
:::

:::definition{term="Interest"}
The payment a bank or other borrower makes for the use of savings; for the saver it is a reward for saving.
:::

:::definition{term="Return on an investment"}
The income and gain earned from an investment, often expressed as a percentage of the amount invested.
:::

## Sources of personal income

| Source | Earned by | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wages and salaries | Employees, in return for labour | A nurse's monthly salary |
| Profit | Self-employed people and business owners | A market vendor's surplus after costs |
| Rent | Owners of land and buildings | Renting out a room or a shop |
| Interest | Savers and lenders | Interest on a savings account |
| Dividends | Shareholders | A share of a company's profit |
| Transfer payments | People receiving support | Pensions, social welfare grants, remittances from relatives |

Wages, salaries and profit are **earned income** (from work); rent, interest and dividends come from owning assets.

## Saving

People save income they do not need to spend now. The amount they can save is:

Saving = income − spending on consumption

### Reasons for saving

- **Emergencies**: illness, funerals, loss of a job.
- **Future purchases**: school fees, a bicycle, furniture, a house deposit.
- **Retirement**: income for old age.
- **Starting or expanding a business**: owners' savings are the most common source of start-up capital.
- **Earning interest**: money in a savings account grows.
- **Security and peace of mind**.

### Methods (places) of saving

- **At home** ("under the mattress"): easy to reach but earns nothing and can be lost or stolen.
- **Savings account** at a commercial bank or building society: safe, earns interest, money can be withdrawn.
- **Fixed (time) deposit**: money left for an agreed period at a higher interest rate; early withdrawal may lose interest.
- **Post office savings bank**: convenient for small savers in many towns.
- **Savings clubs and rotating savings groups**: members contribute each month and take turns to receive the pool; they encourage discipline but carry risk if a member defaults.
- **Mobile money savings** products offered by mobile operators with partner banks.
- **Insurance and pension schemes**: regular contributions build up a fund for the future.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: simple interest on savings]
Chipo deposits US\$600 in a savings account. Assume the bank pays simple interest of 5% a year and she leaves the money for 2 years.

1. Interest for one year: 5% of US\$600 = US\$30.
2. Interest for 2 years: US\$30 × 2 = US\$60.
3. Total in the account after 2 years: US\$600 + US\$60 = US\$660.
:::

:::formula{name="Simple interest"}
Interest = amount saved × rate (%) × number of years ÷ 100
:::

## Investment

A personal investment uses savings to buy an asset that should produce income or grow in value.

| Investment | How it earns | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Savings account or fixed deposit | Interest | Inflation may be higher than the interest rate |
| Shares on the stock exchange | Dividends and a rise in the share price | Share prices can fall; dividends are not guaranteed |
| Government or company bonds and debentures | Fixed interest | The borrower may fail to pay |
| Property (houses, land, a shop) | Rent and a rise in value | Costly to buy, slow to sell, repairs |
| Livestock (cattle, goats, chickens) | Offspring, milk, eggs, sale | Disease, drought, theft |
| A small business | Profit | The business may fail |

### Reasons for investing

- To earn **extra income** (dividends, rent, interest, profit).
- To **protect savings from inflation**, because some assets rise in value as prices rise.
- To **build wealth** for retirement or for children.
- To **own part of a business** and share in its success.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: comparing two investments]
Farai has US\$400. He compares two choices for one year:

- **Fixed deposit:** assume interest of 6% a year.
- **Shares:** he expects a dividend of US\$30 and the shares to be worth US\$420 at the end of the year.

1. Fixed deposit return: 6% of US\$400 = US\$24, which is 6%.
2. Shares return: dividend US\$30 + gain in value (US\$420 − US\$400 = US\$20) = US\$50.
3. Shares return as a percentage: 50 ÷ 400 × 100 = 12.5%.
4. The shares are expected to earn more, but the dividend and the share price are not guaranteed, while the fixed deposit's interest is agreed in advance. Farai must weigh the higher return against the higher risk.
:::

## Saving and investment compared

| Saving | Investment |
| --- | --- |
| Setting aside income that is not spent | Using savings to buy an income-earning asset |
| Main aim is safety and availability (liquidity) | Main aim is return: income and growth |
| Low risk, low return | Higher risk, possibly higher return |
| Money can usually be withdrawn quickly | Assets may take time to sell |

Saving comes first: a person cannot invest without savings. In the economy, savings deposited in banks are lent to businesses, which use them for **business investment** in machinery and buildings.

:::example{title="A savings group in Mutare"}
Ten market vendors in Mutare each contribute US\$20 every month. Each month one member receives the full pool. The group helps members buy stock in bulk, but it earns no interest and depends on every member paying. Two members later open bank savings accounts so that their money earns interest and is safe if the group breaks up.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Treating saving and investment as the same thing"}
Saving is not spending income; investment is buying an asset with savings in order to earn a return. Use the words carefully.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring risk"}
A higher expected return almost always comes with a higher risk. A good answer names both.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the gain in value"}
The return on shares or property includes both the income (dividend or rent) **and** any rise in the value of the asset.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Distinguish clearly"}
For "distinguish between saving and investment" give one sentence on each and a clear contrast, ideally with an example of each.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Advise with reasons"}
When asked which investment suits a person, consider their age, income, how soon they need the money and how much risk they can take, then recommend.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board coverage"}
ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 1) and Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 1) both cover this topic. Cambridge 0264 does not examine personal finance.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Personal income comes from wages and salaries, profit, rent, interest, dividends and transfer payments.
- Saving is income not spent now; people save for emergencies, future purchases, retirement and business.
- People save at home, in bank accounts, fixed deposits, savings banks, savings clubs, mobile money and insurance schemes.
- Investment uses savings to buy income-earning assets such as shares, property, livestock or a business.
- Saving puts safety first; investment seeks a higher return at a higher risk.
:::
