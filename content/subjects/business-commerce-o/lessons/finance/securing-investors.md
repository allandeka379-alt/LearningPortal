## Introduction

Tendai and Rudo run a small poultry enterprise near Marondera. Demand for their chickens and eggs is growing, and they want to build a second fowl run and buy an incubator. They cannot fund this themselves, so they need **investors**: people or organisations willing to put money into the enterprise in return for a share of the profits or for interest. Investors have many choices, so an enterprise must convince them that their money will be safe and will earn a good return.

This Form 4 topic in Business Enterprise and Skills asks how enterprises secure investors. Questions often give a short scenario and ask you to explain what an enterprise should do to attract investment and why each step builds confidence.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Investor"}
A person or organisation that provides money to an enterprise expecting a return, such as a share of profits (dividends), interest or a rise in the value of their stake.
:::

:::definition{term="Corporate governance"}
The system of rules, practices and processes by which an organisation is directed and controlled, so that it is run honestly, openly and in the interests of its owners and other stakeholders.
:::

:::definition{term="Business plan"}
A written document that describes an enterprise, its objectives, its market, its operations and its financial forecasts, and explains how it will succeed.
:::

:::definition{term="Track record"}
The past performance of an enterprise or its owners: its history of sales, profits, repayments and keeping promises.
:::

:::definition{term="Brand reputation"}
How customers, suppliers and the public see an enterprise's name and products: for quality, reliability and honesty.
:::

## What investors look for

- **Return:** expected profits, dividends or interest compared with other uses of their money.
- **Safety (risk):** how likely they are to lose their money.
- **Growth potential:** a growing market and a product customers want.
- **Management ability:** owners and managers with the skills and honesty to run the enterprise.
- **Information:** clear, accurate records and accounts so they can judge performance.
- **An exit:** a way to get their money back later, such as selling their shares.

## Ways enterprises attract investors

### 1. A favourable policy environment

Investors are attracted to countries and sectors where government policies are stable, predictable and supportive: clear property rights, consistent tax rules, fair courts that enforce contracts, simple business registration, and incentives such as tax relief in priority sectors or special economic zones. An enterprise cannot set national policy, but it can **choose** sectors and locations where policy supports investment, **use** available incentives, and show investors that it **complies** with all regulations.

### 2. Good corporate governance

Investors trust an enterprise that is run openly and honestly:

- proper, up-to-date **financial records** and audited accounts;
- clear separation between the owners' personal money and the business's money;
- a **board** or management team with defined roles, and decisions recorded;
- **transparency**: regular reports to investors and honest disclosure of problems;
- **accountability**: managers answer for results; conflicts of interest are declared;
- compliance with the law, including paying tax and registering the business.

### 3. A reputable brand

A respected name reduces risk for investors because loyal customers mean steadier sales. Enterprises build reputation through consistent quality, honest dealings with customers and suppliers, good customer service, social responsibility and positive publicity.

### 4. A sound business plan

A strong business plan shows investors exactly what their money will do:

| Section of the plan | What it tells the investor |
| --- | --- |
| Executive summary | The idea and how much finance is needed |
| Description of the enterprise and objectives | What the business does and aims to achieve |
| Market research and marketing plan | Who the customers are and how they will be reached |
| Operations plan | How the product will be made or the service delivered |
| Management and staffing | Who will run it and their experience |
| Financial forecasts | Expected sales, costs, profits and cash flow; break-even |
| Use of funds and expected return | How the money will be spent and what investors will earn |

### 5. A proven track record

An enterprise that has traded profitably, repaid loans on time and kept its promises is a lower risk. New enterprises can build a record by starting small, keeping careful records from the first day, using bank accounts so that transactions are recorded, and repaying small loans promptly.

### Other ways

- Offering **security** (collateral) or guarantees for loans.
- Offering an attractive **share of profits** or ownership.
- **Networking**: business associations, investor forums, trade fairs and agencies that support small and medium enterprises.
- Joining with a **partner** who has experience or assets.

:::example{title="Preparing to meet an investor"}
Before approaching a local investor, Tendai and Rudo open a separate business bank account, bring their sales and expense records up to date, and ask an accountant to prepare a simple statement of their last two years' results. They write a business plan showing that demand from two supermarkets and several schools exceeds their output, and they explain how the new fowl run will raise output and profit. They offer the investor a fixed share of profits and monthly reports. The investor agrees, impressed by their records and their record of supplying customers reliably.
:::

## Why securing investors matters

- Provides the **finance** needed to grow beyond the owners' own savings.
- Investors often bring **skills, contacts and advice** as well as money.
- External investment signals **confidence**, making it easier to win customers, suppliers and lenders.
- For the economy, investment creates **jobs**, output and tax revenue.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Talking only about money"}
Investors invest in people and systems as well as ideas. Mention records, governance, management skills and reputation, not just profits.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying an enterprise controls the policy environment"}
Government sets policy. An enterprise can choose favourable sectors, use incentives and comply with rules, but it cannot change national policy alone.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing business plan headings without purpose"}
Explain how each part of a business plan reduces the investor's uncertainty.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Explain the link to confidence"}
For each method, say what the enterprise does **and** why it makes an investor more confident: lower risk, better information or higher expected return.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Apply to the enterprise"}
Use the scenario's details: the product, the owners' experience, the records they keep, the market.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Investors look for return, safety, growth, capable management, good information and a way to exit.
- A stable, supportive policy environment attracts investment; enterprises choose favourable sectors and comply with rules.
- Good corporate governance means accurate records, transparency, accountability and legal compliance.
- A reputable brand and a proven track record lower risk for investors.
- A sound business plan explains the market, operations, management, forecasts and expected return.
:::
