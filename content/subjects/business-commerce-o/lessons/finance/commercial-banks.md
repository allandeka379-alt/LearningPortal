## Introduction

Tendai owns a hardware shop in Kwekwe. Each day he deposits takings at his bank, pays suppliers by electronic transfer, lets customers pay by card, and uses an overdraft when sales are slow. Without a commercial bank his business would have to keep large amounts of cash, which is risky and slow to use.

Commercial banks appear in ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 2, the banking system and commercial banks) and in Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 3, financial institutions). You must classify banks, describe their services, distinguish between types of account, and complete simple banking documents such as a deposit slip or a cheque.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Commercial bank"}
A profit-making company that accepts deposits from the public, lends money and provides payment services to individuals and businesses.
:::

:::definition{term="Current account"}
A bank account used for everyday payments by cheque, card and transfer; it usually earns little or no interest and may have an overdraft facility.
:::

:::definition{term="Savings (deposit) account"}
An account for money that is not needed immediately; it earns interest and is not used for cheques.
:::

:::definition{term="Overdraft"}
A facility that allows a current-account holder to withdraw more than the balance in the account, up to an agreed limit.
:::

:::definition{term="RTGS (real-time gross settlement)"}
An electronic system that transfers funds between accounts at different banks immediately, one payment at a time.
:::

## Classification of banks

| Type | Main customers and role |
| --- | --- |
| Central bank (the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe) | Bank of the government and of other banks; issues currency and controls the banking system |
| Commercial (retail) banks | The general public and businesses: deposits, loans and payments |
| Merchant banks | Large companies: big loans, advice on mergers and share issues, foreign trade finance |
| Building societies | Savers and home buyers: mortgages for houses |
| Savings banks | Small savers, often through the post office |
| Development and microfinance institutions | Long-term development finance; small loans to small businesses and individuals |

The other financial institutions and the central bank have their own lessons; this lesson concentrates on commercial banks.

## Functions and services of commercial banks

![Services of a commercial bank in four groups: accepting deposits (current, savings, fixed deposit), lending (overdrafts, loans, mortgages and asset finance), making payments (cheques, cards, EFT and RTGS, standing and debit orders, POS) and other services such as foreign exchange and letters of credit.](media:diagrams/commercial-bank-services.png "Figure: the main services of a commercial bank")

### Accepting deposits

| Feature | Current account | Savings account | Fixed deposit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main use | Everyday payments | Saving | Saving for a fixed period |
| Interest | Little or none | Paid | Higher rate |
| Cheque book and card | Yes | Card for withdrawals; no cheques | No |
| Withdrawals | On demand | On demand (sometimes limited) | At the end of the term |
| Overdraft | Possible | No | No |
| Bank charges | Usually charged | Few | Few |

### Lending

- **Overdrafts**: short-term, flexible; interest only on the amount overdrawn.
- **Loans**: a fixed sum for an agreed period, repaid in instalments; often secured by **collateral**.
- **Mortgages** for property, and **asset finance** for vehicles and equipment.

### Making payments (the money transmission service)

- **Cheques**: a written order by an account holder (the drawer) to the bank (the drawee) to pay a sum to a named person (the payee). A **crossed cheque** (two parallel lines) must be paid into a bank account, which makes it safer.
- **Debit cards** take money straight from the account at shops and **automated teller machines (ATMs)**; **credit cards** let the holder pay later up to a limit.
- **Electronic funds transfers**, including **RTGS** for large and urgent payments between banks.
- **Standing orders**: the customer instructs the bank to pay a **fixed** amount to the same payee at regular intervals (for example rent).
- **Debit orders (direct debits)**: the payee is authorised to collect amounts, which may vary, from the account (for example insurance premiums or a utility bill).
- **Point-of-sale (POS) machines** for card payments in shops.

### Other services

Safe custody of valuables and documents; foreign exchange and payments abroad; letters of credit for importers; financial advice; night safes for depositing takings after hours; bank statements and references for customers; payroll services for employers.

## Banking documents

**Deposit slip:** the customer writes the date, account name and number, the notes and coins or cheques paid in, the total, and signs. The bank stamps the counterfoil as a receipt.

**Cheque:** the drawer writes the date, the payee's name, the amount in words and in figures (which must agree), and signs. Writing "Account payee only" between crossing lines adds protection.

**Withdrawal slip:** used to take cash from a savings account over the counter.

**Bank statement:** a record sent by the bank showing all payments in (credits), payments out (debits) and the balance after each one.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: following a bank statement]
Tendai's current account at 1 March has a balance of US\$850.

| Date | Details | Debit US\$ | Credit US\$ | Balance US\$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 Mar | Balance brought forward |  |  | 850 |
| 4 Mar | Cash deposit |  | 400 | 1,250 |
| 9 Mar | Cheque 000214 (supplier) | 620 |  | 630 |
| 15 Mar | Standing order: insurance | 150 |  | 480 |
| 31 Mar | Bank charges | 12 |  | 468 |

1. A deposit is a credit: 850 + 400 = US\$1,250.
2. Each payment out is a debit: 1,250 − 620 = 630; 630 − 150 = 480; 480 − 12 = US\$468.
3. The closing balance is US\$468 in Tendai's favour.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: an overdraft]
Rudo has US\$300 in her current account and an overdraft limit of US\$500. She pays a supplier US\$750 by transfer. Assume overdraft interest of 2% a month.

1. New balance: US\$300 − US\$750 = US\$450 overdrawn (shown as (450) or "450 Dr").
2. This is within her US\$500 limit, so the bank pays.
3. Interest for one month on the overdrawn amount: 2% of US\$450 = US\$9.
4. Interest is charged only on the amount overdrawn, not on the US\$500 limit.
:::

## Benefits of commercial banks

- **To individuals:** safety of money, interest on savings, convenient payments, loans for houses and education.
- **To businesses:** finance for working capital and expansion, easier and safer payments, advice and foreign exchange for trade.
- **To the economy:** savings are channelled into loans for investment, trade is made easier, and the government can manage the money supply through the banks.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing standing orders and debit orders"}
A standing order is set up by the customer for a **fixed** amount. A debit order lets the **payee** collect an amount that may vary.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the parties to a cheque"}
The **drawer** writes and signs the cheque, the **drawee** is the bank, and the **payee** receives the money.
:::

:::mistake{title="Reversing debits and credits on a statement"}
On a bank statement a **credit** increases the customer's balance and a **debit** reduces it.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Banks include the central bank, commercial banks, merchant banks, building societies, savings banks and development or microfinance institutions.
- Commercial banks accept deposits (current, savings and fixed deposit accounts), lend (overdrafts, loans, mortgages) and make payments.
- Payment services include cheques, debit and credit cards, ATMs, RTGS and other electronic transfers, standing orders and debit orders.
- Other services: safe custody, foreign exchange, advice, night safes, statements and references.
- On a bank statement, credits add to the balance and debits reduce it; overdraft interest is charged only on the amount overdrawn.
:::
