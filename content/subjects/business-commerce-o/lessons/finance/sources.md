## Introduction

Farai has run a successful welding business in Kwekwe for three years and wants to buy a second welding machine and a small truck. He could use the profit he has kept in the business, borrow from a bank, buy the truck on hire purchase, lease it, bring in a partner, or ask relatives for help. Each source has a cost and a risk, and the right choice depends on his business and on what the money is for.

Sources of finance are examined in all three syllabi: ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 3, business finance), Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 1, sources of finance) and Cambridge 0264 (5.1.2). Expect to describe sources, give advantages and disadvantages, and, most importantly, **recommend and justify** a source for a given business.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Internal source of finance"}
Finance obtained from within the business itself, such as retained profit or the sale of assets.
:::

:::definition{term="External source of finance"}
Finance obtained from outside the business, such as loans, share capital or trade credit.
:::

:::definition{term="Retained profit"}
Profit kept in the business after tax and payments to owners, available to be reinvested.
:::

:::definition{term="Leasing"}
Renting an asset such as a vehicle or machine for an agreed period; the leasing company remains the owner.
:::

:::definition{term="Venture capital"}
Finance provided by investors (venture capitalists) to new or growing businesses with high potential, usually in return for a share of ownership.
:::

:::definition{term="Crowdfunding"}
Raising finance from a large number of people, each contributing a small amount, usually through an online platform.
:::

## Internal sources

| Source | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Owners' savings** (sole traders and partners) | No interest; no loss of control; quick | Limited amount; owner risks personal savings |
| **Retained profit** | No interest or repayment; no loss of control | Not available to new or loss-making businesses; less paid to owners; may be too small |
| **Sale of assets** (unused land, old vehicles) | Turns idle assets into cash | Takes time to find a buyer; asset may be needed later; only available if assets exist |
| **Working capital** (reducing inventory, collecting debts faster) | No cost; improves efficiency | Limited; too little stock or strict credit terms may lose customers |

## External sources

### Short-term external sources

| Source | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bank overdraft** | Flexible; interest only on the amount used; quick to arrange | High interest; bank can withdraw it at short notice |
| **Trade credit** (buy now, pay suppliers later, often in 30 days) | Usually no interest if paid on time; eases cash flow | Lose cash discounts; late payment damages supplier relations |
| **Debt factoring** (selling trade receivables to a factor) | Immediate cash from credit sales | The factor keeps part of the debt as a fee |

### Medium- and long-term external sources

| Source | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bank loan** | Large sums; fixed repayment plan; no loss of control | Interest; often needs collateral; must be repaid even if profits fall |
| **Hire purchase** | Asset used at once; payments spread | Costs more than the cash price; asset repossessed if payments stop |
| **Leasing** | No large outlay; maintenance often included; easy to upgrade | Never owned; total cost may exceed the price |
| **Share capital** (limited companies) | Large sums; no interest; never repaid | Dividends expected; owners' control diluted; costly to issue; not available to sole traders |
| **Debentures** (long-term loans to companies) | Large sums for long periods; no loss of control | Interest must be paid even in loss years; must be repaid |
| **Venture capital** | Finance for risky ventures banks will not fund; investors bring expertise | Investors take a share of ownership and profits |
| **Government grants** | Usually need not be repaid | Conditions attached; limited and competitive |
| **Crowdfunding** | Reaches many small investors; tests public interest | Platform fees; idea can be copied if target not met |
| **Family, friends and community sources** (including savings clubs) | Flexible, low or no interest | Small sums; disputes may harm relationships |
| **Microfinance loans** | Small loans without collateral | High interest; short repayment periods |

:::worked-solution[Example 1: the cost of a bank loan]
Farai borrows US\$5,000 to buy a welding machine. Assume flat interest of 12% a year for 2 years, repaid in 24 equal monthly instalments.

1. Interest per year: 12% of US\$5,000 = US\$600.
2. Interest for 2 years: US\$600 × 2 = US\$1,200.
3. Total to repay: US\$5,000 + US\$1,200 = US\$6,200.
4. Monthly instalment: US\$6,200 ÷ 24 = US\$258.33.
5. Farai must be sure his monthly cash flow can cover about US\$258 before he signs.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: leasing or hire purchase for a truck]
Farai compares two ways of getting a small truck for two years:

- **Leasing:** 24 monthly payments of US\$240; the truck is returned at the end.
- **Hire purchase:** a deposit of US\$1,000 and 24 monthly payments of US\$230; he owns the truck at the end.

1. Total leasing cost: 24 × US\$240 = US\$5,760.
2. Total HP cost: US\$1,000 + 24 × US\$230 = US\$1,000 + US\$5,520 = US\$6,520.
3. HP costs US\$760 more over two years and needs US\$1,000 at the start, but Farai owns a truck with a resale value at the end.
4. If he needs the truck for many years, HP is likely to be better; if he wants to keep cash now and upgrade soon, leasing suits him.
:::

### Internal or external: a quick comparison

Internal sources are cheaper and keep control with the owners, but they are limited in size and are not available to a new business. External sources can provide much larger sums but cost interest or dividends, may need security, and some (shares, partners, venture capital) bring new owners into the business.

## Factors in choosing a source of finance

- **Purpose and time period:** long-term assets need long-term finance; short-term needs need short-term finance.
- **Amount needed:** small sums from savings or overdraft; large sums from loans or shares.
- **Legal form of the business:** only companies can issue shares and debentures; sole traders rely on savings, loans and family.
- **Size and age of the business:** new and small firms find it harder to borrow; established firms have retained profit and a track record.
- **Cost:** interest, fees and dividends.
- **Control:** selling shares or bringing in partners dilutes control; loans do not.
- **Risk and existing debt:** a business with large loans already may be refused more, or may be unwise to borrow more.
- **Security (collateral)** available for a loan.

## Recommending a source

A good recommendation:

1. names the source;
2. links it to the **purpose** and **period** of the need and to the **type** of business;
3. weighs its main advantage against its main disadvantage **in this case**;
4. compares it with at least one alternative and explains why it is better here.

:::example{title="A justified recommendation"}
"A bank loan is the best source for Farai's welding machine. The machine will be used for many years, so a two-year loan spreads the cost while the machine earns money. Farai is a sole trader, so he cannot sell shares, and his retained profit is too small. Although he must pay interest, the instalment of about US\$258 a month is affordable from his profit, and unlike a partner, a bank takes no share of control."
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Recommending share capital to a sole trader"}
Sole traders and partnerships cannot issue shares. Check the legal form before recommending.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling trade credit a loan from the bank"}
Trade credit is given by **suppliers**, who let the business pay later for goods.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing without judging"}
"Recommend" questions need a decision and a reason why the chosen source is **better than another** for this business.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Use the case"}
Cambridge part (d) questions such as "Recommend which source X should use. Justify your answer" reward application: use the business's size, legal form, profit and purpose.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Internal or external?"}
Classify carefully: retained profit and sale of assets are internal; loans, overdrafts, trade credit, leasing, hire purchase and share capital are external.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board coverage"}
All three syllabi cover sources of finance. ZIMSEC Commerce links this to shares and debentures and to long-term and short-term capital; Business Enterprise and Skills stresses sources for small enterprises; Cambridge 0264 stresses choosing and recommending.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Internal sources: owners' savings, retained profit, sale of assets, better management of working capital.
- Short-term external sources: overdrafts, trade credit, debt factoring.
- Long-term external sources: loans, hire purchase, leasing, share capital, debentures, venture capital, grants, crowdfunding, family and community, microfinance.
- Choose by purpose and period, amount, legal form, size, cost, control, risk and security.
- A recommendation names a source, applies it to the case, weighs it and compares it with an alternative.
:::
