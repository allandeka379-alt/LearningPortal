## Introduction

A tobacco farmer in Mashonaland cures his crop using firewood from the indigenous forest near his farm. Each season he must travel further to find trees. A neighbour planted a woodlot of fast-growing trees five years ago and now cuts only what grows back each year. Both farmers need wood; only one is using it in a way that can continue. This is the idea of **sustainability**: meeting today's needs without destroying the resources that future generations, and the business itself, will need.

Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 2) studies the acquisition, ownership and sustainable use of resources such as land, water, forests and wildlife. Cambridge 0264 (topic 4.3) asks how businesses can produce goods and services sustainably and the advantages and disadvantages of doing so. Pollution, external costs and pressure groups are covered in separate lessons; here the focus is what the business itself does.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Sustainable production"}
Producing goods and services in ways that use resources efficiently and do not damage the environment, so that production can continue in the long term.
:::

:::definition{term="Renewable resource"}
A resource that can be replaced naturally, such as solar energy, wind, water, and forests that are replanted.
:::

:::definition{term="Non-renewable resource"}
A resource that cannot be replaced once used, such as coal, oil and minerals.
:::

:::definition{term="Recycling"}
Processing used materials (paper, plastic, glass, metal) so that they can be made into new products.
:::

## Methods of sustainable production

| Method | What it means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renewable energy | Using solar, wind, hydro or biogas instead of fossil fuels | Solar panels on a factory roof; biogas from animal waste on a farm |
| Using fewer resources | Efficient machines and processes that use less energy, water and raw material | Drip irrigation; energy-saving lights and motors |
| Reducing waste | Cutting off-cuts, spoilage and over-production | Accurate cutting of fabric; better storage of fresh produce |
| Reusing | Using items again instead of throwing them away | Returnable glass bottles and crates |
| Recycling | Turning waste into new materials | Collecting plastic bottles and cans; recycling paper |
| Environmentally friendly products | Products that last longer, use less energy or are biodegradable | Energy-saving stoves; natural cleaning products |
| Environmentally friendly packaging | Less packaging, recycled or biodegradable materials | Paper bags instead of plastic; refill packs |

## Sustainable use of natural resources

**Land**: avoiding overgrazing and soil erosion through crop rotation, contour ridges, conservation farming and controlled stocking of cattle.

**Water**: drip irrigation, harvesting rainwater, repairing leaks, treating and reusing water, and not polluting rivers with waste.

**Forests**: cutting only what can regrow, replanting, planting woodlots for firewood and timber, using efficient stoves and kilns, preventing veld fires.

**Wildlife**: protecting habitats, preventing poaching, and earning income through well-managed tourism (for example, community projects in which local people share income from wildlife tourism and so have a reason to protect animals).

:::example{title="Sustainable resource use in a lodge"}
A safari lodge near Hwange uses solar power for lighting and water heating, harvests rainwater, treats grey water for its garden and employs local people as guides. Guests are attracted by the lodge's careful approach, and the wildlife that brings them is protected.
:::

## Saving money through sustainability

Some sustainable actions **reduce costs**:

:::worked-solution[Savings from solar power]
A small factory in Masvingo pays US\$380 a month for electricity. Solar panels would cut this bill by 65%.
1. Monthly saving = 65% × US\$380 = 0.65 × 380 = US\$247.
2. Annual saving = US\$247 × 12 = **US\$2,964**.
3. If the panels cost US\$9,000, the saving covers the cost in 9,000 ÷ 247 = about 36.4 months, that is just over three years. After that the saving adds to profit, and the factory is protected from power cuts during the day.
:::

:::worked-solution[Savings from reducing waste]
A clothing workshop uses 2,000 metres of fabric a month at US\$3 a metre and wastes 15% as off-cuts. Better cutting patterns reduce waste to 5%.
1. Waste before = 15% × 2,000 = 300 metres; after = 5% × 2,000 = 100 metres.
2. Fabric saved = 300 − 100 = 200 metres a month.
3. Monthly saving = 200 × US\$3 = **US\$600**, assuming the same number of garments is made with less fabric.
:::

## Advantages of becoming more sustainable

- **Lower costs** in the long run: less energy, water, raw materials and waste disposal.
- **Better reputation and brand image**: many customers, especially in export markets and tourism, prefer environmentally responsible businesses.
- **Access to customers and contracts** that require environmental standards (some supermarkets, exporters and international buyers).
- **Avoiding fines and complying with laws** on pollution and waste.
- **Securing future supplies** of raw materials such as timber and water.
- **Motivated employees** who are proud of their employer.

## Disadvantages

- **High initial costs**: solar panels, new machines, treatment plants, training.
- **Higher costs per unit** in some cases (recycled or biodegradable packaging may cost more), possibly leading to higher prices.
- **Time before savings appear**, which is difficult for a business with cash-flow problems.
- **Competitors** that ignore sustainability may have lower costs and prices in the short run.
- **Changes to methods** may need new skills and cause disruption.

A business must weigh these. Often a sensible approach is to start with actions that save money quickly (reducing waste, repairing leaks) and use the savings to fund larger investments.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking sustainability only costs money"}
Many sustainable actions reduce costs. Show both the cost and the saving.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing reusing and recycling"}
Reusing uses an item again in its existing form (a returnable bottle). Recycling processes it into new material.
:::

:::mistake{title="Vague answers"}
"Be eco-friendly" earns little. Name a specific method that fits the business (solar water heating for a hotel, drip irrigation for a farm).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Applying to the case"}
Choose methods that suit the business: farms (water, soil, energy), factories (energy, waste, packaging), tourism (wildlife, water, energy). Then explain the effect on costs, revenue and reputation.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
Cambridge 0264 asks about methods of sustainable production and their advantages and disadvantages for a business. Business Enterprise and Skills links sustainability to the acquisition and use of resources, such as land, water, forests and wildlife, by enterprises and communities.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Sustainable production uses resources efficiently and protects the environment so production can continue.
- Methods: renewable energy, fewer resources, less waste, reusing, recycling, environmentally friendly products and packaging.
- Sustainable use of land, water, forests and wildlife protects the resources businesses depend on.
- Advantages: lower long-run costs, better reputation, access to customers, avoiding fines, secure supplies.
- Disadvantages: high initial costs, sometimes higher unit costs, slow savings and cheaper unsustainable rivals.
:::
