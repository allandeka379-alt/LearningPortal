## Introduction

Chipo wants to open a small factory in Bulawayo making school backpacks. Before she signs a lease, she asks: how many backpacks must I sell each month just to cover my costs? Below that number she loses money; above it she makes a profit. That number is her **break-even output**.

Break-even analysis is one of the most useful tools for planning a new business, a new product or a change in price. ZIMSEC Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 4) examines the break-even point and the margin of safety; Cambridge 0264 prints the formulas for contribution, break-even output and margin of safety and expects you to calculate them and explain the effect of changes. This lesson covers the calculations; the next lesson covers break-even charts and the uses and limits of break-even analysis.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Break-even"}
The level of output (sales) at which total revenue equals total costs, so the business makes neither a profit nor a loss.
:::

:::definition{term="Contribution per unit"}
Selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit: the amount each unit sold contributes towards paying fixed costs and then to profit.
:::

:::definition{term="Break-even output"}
The number of units that must be sold to break even.
:::

:::definition{term="Break-even revenue"}
The sales revenue at the break-even output (break-even output × selling price).
:::

:::definition{term="Margin of safety"}
The amount by which actual (or planned) sales exceed the break-even level of sales, in units.
:::

## The formulas

:::formula{name="Contribution per unit"}
Contribution per unit = selling price per unit − variable cost per unit
:::

:::formula{name="Break-even output"}
Break-even output = total fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit
:::

:::formula{name="Break-even revenue"}
Break-even revenue = break-even output × selling price per unit
:::

:::formula{name="Margin of safety"}
Margin of safety = actual sales (units) − break-even sales (units)
:::

### Why contribution works

Each unit sold brings in its price. Part of that price pays for the unit's own variable costs (materials, packaging). What is left, the **contribution**, goes towards the fixed costs (rent, salaries). Once enough units have been sold for the contributions to pay all the fixed costs, the business has broken even. Every unit sold after that adds its full contribution to **profit**.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: Chipo's backpacks]
Chipo's fixed costs are US\$6,000 a month. Each backpack sells for US\$20 and has a variable cost of US\$12. She expects to sell 1,000 backpacks a month.

1. Contribution per unit = US\$20 − US\$12 = **US\$8**.
2. Break-even output = 6,000 ÷ 8 = **750 backpacks** a month.
3. Break-even revenue = 750 × US\$20 = **US\$15,000**.
4. Margin of safety = 1,000 − 750 = **250 backpacks**. Sales could fall by 250 a month (25% of expected sales) before she makes a loss.
5. Check: profit at 1,000 = 1,000 × 20 − (6,000 + 1,000 × 12) = 20,000 − 18,000 = US\$2,000, which equals 250 × US\$8 (margin of safety × contribution).
:::

### Profit from contribution

Once the break-even output is known, profit at any level of output can be found quickly:

:::formula{name="Profit from contribution"}
Profit = (contribution per unit × units sold) − fixed costs
:::

## The effect of changes

| Change | Effect on contribution | Effect on break-even output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selling price rises | Rises | **Falls** (fewer units needed) |
| Selling price falls | Falls | Rises |
| Variable cost per unit rises | Falls | **Rises** |
| Variable cost per unit falls | Rises | Falls |
| Fixed costs rise | No change | **Rises** |
| Fixed costs fall | No change | Falls |

:::worked-solution[Example 2: what if?]
Starting from Chipo's figures (fixed costs US\$6,000, price US\$20, variable cost US\$12, break-even 750):

1. **Price rises to US\$22:** contribution = 22 − 12 = US\$10; break-even = 6,000 ÷ 10 = **600** backpacks. (But sales may fall at the higher price.)
2. **Fixed costs rise to US\$7,200** (a bigger workshop): contribution still US\$8; break-even = 7,200 ÷ 8 = **900** backpacks. The margin of safety at 1,000 sales falls to 100.
3. **Variable cost rises to US\$14** (dearer fabric): contribution = 20 − 14 = US\$6; break-even = 6,000 ÷ 6 = **1,000** backpacks. At expected sales of 1,000 she would only just break even.
:::

### When the answer is not a whole number

You cannot sell part of a unit. If the break-even output is not a whole number, round **up**, because rounding down would leave the business just short of covering its costs.

:::worked-solution[Example 3: rounding up]
A food stall has fixed costs of US\$5,000 a month; each meal sells for US\$9 and costs US\$5.50 to make.

1. Contribution = 9 − 5.50 = US\$3.50.
2. Break-even = 5,000 ÷ 3.50 = 1,428.57… meals.
3. Round up: the stall must sell **1,429** meals to break even (1,428 meals would leave a small loss).
:::

## Using break-even results

- **Starting a business:** is the break-even output realistic compared with the expected market?
- **Setting prices:** how does a price change affect the break-even point and the margin of safety?
- **Choosing between options:** which location, supplier or machine gives the lower break-even point and larger margin of safety?
- **Supporting a loan application:** banks like to see a business plan showing a clear margin of safety.

A large margin of safety means the business is less at risk if sales fall. A small or negative margin of safety is a warning.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Dividing fixed costs by the selling price"}
Break-even output = fixed costs ÷ **contribution** (price − variable cost), not ÷ price.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving the margin of safety in dollars when units are asked"}
The Cambridge formula gives the margin of safety in **units**. If revenue is wanted, multiply by the price and say so.
:::

:::mistake{title="Rounding the break-even output down"}
Always round up to the next whole unit, unless the question says otherwise.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying fixed costs change contribution"}
Contribution depends only on price and variable cost per unit. A change in fixed costs changes the break-even output, not the contribution.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Write the formula and the unit"}
State "break-even output = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit", substitute, and give the answer in **units** (and revenue in US\$ if asked).
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Explain the effect, then judge"}
When a question changes the price or costs, recalculate, compare with the old figure and comment on whether the change is sensible, for example whether customers will still buy at the higher price.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board coverage"}
ZIMSEC Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 4) and Cambridge 0264 both examine break-even and the margin of safety. Cambridge prints the formulas.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Break-even is where total revenue equals total costs: no profit, no loss.
- Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit; break-even output = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit; round up to a whole unit.
- Break-even revenue = break-even output × price; margin of safety = actual sales − break-even sales (units).
- Profit = contribution per unit × units sold − fixed costs.
- Break-even output falls when price rises or costs fall, and rises when price falls or costs rise.
:::
