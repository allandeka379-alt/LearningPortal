## Introduction

Farai makes concrete blocks in a small yard outside Masvingo. Every month he pays rent for the yard and the wage of his security guard, whether he makes one block or ten thousand. Every block also needs cement, sand and water, so the more blocks he makes, the more he spends on materials. To set a price, decide whether to accept a large order or choose between suppliers, Farai needs to understand these different kinds of cost.

Both ZIMSEC Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 4) and Cambridge 0264 examine business costs. Cambridge prints the formulas for total cost and average cost; both boards expect you to classify costs, calculate them and use them in decisions. This lesson also prepares you for break-even analysis, which uses the same cost ideas.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Fixed costs"}
Costs that do not change with the level of output in the short run, for example rent, salaries of managers, insurance and loan interest. They must be paid even when output is zero.
:::

:::definition{term="Variable costs"}
Costs that change directly with the level of output, for example raw materials, packaging and piece-rate wages. When output is zero, variable costs are zero.
:::

:::definition{term="Total cost"}
Fixed costs plus total variable costs at a given level of output.
:::

:::definition{term="Average cost (unit cost)"}
Total cost divided by the number of units produced: the cost of making one unit.
:::

:::definition{term="Direct costs"}
Costs that can be linked to a particular product, such as the timber in a table or the wages of the carpenter who makes it.
:::

:::definition{term="Indirect costs (overheads)"}
Costs that cannot be linked to a single product because they are shared by the whole business, such as rent, the manager's salary and electricity for the office.
:::

## The formulas

:::formula{name="Total variable cost"}
Total variable cost = variable cost per unit × number of units
:::

:::formula{name="Total cost"}
Total cost = fixed costs + total variable costs
:::

:::formula{name="Average cost"}
Average cost per unit = total cost ÷ total output
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 1: costs at three levels of output]
Farai's fixed costs are US\$2,000 a month. Each block has a variable cost of US\$3.

| Output (blocks) | Fixed costs US\$ | Total variable cost US\$ | Total cost US\$ | Average cost US\$ |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 500 | 2,000 | 1,500 | 3,500 | 7.00 |
| 1,000 | 2,000 | 3,000 | 5,000 | 5.00 |
| 2,000 | 2,000 | 6,000 | 8,000 | 4.00 |

1. At 1,000 blocks: total variable cost = 1,000 × US\$3 = US\$3,000.
2. Total cost = US\$2,000 + US\$3,000 = US\$5,000.
3. Average cost = US\$5,000 ÷ 1,000 = US\$5.00 per block.
4. As output rises, average cost falls (from US\$7.00 to US\$4.00), because the fixed costs are **spread over more units**.
:::

## Classifying costs

| Cost | Fixed or variable? | Direct or indirect? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rent of the factory | Fixed | Indirect |
| Raw materials for the product | Variable | Direct |
| Salary of the accountant | Fixed | Indirect |
| Wages paid per item made (piece rate) | Variable | Direct |
| Packaging for each product | Variable | Direct |
| Insurance of the building | Fixed | Indirect |

Fixed and variable describe how a cost behaves as **output** changes; direct and indirect describe whether it can be traced to **one product**. Some costs are semi-variable: an electricity bill may have a fixed monthly charge plus a charge per unit used.

Fixed costs are only fixed in the **short run**. If Farai moves to a larger yard, his rent will rise to a new fixed level.

## Using cost data to make decisions

Cost information helps managers decide:

- **What price to set:** the price must cover average cost if the business is to make a profit.
- **Whether to continue making a product:** if revenue does not cover costs, the business may stop or change the product.
- **Whether to accept an extra order:** if the business has spare capacity and fixed costs are already covered, an order is worth accepting if its price is above the variable cost per unit.
- **Which supplier to use:** comparing total costs, including delivery, from different suppliers.
- **What quantity to produce:** larger output lowers average cost, but only if the goods can be sold.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: should Farai accept a special order?]
Farai is producing 1,000 blocks a month and selling them at US\$7. His yard has room to make more. A builder offers to buy an extra 300 blocks at only US\$5.50 each.

1. The fixed costs (US\$2,000) are already paid by the normal sales, so the order adds only variable costs.
2. Extra revenue = 300 × US\$5.50 = US\$1,650.
3. Extra cost = 300 × US\$3 = US\$900.
4. Extra profit = US\$1,650 − US\$900 = **US\$750**.
5. Farai should accept, as long as his regular customers do not find out and demand the lower price, and he has enough materials and labour.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: setting a price from average cost]
At 2,000 blocks a month Farai's average cost is US\$4.00 (Example 1). If he wants a profit of 25% on cost, his price would be US\$4.00 + 25% of US\$4.00 = US\$4.00 + US\$1.00 = **US\$5.00** per block. If he sells only 500 blocks, his average cost rises to US\$7.00 and a US\$5.00 price would cause a loss, so the price decision depends on how many he expects to sell.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying fixed costs never change"}
Fixed costs do not change with **output**, but they can change for other reasons, such as a rent increase or moving to bigger premises.
:::

:::mistake{title="Dividing fixed costs only to find average cost"}
Average cost = **total** cost ÷ output. Fixed cost ÷ output is average fixed cost only.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up fixed/variable with direct/indirect"}
Raw materials are both variable and direct; rent is fixed and indirect; but these are two different ways of classifying.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Fixed costs do not change with output in the short run; variable costs change directly with output.
- Total variable cost = variable cost per unit × units; total cost = fixed + total variable costs; average cost = total cost ÷ output.
- Direct costs can be traced to a product; indirect costs (overheads) are shared.
- Average cost falls as output rises because fixed costs are spread over more units.
- Cost data helps decide prices, special orders, suppliers, output levels and whether to continue a product.
:::
