## Introduction

Chipo noticed that pupils at her school in Chinhoyi had nowhere to buy stationery nearby. She bought exercise books, pens and rulers in bulk from a wholesaler, sold them from a table at the school gate and, within a year, rented a small shop. Chipo is an **entrepreneur**: she spotted an opportunity, risked her savings and organised resources to meet a need.

Every board starts business studies with enterprise. ZIMSEC Commerce (Form 1) asks about the qualities of an entrepreneur and the importance of enterprise skills to the economy. Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 1) uses the words **enterprising** and **enterpriser** and asks for skills, attributes, benefits and drawbacks. Cambridge 0264 (topic 1.3) covers the characteristics of successful entrepreneurs.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Enterprise"}
The willingness and ability to take risks and organise the other factors of production (land, labour and capital) to start and run a business. The word also means a business itself.
:::

:::definition{term="Entrepreneur (enterpriser)"}
A person who identifies a business opportunity, takes the risk of investing time and money, and organises resources to produce goods or services for profit. Business Enterprise and Skills uses the term enterpriser.
:::

:::definition{term="Enterprising"}
Showing initiative: spotting opportunities and acting on them with energy and creativity, whether in a business, a job or the community.
:::

:::definition{term="Profit"}
The reward for enterprise: the amount by which revenue exceeds costs.
:::

## Enterprise as a factor of production

Land, labour and capital do nothing on their own. The entrepreneur brings them together, decides what to produce and how, and **bears the risk** that the business may fail. The reward for this risk is **profit**; the penalty is **loss**.

| Factor of production | Reward |
| --- | --- |
| Land | Rent |
| Labour | Wages |
| Capital | Interest |
| Enterprise | Profit |

## Enterprising people outside business

Being **enterprising** is not only for business owners. An employee who suggests a cheaper way of delivering goods, a pupil who organises a fundraising car wash, or a community group that starts a vegetable garden to feed orphans are all enterprising: they notice a problem or opportunity, take the initiative and organise people and resources to act. Business Enterprise and Skills stresses that these skills can be **learned and practised** at school through enterprise projects, and that they are valued by employers as well as needed by people who start businesses.

## Skills and personal qualities of successful entrepreneurs

Examiners use both **skills** (which can be learned) and **qualities or attributes** (personal characteristics).

| Quality or skill | What it means | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Risk-taking** | Willing to invest savings and time without a guaranteed return | Every new business may fail |
| **Innovation and creativity** | Thinks of new products or better ways of doing things | Makes the business different from competitors |
| **Determination (hard work, resilience)** | Keeps going after setbacks; works long hours | Most new businesses face early problems |
| **Self-confidence** | Believes in the idea and can persuade others | Needed to win customers, lenders and partners |
| **Leadership** | Motivates and directs workers | Workers must share the entrepreneur's goals |
| **Communication and negotiation** | Explains ideas clearly; bargains with suppliers and customers | Better deals and loyal customers |
| **Decision-making and problem-solving** | Weighs options and acts quickly | Opportunities do not wait |
| **Planning and organising** | Sets goals, manages time and resources | Keeps costs and work under control |
| **Initiative** | Acts without waiting to be told | Spots and seizes opportunities first |
| **Financial and record-keeping skills** | Keeps accounts, controls cash | Knows whether the business is making a profit |

:::example{title="Qualities in action"}
When a supplier stopped delivering, Chipo hired a pick-up and collected stock herself (**determination** and **initiative**). She persuaded the school to let her sell uniforms on open days (**negotiation**). She began selling printed revision notes made with a friend's printer (**innovation**).
:::

## Benefits and drawbacks of being an entrepreneur

### Benefits

- **Independence:** being your own boss and making your own decisions.
- **Profit:** keeping the rewards of your effort; income can grow with the business.
- **Personal satisfaction (self-actualisation):** turning an idea into reality.
- **Flexibility** in choosing what work to do.
- **Recognition** in the community and the chance to help others by creating jobs.

### Drawbacks

- **Risk of losing savings** or borrowed money; with unlimited liability, even personal possessions.
- **Irregular income:** no guaranteed wage, especially at the start.
- **Long hours and stress;** responsibility for workers' jobs.
- **No paid leave or pension** unless the entrepreneur arranges them.
- **Many skills needed:** the owner may have to handle selling, buying, finance and staff.

## Importance of enterprise to the economy

1. **Creates employment:** new businesses hire workers, reducing unemployment.
2. **Increases output (economic growth):** more goods and services are produced.
3. **Innovation:** new products and methods improve living standards and efficiency.
4. **Competition:** new firms challenge existing ones, keeping prices down and quality up.
5. **Uses local resources** and adds value, for example processing farm produce.
6. **Tax revenue** for the government.
7. **Exports and import substitution:** some enterprises earn foreign currency or replace imports.
8. **Develops communities:** businesses provide services and role models in towns and rural areas.

:::example{title="Enterprise and the community"}
A group of young people in Masvingo start making peanut butter from locally grown groundnuts. They buy from small-scale farmers, giving the farmers a market; they employ four workers; they sell at local shops, replacing some imported spreads; and once registered they pay tax. One small enterprise benefits farmers, workers, consumers and the government.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing qualities without explaining them"}
"Risk-taker" earns one mark at most. Explain why the quality helps: "she is willing to risk her savings, which is necessary because any new business may fail".
:::

:::mistake{title="Thinking only owners can be enterprising"}
Employees, pupils and community members can be enterprising too, by showing initiative and creativity.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying profit is guaranteed"}
Profit is the **reward for risk**, not a certainty. Many new businesses make losses.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Apply to the person in the case"}
Pick qualities shown by the entrepreneur in the case and quote what they did as evidence.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Benefits versus drawbacks"}
"Discuss whether Tendai should become an entrepreneur" needs benefits and drawbacks applied to Tendai, then a conclusion.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
Business Enterprise and Skills prefers **enterpriser**; Commerce and Cambridge use **entrepreneur**. Use the board's word, or give both. Commerce emphasises the importance of enterprise skills to the economy; Cambridge links entrepreneurs to business plans and government support (covered in later lessons).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Enterprise is the factor of production that takes risks and organises land, labour and capital; its reward is profit.
- An entrepreneur (enterpriser) identifies opportunities, takes risks and organises resources.
- Key qualities: risk-taking, innovation, determination, self-confidence, leadership, communication, decision-making and planning.
- Being an entrepreneur brings independence, profit and satisfaction, but also risk, irregular income and stress.
- Enterprise creates jobs, output, innovation, competition, tax revenue and exports.
:::
