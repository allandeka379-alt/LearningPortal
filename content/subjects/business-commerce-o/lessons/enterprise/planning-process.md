## Introduction

A group of Form 3 pupils in Masvingo wants to run a school enterprise project. One pupil suggests buying fruit and selling it immediately. Another says they should first find out what pupils want, agree what they are trying to achieve, compare a few ideas and then plan who does what. The second pupil is describing the **business planning process**: a sequence of steps that turns a vague wish into a project that can be carried out and checked.

Business Enterprise and Skills examines the planning process in Form 2 and the **implementation** of a business plan on a project in Form 4. You must be able to describe each step, explain why it matters and apply the steps to a project.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Business planning"}
Deciding in advance what a business wants to achieve and how it will achieve it, then checking progress.
:::

:::definition{term="Environmental analysis"}
Studying the internal strengths and weaknesses of the business and the external opportunities and threats it faces.
:::

:::definition{term="Destiny, cause and calling"}
The three parts of a business's purpose: its destiny (where it wants to be in the future, its vision), its cause (the need or problem it exists to address) and its calling (the founders' personal passion and commitment that drive it).
:::

:::definition{term="Objective"}
A specific target the business aims to achieve, usually within a stated time.
:::

:::definition{term="Control"}
Comparing actual results with planned results and taking action to correct differences.
:::

## The steps in the planning process

### 1. Environmental analysis

The planners study the situation. **Internal** factors: skills, money, equipment, time (strengths and weaknesses). **External** factors: customers, competitors, prices, the economy, government rules, technology (opportunities and threats). This is often set out as a **SWOT analysis**.

### 2. Purpose: destiny, cause and calling

Before choosing targets, the enterprise defines **why it exists**:

- **Destiny:** the long-term picture of where the enterprise is heading, often written as a **vision statement**, for example "to be the most trusted bakery in Gweru".
- **Cause:** the need or problem it serves, often written as a **mission statement**, for example "to supply fresh, affordable bread to township households".
- **Calling:** the personal passion and values of the founders, which keep them committed when things get hard.

A clear purpose guides every later decision and motivates the people involved.

### 3. Objective setting

The planners turn the purpose into **objectives**. Good objectives are **SMART**:

| Letter | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S | Specific | Sell freshly baked bread |
| M | Measurable | 500 loaves a week |
| A | Achievable | Possible with one oven and two workers |
| R | Realistic (relevant) | Fits the purpose and the market |
| T | Time-bound | By the end of the third month |

### 4. Options generation, evaluation and selection

- **Generate** several ways of reaching the objectives (brainstorm ideas: different products, locations, prices, methods).
- **Evaluate** each option against criteria: cost, expected revenue and profit, risk, skills needed, time, fit with the purpose.
- **Select** the best option and record why.

:::example{title="Evaluating options for a school project"}
| Option | Start-up cost | Expected demand | Risk | Fits our skills? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Selling fruit | Low | Medium | Fruit spoils | Yes |
| Making freezits | Medium (freezer) | High in Terms 1 and 3 | Power cuts | Yes |
| Printing and binding notes | High (printer) | Medium | Machine breakdown | Partly |

The pupils select freezits: demand is highest, the cost is manageable if they borrow a freezer, and they can reduce the power-cut risk by producing early in the morning.
:::

### 5. Implementation

The plan is put into action:

- draw up an **action plan**: tasks, who is responsible, deadlines and resources;
- raise and allocate **finance** and other resources;
- communicate the plan to everyone involved;
- carry out the tasks: buy materials, produce, advertise, sell, keep records.

### 6. Review and control

Planners regularly compare **actual results with targets**: sales, costs, profit, quality, deadlines. If results differ, they find the reason and act: change price, cut costs, increase promotion or even revise the objective. Control closes the loop, and the information feeds into the next round of planning.

| Week | Target sales (freezits) | Actual sales | Difference | Action |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 200 | 180 | (20) | More posters in classrooms |
| 2 | 200 | 210 | 10 | Keep the same plan |
| 3 | 200 | 150 | (50) | Cold weather: add hot buns as a second product |

## Implementing and managing a business plan on a project

When you run a project, you act as its managers:

- **Planning:** confirm objectives and the action plan.
- **Organising:** divide tasks (production, sales, finance, marketing) and set a timetable.
- **Leading:** motivate team members, solve disagreements.
- **Co-ordinating:** make sure tasks fit together (materials bought before production day).
- **Controlling:** keep a sales and cash record, compare with targets weekly and adjust.

At the end, write a short **evaluation**: were the objectives met, what went well, what went wrong and what would you do differently?

## Why each step matters

| Step | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Environmental analysis | Bases the plan on facts, revealing opportunities and threats |
| Destiny, cause and calling | Gives direction and motivation |
| Objective setting | Provides targets that can be measured |
| Options generation and selection | Avoids rushing into the first idea; chooses the best use of resources |
| Implementation | Turns ideas into action with clear responsibilities |
| Review and control | Detects problems early and keeps the plan on course |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Skipping straight to implementation"}
Starting without analysing the environment or comparing options is the most common cause of failed projects.
:::

:::mistake{title="Vague objectives"}
"Make a lot of money" cannot be measured. Use SMART objectives with figures and dates.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating control as punishment"}
Control is about **correcting** the plan, not blaming people. It should lead to action.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Describe the steps in order"}
For "describe the business planning process", give each step in order with one sentence on what happens and one on why it matters.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Apply to a project"}
In project questions, use the project's details: the product, the team and the targets.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
This concept is examined only in Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 2 planning process; Form 4 implementation). Commerce and Cambridge cover the business plan but not these steps.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Business planning moves through environmental analysis, purpose (destiny, cause and calling), objective setting, options generation, evaluation and selection, implementation, and review and control.
- A clear purpose and SMART objectives give direction and measurable targets.
- Options are compared on cost, demand, risk, skills and fit before one is selected.
- Implementation needs an action plan with tasks, people, deadlines and resources.
- Review and control compare actual results with targets and lead to corrective action.
:::
