## Introduction

Farai lost his job at a factory in Kwekwe. Instead of waiting for another job, he studied what people in his area were short of, noticed that many households wanted solar lighting, borrowed from his savings club, found a supplier and started installing small solar kits. What did Farai actually **do** as an enterpriser, and what **pushed** him to start?

Business Enterprise and Skills answers these two questions in separate parts of the syllabus: the **role of the enterpriser** (Form 2) and the **drivers towards enterprising** (Form 3). Exam questions ask you to explain each role, to explain the drivers and to evaluate which drivers matter most in a given community.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Role of the enterpriser"}
The tasks an enterpriser performs in creating and running a business, from spotting an opportunity to managing the enterprise.
:::

:::definition{term="Environmental scanning"}
Studying the business environment (customers, competitors, the economy, government policy, technology and society) to find opportunities and threats.
:::

:::definition{term="Mobilising resources"}
Gathering the land, labour, capital, materials and information a business needs.
:::

:::definition{term="Driver towards enterprising"}
A factor that encourages or pushes people to start their own enterprises.
:::

:::definition{term="Self-actualisation"}
Reaching one's full potential: the desire to achieve personal goals and fulfil one's talents.
:::

## The role of the enterpriser

The roles follow a logical order, from idea to running business.

### 1. Scanning the environment

The enterpriser studies the surroundings: what customers buy and complain about, what competitors offer, prices, new technology, government policies and social changes. This reveals **gaps** in the market and **threats** to avoid.

### 2. Identifying business opportunities

From the scan, the enterpriser chooses an idea that meets a real need and that people will pay for. They check whether there is enough demand and whether they can meet it.

### 3. Mobilising necessary resources

The enterpriser gathers what is needed:

- **capital** (savings, family, loans, grants);
- **premises and equipment**;
- **labour** with the right skills;
- **raw materials** and reliable suppliers;
- **information** and licences.

### 4. Proper allocation of resources

Resources are limited, so the enterpriser decides how to use them: how much to spend on stock versus equipment, which jobs each worker does, and which customers to target. Good allocation avoids waste and keeps costs low.

### 5. Setting up the business

The enterpriser chooses the form of organisation, registers the business, obtains licences, prepares premises, recruits workers and launches the product.

### 6. Managing the business

Once trading, the enterpriser **plans, organises, leads, co-ordinates and controls**: setting targets, keeping records, controlling cash, motivating workers, dealing with problems and looking for chances to grow.

:::example{title="Farai's roles"}
| Role | What Farai did |
| --- | --- |
| Scanning the environment | Noticed frequent power cuts and households asking about solar lights |
| Identifying an opportunity | Installing small solar kits for homes and tuck shops |
| Mobilising resources | Savings-club loan, a supplier in Harare, a trained electrician friend |
| Allocating resources | Spent most money on stock, little on premises (worked from home) |
| Setting up | Registered the business, obtained a licence, printed price lists |
| Managing | Kept a sales book, followed up customers, trained a second installer |
:::

## Drivers towards enterprising

The syllabus names four groups of drivers. Some **pull** people towards enterprise (attractive opportunities); others **push** them (lack of alternatives).

### Government policies

Governments encourage enterprise through low-interest loans and grants for youth and women, training programmes, SME workspaces, simpler registration, tax incentives and policies that reserve some sectors or contracts for local businesses. Where policies make starting a business easier and cheaper, more people start.

### Demographic and economic changes

- **Population growth** and **urbanisation** create demand for food, transport, housing and services in towns.
- A **young population** with few formal jobs pushes school leavers into self-employment.
- **Retrenchments** and **unemployment** push people to create their own jobs, as Farai did.
- **Changes in incomes** create new demand: rising incomes increase demand for services; falling incomes increase demand for cheap, small-quantity goods.
- **New technology** (mobile phones, mobile money, the internet) lowers the cost of starting and reaching customers.

### Self-actualisation

Many enterprisers want independence, to be their own boss, to use their talents and to achieve something meaningful. This is an inner, personal driver that keeps them going through difficulties.

### Community enterprising heroes (role models)

Successful local business people inspire others. When young people see someone from their own community build a business, they believe they can do the same, and the role model may also mentor, employ or supply them.

:::example{title="Evaluating the drivers"}
In a rural district with high youth unemployment, the **push** of unemployment may be the strongest driver: young people start enterprises because there are few jobs. But push alone often leads to many similar, small businesses. **Government support** and **role models** turn this push into better, growing enterprises by providing finance, skills and confidence.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing roles with qualities"}
A **role** is a task (mobilising resources). A **quality** is a personal characteristic (determination). Do not answer a "role" question with qualities.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing a driver without showing how it encourages enterprise"}
"Population growth" alone is incomplete. Explain the link: "a growing population increases demand for food and transport, creating opportunities for new businesses".
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the order"}
The roles follow a sequence. Scanning and identifying come before mobilising and setting up.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Use the case evidence"}
For "explain the roles Farai performed", match each role to something Farai did in the case.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Evaluate drivers"}
"Evaluate the drivers towards enterprising in your community" needs several drivers explained, a judgement on which is most important and why.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Board differences"}
This concept is examined only in Business Enterprise and Skills (Form 2 for the role, Form 3 for the drivers). Commerce and Cambridge cover the qualities of entrepreneurs but not these lists.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The enterpriser scans the environment, identifies opportunities, mobilises and allocates resources, sets up and manages the business.
- Drivers towards enterprising include government policies, demographic and economic changes, self-actualisation and community role models.
- Some drivers pull people towards opportunity; others push them because jobs are scarce.
- Explain each role or driver and link it to the case.
- Evaluate drivers by judging which matters most in a given community and why.
:::
