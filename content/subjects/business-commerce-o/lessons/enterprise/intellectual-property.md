## Introduction

Chipo, an engineer in Kwekwe, spends two years developing a low-cost solar water pump for small-scale farmers. Within weeks of her first sales, a rival copies the design and sells it cheaper. A musician in Harare finds his songs sold on pirated flash drives at a bus terminus. A soap maker discovers fake bars carrying her brand name and logo. In each case someone has taken the product of another person's mind: their **intellectual property**.

Intellectual property is a ZIMSEC Commerce topic (Form 3). Examiners ask you to define the types of intellectual property, to explain why it should be protected, to describe infringements and their remedies, and to state the functions of the national office, ARIPO and WIPO.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Intellectual property (IP)"}
Creations of the mind, such as inventions, brand names, designs, books, music and software, that the law allows their owners to protect and control.
:::

:::definition{term="Infringement"}
Using, copying or selling someone else's intellectual property without permission.
:::

## Types of intellectual property

| Type | What it protects | Example | How protection is obtained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patent | A new invention (a product or a process) | A new design of solar water pump | Apply and register; the invention must be new and useful. Protection lasts a limited time (internationally about 20 years) |
| Trademark | A name, logo, symbol or slogan that identifies a business's goods or services | The name and logo on a brand of soap | Register the mark; registration can be renewed again and again |
| Copyright | Original creative work: books, music, films, art, photographs, software | A song, a textbook, a computer program | Automatic when the work is created; lasts many years |
| Industrial design | The shape, pattern or appearance of a product | The shape of a chair or the pattern on fabric | Register the design |
| Geographical indication | A name showing that a product comes from a particular place and has qualities linked to it | A tea or coffee named after the highland region where it is grown | Register the name for producers of that region |

:::example{title="One product, several types of IP"}
A business in Bulawayo makes a new kind of energy-saving cooking stove.
- The way the stove burns fuel efficiently can be protected by a **patent**.
- The brand name and logo on the stove are protected by a **trademark**.
- The stove's distinctive shape can be registered as an **industrial design**.
- The instruction booklet is protected by **copyright**.
:::

## Why intellectual property is protected

- **Rewards creativity and invention**: the owner can earn income from the work, for example by selling it or charging a **royalty** for its use (a licence).
- **Encourages innovation and investment**: businesses spend on research only if rivals cannot simply copy the result.
- **Protects consumers**: trademarks tell buyers who made a product, so they are not misled by fakes that may be unsafe.
- **Protects reputation and goodwill**: a brand's good name is not damaged by poor-quality copies.
- **Supports the economy**: creative industries (music, film, software) and inventors create jobs, and the government collects taxes on legitimate sales.
- **Attracts foreign investment**: firms are more willing to bring new technology to a country that protects IP.

## Infringements

| Infringement | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Piracy | Copying and selling copyright work without permission | Selling copied music, films or software |
| Counterfeiting | Making fake goods that copy a trademark or brand | Fake branded batteries, clothes or medicines |
| Plagiarism | Presenting someone else's work or ideas as your own | Copying an author's text into your own book without credit |
| Patent infringement | Making or selling a patented invention without permission | Copying a patented pump design |

Infringements harm the owner (lost sales and royalties), honest traders (unfair competition), consumers (fakes may be poor or dangerous) and the government (lost tax).

## Remedies for infringement

- **Court action**: the owner can sue for damages (compensation) and ask the court for an **injunction** (an order to stop).
- **Seizure and destruction** of pirated or counterfeit goods, including by police and customs officers at borders.
- **Fines and imprisonment** for offenders under the law.
- **Account of profits**: the infringer may be ordered to hand over profits made from the copies.
- **Prevention**: registering IP, using security labels and holograms, and educating the public about the harm caused by fakes.

## Organisations that protect intellectual property

### The Zimbabwe Intellectual Property Office (the patents office)

The national office administers the law on intellectual property in Zimbabwe. Its functions include:

- **Registering** patents, trademarks and industrial designs and keeping the official registers.
- **Examining applications** to check that an invention is new or that a mark is not already in use.
- **Providing information** so that people can search the registers and avoid copying.
- **Raising awareness** of the importance of protecting IP.

### ARIPO (African Regional Intellectual Property Organization)

ARIPO is an organisation of African member states with its headquarters in **Harare**. Its functions include:

- Allowing an applicant to seek protection for patents, trademarks and designs in **several member countries through one application**.
- Promoting cooperation between member states on IP law.
- Training and providing information on IP in Africa.

### WIPO (World Intellectual Property Organization)

WIPO is a specialised agency of the **United Nations**, based in Geneva. Its functions include:

- **Promoting the protection** of IP throughout the world.
- **Administering international treaties** on IP.
- Providing **international application systems** so that an inventor or brand owner can seek protection in many countries.
- **Helping developing countries** with training and advice to build their IP systems.
- **Settling disputes** over IP through mediation and arbitration.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up patents and copyright"}
A **patent** protects an invention and must be applied for. **Copyright** protects creative work such as music and books and arises automatically.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing piracy and counterfeiting"}
Piracy copies **copyright** work (music, films, software). Counterfeiting copies **branded goods** and their trademarks.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying IP protection lasts for ever"}
Patents and copyright last for limited periods. Only trademark registration can be renewed again and again.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Match type to example"}
Many questions give examples ("a song", "a logo", "a new machine") and ask for the type of IP. Practise classifying quickly.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Explain who is harmed"}
For "explain the effects of piracy", go beyond the owner: honest traders, consumers and the government all lose.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Know the three organisations"}
National office: registers IP in Zimbabwe. ARIPO: regional, one application for several African countries, headquarters in Harare. WIPO: worldwide, United Nations, treaties and international systems.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Intellectual property is creations of the mind that the law protects.
- Types: patents (inventions), trademarks (brands), copyright (creative work), industrial designs (appearance), geographical indications (place of origin).
- Protection rewards creativity, encourages innovation, protects consumers and brands and supports the economy.
- Infringements include piracy, counterfeiting, plagiarism and patent infringement; remedies include damages, injunctions, seizure, fines and imprisonment.
- The Zimbabwe office registers IP; ARIPO offers regional protection; WIPO promotes protection worldwide.
:::
