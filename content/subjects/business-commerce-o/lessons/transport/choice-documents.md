## Introduction

A furniture maker in Chinhoyi has an order from a hotel in Victoria Falls. Should the beds go by lorry or by rail? A flower grower in the Eastern Highlands must get roses to a buyer in Europe before they wilt. A Bulawayo importer is bringing machinery from overseas through a port in a neighbouring country. Each business must choose a mode of transport, and each consignment travels with documents that prove what was sent, who carries it and who owns it.

ZIMSEC Commerce teaches the choice of transport, transport documents and port authorities in Form 2. Business Enterprise and Skills (BES) asks you to explain the factors influencing the choice of transport, also in Form 2. Expect "explain the factors", "advise the business" and "state the purpose of" a document.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Consignment note"}
A document that goes with goods sent by road or rail, giving the sender, the receiver, the carrier and a description of the goods. The receiver signs it to confirm the goods arrived.
:::

:::definition{term="Delivery note"}
A document sent with goods by the seller listing the goods delivered. The buyer signs a copy as proof of delivery.
:::

:::definition{term="Bill of lading"}
The main document for goods sent by sea. It is a receipt for the goods from the shipping company, evidence of the contract of carriage and a **document of title**: whoever holds it can claim the goods.
:::

:::definition{term="Airway bill (air waybill)"}
The document for goods sent by air. It is a receipt and evidence of the contract of carriage, but unlike a bill of lading it is **not** a document of title.
:::

:::definition{term="Dry port (inland port)"}
An inland terminal linked to a sea port by road or rail, where containers are received, stored and cleared through customs away from the coast.
:::

## Factors influencing the choice of transport

- **Cost**: the business compares freight charges, handling and insurance. Low-value bulky goods need the cheapest mode.
- **Speed and urgency**: urgent orders and perishables need fast transport (road for short distances, air for long).
- **Distance**: road is usually best for short distances, rail and sea for long distances with bulky goods, air for long distances with urgent goods.
- **Nature of the goods**: perishable (fresh produce, flowers), fragile (glassware), bulky (cement, coal), liquid (fuel) or dangerous (chemicals) goods each suit certain modes.
- **Value of the goods**: valuable goods need secure transport with a low risk of theft and damage.
- **Safety and security**: risk of accidents, theft and damage on each mode.
- **Reliability**: the mode that keeps to time, so that production and sales are not disrupted.
- **Accessibility**: whether the mode reaches the destination; door-to-door service avoids reloading.
- **Size and weight of the consignment**: a few cartons or a trainload.
- **Availability of insurance and documents** for the mode, and any **legal restrictions** (for example on dangerous goods).

:::example{title="Comparing the cost of road and rail"}
Sipho must send 30 tonnes of fertiliser 500 km. A road haulier charges US\$45 a tonne, door to door. Rail charges US\$32 a tonne, but Sipho must pay US\$180 for a lorry to the station and US\$180 at the other end.

| Cost item | Road US\$ | Rail US\$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Haulage (30 tonnes) | 1,350 | 960 |
| Lorry to and from the stations | 0 | 360 |
| Total | 1,350 | 1,320 |

Rail is US\$30 cheaper, but the goods are handled twice more and may take longer. If the fertiliser is needed urgently before the rains, road may be worth the small extra cost.
:::

:::example{title="Advising a flower exporter"}
Thandeka's farm grows roses for export to Europe. The roses perish within days, are light, and are high in value relative to their weight. Air transport is the right choice: it is expensive, but speed preserves the value of the flowers. Road transport takes them from the farm to the airport in refrigerated vans.
:::

## Documents used in transport

| Document | Used for | Main purposes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consignment note | Road and rail | Describes the goods and parties; instruction to the carrier; signed by the receiver as proof of delivery |
| Delivery note | Any delivery by the seller's own vehicle or a carrier | Lists goods delivered; buyer checks goods against it and signs; seller keeps the signed copy |
| Bill of lading | Sea | Receipt for goods loaded; evidence of the contract of carriage; document of title (can be transferred to a buyer or a bank) |
| Airway bill | Air | Receipt for goods; evidence of the contract of carriage; instructions for handling; not a document of title |

A bill of lading is usually issued in a set of originals. The importer, or the importer's bank, must present an original to collect the goods at the port. A bill of lading that states the goods were received in good condition is called **clean**; one that notes damage is **claused** (dirty).

## Port authorities

A port authority manages a sea port, a lake port or an airport. Its roles include:

- providing and maintaining **berths, quays, cranes and warehouses**;
- **pilotage and towing**: guiding ships safely into and out of the harbour;
- **cargo handling**: loading and unloading ships, and storing goods awaiting collection;
- providing **security and safety** and fire services;
- working with **customs** and other officials so goods can be cleared;
- charging **port dues** for these services.

## A landlocked country and the sea

Zimbabwe has no coastline, so its imports and exports by sea pass through ports in neighbouring countries, such as **Beira** in Mozambique and **Durban** in South Africa, and then travel by road or rail through border posts. This adds cost and time, and depends on agreements with transit countries.

**Dry ports** help. Containers can travel sealed by rail or road from the sea port to an inland terminal, where they are cleared by customs and stored until collected. Benefits:

- less congestion and waiting at the sea port;
- customs clearance close to the importer, so less travel to the coast;
- goods stay sealed in containers, so less theft and damage;
- cheaper bulk movement by rail between the coast and the dry port.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Choosing the cheapest mode every time"}
Cost is only one factor. Perishable or urgent goods may justify an expensive mode, because a cheap but slow mode can destroy their value.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling the airway bill a document of title"}
Only the bill of lading is a document of title. The airway bill is a receipt and contract of carriage.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up the consignment note and the delivery note"}
The consignment note is an instruction to a carrier about goods sent by road or rail; the delivery note is the seller's list of goods delivered, signed by the buyer.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Explain the factor in context"}
Write "the roses are perishable, so they need a fast mode such as air", not just "nature of goods". Each factor applied to the case earns more than a list.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Advise or recommend"}
State your choice, give the reasons from the case, and admit and answer one drawback ("air is costly, but the roses' value is lost if they wilt").
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Documents"}
For each document, know the mode it belongs to and two purposes. Commerce Paper 1 often asks which document is a document of title.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- The choice of transport depends on cost, speed, urgency, distance, the nature and value of the goods, safety, reliability and accessibility.
- Consignment notes (road and rail) and delivery notes go with goods and are signed as proof of delivery.
- The bill of lading (sea) is a receipt, a contract of carriage and a document of title; the airway bill (air) is not a document of title.
- Port authorities provide berths, handling, storage, pilotage and security, and charge port dues.
- Landlocked Zimbabwe uses neighbouring sea ports and inland dry ports, with road and rail links.
:::
