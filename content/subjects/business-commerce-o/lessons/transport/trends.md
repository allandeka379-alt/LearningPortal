## Introduction

A generation ago a trader sending goods from Harare to Beitbridge had little idea where the lorry was until it arrived. Today the owner of a small haulage business in Kwekwe can watch each truck move on a phone map, a bus passenger in Mutare can pay for a seat with mobile money, and a container sealed at a factory in Asia can travel by ship, rail and road to Bulawayo without being opened. These changes are the **trends in transport** you study in ZIMSEC Commerce Form 3.

Examiners usually ask you to **describe** a trend and **explain** or **discuss** its advantages and disadvantages to businesses, so learn both sides of each development.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Containerisation"}
The transport of goods in large standard-sized steel boxes (containers) that can be moved between ships, trains and lorries without unpacking the goods.
:::

:::definition{term="Toll gate"}
A point on a road where users pay a fee (a toll) to use the road. The money is used to build and maintain roads.
:::

:::definition{term="Vehicle tracking system"}
Equipment fitted to a vehicle that uses satellite positioning and mobile networks to show its location, speed and route to the owner in real time.
:::

:::definition{term="E-ticketing"}
Booking and paying for a journey electronically (online, by phone or with a card) and receiving an electronic ticket instead of a paper one.
:::

:::definition{term="Satellite positioning (GPS)"}
A system that uses signals from satellites to find an exact position on Earth, used for navigation, route planning and tracking.
:::

## Containerisation

Containers come in standard sizes, so cranes, ships, wagons and trailers are all built to handle them. Goods are packed (stuffed) at the factory or warehouse, the container is sealed, and it is not opened again until it reaches the buyer or customs.

**Advantages**

- Fast loading and unloading by crane: ships spend less time in port.
- Less theft and damage, because goods are sealed inside a steel box.
- Less packaging needed for individual items.
- Easy transfer between sea, rail and road (**intermodal transport**), ideal for landlocked countries.
- Containers can be stored in the open and used as temporary stores.

**Disadvantages**

- Expensive specialised cranes, ships, trailers and terminals are needed.
- Fewer jobs for dock workers who once loaded goods by hand.
- Empty containers must be returned, which costs money.
- Not suitable for some goods (very large machinery, small loads), and roads must take heavy loads.

## Toll gates

Toll fees are paid at gates along major roads.

**Advantages**: funds for road building, repairs and maintenance; better roads reduce vehicle damage, fuel use and journey times; users pay in proportion to use.

**Disadvantages**: higher transport costs, which businesses may pass on in prices; queues at busy gates delay deliveries; some drivers use poorer side roads to avoid tolls.

## Vehicle tracking systems

A tracking unit sends the vehicle's position to a computer or phone. Owners can see where a lorry is, whether it is speeding or idling, and when it stops.

**Advantages**: deters and helps recover stolen vehicles and cargo; owners monitor drivers' routes and speed; customers can be told accurate arrival times; insurers may charge lower premiums for tracked vehicles; fuel misuse is easier to detect.

**Disadvantages**: cost of the units and monthly subscriptions; depends on network coverage; drivers may feel spied upon.

## E-ticketing

Bus, rail and air passengers book seats and pay online or by mobile money, receiving a ticket by text message, email or app.

**Advantages**: no queues at ticket offices; booking from anywhere at any time; less cash handling and fraud; transport operators know demand in advance and can plan; fewer lost tickets.

**Disadvantages**: needs a phone, internet access and an electronic payment method; network failures stop sales; risk of online fraud; fewer jobs for ticket clerks.

## Satellite positioning (GPS)

GPS lets drivers, pilots and ship captains find their exact position and the best route.

**Advantages**: shorter, faster routes save fuel and time; deliveries reach unfamiliar addresses; route planning for many deliveries at once; works with tracking systems.

**Disadvantages**: devices and data cost money; maps may be out of date; drivers may follow unsuitable routes without local knowledge.

## Growth of air cargo

More goods now travel by air, often in the holds of passenger planes or on dedicated cargo planes.

**Advantages**: perishable exports such as flowers, fresh vegetables and fruit can reach distant markets; urgent spare parts and medicines arrive quickly; online shopping relies on fast delivery.

**Disadvantages**: high cost; limited capacity; needs cold storage and good road links at airports.

## The impact of ICT on transport

ICT links the trends together: online booking of freight, electronic transport documents, mobile-money payments, tracking and GPS, and computerised timetables and route planning.

:::example{title="A small haulage business goes digital"}
Nyasha runs four lorries from Kwekwe. She fits tracking units and finds that one driver takes long detours. Customers book loads by phone message and pay by mobile money; she gives them live arrival times. Her fuel costs fall and customers return, but she now pays monthly fees for tracking and data, and a network failure on one route leaves her unable to see a lorry for several hours.
:::

| Trend | Main advantage | Main disadvantage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Containerisation | fast, secure transfer between modes | expensive handling equipment |
| Toll gates | money for better roads | higher transport costs |
| Vehicle tracking | security and control of fleets | subscription cost; needs network |
| E-ticketing | convenience; less cash | needs phone, data and electronic payment |
| GPS | efficient routes | cost; outdated maps |
| Air cargo | fast access to distant markets | high cost |

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Describing only the benefits"}
Most questions say "discuss" or "explain the advantages and disadvantages". Give at least one disadvantage for each trend.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing tracking with GPS"}
GPS finds a position; a tracking system **sends** that position to the owner. A tracking system uses GPS, but a driver can use GPS for navigation without being tracked.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Link the trend to a business benefit"}
"Containers reduce theft" is good; "containers reduce theft, so insurance and replacement costs fall" is better. Show the effect on costs, speed, safety or customers.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Evaluate"}
In a 'discuss' question, end with a judgement: for example, tracking is worth its cost for a business carrying valuable loads over long distances.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Containerisation: sealed standard boxes move between ships, trains and lorries quickly and securely, but need expensive equipment.
- Toll gates pay for roads but raise transport costs.
- Vehicle tracking and GPS improve security, routing and customer service, at a cost and with network limits.
- E-ticketing gives convenient electronic booking and payment but needs phones, data and electronic money.
- Air cargo and ICT open distant markets for perishable and urgent goods.
:::
