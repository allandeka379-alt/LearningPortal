## Introduction

Before Rudo's family fences their vegetable garden in Chinhoyi, they need to know how many metres of fencing to buy: that is the **perimeter**. Before they buy fertiliser, they need to know how much ground it must cover: that is the **area**. Painters, builders, tilers and farmers use these two measurements every day.

In this lesson you will learn the area formulas for the four basic straight-sided shapes and use them backwards to find a missing length. You should already know how to convert between units (remember $1\ \text{m}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Perimeter"}
The total distance around the outside of a shape, measured in units of length (cm, m, km).
:::

:::definition{term="Area"}
The amount of surface a shape covers, measured in square units ($\text{cm}^2$, $\text{m}^2$, hectares).
:::

:::definition{term="Perpendicular height"}
The height measured at right angles ($90^\circ$) to the base. It is **not** the length of a sloping side.
:::

:::formula{name="Areas of the basic shapes"}
| Shape | Area |
| --- | --- |
| Rectangle (length $l$, width $w$) | $A = lw$ |
| Triangle (base $b$, perpendicular height $h$) | $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$ |
| Parallelogram (base $b$, perpendicular height $h$) | $A = bh$ |
| Trapezium (parallel sides $a$ and $b$, distance $h$ between them) | $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$ |
:::

Only the triangle formula is printed on Cambridge papers, so learn the others. A parallelogram can be cut and rearranged into a rectangle, which is why its area is base times perpendicular height. A trapezium is two triangles, or "the mean of the parallel sides times the height".

## Rectangles and perimeter

:::worked-solution[Example 1: a vegetable garden]
A rectangular garden is 12.5 m long and 8 m wide.

1. Area $= 12.5 \times 8 = 100\ \text{m}^2$.
2. Perimeter $= 2(12.5 + 8) = 2 \times 20.5 = 41$ m.
3. So the family needs 41 m of fencing to go all the way round.
:::

## Triangles, parallelograms and trapezia

:::worked-solution[Example 2: a triangle and a parallelogram]
1. A triangle has base 9 cm and perpendicular height 6 cm. Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times 9 \times 6 = 27\ \text{cm}^2$.
2. A parallelogram has base 14 cm, sloping sides of 6 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. Area $= 14 \times 5 = 70\ \text{cm}^2$. The 6 cm sloping side is used only for the perimeter: $2(14 + 6) = 40$ cm.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: a trapezium-shaped plot]
A plot is a trapezium with parallel sides of 7 m and 11 m that are 4 m apart.

1. $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$.
2. $A = \frac{1}{2}(7 + 11) \times 4 = \frac{1}{2} \times 18 \times 4 = 36\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

## Finding a missing length

Substitute what you know into the formula and solve the equation.

:::worked-solution[Example 4: missing lengths]
1. A rectangle has area $84\ \text{cm}^2$ and width 6 cm. Then $6l = 84$, so $l = 14$ cm, and its perimeter is $2(14 + 6) = 40$ cm.
2. A triangle has area $30\ \text{cm}^2$ and base 12 cm. Then $\frac{1}{2} \times 12 \times h = 30$, so $6h = 30$ and $h = 5$ cm.
3. A square has perimeter 36 cm. Each side is $36 \div 4 = 9$ cm, so its area is $9^2 = 81\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 5: the height of a trapezium]
A trapezium-shaped flower bed has parallel sides of 6 m and 7 m and an area of $52\ \text{m}^2$. Find the distance between the parallel sides.

1. $\frac{1}{2}(6 + 7) \times h = 52$.
2. $6.5h = 52$.
3. $h = 52 \div 6.5 = 8$ m.
4. Check: $\frac{1}{2} \times 13 \times 8 = 52$.
:::

:::example{title="Same perimeter, different area"}
A $12 \times 6$ rectangle and a $9 \times 9$ square both have a perimeter of 36 cm, but their areas are $72\ \text{cm}^2$ and $81\ \text{cm}^2$. Shapes with the same perimeter do **not** always have the same area.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the sloping side as the height"}
For a parallelogram or triangle, the height must be **perpendicular** to the base. Using the 6 cm sloping side above gives $14 \times 6 = 84\ \text{cm}^2$, which is wrong.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the half"}
The area of a triangle is **half** of base times height, and the area of a trapezium is **half** the sum of the parallel sides times the height.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up area and perimeter units"}
Perimeter is a length (m); area is in square units ($\text{m}^2$). Writing "41 $\text{m}^2$" for a perimeter loses marks.
:::

:::mistake{title="Different units in one formula"}
A rectangle 2 m by 45 cm has area $2 \times 0.45 = 0.9\ \text{m}^2$ (or $200 \times 45 = 9000\ \text{cm}^2$), never $2 \times 45 = 90$.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write the formula, then the substitution, then the answer with units, for example "$A = \frac{1}{2}(7 + 11) \times 4 = 36\ \text{m}^2$". Each line can earn credit.
:::

:::exam-tip
Diagrams in examinations are usually marked "not to scale", so use the numbers given, not measurements from the diagram.
:::

:::exam-tip
In a real-life question (fencing, paint, tiles), finish by answering the question asked: the number of tins to **buy** must be a whole number, rounded up.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Perimeter is the distance around a shape; area is the surface it covers in square units.
- Rectangle $A = lw$; triangle $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$; parallelogram $A = bh$; trapezium $A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$.
- Always use the **perpendicular** height, never a sloping side.
- To find a missing length, substitute into the formula and solve.
- Shapes with equal perimeters can have different areas.
:::
