## Introduction

Cement is sold in 50 kg bags, diesel by the litre, land in hectares and fabric by the metre. A builder in Kwekwe measuring a slab in metres may need its volume in cubic metres to order concrete, and a farmer needs to know how many litres a tank in cubic metres holds. Converting units correctly is a skill that every mensuration question depends on.

The most common examination errors are with **area** and **volume** units, because the conversion factors are squared and cubed. This lesson shows you why, so that you never have to guess.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Metric units"}
The system of units based on powers of 10: metres for length, grams and kilograms for mass, litres for capacity. The SI units of length, mass and volume are the metre, the kilogram and the cubic metre; the litre is a metric unit used alongside them.
:::

:::definition{term="Capacity"}
The amount of liquid a container can hold, measured in millilitres (ml) and litres (l).
:::

:::definition{term="Hectare (ha)"}
A unit of land area equal to $10\,000\ \text{m}^2$, the area of a square 100 m by 100 m.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Basic conversions"}
| Length | Mass | Capacity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $10\ \text{mm} = 1\ \text{cm}$ | $1000\ \text{mg} = 1\ \text{g}$ | $1000\ \text{ml} = 1\ \text{litre}$ |
| $100\ \text{cm} = 1\ \text{m}$ | $1000\ \text{g} = 1\ \text{kg}$ | $1\ \text{ml} = 1\ \text{cm}^3$ |
| $1000\ \text{m} = 1\ \text{km}$ | $1000\ \text{kg} = 1\ \text{tonne}$ | $1000\ \text{litres} = 1\ \text{m}^3$ |
:::

To change to a **smaller** unit, multiply (there will be more of them). To change to a **larger** unit, divide.

## Area units: square the factor

A square 1 m by 1 m is also 100 cm by 100 cm, so

$$
1\ \text{m}^2 = 100 \times 100\ \text{cm}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2
$$

In the same way, $1\ \text{cm}^2 = 10 \times 10 = 100\ \text{mm}^2$ and $1\ \text{km}^2 = 1000 \times 1000 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: converting areas]
1. $3.2\ \text{m}^2 = 3.2 \times 10\,000 = 32\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
2. $45\,000\ \text{cm}^2 = 45\,000 \div 10\,000 = 4.5\ \text{m}^2$.
3. $750\,000\ \text{mm}^2 = 750\,000 \div 1\,000\,000 = 0.75\ \text{m}^2$ (because $1\ \text{m}^2 = 1000 \times 1000\ \text{mm}^2$).
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: a field in hectares]
A rectangular field measures 250 m by 180 m. Find its area in hectares.

1. Area $= 250 \times 180 = 45\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
2. $45\,000 \div 10\,000 = 4.5$ ha.
:::

## Volume units: cube the factor

A cube 1 m by 1 m by 1 m is also 100 cm by 100 cm by 100 cm, so

$$
1\ \text{m}^3 = 100 \times 100 \times 100\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3
$$

Similarly, $1\ \text{cm}^3 = 10 \times 10 \times 10 = 1000\ \text{mm}^3$.

## Volume and capacity

The link between volume and capacity is $1\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{ml}$. So $1000\ \text{cm}^3 = 1$ litre and $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3 = 1000$ litres.

:::worked-solution[Example 3: how many litres does the tank hold?]
A cuboid water tank measures 1.2 m by 0.8 m by 0.5 m. Find its capacity in litres.

1. Volume $= 1.2 \times 0.8 \times 0.5 = 0.48\ \text{m}^3$.
2. $0.48 \times 1000 = 480$ litres.
3. Check another way: $120 \times 80 \times 50 = 480\,000\ \text{cm}^3$, and $480\,000 \div 1000 = 480$ litres.
:::

:::example{title="Rain on a roof"}
25 mm of rain falls on a flat roof of $96\ \text{m}^2$. The water forms a layer $0.025$ m deep, so its volume is $96 \times 0.025 = 2.4\ \text{m}^3$, which is 2400 litres. Change every length into the **same unit** before multiplying.
:::

## Choosing suitable units

Choose a unit that gives a sensible number: the length of a pencil in centimetres, the distance from Harare to Mutare in kilometres, the mass of a tablet in milligrams, the mass of a lorry load of maize in tonnes, and the area of a farm in hectares.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the length factor for areas and volumes"}
$1\ \text{m}^2$ is not $100\ \text{cm}^2$, and $1\ \text{m}^3$ is not $100\ \text{cm}^3$. Square the factor for area ($10\,000$) and cube it for volume ($1\,000\,000$).
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing units in a calculation"}
A tank 1.2 m by 80 cm by 50 cm does not have a volume of $1.2 \times 80 \times 50 = 4800$ of anything. Put every length in the same unit first.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing m³ and litres"}
$1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000$ litres, but $1\ \text{litre} = 1000\ \text{cm}^3$. Write the chain $\text{m}^3 \to \text{cm}^3 \to \text{litres}$ if you are unsure.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Check the units asked for in the **answer line** before you start. Convert lengths at the beginning rather than converting an area or volume at the end, when it is easier to slip.
:::

:::exam-tip
Do a reasonableness check: a classroom floor of about $50\ \text{m}^2$ or a water tank of a few thousand litres is sensible; a classroom floor of $0.005\ \text{m}^2$ is not.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Length: $10\ \text{mm} = 1\ \text{cm}$, $100\ \text{cm} = 1\ \text{m}$, $1000\ \text{m} = 1\ \text{km}$; mass and capacity go up in thousands.
- Area factors are squared: $1\ \text{m}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$, $1\ \text{km}^2 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$; $1\ \text{ha} = 10\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
- Volume factors are cubed: $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$.
- $1\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{ml}$, $1000\ \text{cm}^3 = 1$ litre, $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000$ litres.
- Put all lengths in the same unit before calculating, and choose units that give sensible numbers.
:::
