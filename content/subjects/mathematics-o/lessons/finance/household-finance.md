## Introduction

Every household has to manage money: wages come in, and rent, food, school fees, electricity, water and transport go out. Being able to check a payslip, a utility bill or a bank statement protects a family from mistakes and helps it plan.

In examinations these questions are usually long and wordy, but the mathematics is simple: careful reading, the four operations, percentages and clear setting out. All the prices, wages and tariffs in this lesson are **invented for practice**. Real tariffs change often, so in an examination always use the figures given in the question.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Wage and salary"}
A wage is usually paid per hour, day or week. A salary is a fixed amount per year, usually paid in equal monthly parts.
:::

:::definition{term="Overtime"}
Hours worked beyond the normal working week, often paid at a higher rate. "Time-and-a-half" means $1.5 \times$ the normal hourly rate; "double time" means $2 \times$ the normal rate.
:::

:::definition{term="Piece work"}
Payment for each item made or unit of work done, for example US\$0.35 for each shirt sewn.
:::

:::definition{term="Gross pay and net pay"}
Gross pay is the total earned before deductions. Net pay ("take-home pay") is what is left after deductions such as pension contributions, medical aid and tax.
:::

:::definition{term="Tariff and fixed charge"}
A tariff is the price charged per unit used, such as per kilowatt-hour (kWh) of electricity or per cubic metre of water. A fixed charge is paid every month, however much is used.
:::

:::definition{term="Insurance premium"}
The amount paid (monthly or yearly) to an insurance company for cover against a loss.
:::

## Wages, overtime and piece work

:::worked-solution[Example 1: a week with overtime]
Farai works a normal 40-hour week at US\$4.50 per hour. One week he also works 6 hours of overtime at time-and-a-half.

1. Basic pay: $40 \times 4.50 = 180$ dollars.
2. Overtime rate: $1.5 \times 4.50 = 6.75$ dollars per hour.
3. Overtime pay: $6 \times 6.75 = 40.50$ dollars.
4. Total (gross) pay: $180 + 40.50 = 220.50$, so US\$220.50.
:::

For piece work, multiply the number of items by the rate per item: 420 shirts at US\$0.35 each earn $420 \times 0.35 = 147$ dollars.

Net pay is found by subtracting every deduction. If gross pay is US\$850, the pension contribution is 5% of gross pay and medical aid costs US\$30, then net pay $= 850 - 42.50 - 30 = 777.50$ dollars.

## Household bills

Utility bills usually have a fixed charge plus a charge for the units used. The units used are the difference between two **meter readings**.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: an electricity bill with a stepped tariff]
In this example an electricity supplier charges a fixed charge of US\$3.00 per month and these tariffs:

| Units used in the month | Price per kWh |
| --- | --- |
| first 50 kWh | US\$0.04 |
| next 150 kWh | US\$0.09 |
| every kWh above 200 | US\$0.14 |

The meter reads 15,862 at the start of the month and 16,147 at the end.

1. Units used: $16\,147 - 15\,862 = 285$ kWh.
2. First 50 kWh: $50 \times 0.04 = 2.00$.
3. Next 150 kWh: $150 \times 0.09 = 13.50$.
4. Remaining $285 - 200 = 85$ kWh: $85 \times 0.14 = 11.90$.
5. Total: $3.00 + 2.00 + 13.50 + 11.90 = 30.40$, so the bill is US\$30.40.
:::

A water bill works in the same way: with a fixed charge of US\$5.00 and US\$1.20 per cubic metre, a household using $18\ \text{m}^3$ pays $5 + 18 \times 1.20 = 26.60$ dollars.

## Bank statements

On a bank statement, **credits** are money paid in and **debits** are money taken out. Each new balance is the previous balance plus the credit or minus the debit.

:::worked-solution[Example 3: completing a bank statement]
| Date | Details | Debit (US\$) | Credit (US\$) | Balance (US\$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 May | Opening balance | | | 412.30 |
| 3 May | Salary | | 850.00 | 1262.30 |
| 6 May | Rent | 300.00 | | ? |

1. The salary is a credit: $412.30 + 850.00 = 1262.30$.
2. The rent is a debit: $1262.30 - 300.00 = 962.30$.
3. The balance after the rent is US\$962.30.
:::

## Budgets and insurance

A budget shows how income is shared between spending and saving, often as percentages or as a pie chart.

:::example{title="A monthly budget"}
A family's monthly income is US\$720. Rent takes 25%, which is $0.25 \times 720 = 180$ dollars. In a pie chart, rent would have an angle of $25\% \times 360^\circ = 90^\circ$.
:::

Insurance premiums are often a percentage of the value insured, or an amount per US\$1000 insured. A house insured for US\$45,000 at a premium of 0.4% per year costs $0.004 \times 45\,000 = 180$ dollars a year, or US\$15 a month.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Paying all the hours at the overtime rate"}
Only the overtime hours earn the higher rate. 46 hours with 6 hours of overtime is $40 \times 4.50 + 6 \times 6.75$, not $46 \times 6.75$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the fixed charge"}
The fixed charge is paid every month, even if few units are used. Add it once to the cost of the units.
:::

:::mistake{title="Charging every unit at the top tariff"}
With a stepped tariff, only the units **above** each limit are charged at the higher price. Split the units into bands first.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up debit and credit"}
A credit increases the balance (money in); a debit decreases it (money out).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Bill questions are long. Underline each charge as you read it and tick it off when you have used it in your working.
:::

:::exam-tip
Lay out money calculations in a column with labels, and give money answers to the nearest cent, such as US\$30.40 (not US\$30.4).
:::

:::exam-tip
When working out hours from clock times, subtract unpaid breaks. From 07:30 to 16:00 is 8 hours 30 minutes; with a 30-minute lunch break, that is 8 paid hours.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Gross pay $=$ basic pay $+$ overtime (or piece-work earnings); net pay $=$ gross pay $-$ deductions.
- Time-and-a-half is $1.5 \times$ the normal rate; double time is $2 \times$ the normal rate; only overtime hours get it.
- Utility bill $=$ fixed charge $+$ cost of units, with units from meter readings and split into tariff bands.
- On a bank statement, add credits and subtract debits to find each balance.
- Budgets and premiums use percentages of an income or a value.
:::
