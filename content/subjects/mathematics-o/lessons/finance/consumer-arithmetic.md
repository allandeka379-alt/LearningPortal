## Introduction

A vendor at Mbare Musika buys tomatoes in bulk early in the morning and sells them by the kilogram during the day. A furniture shop in Gweru sells a lounge suite either for cash or "on terms" with a deposit and monthly payments. An estate agent in Harare earns a percentage of every house she sells. All of these are **consumer arithmetic**: the mathematics of buying and selling.

Every calculation in this lesson uses the percentage skills you already know, especially multipliers and reverse percentages. Prices and rates in the examples are invented for practice.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Cost price (CP)"}
The price a trader pays for an item.
:::

:::definition{term="Selling price (SP)"}
The price at which the trader sells the item.
:::

:::definition{term="Profit and loss"}
Profit $=$ SP $-$ CP when SP is greater than CP. Loss $=$ CP $-$ SP when SP is less than CP.
:::

:::definition{term="Discount"}
A reduction from the marked (normal) price, usually given as a percentage of the marked price.
:::

:::definition{term="Commission"}
Payment to a salesperson or agent calculated as a percentage of the value of the sales.
:::

:::definition{term="Hire purchase (HP)"}
A way of buying an item by paying a **deposit** now and the rest in regular **instalments**. The total HP price is usually more than the cash price.
:::

:::formula{name="Percentage profit and loss"}
$$
\text{percentage profit} = \frac{\text{profit}}{\text{cost price}} \times 100 \qquad \text{percentage loss} = \frac{\text{loss}}{\text{cost price}} \times 100
$$
:::

Unless a question says otherwise, percentage profit and loss are calculated on the **cost price**.

## Profit and loss

:::worked-solution[Example 1: a tomato vendor's profit]
A vendor buys 50 kg of tomatoes for US\$30 and sells all of them at US\$0.80 per kilogram.

1. Selling price: $50 \times 0.80 = 40$ dollars.
2. Profit: $40 - 30 = 10$ dollars.
3. Percentage profit: $\frac{10}{30} \times 100 = 33.3\%$ (3 s.f.).
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: a loss on a phone]
Tatenda buys a phone for US\$120 and later sells it for US\$93.

1. Loss: $120 - 93 = 27$ dollars.
2. Percentage loss: $\frac{27}{120} \times 100 = 22.5\%$.
:::

### Finding the selling price or the cost price

To sell at a 35% profit, multiply the cost price by $1.35$. To find the cost price **from** the selling price, divide by the multiplier.

:::worked-solution[Example 3: finding the cost price]
A trader sells a bag for US\$84 and makes a profit of 40% on the cost price. Find the cost price.

1. SP $= 140\%$ of CP, so CP $\times 1.4 = 84$.
2. CP $= 84 \div 1.4 = 60$, so the cost price is US\$60.
3. Check: $60 \times 1.4 = 84$.
:::

Notice that the profit, US\$24, is 40% of the cost price but only $\frac{24}{84} = 28.6\%$ of the selling price.

## Discount

:::worked-solution[Example 4: a 12% discount]
A bicycle is marked at US\$250. In a sale it is sold at a discount of 12%.

1. Multiplier: $1 - 0.12 = 0.88$.
2. Sale price: $250 \times 0.88 = 220$, so US\$220.
3. The discount itself is $250 - 220 = 30$ dollars.
:::

Two discounts one after the other are **not** added. A 10% discount followed by a further 5% discount gives $0.9 \times 0.95 = 0.855$, which is a total discount of 14.5%, not 15%.

## Commission

:::worked-solution[Example 5: salary plus commission]
A car salesman earns a basic wage of US\$400 a month plus 5% commission on his sales above US\$2000. One month his sales are US\$6800.

1. Sales above US\$2000: $6800 - 2000 = 4800$ dollars.
2. Commission: $0.05 \times 4800 = 240$ dollars.
3. Total pay: $400 + 240 = 640$, so US\$640.
:::

## Hire purchase

:::worked-solution[Example 6: hire purchase or cash?]
A lounge suite has a cash price of US\$1200. On hire purchase the customer pays a deposit of 25% of the cash price and then 12 monthly instalments of US\$85.

1. Deposit: $0.25 \times 1200 = 300$ dollars.
2. Instalments: $12 \times 85 = 1020$ dollars.
3. HP price: $300 + 1020 = 1320$ dollars.
4. Extra cost: $1320 - 1200 = 120$ dollars, which is $\frac{120}{1200} \times 100 = 10\%$ more than the cash price.
:::

Hire purchase lets a family buy an item before they have saved the full price, but the extra money paid is the cost of borrowing.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the selling price as the base"}
Percentage profit is calculated on the **cost price**. A profit of US\$24 on a cost price of US\$60 is 40%, not $\frac{24}{84} = 28.6\%$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Subtracting the percentage from the selling price"}
To find the cost price when a bag sells for US\$84 at a 40% profit, do **not** work out $84 \times 0.6$. Divide by $1.4$: the cost price is US\$60.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the deposit"}
The hire purchase price is the deposit **plus** all the instalments. Leaving out the deposit makes HP look cheaper than it is.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adding successive discounts"}
10% off and then 5% off is $0.9 \times 0.95 = 0.855$ of the price: 14.5% off, not 15%.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Set out money working clearly with labels, for example "Deposit $=$ US\$300", "Instalments $= 12 \times 85 =$ US\$1020". Clear labels earn method marks.
:::

:::exam-tip
When a question asks "how much more" or "how much is saved", give the **difference**, not one of the totals.
:::

:::exam-tip
For "find the cost price" or "find the marked price" questions, write an equation such as $\text{CP} \times 1.4 = 84$ before solving. It shows the examiner you understand the reverse step.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Profit $=$ SP $-$ CP; loss $=$ CP $-$ SP; percentages are of the cost price.
- Selling price for a profit of $p\%$: CP $\times \left(1 + \frac{p}{100}\right)$; cost price from SP: divide by the multiplier.
- Sale price after a discount of $d\%$: marked price $\times \left(1 - \frac{d}{100}\right)$; successive discounts multiply.
- Commission is a percentage of the value of sales, often added to a basic wage.
- Hire purchase price $=$ deposit $+$ all instalments; compare it with the cash price.
:::
