## Introduction

Zimbabwe uses more than one currency in daily life. Prices may be shown in US dollars and in ZiG, cross-border traders buy goods in South Africa with rand, and tourists at Victoria Falls arrive with pounds, euros or other currencies. Changing money from one currency to another uses an **exchange rate**.

Exchange rates change every day, so in an examination you must always use the rate given in the question. **All the rates in this lesson are invented for practice; they are not real rates.**

You should already be able to multiply and divide decimals and round money to the nearest cent.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Exchange rate"}
The value of one currency in terms of another, for example US\$1 $=$ R18.50 (South African rand).
:::

:::definition{term="Buying rate and selling rate"}
The rates a bank or bureau de change uses when it **buys** a foreign currency from you and when it **sells** that currency to you. The difference between them is how the bureau makes its profit, so a customer always loses a little in a round trip.
:::

:::definition{term="Commission"}
A charge for changing money, usually a percentage of the amount changed.
:::

## Converting between currencies

If the rate is $1$ unit of currency A $= r$ units of currency B:

- to change A into B, **multiply** by $r$;
- to change B into A, **divide** by $r$.

A quick check: you should end up with **more** units of the currency that is worth less. One US dollar buys many rand, so a number of US dollars becomes a much larger number of rand.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: US dollars and rand, with US\$1 = R18.50]
1. US\$240 into rand: $240 \times 18.50 = 4440$, so R4440.
2. R925 into US dollars: $925 \div 18.50 = 50$, so US\$50.
3. Check: rand amounts are bigger numbers than the matching dollar amounts.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: a ZiG price, with US\$1 = ZiG 26.80]
A bag of maize meal is priced at ZiG 201. Find its price in US dollars.

1. Change ZiG into US dollars by dividing: $201 \div 26.80 = 7.50$.
2. The price is US\$7.50.
:::

### Linking three currencies

If £1 $=$ US\$1.25 and US\$1 $=$ ZiG 26.80, then £1 $= 1.25 \times 26.80 =$ ZiG 33.50. Go through the shared currency one step at a time.

## Buying and selling rates

:::worked-solution[Example 3: a round trip through a bureau de change]
A bureau de change in Beitbridge uses these rates (invented for this example):

| | Rand per US\$1 |
| --- | --- |
| The bureau **buys** US dollars at | R18.20 |
| The bureau **sells** US dollars at | R18.90 |

Tendai changes US\$300 into rand, then changes all the rand straight back into US dollars.

1. The bureau buys his dollars: $300 \times 18.20 = 5460$, so he gets R5460.
2. To change back, the bureau sells him dollars at R18.90 each: $5460 \div 18.90 = 288.888\ldots$
3. He gets US\$288.89, so the round trip costs him $300 - 288.89 = 11.11$ dollars.
:::

The bureau always uses the rate that is better for **itself**: it gives you fewer rand for your dollars and charges you more rand for its dollars.

## Commission and comparing prices

:::worked-solution[Example 4: commission]
Nyasha changes US\$500 into pounds at £1 $=$ US\$1.25. The bureau deducts 2% commission.

1. Pounds before commission: $500 \div 1.25 = 400$, so £400.
2. Commission: $0.02 \times 400 = 8$, so £8.
3. She receives $400 - 8 = 392$ pounds.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 5: where is it cheaper?]
A phone costs R4250 in Musina and US\$245 in Bulawayo. The rate is US\$1 $=$ R18.50.

1. Convert the Musina price: $4250 \div 18.50 = 229.729\ldots \approx 229.73$ dollars.
2. Compare: $245 - 229.73 = 15.27$.
3. The phone is US\$15.27 cheaper in Musina (before any travel costs or duty).
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Multiplying when you should divide"}
With US\$1 $=$ ZiG 26.80, changing ZiG 201 into dollars by multiplying gives 5386.80, an impossibly large number of dollars. Ask which currency should give the bigger number.
:::

:::mistake{title="Turning the rate upside down"}
If US\$1 $=$ R18.50, then R1 $= \frac{1}{18.50}$ US dollars, about 5 cents, not US\$18.50.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the wrong one of the buying and selling rates"}
Decide whether the bureau is buying or selling the foreign currency. The customer always gets the less favourable rate.
:::

:::mistake{title="Rounding too early"}
Keep full calculator values through a chain of conversions and round to the nearest cent only at the end.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write the rate as a statement, such as "US\$1 $=$ R18.50", and show the operation, such as "$925 \div 18.50$". This earns the method mark.
:::

:::exam-tip
Give money to 2 decimal places in the currency asked for, and include the currency symbol or name in your answer.
:::

:::exam-tip
In comparison questions, convert both prices into the **same** currency, then state clearly which is cheaper and by how much.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- With 1 unit of A $= r$ units of B: A to B multiply by $r$; B to A divide by $r$.
- Check that the currency worth less ends up with the bigger number.
- Link three currencies through the one they share.
- The bureau buys foreign currency from you at its buying rate and sells to you at its selling rate; a round trip loses money.
- Commission is a percentage deducted from the amount changed. Always use the rates given in the question.
:::
