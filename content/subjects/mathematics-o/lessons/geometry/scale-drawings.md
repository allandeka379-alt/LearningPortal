## Introduction

A map of Matobo National Park fits on a single sheet of paper, yet it shows hills, dams and roads spread over many kilometres. A builder in Gweru works from a plan in which a whole classroom block is only a few centimetres long. Maps, plans and models are all **scale drawings**: every length is reduced (or enlarged) by the same factor.

In this lesson you will learn how to read and write scales, convert between map distances and real distances, work with areas on maps, and plan your own scale drawings. You should already be able to convert between millimetres, centimetres, metres and kilometres.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Scale"}
The ratio of a length on a drawing or map to the real length it represents. A scale of $1 : 50\,000$ means that 1 unit on the map represents $50\,000$ of the **same** units on the ground.
:::

:::definition{term="Representative fraction (RF)"}
The scale written as a fraction with numerator 1, such as $\frac{1}{50\,000}$. It has no units.
:::

:::definition{term="Scale drawing"}
An accurate drawing of an object in which every length is multiplied by the same scale factor, so all the angles stay the same.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Units you need"}
$1\ \text{km} = 1000\ \text{m} = 100\,000\ \text{cm}$, and $1\ \text{hectare (ha)} = 10\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

## Reading and writing scales

To write a scale as $1 : n$, put both parts in the **same unit**.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: "1 cm represents 2.5 km" as a ratio]
1. Change 2.5 km into centimetres: $2.5 \times 100\,000 = 250\,000$ cm.
2. The scale is $1 : 250\,000$, and the representative fraction is $\frac{1}{250\,000}$.
:::

A scale of $1 : n$ can also be described in words: on a $1 : 50\,000$ map, 1 cm represents $50\,000$ cm, which is 500 m or $0.5$ km.

## Map distance and actual distance

- Map to ground: **multiply** by $n$, then convert the units.
- Ground to map: convert to the map unit (usually cm), then **divide** by $n$.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: from the map to the ground]
On a map of scale $1 : 50\,000$, two villages are 7.4 cm apart. Find the actual distance in kilometres.

1. Actual distance $= 7.4 \times 50\,000 = 370\,000$ cm.
2. $370\,000 \div 100\,000 = 3.7$ km.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: from the ground to the map]
Two towns are 12 km apart. How far apart are they on a map of scale $1 : 200\,000$?

1. $12\ \text{km} = 1\,200\,000$ cm.
2. Map distance $= 1\,200\,000 \div 200\,000 = 6$ cm.
:::

You can also **find** a scale. If an 18 km road is 9 cm long on a map, then 1 cm represents 2 km $= 200\,000$ cm, so the scale is $1 : 200\,000$.

## Areas on maps

If every length is multiplied by $n$, every area is multiplied by $n^2$. So the **area scale** is the square of the length scale.

:::worked-solution[Example 4: the area of a farm]
On a map of scale $1 : 25\,000$, a farm covers $12\ \text{cm}^2$. Find its actual area in hectares.

1. 1 cm represents $25\,000$ cm $= 250$ m.
2. So $1\ \text{cm}^2$ represents $250\ \text{m} \times 250\ \text{m} = 62\,500\ \text{m}^2$.
3. $12\ \text{cm}^2$ represents $12 \times 62\,500 = 750\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
4. In hectares: $750\,000 \div 10\,000 = 75$ ha.
:::

Converting to metres **before** squaring keeps the numbers manageable and avoids errors with very large numbers of square centimetres.

## Making a scale drawing

1. Choose a scale that makes the drawing a sensible size on your page.
2. Convert every real length into a drawing length.
3. Draw with a sharp pencil and ruler (and protractor or compasses for angles), and label the lengths and the scale.

![A classroom 12 m by 8 m drawn at scale 1 : 200, so 1 cm represents 2 m: the plan is a rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm and its diagonal measures about 7.2 cm, which is about 14.4 m in the real room.](media:diagrams/scale-drawing-classroom-1-200.png "Figure: every length on the plan is divided by 200; angles do not change")

:::example{title="Planning a classroom drawing"}
A classroom is 12 m long and 8 m wide. At a scale of $1 : 200$, 1 cm on the drawing represents 2 m. The drawing is a rectangle $12 \div 2 = 6$ cm long and $8 \div 2 = 4$ cm wide. If you measure the diagonal of your drawing as about 7.2 cm, the real diagonal is about $7.2 \times 2 = 14.4$ m.
:::

Angles are **not** scaled: a right angle in the room is still a right angle on the plan.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing units in the scale"}
"1 cm represents 2.5 km" is not $1 : 2.5$. Both parts of a ratio scale must be in the same unit: $1 : 250\,000$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Using the length scale for areas"}
On a $1 : 25\,000$ map, $1\ \text{cm}^2$ does not represent $25\,000\ \text{cm}^2$. It represents $25\,000^2\ \text{cm}^2 = 62\,500\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Wrong conversion between cm and km"}
There are $100\,000$ cm in 1 km, not 1000. Check: 1 km $= 1000$ m and each metre is 100 cm.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Write the meaning of the scale in friendly units first, such as "1 cm represents 250 m". Then every map distance becomes one simple multiplication.
:::

:::exam-tip
When a scale drawing is required, examiners check your lengths and angles against the correct ones and allow only a small tolerance. Use a sharp pencil, measure carefully and leave your construction lines visible.
:::

:::exam-tip
A "larger scale" map, such as $1 : 10\,000$, shows **more detail** of a smaller area than a $1 : 250\,000$ map.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A scale $1 : n$ means 1 unit on the drawing represents $n$ of the same units in real life; the RF is $\frac{1}{n}$.
- Map to ground: multiply by $n$; ground to map: divide by $n$; then convert units ($1\ \text{km} = 100\,000\ \text{cm}$).
- The area scale is the square of the length scale: on a $1 : n$ map, $1\ \text{cm}^2$ represents $n^2\ \text{cm}^2$.
- $1$ hectare $= 10\,000\ \text{m}^2$ and $1\ \text{km}^2 = 100$ hectares.
- In a scale drawing every length is scaled by the same factor and angles stay the same.
:::
