## Introduction

The floor plan of a round rondavel, a bicycle wheel, the face of a clock: circles appear everywhere, and the angles inside them follow some beautiful rules. If you know a single angle in a circle diagram, these rules, called **circle theorems**, often let you find every other angle without measuring.

This lesson covers six theorems and how to quote them as reasons. **Cambridge Core** candidates need the first two (the angle in a semicircle and the tangent–radius angle). **Cambridge Extended** and **ZIMSEC** candidates need them all.

You should know the parts of a circle (radius, chord, tangent, arc, segment) and the basic angle facts, especially that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal: two radii and a chord make an isosceles triangle.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Subtended angle"}
The angle that a chord or arc makes at a point. In triangle $ACB$ with $C$ on the circle, the chord $AB$ **subtends** angle $ACB$ at $C$.
:::

:::definition{term="Cyclic quadrilateral"}
A quadrilateral whose four vertices all lie on a circle.
:::

:::definition{term="Alternate segment"}
When a chord is drawn from the point where a tangent touches the circle, the segment on the **other** side of the chord from a given angle between the tangent and the chord.
:::

## The six theorems

:::keyfact{title="Theorems for every candidate"}
1. **The angle in a semicircle is a right angle.** If $AB$ is a diameter and $C$ is on the circle, angle $ACB = 90^\circ$.
2. **A tangent is perpendicular to the radius** at the point of contact.
:::

:::keyfact{title="Theorems for Extended and ZIMSEC"}
3. **The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference** subtended by the same arc.
4. **Angles in the same segment are equal.**
5. **Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180^\circ$.**
6. **Alternate segment theorem:** the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
:::

## Worked examples

:::worked-solution[Example 1: an angle in a semicircle]
$AB$ is a diameter of a circle and $C$ is a point on the circle. Angle $CAB = 34^\circ$. Find angle $ABC$.
1. Angle $ACB = 90^\circ$ (angle in a semicircle).
2. Angle $ABC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 34^\circ = 56^\circ$ (angles in a triangle).
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: a tangent and a radius]
$O$ is the centre of a circle. A tangent from the point $P$ touches the circle at $T$. Angle $TOP = 58^\circ$. Find angle $OPT$.
1. Angle $OTP = 90^\circ$ (tangent perpendicular to radius).
2. Angle $OPT = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 58^\circ = 32^\circ$ (angles in a triangle).
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: the angle at the centre]
$A$ and $B$ are points on a circle with centre $O$, and angle $AOB = 130^\circ$. $C$ is on the major arc $AB$ and $D$ is on the minor arc $AB$. Find angle $ACB$ and angle $ADB$.
1. Angle $ACB = 130^\circ \div 2 = 65^\circ$ (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference).
2. For $D$, the arc used is the **major** arc, which subtends the reflex angle $AOB = 360^\circ - 130^\circ = 230^\circ$ at the centre.
3. Angle $ADB = 230^\circ \div 2 = 115^\circ$.
4. Check: $ACBD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral and $65^\circ + 115^\circ = 180^\circ$.
:::

:::example{title="Angles in the same segment"}
$A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ lie on a circle, with $C$ and $D$ on the same side of the chord $AB$. Angles $ACB$ and $ADB$ are both subtended by $AB$ from the same segment, so they are equal. If angle $ACB = 40^\circ$, then angle $ADB = 40^\circ$ (angles in the same segment are equal).
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 4: a cyclic quadrilateral]
$ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral with angle $A = (3x + 10)^\circ$ and angle $C = 2x^\circ$. Find both angles.
1. $A$ and $C$ are opposite angles, so $(3x + 10) + 2x = 180$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180^\circ$).
2. $5x = 170$, so $x = 34$.
3. Angle $A = 112^\circ$ and angle $C = 68^\circ$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 5: the alternate segment theorem]
The line $TAS$ is a tangent to a circle at $A$. $AB$ is a chord and $C$ is a point on the circle on the opposite side of $AB$ from $T$. Angle $TAB = 62^\circ$. Find angle $ACB$.
1. Angle $TAB$ is between the tangent and the chord $AB$.
2. $C$ is in the alternate segment, so angle $ACB$ = angle $TAB = 62^\circ$ (alternate segment theorem).
:::

A useful extra fact follows from theorem 5: an **exterior** angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Halving the wrong way"}
The angle at the **centre** is the larger one: it is twice the angle at the circumference, not half.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the reflex angle"}
If the point on the circumference is on the **minor** arc, it uses the reflex angle at the centre. Example 3 shows the difference: $65^\circ$ on the major arc but $115^\circ$ on the minor arc.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling any quadrilateral cyclic"}
Opposite angles add up to $180^\circ$ only when **all four** vertices are on the circle. If one vertex is the centre $O$, the quadrilateral is not cyclic.
:::

:::mistake{title="Missing the isosceles triangle"}
Two radii from the centre to the circle are equal, so the triangle they make with a chord is isosceles. Many questions need this before any circle theorem.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Learn the reasons word for word, for example "angle in a semicircle", "angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference", "angles in the same segment", "opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral". Phrases such as "circle rule" earn nothing.
:::

:::exam-tip
Mark the centre's radii as equal on your sketch straight away, and mark every right angle from a diameter or a tangent.
:::

:::exam-tip
When a tangent and a chord meet, check for the alternate segment theorem; when a diameter appears, check for the angle in a semicircle.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Angle in a semicircle $= 90^\circ$; tangent $\perp$ radius at the point of contact.
- Angle at the centre $= 2 \times$ angle at the circumference (same arc).
- Angles in the same segment are equal.
- Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180^\circ$.
- Angle between a tangent and a chord = angle in the alternate segment.
:::
