## Introduction

A builder in Bulawayo reads a plan that says the walls must be *perpendicular*, the roof beams *parallel* and the window arches *arcs of a circle*. A surveyor marking out a plot near Marondera records the *vertices* of the boundary and the *acute* and *obtuse* angles between its sides. Geometry has its own precise vocabulary, and examiners expect you to use it accurately: you cannot give a reason such as "alternate angles are equal", or answer "name this solid", without knowing the words.

This lesson collects the terms you will use in every other geometry topic. Most of them describe things you already recognise; the aim is to know their exact meaning.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Point, line and line segment"}
A **point** marks a position and has no size. A **line** is straight and goes on for ever in both directions. A **line segment** is the part of a line between two end points, such as $AB$.
:::

:::definition{term="Plane"}
A flat surface that extends in every direction, like a very large sheet of paper or the surface of a still dam.
:::

:::definition{term="Parallel lines"}
Lines in the same plane that never meet, however far they are extended. They stay the same distance apart. We write $AB \parallel CD$.
:::

:::definition{term="Perpendicular lines"}
Lines that meet at a right angle ($90^\circ$). We write $AB \perp CD$.
:::

:::definition{term="Vertex"}
A corner: the point where two sides of a shape, or two or more edges of a solid, meet. The plural is **vertices**.
:::

## Types of angle

An angle measures an amount of turn. A full turn is $360^\circ$.

| Name | Size |
| --- | --- |
| Acute angle | between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ |
| Right angle | exactly $90^\circ$ |
| Obtuse angle | between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ |
| Straight angle | exactly $180^\circ$ |
| Reflex angle | between $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$ |

:::keyfact{title="Complementary and supplementary angles"}
Two angles are **complementary** if they add up to $90^\circ$. Two angles are **supplementary** if they add up to $180^\circ$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 1: describing an angle of $28^\circ$]
1. $28^\circ$ is between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, so it is acute.
2. Its complement is $90^\circ - 28^\circ = 62^\circ$.
3. Its supplement is $180^\circ - 28^\circ = 152^\circ$.
4. The reflex angle that completes a full turn with it is $360^\circ - 28^\circ = 332^\circ$.
:::

Angles are named with three letters. Angle $ABC$ (also written $\angle ABC$ or $A\hat{B}C$) is the angle at $B$ between the lines $BA$ and $BC$. The **middle letter is always the vertex**.

## Triangles and quadrilaterals

Triangles are named by their sides or by their angles.

- **Equilateral**: three equal sides and three angles of $60^\circ$.
- **Isosceles**: two equal sides; the two angles opposite them (the base angles) are equal.
- **Scalene**: no equal sides and no equal angles.
- **Right-angled**, **acute-angled** (all three angles acute) or **obtuse-angled** (one obtuse angle).

The special quadrilaterals are the **square**, **rectangle**, **rhombus** (four equal sides), **parallelogram** (two pairs of parallel sides), **trapezium** (exactly one pair of parallel sides) and **kite** (two pairs of equal adjacent sides). Their properties are studied in the lesson on polygons.

## Polygons

A **polygon** is a closed flat shape with straight sides. A **regular polygon** has all its sides equal and all its angles equal.

| Sides | Name | Sides | Name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | triangle | 7 | heptagon |
| 4 | quadrilateral | 8 | octagon |
| 5 | pentagon | 9 | nonagon |
| 6 | hexagon | 10 | decagon |

An **interior angle** is inside the polygon at a vertex. An **exterior angle** is the angle between one side and the next side extended; at each vertex the interior and exterior angles add up to $180^\circ$.

## Parts of a circle

:::definition{term="Radius, diameter and circumference"}
A **radius** joins the centre to a point on the circle. A **diameter** is a chord through the centre; it is twice as long as the radius. The **circumference** is the distance all the way round the circle.
:::

:::definition{term="Chord, tangent and arc"}
A **chord** is a line segment joining two points on the circle. A **tangent** is a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point. An **arc** is part of the circumference: the shorter one is the minor arc and the longer one the major arc.
:::

:::definition{term="Sector and segment"}
A **sector** is the region between two radii and an arc, like a slice of a round cake. A **segment** is the region between a chord and an arc. A **semicircle** is half of a circle, cut off by a diameter.
:::

## Solids and nets

A **solid** (a three-dimensional shape) has **faces** (flat or curved surfaces), **edges** (where two faces meet) and **vertices** (corners).

- A **prism** has the same cross-section all the way through: two identical end faces joined by rectangles. A cuboid is a prism with a rectangular cross-section.
- A **pyramid** has a polygon for its base and triangular faces that meet at one point, the **apex**.
- A **cylinder** has two circular ends and a curved surface; a **cone** has a circular base, a curved surface and an apex; a **sphere** is perfectly round.

A **net** is a flat shape that folds up to make a solid.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: faces, edges and vertices of a triangular prism]
1. Faces: two triangles at the ends and three rectangles round the sides, so $2 + 3 = 5$ faces.
2. Edges: each triangle has 3 edges, and 3 more edges join the two triangles, so $3 + 3 + 3 = 9$ edges.
3. Vertices: each triangle has 3 corners, so $3 + 3 = 6$ vertices.
4. A useful check for solids with flat faces like this one is Euler's rule, faces $+$ vertices $-$ edges $= 2$: here $5 + 6 - 9 = 2$.
:::

## Similar, congruent and scale factor

:::definition{term="Congruent"}
Exactly the same shape **and** size. One shape would fit exactly on top of the other (it may need to be turned over).
:::

:::definition{term="Similar"}
The same shape but not necessarily the same size: corresponding angles are equal and every length is multiplied by the same **scale factor**.
:::

A photograph enlarged from $10\ \text{cm}$ by $15\ \text{cm}$ to $20\ \text{cm}$ by $30\ \text{cm}$ is similar to the original, with scale factor $2$.

Cambridge Extended candidates should also know that the **perpendicular bisector** of a line segment $AB$ is the line that crosses $AB$ at its midpoint at right angles.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up complementary and supplementary"}
Complementary angles add up to $90^\circ$; supplementary angles add up to $180^\circ$. A memory aid: **C** comes before **S** in the alphabet, and $90$ comes before $180$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling a straight angle reflex"}
An angle of exactly $180^\circ$ is a straight angle. A reflex angle is **more than** $180^\circ$ and less than $360^\circ$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Sector or segment?"}
A sector is bounded by two **radii** and an arc, so it always has a corner at the centre. A segment is bounded by a **chord** and an arc, so it has no corner at the centre.
:::

:::mistake{title="Reading three-letter angles"}
Angle $PQR$ is the angle at $Q$, the middle letter, not the angle at $P$.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Name" or "write down" means one word or phrase is enough, but spell it correctly: *parallelogram*, *isosceles*, *perpendicular*.
:::

:::exam-tip
When a diagram is marked "**not drawn to scale**", never measure it. Use the facts you are given (for example "$AB$ is a diameter") and the vocabulary to decide what you know.
:::

:::exam-tip
Use symbols correctly: $AB \parallel CD$ means "$AB$ is parallel to $CD$", and $AB \perp CD$ means "$AB$ is perpendicular to $CD$".
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Acute $< 90^\circ$, right $= 90^\circ$, obtuse between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, straight $= 180^\circ$, reflex between $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.
- Complementary angles add up to $90^\circ$; supplementary angles add up to $180^\circ$.
- Angle $ABC$ is the angle at $B$, the middle letter.
- Circle parts: radius, diameter, chord, tangent, arc, sector, segment and circumference.
- Solids have faces, edges and vertices; a net folds up to make a solid.
- Congruent means the same shape and size; similar means the same shape, with all lengths multiplied by the same scale factor.
:::
