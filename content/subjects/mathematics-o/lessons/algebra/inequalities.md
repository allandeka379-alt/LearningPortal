## Introduction

A minibus can carry **at most** 18 passengers. A learner needs **at least** 50% to pass a test. A notebook budget must be **no more than** US\$25. Statements like these do not give a single value: they give a range of values. In mathematics we write them as **inequalities**.

In this lesson you will read and write inequality symbols, show inequalities on a number line, solve them algebraically and find the whole-number solutions. Cambridge Core candidates need to represent and interpret inequalities; Cambridge Extended and ZIMSEC candidates also solve them, and ZIMSEC includes pairs of inequalities that must both be true. (Inequalities in two variables, shown as regions on a graph, are a separate lesson.)

You should be able to solve linear equations.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Inequality symbols"}
$x < 3$: $x$ is less than 3. $x \leq 3$: $x$ is less than or equal to 3. $x > 3$: $x$ is greater than 3. $x \geq 3$: $x$ is greater than or equal to 3.
:::

:::definition{term="Strict and inclusive inequalities"}
$<$ and $>$ are **strict**: the end value is not included. $\leq$ and $\geq$ are **inclusive**: the end value is included.
:::

:::keyfact{title="The one rule that differs from equations"}
When you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a **negative** number, reverse the inequality sign. For example $-4x < 12$ gives $x > -3$.
:::

## Showing inequalities on a number line

Draw a circle at the end value and a line or arrow over the values that satisfy the inequality.

| Inequality | Circle at the end value | Line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $x > 3$ | open (empty) circle at 3 | arrow to the right |
| $x \leq -2$ | closed (filled) circle at $-2$ | arrow to the left |
| $-2 \leq x < 5$ | closed at $-2$, open at 5 | line joining them |

## Solving inequalities

Solve exactly as you would an equation, keeping the inequality sign. Reverse the sign only when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: solve $7 - 3x \leq 19$]
1. Subtract 7 from both sides: $-3x \leq 12$.
2. Divide by $-3$ and **reverse** the sign: $x \geq -4$.
3. Check with $x = 0$ (which should work): $7 - 0 = 7 \leq 19$. ✓
:::

You can avoid dividing by a negative by collecting the unknown on the side where it stays positive. For $5 - 2x > x + 11$: add $2x$ and subtract 11 to get $-6 > 3x$, so $-2 > x$, which is $x < -2$.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: solve $\dfrac{x + 1}{2} \geq \dfrac{x + 5}{3}$]
1. Multiply both sides by 6 (positive, so the sign stays): $3(x + 1) \geq 2(x + 5)$.
2. Expand: $3x + 3 \geq 2x + 10$.
3. Subtract $2x$ and 3: $x \geq 7$.
:::

## Double inequalities

A double inequality such as $-5 < 2x + 1 \leq 9$ has three parts. Do the same operation to all three.

:::worked-solution[Example 3: solve $-5 < 2x + 1 \leq 9$ and list the integer solutions]
1. Subtract 1 from every part: $-6 < 2x \leq 8$.
2. Divide every part by 2: $-3 < x \leq 4$.
3. Integers: $-3$ is excluded and 4 is included.
4. The integer solutions are $-2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$.
:::

## Two inequalities together (ZIMSEC)

When two inequalities must both be true, solve each one and find the values they share.

:::worked-solution[Example 4: solve $x - 4 < 1$ and $3x + 2 \geq -4$]
1. First: $x < 5$.
2. Second: $3x \geq -6$, so $x \geq -2$.
3. Both: $-2 \leq x < 5$. On a number line: closed circle at $-2$, open circle at 5, joined by a line.
:::

## Inequalities in context

Chipo has US\$30, pays US\$5 for transport and buys books at US\$3.25 each. Then $5 + 3.25n \leq 30$, so $n \leq 7.69\ldots$. She can buy at most **7** books: round **down**, because 8 would cost too much. If instead you need enough buses for 210 people at 60 per bus, $60n \geq 210$ gives $n \geq 3.5$, so round **up** to 4 buses.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Forgetting to reverse the sign"}
$-4x < 12$ does **not** give $x < -3$. Test $x = 0$: $0 < 12$ is true, but $0 < -3$ is false. The correct answer is $x > -3$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Reversing when it is not needed"}
Only multiplying or dividing by a **negative** number reverses the sign. Adding or subtracting a negative number, or dividing by a positive number, does not.
:::

:::mistake{title="Including or excluding the wrong ends"}
For $-1 < x \leq 4$ the integers are $0, 1, 2, 3, 4$: $-1$ is excluded and 4 is included. Check each end separately.
:::

:::mistake{title="Doing different things to the parts of a double inequality"}
In $-5 < 2x + 1 \leq 9$, subtract 1 from **all three** parts and then divide **all three** by 2.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Give the answer as an inequality, such as $x \geq -4$, not as an equation. If the question asks for integers, list them all, separated by commas.
:::

:::exam-tip
Check your answer by testing a value inside your range and one outside it in the original inequality.
:::

:::exam-tip
On a number line, draw clear open and closed circles and label the numbers. A missing circle or the wrong type of circle loses the mark.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- $<$ and $>$ are strict (open circle); $\leq$ and $\geq$ are inclusive (closed circle).
- Solve inequalities like equations, but reverse the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.
- For a double inequality, do the same to all three parts.
- For integer solutions, check whether each end value is included.
- In context, decide whether to round up or down.
:::
