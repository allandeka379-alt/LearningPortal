## Introduction

A shop in Bulawayo advertises "25% off", a recipe needs $\frac{3}{4}$ of a cup of sugar and a fuel gauge shows $0.6$ of a tank. These are three ways of writing parts of a whole. You must be able to move between them quickly and choose the most useful form for the job.

You should already know your multiplication tables and how to multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1000.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Proper fraction, improper fraction and mixed number"}
In a proper fraction the numerator is smaller than the denominator ($\frac{3}{5}$). In an improper fraction it is equal to or larger than the denominator ($\frac{19}{8}$). A mixed number has a whole part and a fraction part ($2\frac{3}{8}$).
:::

:::definition{term="Equivalent fractions and simplest form"}
Equivalent fractions have the same value, such as $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{9}{12}$. A fraction is in its simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1.
:::

:::definition{term="Percentage"}
A number of parts per hundred: $35\% = \frac{35}{100} = 0.35$.
:::

:::definition{term="Terminating and recurring decimals"}
A terminating decimal ends ($\frac{3}{8} = 0.375$). A recurring decimal repeats a digit or block of digits for ever ($\frac{5}{12} = 0.41666\ldots$).
:::

## Simplifying and mixed numbers

Divide the numerator and denominator by their HCF: $\frac{36}{48} = \frac{36 \div 12}{48 \div 12} = \frac{3}{4}$.

To change $2\frac{3}{8}$ to an improper fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator: $\frac{2 \times 8 + 3}{8} = \frac{19}{8}$. To go back, divide: $19 \div 8 = 2$ remainder $3$, so $\frac{19}{8} = 2\frac{3}{8}$.

## Converting between the three forms

:::keyfact{title="The conversions"}
- Fraction to decimal: divide the numerator by the denominator.
- Decimal to fraction: use place value, then simplify ($0.65 = \frac{65}{100} = \frac{13}{20}$).
- Decimal to percentage: multiply by 100 ($0.375 \to 37.5\%$).
- Percentage to decimal: divide by 100 ($7\% \to 0.07$).
:::

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $\frac{1}{2}$ | $0.5$ | $50\%$ |
| $\frac{1}{4}$ | $0.25$ | $25\%$ |
| $\frac{1}{5}$ | $0.2$ | $20\%$ |
| $\frac{1}{8}$ | $0.125$ | $12.5\%$ |
| $\frac{1}{10}$ | $0.1$ | $10\%$ |
| $\frac{1}{3}$ | $0.333\ldots$ | $33\frac{1}{3}\%$ |

:::worked-solution[Example 1: write $\frac{7}{40}$ as a decimal and as a percentage]
1. Divide: $7 \div 40 = 0.175$. (Or: $\frac{7}{40} = \frac{7 \times 25}{40 \times 25} = \frac{175}{1000}$.)
2. Multiply by 100: $0.175 \times 100 = 17.5\%$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: write $0.125$ and $135\%$ as fractions in their simplest form]
1. $0.125$ has three decimal places, so it is $\frac{125}{1000}$.
2. The HCF of 125 and 1000 is 125, so $\frac{125}{1000} = \frac{1}{8}$.
3. $135\% = \frac{135}{100} = \frac{27}{20}$, which is $1\frac{7}{20}$ as a mixed number.
:::

A fraction in its simplest form gives a terminating decimal only when its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5. So $\frac{9}{40}$ terminates ($40 = 2^3 \times 5$) but $\frac{5}{12}$ recurs ($12 = 2^2 \times 3$).

## Fractions of quantities

To find a fraction of an amount, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator. To write one quantity as a fraction or percentage of another, put the part over the whole.

:::worked-solution[Example 3: maize and marks]
A farmer in Mashonaland East stores 120 kg of maize and uses $\frac{3}{8}$ of it. Rudo scores 18 out of 24 in a test.
1. $\frac{1}{8}$ of 120 kg $= 120 \div 8 = 15$ kg, so $\frac{3}{8}$ of 120 kg $= 3 \times 15 = 45$ kg.
2. Rudo's score as a fraction: $\frac{18}{24} = \frac{3}{4}$.
3. As a percentage: $\frac{3}{4} \times 100\% = 75\%$.
:::

## Recurring decimals (Cambridge Extended)

Recurring digits are shown with dots: $0.\dot{3} = 0.333\ldots$, $0.\dot{2}\dot{7} = 0.272727\ldots$ and $0.\dot{1}2\dot{3} = 0.123123\ldots$ (the dots mark the first and last digits of the repeating block). Every recurring decimal is rational, and you can find its fraction with a little algebra.

:::worked-solution[Example 4: write $0.\dot{2}\dot{7}$ as a fraction in its simplest form]
1. Let $x = 0.272727\ldots$
2. The block has two digits, so multiply by 100: $100x = 27.272727\ldots$
3. Subtract: $100x - x = 27.2727\ldots - 0.2727\ldots$, so $99x = 27$.
4. $x = \frac{27}{99} = \frac{3}{11}$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 5: write $0.4\dot{5}$ as a fraction]
1. Let $x = 0.4555\ldots$
2. $10x = 4.555\ldots$ and $100x = 45.555\ldots$ (both have the same recurring tail).
3. Subtract: $100x - 10x = 45.555\ldots - 4.555\ldots$, so $90x = 41$.
4. $x = \frac{41}{90}$.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using the wrong power of ten"}
$0.125$ has three decimal places, so it is $\frac{125}{1000}$, not $\frac{125}{100}$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Rounding when an exact value is needed"}
$\frac{2}{3} = 0.666\ldots$, which is **not** exactly $0.67$. Use the fraction or recurring notation when the question asks for an exact value.
:::

:::mistake{title="Moving the decimal point the wrong way"}
$0.07 = 7\%$, not $70\%$ or $0.7\%$. Multiply by 100 to change a decimal into a percentage.
:::

:::mistake{title="Leaving a fraction unsimplified"}
$\frac{27}{99}$ is correct in value but not in simplest form. Always cancel by the HCF: $\frac{3}{11}$.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form" means a final mark is lost for $\frac{27}{99}$. Check whether the numerator and denominator share a factor.
:::

:::exam-tip
In "show that" recurring-decimal questions, write the two equations (such as $100x = 27.27\ldots$ and $x = 0.27\ldots$) and the subtraction clearly: the method is what earns the marks.
:::

:::exam-tip
Learn the common equivalents in the table above. They speed up non-calculator questions and help you check answers.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Simplify a fraction by dividing the numerator and denominator by their HCF.
- Fraction to decimal: divide; decimal to percentage: multiply by 100; decimal to fraction: use place value and simplify.
- To find $\frac{a}{b}$ of an amount, divide by $b$ and multiply by $a$.
- In simplest form, only denominators with prime factors 2 and 5 give terminating decimals.
- Extended: write a recurring decimal as $x$, multiply by a power of 10, subtract to remove the recurring part and solve.
:::
