## Introduction

Cambridge IGCSE Papers 1 and 2 are taken without a calculator, and ZIMSEC candidates should expect a non-calculator paper too. Every question on such a paper depends on accurate arithmetic: negative numbers, fractions, decimals and the order in which operations are done. A slip in a simple step can cost every mark in a long question, so these skills are worth making automatic.

You should already know your multiplication tables to $12 \times 12$.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Directed (negative) numbers"}
Numbers with a sign showing direction from zero, such as $+5$ and $-3$. They describe temperatures below zero, bank overdrafts and heights below sea level.
:::

:::definition{term="Order of operations"}
The agreed order for working out a calculation: **B**rackets, then **I**ndices (powers and roots), then **D**ivision and **M**ultiplication (from left to right), then **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (from left to right). This is often remembered as BIDMAS or BODMAS.
:::

## Order of operations

:::worked-solution[Example 1: work out $18 - 3 \times (7 - 2)^2 \div 5$]
1. Brackets: $7 - 2 = 5$.
2. Indices: $5^2 = 25$.
3. Multiplication and division from left to right: $3 \times 25 = 75$, then $75 \div 5 = 15$.
4. Subtraction: $18 - 15 = 3$.
:::

A fraction line acts like a bracket: in $\dfrac{12 + 8}{4}$ the whole top is worked out first, giving $\frac{20}{4} = 5$.

## Directed numbers

:::keyfact{title="Rules for signs"}
- Adding a negative is the same as subtracting: $6 + (-8) = 6 - 8 = -2$.
- Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: $-6 - (-9) = -6 + 9 = 3$.
- Multiplying or dividing: same signs give a **positive** answer, different signs give a **negative** answer. $(-4) \times (-5) = 20$ and $-36 \div 4 = -9$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 2: overnight temperatures in Nyanga]
At 05:00 the temperature in Nyanga was $-3^\circ\text{C}$. By 13:00 it had risen to $14^\circ\text{C}$. By how many degrees did it rise?
1. Rise $=$ later temperature $-$ earlier temperature $= 14 - (-3)$.
2. Subtracting a negative means adding: $14 + 3 = 17$.
3. The temperature rose by $17^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

## Fractions and mixed numbers

:::keyfact{title="Methods for fractions"}
- **Add or subtract**: write the fractions with a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators.
- **Multiply**: change mixed numbers to improper fractions, cancel common factors, then multiply the numerators and the denominators.
- **Divide**: multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction ("keep, change, flip").
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 3: work out $2\frac{1}{3} + 1\frac{3}{4}$ and $3\frac{1}{2} - 1\frac{4}{5}$]
1. $2\frac{1}{3} + 1\frac{3}{4} = \frac{7}{3} + \frac{7}{4} = \frac{28}{12} + \frac{21}{12} = \frac{49}{12} = 4\frac{1}{12}$.
2. $3\frac{1}{2} - 1\frac{4}{5} = \frac{7}{2} - \frac{9}{5} = \frac{35}{10} - \frac{18}{10} = \frac{17}{10} = 1\frac{7}{10}$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Example 4: work out $2\frac{2}{5} \times 1\frac{1}{4}$ and $3\frac{3}{4} \div 1\frac{1}{2}$]
1. $2\frac{2}{5} \times 1\frac{1}{4} = \frac{12}{5} \times \frac{5}{4}$. Cancel the 5s and divide 12 and 4 by 4: $\frac{3}{1} \times \frac{1}{1} = 3$.
2. $3\frac{3}{4} \div 1\frac{1}{2} = \frac{15}{4} \div \frac{3}{2} = \frac{15}{4} \times \frac{2}{3}$.
3. Cancel: $\frac{5}{2} \times \frac{1}{1} = \frac{5}{2} = 2\frac{1}{2}$.
:::

:::example{title="Fractions and the order of operations"}
Work out $\left(\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{3}\right) \times 2\frac{2}{5}$.

The bracket comes first: $\frac{9}{12} - \frac{4}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$. Then multiply: $\frac{5}{12} \times \frac{12}{5} = 1$. Notice that the improper fraction $\frac{12}{5}$ is the reciprocal of $\frac{5}{12}$, so the product is exactly 1.
:::

## Decimals without a calculator

- **Multiplying**: ignore the decimal points, multiply the whole numbers, then give the answer as many decimal places as the two numbers have together. $0.36 \times 0.4$: $36 \times 4 = 144$, and $2 + 1 = 3$ decimal places, so $0.144$.
- **Dividing by a decimal**: multiply both numbers by the same power of 10 so that you divide by a whole number. $7.2 \div 0.08 = 720 \div 8 = 90$.

:::worked-solution[Example 5: work out $(2.5 - 0.7)^2 \div 0.9$]
1. Brackets: $2.5 - 0.7 = 1.8$.
2. Square: $1.8^2 = 3.24$ (because $18^2 = 324$ and there are two decimal places).
3. Divide: $3.24 \div 0.9 = 32.4 \div 9 = 3.6$.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Working from left to right regardless of the operation"}
$5 + 3 \times 4 = 5 + 12 = 17$, not $8 \times 4 = 32$. Multiplication comes before addition.
:::

:::mistake{title="Squaring a negative number without brackets"}
$-3^2$ means $-(3^2) = -9$, but $(-3)^2 = 9$. The index applies only to what it touches.
:::

:::mistake{title="Adding the denominators"}
$\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} \neq \frac{3}{7}$. Use a common denominator: $\frac{8}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Multiplying mixed numbers part by part"}
$2\frac{2}{5} \times 1\frac{1}{4}$ is not $2 \times 1$ plus $\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{1}{4}$. Change to improper fractions first.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
On non-calculator papers, write every step. Showing $\frac{15}{4} \times \frac{2}{3}$ earns the method mark even if a later cancelling slip spoils the answer.
:::

:::exam-tip
If a question says "give your answer as a mixed number" or "in its simplest form", an unsimplified improper fraction loses the final mark.
:::

:::exam-tip
Check a decimal answer with a rough estimate: $7.2 \div 0.08$ must be large, because 0.08 goes into 7.2 many times.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Use BIDMAS: brackets, indices, then division and multiplication, then addition and subtraction, working left to right within each level.
- Subtracting a negative is adding; same signs multiply or divide to give a positive, different signs a negative.
- Add and subtract fractions with a common denominator; multiply after changing to improper fractions; divide by multiplying by the reciprocal.
- Count decimal places when multiplying decimals; make the divisor a whole number when dividing.
:::
