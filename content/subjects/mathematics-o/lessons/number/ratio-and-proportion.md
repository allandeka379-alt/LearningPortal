## Introduction

A builder in Masvingo mixes concrete using 1 bucket of cement, 2 buckets of sand and 4 buckets of stones. A family shares the profit from selling tomatoes at a market in the ratio of the hours each person worked. A recipe for 12 scones has to be changed for 30 guests. All of these are **ratio and proportion** problems.

Ratios compare quantities. Proportion tells you how one quantity changes when another changes. These ideas appear in almost every examination paper, often hidden inside questions about money, maps, mixtures and work.

You should already be able to find the highest common factor (HCF) of two numbers, simplify fractions and convert between units such as grams and kilograms.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Ratio"}
A comparison of two or more quantities of the same kind, written with colons, for example $3 : 5$. The order matters: "boys to girls is $3 : 5$" is not the same as "girls to boys is $3 : 5$".
:::

:::definition{term="Simplest form"}
A ratio is in its simplest form when its parts are whole numbers with no common factor other than 1. For example, $12 : 18 = 2 : 3$.
:::

:::definition{term="Direct proportion"}
Two quantities are in direct proportion when their ratio always stays the same, so that doubling one doubles the other (and halving one halves the other). The cost of identical bags of maize meal is directly proportional to the number of bags.
:::

:::definition{term="Inverse proportion"}
Two quantities are in inverse proportion when one increases as the other decreases so that their **product stays the same**: doubling one halves the other. The number of workers and the time to finish a job are inversely proportional.
:::

:::definition{term="Unitary method"}
A method that first finds the value of **one** unit (one item, one person, one day) and then multiplies to get the value required.
:::

## Simplifying ratios

To simplify a ratio, divide every part by the same number, ideally the HCF. The quantities must be in the **same units** first, and the final ratio has no units.

:::worked-solution[Example 1: write $450\ \text{g} : 1.2\ \text{kg}$ in its simplest form]
1. Change to the same units: $1.2\ \text{kg} = 1200\ \text{g}$, so the ratio is $450 : 1200$.
2. The HCF of 450 and 1200 is 150.
3. $450 \div 150 = 3$ and $1200 \div 150 = 8$.
4. The ratio is $3 : 8$.
:::

If a ratio contains fractions or decimals, multiply every part by the same number to make whole numbers first. For example, $\frac{1}{3} : \frac{1}{4}$ becomes $4 : 3$ after multiplying both parts by 12, and $0.5 : 2$ becomes $1 : 4$ after multiplying by 2.

:::keyfact{title="The form 1 : n"}
To write a ratio in the form $1 : n$, divide both parts by the **first** part. For example, $5 : 8 = 1 : \frac{8}{5} = 1 : 1.6$. Here $n$ may be a decimal. For the form $n : 1$, divide by the second part: $5 : 8 = 0.625 : 1$.
:::

## Sharing in a given ratio

To share a quantity in a ratio:

1. Add the parts of the ratio to find the total number of parts.
2. Divide the quantity by the total number of parts to find **one part**.
3. Multiply one part by each number in the ratio.
4. Check that the shares add up to the original quantity.

:::worked-solution[Example 2: share US\$360 between Tendai, Rudo and Farai in the ratio $2 : 3 : 4$]
1. Total parts: $2 + 3 + 4 = 9$.
2. One part: $360 \div 9 = 40$, so one part is worth US\$40.
3. Tendai: $2 \times 40 = 80$; Rudo: $3 \times 40 = 120$; Farai: $4 \times 40 = 160$.
4. Check: $80 + 120 + 160 = 360$. Tendai gets US\$80, Rudo US\$120 and Farai US\$160.
:::

Sometimes you are told one share, or the **difference** between two shares, instead of the total. Work out how many parts that information represents.

:::worked-solution[Example 3: a difference between shares]
Nyasha and Tatenda share some money in the ratio $5 : 3$. Nyasha receives US\$14 more than Tatenda. How much money was shared?

1. The difference between the shares is $5 - 3 = 2$ parts.
2. So 2 parts are worth US\$14 and one part is worth $14 \div 2 = 7$ dollars.
3. The total is $5 + 3 = 8$ parts, worth $8 \times 7 = 56$ dollars.
4. US\$56 was shared (Nyasha US\$35, Tatenda US\$21, and $35 - 21 = 14$).
:::

## Direct proportion and the unitary method

When two quantities are in direct proportion, find the value for one unit, then multiply.

:::worked-solution[Example 4: scaling a recipe]
A recipe for 12 scones uses 300 g of flour. How much flour is needed for 20 scones?

1. Flour for one scone: $300 \div 12 = 25$ g.
2. Flour for 20 scones: $20 \times 25 = 500$ g.
:::

### Best value

To decide which size of a product is the best value, compare the **price per unit** (per kilogram, per litre) or the amount you get per dollar.

:::worked-solution[Example 5: which bottle of cooking oil is the best value?]
A shop sells cooking oil in three sizes: 750 ml for US\$1.65, 2 litres for US\$4.20 and 5 litres for US\$10.25.

1. 750 ml: $1.65 \div 0.75 = 2.20$, so US\$2.20 per litre.
2. 2 litres: $4.20 \div 2 = 2.10$, so US\$2.10 per litre.
3. 5 litres: $10.25 \div 5 = 2.05$, so US\$2.05 per litre.
4. The 5-litre bottle has the lowest price per litre, so it is the best value.
:::

## Inverse proportion

When quantities are in inverse proportion, their product is constant. In work problems this product is the total amount of work, measured for example in **worker-days**.

:::worked-solution[Example 6: workers and time]
Six workers take 15 days to weed a field. How long would 9 workers take, working at the same rate?

1. Total work: $6 \times 15 = 90$ worker-days.
2. With 9 workers: $90 \div 9 = 10$ days.
3. More workers means less time, which makes sense for inverse proportion.
:::

Always ask yourself: "If one quantity doubles, does the other double (direct) or halve (inverse)?"

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing units"}
$40\ \text{cm} : 2\ \text{m}$ is not $20 : 1$. Change to the same unit first: $40 : 200 = 1 : 5$.
:::

:::mistake{title="Dividing by the number of people instead of the number of parts"}
Sharing 96 kg in the ratio $3 : 4 : 5$ does **not** mean dividing by 3 people. There are $3 + 4 + 5 = 12$ parts, so one part is 8 kg and the shares are 24 kg, 32 kg and 40 kg.
:::

:::mistake{title="Treating inverse proportion as direct proportion"}
If 5 people take 8 days to build a wall, 10 people do **not** take 16 days. Doubling the workers halves the time: $5 \times 8 = 40$ worker-days, and $40 \div 10 = 4$ days.
:::

:::mistake{title="Reversing the order"}
If the question asks for "girls to boys", the number of girls comes first. Re-read the question before writing your final ratio.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Give your answer in its simplest form" means whole numbers with no common factor. "In the form $1 : n$" means divide by the first part, and $n$ may be a decimal.
:::

:::exam-tip
In sharing questions, write down the value of **one part** clearly. It usually earns a method mark even if a later step goes wrong.
:::

:::exam-tip
In recipe or ingredient questions, the ingredient that runs out first limits the answer. Check every ingredient, and round the number of items **down** (you cannot make part of a cake for an extra guest), but round the amount you must buy **up**.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A ratio compares quantities in the same units; simplify by dividing every part by the HCF.
- For the form $1 : n$, divide every part by the first part.
- To share in a ratio, add the parts, find one part, then multiply.
- Direct proportion: find the value of one unit, then multiply (recipes, costs, best value per unit).
- Inverse proportion: the product stays constant (workers × days), so more of one means less of the other.
:::
