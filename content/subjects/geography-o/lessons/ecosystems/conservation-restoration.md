## Introduction

Every year, from about August to October, smoke from veld fires hangs over much of Zimbabwe. Fires that start to clear land, smoke out bees or chase mice can sweep across grazing land, plantations and homesteads. On many communal grazing lands, gullies cut deeper each rainy season, and the woodland around growth points shrinks as trees are cut for firewood and building.

These are signs of **degraded ecosystems**. The good news is that degradation can be slowed and even reversed. This lesson covers why local ecosystems are threatened, how they can be **conserved** (protected and used wisely) and how damaged ones can be **restored**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Conservation"}
The careful management and protection of an ecosystem so that it can be used without being destroyed, now and in the future.
:::

:::definition{term="Restoration (rehabilitation)"}
Actions that repair a degraded ecosystem so that it returns as closely as possible to its earlier, healthy state.
:::

:::definition{term="Sustainable ecosystem management"}
Using an ecosystem to meet present needs in a way that does not reduce its ability to meet the needs of future generations.
:::

:::definition{term="Carrying capacity"}
The largest number of animals (or people) an area can support without being damaged.
:::

## Threats to local ecosystems

| Threat | Main causes | Effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Veld fires | Land clearing, hunting, careless smokers, honey collection, cigarette ends from vehicles | Loss of grazing, trees and wildlife; bare soil open to erosion; loss of homes and crops |
| Deforestation | Firewood, charcoal, curing tobacco, clearing for farms and settlements, timber | Loss of habitat and biodiversity; soil erosion; drier streams |
| Overgrazing | Too many livestock on limited communal land; no rotation | Bare ground, loss of palatable grasses, bush encroachment, soil compaction and erosion |
| Soil erosion | Bare soil, cultivation on slopes and stream banks, overgrazing | Gullies, silted rivers and dams, loss of fertile topsoil |

These threats are linked. Cutting trees and overgrazing leave the ground bare; the first heavy storm of the rainy season washes away topsoil and starts gullies; silt then fills dams downstream.

## Conservation measures

**Controlling fire**

- **Fireguards**: strips of land cleared of grass and fuel, usually made early in the dry season, that stop a fire spreading.
- **Early, controlled burning** in some protected areas, when grass is still slightly green, to reduce fuel before the hot late-dry-season fires.
- Awareness campaigns and community fire-fighting teams.

**Protecting trees and woodland**

- **Tree planting** (afforestation and reforestation), including woodlots of fast-growing trees for firewood and planting of indigenous and fruit trees.
- **Controlled cutting**: cutting selected trees and leaving stumps to coppice (regrow).
- **Alternative energy** to reduce the demand for wood: **biogas** digesters fed with cattle dung, **solar** cookers and solar lighting, and fuel-efficient stoves.

**Protecting soil and grazing**

- **Terracing** and **contour ridges** on slopes to slow runoff.
- **Destocking**: reducing livestock numbers to the carrying capacity of the land.
- **Rotational grazing**: dividing grazing land into paddocks and moving animals between them so grass can recover.

**Legislation and institutions**

- The **Environmental Management Act** gives the Environmental Management Agency (EMA) powers to control activities that damage the environment, such as starting veld fires, cultivating stream banks and illegal sand mining.
- Laws protect certain trees and forests, and local authorities and traditional leaders make by-laws on grazing and cutting.

**Indigenous knowledge systems (IKS)**

Many communities have long protected parts of their environment through custom: sacred groves and hills where cutting is forbidden, taboos against cutting certain fruit trees, and rules enforced by chiefs and headmen about when grass and reeds may be harvested. These customs conserve biodiversity because people respect them.

## Restoring degraded ecosystems

:::example
**Reclaiming a gully (an annotated sequence).** A gully 2 m deep has formed where a cattle track runs down a slope.

1. **Stop the cause**: divert the cattle track and runoff away from the head of the gully.
2. **Build check dams**: walls of stones, brushwood or sandbags across the gully floor slow the water so it drops its silt.
3. **Fill and reshape**: as silt builds up behind the check dams, the gully becomes shallower; the sides can be sloped back.
4. **Revegetate**: plant grasses such as vetiver and trees along the gully to bind the soil.
5. **Protect**: fence the area or keep livestock out until the plants are established.
:::

Other restoration methods include reseeding bare grazing land with grass, planting trees on cleared hillsides, and rehabilitating old mine and sand-pit sites by filling and replanting them.

**Benefits of restoration:** soil is held in place, water soaks in so springs and streams recover, grazing and wood return, wildlife comes back and less silt reaches dams.

## Sustainable management

A sustainable approach combines several measures, involves the local community in decisions and gives people a reason to look after the land: for example, a village woodlot that supplies firewood, together with biogas, fireguards and a grazing rota agreed with the headman.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing conservation with preservation"}
Conservation allows careful use of an ecosystem. Preservation means keeping it untouched. Most Zimbabwean examples are conservation.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing measures without explaining them"}
"Fireguards" alone earns little. Explain: "a cleared strip with no fuel, so the fire cannot cross it".
:::

:::mistake{title="Planting trees as the only answer"}
Tree planting fails if the cause of degradation continues. Good answers deal with the cause (fire, overgrazing, fuel demand) as well.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Local ecosystems are threatened by veld fires, deforestation, overgrazing and soil erosion, which reinforce one another.
- Conservation measures include fireguards, tree planting, controlled cutting, alternative energy (biogas, solar), terracing, destocking, rotational grazing, legislation and indigenous knowledge.
- Restoration repairs damage: gully reclamation with check dams, reseeding grass, planting trees and rehabilitating mine sites.
- Sustainable management meets present needs without harming future use, and works best when local people are involved.
:::
