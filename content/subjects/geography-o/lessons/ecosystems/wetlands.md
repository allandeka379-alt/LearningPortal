## Introduction

At the head of many small streams on the Zimbabwean highveld lies a shallow, grassy hollow that stays damp long after the rains have ended. Geographers call it a **vlei** (or a **dambo**, the name used in Zambia and Malawi). A vlei looks like ordinary grassland, but under the grass the soil is soaked like a sponge, and that water slowly feeds the stream through the dry season.

Harare, for example, sits on the high ground of the upper Manyame catchment, and the vleis around the city help feed the rivers and dams that supply its water. Yet wetlands are among the most threatened ecosystems because they look "empty" and easy to plough or build on. This lesson explains what wetlands are, why they matter and how they can be protected.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Wetland"}
An area where the land is covered by shallow water or the soil is saturated (waterlogged) for all or part of the year, with plants and animals adapted to wet conditions.
:::

:::definition{term="Vlei (dambo)"}
A shallow, seasonally waterlogged, grass-covered depression, usually at the head of a stream or along its upper course.
:::

:::definition{term="Ramsar Convention"}
An international treaty on wetlands, signed in 1971 at Ramsar in Iran, under which countries agree to use their wetlands wisely and to list important ones as Wetlands of International Importance (Ramsar sites).
:::

## Types of wetland

| Type | Description | Example setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vlei / dambo | Grassy, seasonally waterlogged hollow; no trees in the wettest part | Highveld headwaters, school and town outskirts |
| Marsh | Shallow water most of the year, dominated by reeds, sedges and grasses | Edges of lakes and dams |
| Swamp | Wetland dominated by trees or tall papyrus, with standing water | Large river deltas and lake margins in Africa |
| Floodplain | Flat land beside a river, flooded in the wet season | The lower Zambezi valley, such as at Mana Pools |

## Why wetlands matter

**Water storage and supply.** Wetlands soak up rain in the wet season and release it slowly, so streams keep flowing in the dry season. Shallow wells dug at the edge of a vlei often have water when others are dry.

**Water purification.** As water moves slowly through dense plants and mud, silt settles out and plants and bacteria take up nutrients and some pollutants. Wetlands act as natural filters before water reaches rivers and dams.

**Flood control.** Like a sponge, a wetland holds back storm water and releases it gradually, reducing flood peaks downstream.

**Grazing and farming.** Vlei grasses stay green into the dry season, providing grazing when upland grass has dried. Many families grow vegetables in small gardens on vlei margins.

**Biodiversity.** Wetlands support frogs, fish, dragonflies, many water birds (herons, egrets, cranes, ducks) and specialised plants such as sedges and reeds.

**Crafts and materials.** Reeds and sedges are used for mats, baskets and thatch; clay is used for bricks and pottery.

**Recreation, education and culture.** Birdwatching, school field trips and, in some places, sacred pools and springs.

:::example
**A vlei as a "diagram in words".** Imagine a cross-section across a highveld vlei. On both sides, gentle slopes of well-drained red soil carry grassland and fields. Towards the centre the soil becomes grey and waterlogged (a gleyed soil), with tall sedges and grasses. A spring seeps out at the lower end and becomes the source of a stream. Rain that falls on the slopes soaks in, moves downslope underground and is stored in the vlei, which releases it slowly to the stream.
:::

## Threats to wetlands

- **Drainage** for farming or building: ditches lower the water table and dry the wetland out.
- **Cultivation** of vlei soils and stream banks: ploughing destroys the vegetation and causes erosion; crops use the stored water.
- **Urban development**: houses, roads, shopping centres and sports facilities built on vleis, especially around growing towns.
- **Pollution**: sewage, fertiliser runoff, industrial waste and solid waste dumped in wetlands; this can cause eutrophication.
- **Overgrazing and burning**: trampling and fire remove the cover and compact the soil.
- **Brick moulding and sand or clay extraction**: leave pits and scars.
- **Over-abstraction** of water from wells and boreholes.

When a wetland is destroyed, streams dry up earlier in the year, floods become more sudden, water quality falls and wildlife is lost.

## Protecting wetlands

- **International agreement:** Zimbabwe is a party to the Ramsar Convention. Its listed Wetlands of International Importance include sites such as Monavale Vlei in Harare and Mana Pools on the Zambezi.
- **National law:** under the Environmental Management Act, activities that may harm wetlands, such as building or draining, need approval from the Environmental Management Agency (EMA), often after an environmental impact assessment.
- **Land-use planning:** councils can zone wetlands as open space where building is not allowed.
- **Community action:** local groups monitor vleis, remove litter, run awareness days and replant indigenous wetland plants.
- **Wise use:** allowing controlled grazing, reed harvesting and small gardens on the edges only, while keeping the core wet and vegetated.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking a wetland must have open water"}
A vlei can look like dry grassland for much of the year. It is a wetland because its soil is saturated, not because there is a lake.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying wetlands \"waste\" land"}
Wetlands provide water, flood control and grazing that people downstream depend on. Converting them often costs more than it gains.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing a dam with a natural wetland"}
A reservoir is a human-made water body. Its marshy edges may be wetland, but the dam itself is not a natural wetland.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC questions often say **explain the importance of wetlands**. Develop each point with "so that" or "which means": "wetlands store water in the rainy season, which means streams keep flowing in the dry season".
:::

:::exam-tip
For **threats**, name the human activity, then the effect on the wetland, then the effect downstream.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Wetlands have saturated soil or shallow water for all or part of the year; types include vleis (dambos), marshes, swamps and floodplains.
- They store and purify water, reduce floods, provide grazing, gardens, crafts materials and habitats for many species.
- Threats include drainage, cultivation, building, pollution, overgrazing, burning and brick moulding.
- Protection comes from the Ramsar Convention, the Environmental Management Act, planning controls, community action and wise use.
:::
