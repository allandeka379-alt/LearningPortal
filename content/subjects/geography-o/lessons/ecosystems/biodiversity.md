## Introduction

In the dry season, families in many parts of Zimbabwe collect wild fruits such as mazhanje, harvest mopane worms from mopane woodland, cut thatching grass and gather firewood. Bees in the miombo woodland produce honey that sells in town markets. All of these come from the variety of living things around us: **biodiversity**.

Biodiversity is not only about rare animals in national parks. It includes the grasses on the school field, the insects in a vlei and the different varieties of sorghum kept by grandparents. This lesson explains the types of biodiversity, what ecosystems give us, and how people can earn a living from ecosystems without destroying them.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Biodiversity"}
The variety of life in an area: the number and variety of species, the genetic variety within species and the variety of habitats and ecosystems.
:::

:::definition{term="Species diversity"}
The number of different species in an area (species richness) and how evenly the individuals are spread among them.
:::

:::definition{term="Genetic diversity"}
The variety of genes within one species, for example the different local varieties of sorghum, millet or indigenous chickens.
:::

:::definition{term="Habitat (ecosystem) diversity"}
The variety of habitats in an area, such as woodland, grassland, vlei, river bank and rocky kopje.
:::

:::definition{term="Ecosystem services"}
The benefits people obtain from ecosystems, from products (food, timber, medicine) to processes (clean water, oxygen, soil protection, pollination).
:::

## Habitats in a local ecosystem

Think of the area around a rural secondary school. Within a short walk you may find:

| Habitat | Typical plants and animals |
| --- | --- |
| Miombo woodland | Msasa and munondo trees, bees, monkeys, many birds |
| Grassland and veld | Grasses, grasshoppers, hares, guinea fowl |
| Vlei (wetland) | Sedges, frogs, egrets, dragonflies |
| Rocky kopje | Rock figs, aloes, dassies (rock hyraxes), lizards |
| River bank (riparian strip) | Tall riverine trees, fish, kingfishers |

The more habitats an area has, the more species it can support. Removing one habitat, for example by draining the vlei, removes the species that depend on it.

## Why ecosystems matter: goods and services

Ecosystems provide **goods** that people use directly:

- **Timber and poles** for building, furniture and fencing.
- **Fuelwood** for cooking; most rural households still depend on it.
- **Food**: wild fruits, mushrooms, mopane worms, fish, honey, game meat.
- **Medicines** from roots, bark and leaves used in traditional healing; some modern medicines also come from plants.
- **Materials for crafts**: grass, reeds and palm leaves for baskets and mats; wood for carvings.

They also provide **services** that are easy to overlook:

- **Oxygen** from photosynthesis and storage of carbon (forests as **carbon sinks**).
- **Soil protection**: roots bind soil and leaf litter shields it from raindrops.
- **Water**: vegetation slows runoff, helps rain soak in and keeps streams flowing; transpiration returns moisture to the air for **rainfall**.
- **Pollination** of crops by bees and other insects.
- **Cultural and spiritual value**: sacred groves, burial sites and places for recreation.

## Using ecosystems for small businesses

The Zimbabwean syllabus asks you to identify and plan small businesses that use local ecosystems **sustainably**, meaning the resource is not used up faster than it can recover.

:::example
**Planning a beekeeping project (a worked plan).** A youth club near a miombo woodland wants an income.

1. **Resource:** flowering msasa and other trees give nectar; bees are free.
2. **Inputs:** hives (made from local timber or old drums), protective clothing, a smoker, containers.
3. **Products:** honey, beeswax for candles and polish.
4. **Market:** local shops, growth points and roadside stalls.
5. **Sustainability:** the trees must stay standing for the bees, so the club has a reason to protect the woodland from cutting and late-season fires; bees also pollinate nearby crops.
:::

Other sustainable businesses include:

- **Eco-tourism**: guided walks, birdwatching, cultural village stays, charging small fees that pay local guides.
- **Crafts**: weaving baskets and mats from grass and reeds, harvested in season so the plants regrow.
- **Harvesting and processing wild foods**: drying mopane worms or making juice and jam from wild fruits, leaving enough for the population to recover.
- **Tree nurseries**: growing indigenous and fruit tree seedlings for sale.

Each business is sustainable only if harvesting is controlled: for example, cutting only some reeds each year, never stripping the bark right round a tree, and taking only mature mopane worms.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Biodiversity means the number of animals"}
Biodiversity is about **variety**, not numbers. A field with 10,000 maize plants has low biodiversity; a vlei with fewer plants but many species has high biodiversity.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing only goods"}
When asked for the benefits of ecosystems, students often list only timber and food. Include services such as soil protection, water supply, pollination and carbon storage.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling any forest business sustainable"}
A charcoal business that clears the woodland is not sustainable. Explain **how** the business keeps the resource from running out.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Paper 2 questions on this topic often say **outline** or **explain** the benefits of ecosystems. Give a benefit and develop it: "roots bind the soil, which reduces soil erosion on slopes".
:::

:::exam-tip
For a business-planning question, cover resource, inputs, product, market and how the business stays sustainable. A short numbered plan earns marks quickly.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Biodiversity is the variety of life: species diversity, genetic diversity and habitat diversity.
- A local area contains several habitats (woodland, grassland, vlei, kopje, river bank), each with its own species.
- Ecosystems provide goods (timber, fuel, food, medicine, craft materials) and services (oxygen, carbon storage, soil protection, water, pollination, cultural value).
- Beekeeping, eco-tourism, crafts, wild-food processing and nurseries can earn income sustainably if harvesting is controlled.
:::
