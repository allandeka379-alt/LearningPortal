## Introduction

Downstream of Kariba, the Zambezi spreads across a wide, flat valley floor. At **Mana Pools National Park** the river winds between sandbanks and islands, and the floodplain is dotted with pools left behind as the river shifted its course. Elephants and buffalo graze the floodplain grass, and fertile alluvial soils support tall trees. These are the landforms of a river's **middle and lower course**, shaped by **lateral erosion** and, above all, **deposition**.

You need to know why rivers deposit (loss of velocity or discharge) and the difference between vertical and lateral erosion from the processes lesson.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Meander"}
A pronounced bend in a river, with a steep river cliff on the outside and a gentle slip-off slope on the inside.
:::

:::definition{term="Oxbow lake"}
A horseshoe-shaped lake formed when a meander is cut off from the main channel.
:::

:::definition{term="Floodplain"}
The wide, flat area of land on either side of a river in its lower course that is covered with water when the river floods; it is built of layers of alluvium.
:::

:::definition{term="Levée"}
A natural raised bank alongside a river channel, built up by deposition during floods.
:::

:::definition{term="Delta"}
An area of deposited sediment at the mouth of a river where it enters the sea or a lake, often crossed by many channels called distributaries.
:::

:::definition{term="Braided channel"}
A river channel split into several smaller channels separated by bars or islands of sediment.
:::

## Meanders: river cliffs and slip-off slopes

**Formation in words** (draw a plan view and a cross-section of a bend):

1. As a river flows, small bends develop. Water flows fastest and deepest on the **outside** of a bend (the thalweg, the line of fastest flow, swings towards the outer bank).
2. On the outside, fast water causes **lateral erosion** by hydraulic action and abrasion. The bank is undercut and collapses, forming a steep **river cliff**.
3. On the **inside** of the bend, water is shallow and slow, so the river **deposits** sand and gravel, building a gentle **slip-off slope** (point bar).
4. A corkscrew-like flow (helicoidal flow) carries eroded material from the outer bank across the bed to the inner bank of the next bend.
5. Erosion on the outside and deposition on the inside make the bend more pronounced, and the meander gradually migrates sideways and downstream.

**Cross-section across a meander**: steep river cliff and deep water on one side; shallow water and a gently sloping slip-off slope on the other. The channel is **asymmetrical**.

:::example{title="Measuring how winding a river is (fictional data)"}
On a map, a river channel between two bridges measures 9.0 km along its course, but the straight-line distance between the bridges is 5.0 km.

1. Sinuosity = channel length ÷ straight-line distance.
2. 9.0 ÷ 5.0 = 1.8.
3. A value above about 1.5 is usually described as meandering; a straight channel has a value close to 1.
:::

## Oxbow lakes

**Formation in words** (draw four plan views):

1. Erosion on the outer banks of two neighbouring bends brings them closer together, so the **neck** of land between them becomes narrow.
2. During a flood, the river has extra energy and cuts through the narrow neck, taking the shortest route.
3. The river now flows along the new, straighter channel. Deposition along the edges of the new channel **seals off** the old bend.
4. The abandoned bend becomes a horseshoe-shaped **oxbow lake**. Over time it fills with silt and vegetation and may become a marsh.

:::example{title="Case study: Mana Pools on the middle Zambezi"}
Mana Pools lies on the Zambezi floodplain below the escarpment in northern Zimbabwe. The name comes from Shona *mana* (four), after four large permanent pools that are remnants of old river channels left as the Zambezi shifted its course across the floodplain. The park shows sandbanks and islands in a wide channel, a flat floodplain of alluvium, and channels abandoned by the river. It is a national park and part of a World Heritage Site, valued for its wildlife.
:::

## Floodplains and levées

**Formation in words** (draw a cross-section of the valley):

1. Meanders migrate sideways and downstream, eroding the valley sides and widening the valley floor.
2. When the river floods, water spreads over the valley floor. It becomes shallow and slows down because of friction, so it deposits **alluvium** (fine silt and sand). Layer upon layer builds a flat **floodplain**.
3. During a flood, the **heaviest, coarsest** material is dropped first, right beside the channel, where the water first loses velocity. Finer silt is carried further across the floodplain.
4. After many floods the coarse material beside the channel builds up into raised banks: **levées**.
5. Between floods, the river deposits on its own bed, so the bed may also rise. In places the river can flow above the level of the surrounding floodplain.

Floodplains are valuable: their alluvial soils are fertile and flat land is easy to farm and build on, but they are also the land most at risk of flooding.

## Braided channels

Where a river carries a very large load and its discharge varies greatly between seasons, it may not be able to carry all its sediment when flow drops. It deposits **bars** of sand and gravel in the channel, and the water divides around them into many smaller, interlinking channels. When the next flood comes the bars may be moved and the pattern changes. Braiding is common in rivers fed by meltwater, such as the Brahmaputra, and in the broad sandy beds of seasonal rivers in dry regions, including parts of southern Zimbabwe.

## Deltas

A delta forms when a river carries more sediment to its mouth than waves, tides and currents can remove.

**Formation in words**:

1. The river enters the still water of a sea or lake, its velocity falls sharply and it deposits its load.
2. Fresh water meeting salt water makes clay particles clump together (flocculation) and sink faster.
3. Deposits build up and the channel splits into smaller **distributaries** that spread sediment over a wide area.
4. The delta grows outwards over time as new layers are added.

| Delta type | Shape | Why | Named example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Arcuate** | Fan-shaped with a curved front | Moderate waves and currents smooth the outer edge | Nile (Egypt) |
| **Bird's-foot** | Long distributaries extending into the sea like a bird's toes | Very large load, weak waves and tides | Mississippi (USA) |
| **Estuarine** | Sediment fills the estuary, often long and narrow | Deposition inside a drowned river mouth, shaped by tides | Many tidal estuaries |

The Zambezi ends in a large delta on the coast of Mozambique. Since large dams such as Kariba and Cahora Bassa were built, much sediment is trapped upstream and the delta receives less.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Putting the river cliff on the inside of a bend"}
The **river cliff** is on the **outside**, where flow is fastest and erosion happens. The **slip-off slope** is on the **inside**, where deposition happens.
:::

:::mistake{title="Saying levées form from fine silt"}
Levées are built from the **coarsest** material, dropped first beside the channel when floodwater loses velocity. Fine silt is carried further across the floodplain.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the flood in oxbow-lake formation"}
The neck is usually breached **during a flood**, when the river has extra energy. Mention it.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Meanders: lateral erosion on the outside forms a river cliff; deposition on the inside forms a slip-off slope; the channel is asymmetrical.
- Oxbow lakes: the neck between two bends narrows, is cut through in a flood and the old bend is sealed off by deposition.
- Floodplains are built by layers of alluvium deposited in floods; levées form from coarse material dropped beside the channel.
- Braided channels form where a large load is deposited as bars when flow drops.
- Deltas form where deposition at the mouth exceeds removal; types are arcuate (Nile), bird's-foot (Mississippi) and estuarine.
:::
