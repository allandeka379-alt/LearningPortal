## Introduction

A student in Gweru checks a phone designed in the USA and assembled in China, watches a football match played in England, and eats rice grown in Asia. A message to a cousin in South Africa arrives in a second, and a parcel ordered online crosses several borders in a week. These everyday events are signs of **globalisation**: the world's countries, economies and people are becoming more connected and more dependent on each other.

This lesson explains what globalisation is, what has caused it, how it affects trade, transport, culture, communications and technology, and why its effects can be good or bad.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Globalisation"}
The process by which the world's economies, cultures and people become increasingly connected and interdependent through trade, investment, communications, transport and the movement of people and ideas.
:::

:::definition{term="Interdependence"}
When countries rely on each other for goods, services, money, workers and markets, so that events in one country affect others.
:::

:::definition{term="Trade liberalisation"}
The removal or reduction of barriers to trade, such as tariffs (import taxes) and quotas.
:::

:::definition{term="Transnational corporation (TNC)"}
A company that operates in more than one country, often with headquarters in one country and production or sales in many others.
:::

## Key features of globalisation

1. **Growth in world trade:** goods and parts cross many borders; a single product may contain components from several continents.
2. **Global production networks:** TNCs design in one country, make parts in others and assemble elsewhere.
3. **Flows of money:** investment, loans and remittances move around the world in seconds.
4. **Global communications:** the internet, mobile phones and satellite television connect people instantly.
5. **Movement of people:** migration for work and study, and international tourism.
6. **Global culture:** the same brands, music, films, sport and fast food are found in many countries.

## Causes of globalisation

### Transport

- **Containerisation:** standard steel containers transfer quickly between ships, trains and lorries, cutting the cost and time of moving goods.
- **Larger ships and aircraft** carry more at a lower cost per unit; jet travel makes business trips and tourism faster.

### Communications and technology

- The **internet**, email, video calls and mobile phones allow firms to manage factories and offices around the world from one headquarters.
- **Satellites and undersea fibre-optic cables** carry data cheaply.
- Online shopping and mobile money let even small businesses reach distant customers.

### Trade liberalisation and politics

- The **World Trade Organization** (set up in 1995) works to reduce barriers to trade between its member countries.
- **Trading blocs** such as the EU, SADC and the African Continental Free Trade Area remove barriers between members.
- Many governments have opened their economies to foreign investment, for example by creating special economic zones.

### Transnational corporations

TNCs search the world for cheap labour, raw materials and new markets, spreading production and their brands to many countries.

## Impacts of globalisation

| Aspect | Positive impacts | Negative impacts |
|---|---|---|
| Trade | More exports and jobs in countries that attract investment; cheaper and wider choice of goods for consumers | Local firms closed by cheap imports; LICs may depend on low-value raw material exports and unstable prices |
| Transport | Cheaper, faster movement of goods and people; growth of ports and airports | More fuel use and greenhouse gas emissions; congestion at ports and borders |
| Culture | Exchange of ideas, music, food and languages; greater awareness of other peoples | Spread of a few global brands and languages can weaken local cultures and traditions |
| Communications | Instant contact; information, education and banking reach remote areas | A **digital divide** between connected and unconnected people and regions; misinformation spreads quickly |
| Technology | New technologies spread to LICs and MICs; mobile phones allow banking without branches | Jobs replaced by automation; dependence on technology controlled by a few large firms |

:::example{title="Detailed specific example: China and globalisation"}
1. **Opening up:** from 1980 China created **special economic zones**, such as Shenzhen next to Hong Kong, offering foreign companies low taxes, cheap land and a large, low-cost workforce.
2. **Growth:** Shenzhen grew from a small town into a city of more than ten million people (approximate). Many TNCs set up factories or used Chinese contract manufacturers to make electronics, clothing and toys.
3. **Trade:** China joined the World Trade Organization in 2001 and became the largest exporter of manufactured goods in the world. Container ports on its coast are among the busiest anywhere.
4. **Benefits:** hundreds of millions of people moved out of extreme poverty; incomes, infrastructure and skills improved; Chinese firms are now TNCs themselves.
5. **Problems:** long hours and low pay in some factories, especially in the early years; severe air and water pollution in industrial cities; rural-urban migration separated families; widening inequality between the rich coastal cities and the poorer interior.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Defining globalisation as 'the world becoming smaller'"}
The world is not smaller. Say that **places become more connected and interdependent** through trade, communications, transport and investment.
:::

:::mistake{title="Only giving economic impacts"}
Cambridge asks about impacts on trade, transport, **culture**, **communications** and **technology**. Cover the range, and give positive and negative effects.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 2 (topic 9)"}
"Explain what is meant by globalisation" usually needs two linked ideas: increasing connections **and** interdependence, with an example. For "describe the impacts", use one positive and one negative per aspect and name a place. In the extended answer, a detailed specific example (such as China or Shenzhen) with real detail earns the top level.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Globalisation is the growing connection and interdependence of the world's economies, cultures and people.
- Features: growth of trade, global production networks, flows of money, global communications, migration and global culture.
- Causes: containerisation and cheaper transport, the internet and mobile phones, trade liberalisation (WTO, trading blocs) and TNCs.
- Impacts on trade, transport, culture, communications and technology are both positive and negative.
- China (Shenzhen special economic zone, WTO membership in 2001) shows rapid growth with social and environmental costs.
:::
