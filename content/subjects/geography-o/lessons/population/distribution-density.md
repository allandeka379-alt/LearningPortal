## Introduction

Fly from Harare to Victoria Falls and the view below changes from crowded suburbs, farms and villages to long stretches of bush with hardly a roof in sight. People are spread very unevenly across Zimbabwe, Africa and the world. Understanding why some places are crowded and others almost empty helps governments plan services, roads and development.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Population distribution"}
The way people are spread out over an area: where they live and where they do not.
:::

:::definition{term="Population density"}
The average number of people per square kilometre in an area.
:::

:::definition{term="Densely populated"}
An area with many people per square kilometre.
:::

:::definition{term="Sparsely populated"}
An area with few people per square kilometre.
:::

## Calculating population density

**Population density = total population ÷ area (km²)**, in people per km².

:::example{title="Worked examples"}
1. **Zimbabwe (approximate values):** about 15.2 million people (2022 census) ÷ about 390,000 km² ≈ **39 people per km²**.
2. **Fictional district:** 186,000 people on 4,650 km² → 186,000 ÷ 4,650 = **40 people per km²**.
3. **Working backwards:** a fictional ward of 120 km² has a density of 55 people per km², so its population = 55 × 120 = **6,600 people**.
:::

Density is an average: a district with 40 people per km² may contain a crowded town and a nearly empty game reserve.

## Factors affecting population distribution and density

| | Dense population | Sparse population |
|---|---|---|
| **Climate** | Moderate temperatures; reliable rainfall for farming | Very dry (deserts), very cold (polar), very hot and humid with disease |
| **Relief** | Lowlands, gentle plateaus, river valleys and deltas | High, steep mountains; rugged terrain |
| **Soils** | Fertile soils (alluvial, volcanic) | Thin, infertile, leached or saline soils |
| **Water** | Rivers, lakes, groundwater | Shortage of surface and groundwater |
| **Vegetation and disease** | Cleared land; few disease vectors | Dense forest; tsetse fly, malaria |
| **Resources** | Minerals, energy sources | Few resources |
| **Economic** | Industry, jobs, ports, trade, commercial farming | Few jobs; subsistence farming only |
| **Transport and services** | Good roads, railways, schools, hospitals | Remote, poor communications |
| **Political and historical** | Capital cities; past land allocation policies | Protected areas (national parks); past forced removals |

## Population distribution in Zimbabwe

**Densely populated areas:**

1. **Harare and Bulawayo** and other towns (Chitungwiza, Mutare, Gweru, Kwekwe, Kadoma, Masvingo), with jobs in industry, commerce and government.
2. The **highveld** (Mashonaland and parts of the Midlands): reliable rainfall, fertile soils, a moderate climate and good roads and railways.
3. The **Eastern Highlands**: high rainfall, fertile soils and plantation agriculture (tea, coffee, timber).
4. Many **communal areas**, where colonial land policy concentrated African farmers on limited land, leading to high rural densities.
5. **Mining towns** along the Great Dyke and elsewhere, and **irrigated estates** in the Lowveld (sugar at Chiredzi and Triangle).

**Sparsely populated areas:**

1. The **Zambezi valley** and the **Lowveld** (natural regions IV and V): hot, low and unreliable rainfall; tsetse fly in the past; poor soils.
2. **National parks** such as Hwange and Gonarezhou, where settlement is restricted.
3. **Large-scale commercial farms and ranches**, with few workers per km².
4. Rugged mountain areas.

## Population distribution in Africa

- **Dense:** the **Nile valley and delta** (water and fertile alluvium in a desert), the **West African coast** (especially southern Nigeria), the **Great Lakes** region (Rwanda, Burundi, Uganda: fertile volcanic soils, reliable rain), the **Ethiopian highlands**, and the coasts and highveld of South Africa.
- **Sparse:** the **Sahara**, **Kalahari** and **Namib** deserts (no water), the dense **Congo rainforest** (difficult to clear, disease), and swamps.

## Population distribution in the world

- **Dense:** **South Asia** (Ganges plain, Bangladesh), **East Asia** (eastern China, Japan, Java in Indonesia), **Western Europe**, and the **north-eastern USA**. These have fertile lowlands, river valleys, ports and industry.
- **Sparse:** **deserts** (Sahara, Arabian, central Australia), **polar and cold lands** (Antarctica, northern Canada, Siberia), **high mountains** (Himalayas, Andes) and **tropical rainforests** (Amazon).

## Reading population distribution maps

1. **Dot maps:** each dot represents a set number of people (for example 1 dot = 1,000 people). Clusters of dots show dense areas; blank areas show sparse areas.
2. **Choropleth maps:** areas are shaded by density category, darker shading for higher density. They show averages for each area, hiding variation within it.
3. When you describe a distribution, **name places**, give **directions**, **quote values** from the key, and note **anomalies** (for example a dense cluster in a dry area may be an irrigation scheme or a mine).

:::example{title="Describing a fictional choropleth map"}
Key: 0-10, 11-50, 51-100 and over 100 people per km². The densest shading (over 100) is in the north-east around the capital and along the railway to the south-west. Most of the south and west is shaded 0-10, apart from one district of 51-100 around an irrigation scheme beside the river. So population is concentrated in the higher-rainfall north-east and along transport routes, with an exception where irrigation provides water.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing distribution and density"}
Distribution is about **where** people live (the pattern). Density is **how many** per km² (a number). "Population is concentrated in the north-east" is distribution; "45 people per km²" is density.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the units"}
Always write "people per km²". A density of 40 means nothing without units.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="ZIMSEC Paper 1 and Paper 2 Section C"}
Density calculations are common: write the formula, substitute and give units. When asked to "explain the differences in population density", give both physical and human factors and name places in Zimbabwe (for example the highveld versus the Zambezi valley).
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Distribution is where people live; density = population ÷ area, in people per km² (Zimbabwe about 39 per km², approximate).
- Dense areas have favourable climate, relief, soils, water, resources, jobs and transport; sparse areas are dry, cold, mountainous, forested, disease-ridden or remote.
- In Zimbabwe, towns, the highveld, the Eastern Highlands and many communal areas are dense; the Zambezi valley, the Lowveld outside irrigated estates and national parks are sparse.
- In Africa, the Nile valley, West African coast and Great Lakes are dense; deserts and the Congo rainforest are sparse. Worldwide, South and East Asia, Western Europe and the north-eastern USA are dense.
- Dot maps show distribution; choropleth maps show average density by area.
:::
