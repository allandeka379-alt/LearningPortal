## Introduction

Walk into a rural primary school in Zimbabwe and the classrooms are full; walk into a town in Japan and you may find schools closed and care homes expanding. The difference is **population structure**: the number of people of each age and sex. It tells governments whether they need more classrooms or more pensions, more maternity wards or more care for the elderly.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Population structure"}
The make-up of a population by age group and sex.
:::

:::definition{term="Population pyramid"}
A horizontal bar chart showing the percentage (or number) of males and females in each age group, with males on the left, females on the right and the youngest at the bottom.
:::

:::definition{term="Youthful population"}
A population with a high proportion of children (under 15), giving a pyramid with a wide base.
:::

:::definition{term="Ageing population"}
A population in which the proportion of elderly people (65 and over) is rising, giving a pyramid with a wide top.
:::

:::definition{term="Dependency ratio"}
The number of dependants (aged 0-14 and 65+) for every 100 people of working age (15-64).
:::

## Reading a population pyramid

When you describe a pyramid, comment on:

![Two population pyramids on the same scale: a youthful pyramid (Zimbabwe-type) with a very wide base, about 40% aged under 15 and only about 3% aged 65 and over, and an ageing pyramid (Germany-type) with a narrow base, a bulge in middle age and about 21% aged 65 and over.](media:diagrams/population-pyramids-youthful-ageing.png "Figure: a youthful and an ageing population pyramid (illustrative shapes)")

1. **The base:** wide = high birth rate; narrow = low birth rate.
2. **The sides:** a steep, concave slope = high death rates at all ages; straight sides = low death rates until old age.
3. **The top:** narrow = short life expectancy; wide = long life expectancy.
4. **Bulges and indentations:** a bulge may show a baby boom or in-migration; a notch may show a war, epidemic, famine, low births in a particular period or out-migration.
5. **Differences between males and females:** for example more young adult males in a mining town (in-migration of male workers) or more elderly women (women live longer).

:::example{title="Comparing two pyramids in words"}
**Zimbabwe (LIC/MIC pattern):** a wide base with roughly two-fifths of people under 15 (approximate), sides that narrow steadily, and a very narrow top with only a few per cent aged 65 and over. This shows high birth rates, fairly high death rates and a shorter life expectancy. In some age groups of young adults, the bars are narrower than expected because of emigration and, in the past, deaths from HIV and AIDS.

**Germany (HIC pattern):** a narrow base, a bulge in the middle-aged groups (people born in the 1950s and 1960s), and a wide top, with around a fifth of people aged 65 and over (approximate). This shows low birth rates, low death rates and long life expectancy. More women than men in the oldest groups.
:::

## Constructing a pyramid

1. Draw a central vertical axis with age groups (0-4, 5-9 … 85+) up the middle.
2. Draw a horizontal scale of percentages in both directions from the centre, the same on both sides.
3. Plot males to the left and females to the right as bars of equal height for each age group.
4. Add a title, the source, the year, and label "Males" and "Females".

## Calculating the dependency ratio

:::example{title="Worked example (fictional data)"}
A fictional country has 6.2 million people aged 0-14, 7.6 million aged 15-64 and 0.4 million aged 65 and over.

1. Dependants = 6.2 + 0.4 = 6.6 million
2. Dependency ratio = 6.6 ÷ 7.6 × 100 = **86.8**

So there are about 87 dependants for every 100 people of working age. Most dependants are young, so this is a **youthful** dependency.
:::

The ratio is only a guide: many 15-19-year-olds are still in school, many over 65s keep working, and in LICs many children work.

## How natural increase and migration shape structure

- **High natural increase** (many births, falling deaths) widens the base: a youthful pyramid.
- **Low or negative natural increase** narrows the base and, with long life expectancy, widens the top: an ageing pyramid.
- **Net in-migration** of young workers widens the 20-39 bars, often more for males (for example mining towns or Gulf states).
- **Net out-migration** of young adults narrows the 20-39 bars in the area they leave, leaving children and elderly behind (common in rural Zimbabwe).

## Youthful populations

**Causes:** high birth rates (children as farm labour and security in old age, limited access to family planning, early marriage, cultural and religious values, high infant mortality encouraging more births) and falling death rates.

| Impacts: challenges | Impacts: opportunities |
|---|---|
| Pressure on schools, teachers and child health services | A large future workforce (a possible "demographic dividend") |
| Need for many new jobs when children grow up; youth unemployment | A large market for goods |
| Pressure on food, housing, water and land | Young, adaptable workers who may innovate |
| High dependency ratio | |

## Ageing populations

**Causes:** low birth rates (family planning, women's education and careers, cost of raising children, later marriage) and long life expectancy (better health care, diet and living conditions).

| Impacts: challenges | Impacts: opportunities |
|---|---|
| Rising cost of pensions, health and care services | Elderly people provide childcare and volunteer work |
| Shrinking workforce and fewer taxpayers | Older people with savings spend on leisure and services ("grey pound") |
| Schools and maternity units close | Jobs in health care and care homes |
| Rural depopulation as the young move away | |

:::example{title="Detailed specific example: Japan's ageing population"}
1. **Structure:** Japan has one of the oldest populations in the world: close to 30% of people are aged 65 and over (approximate), and the population has been falling since about 2010.
2. **Causes:** a total fertility rate well below replacement (about 1.3, approximate), later marriage, high costs of housing and education, long working hours; very high life expectancy (over 80 years) due to good health care and diet.
3. **Impacts:** high spending on pensions and health care; labour shortages in care, farming and construction; closing schools and shrinking rural villages.
4. **Responses:** raising the retirement and pension age; encouraging more women and older people to work; more childcare places and payments to parents; allowing more foreign workers in some sectors; investment in robots and automation for care and industry.
5. **Evaluation:** these measures have increased the workforce, but the birth rate has stayed low, so the population is still ageing and shrinking.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Describing without data"}
"The base is wide" earns less than "about 15% of the population is aged 0-4". Quote the scale.
:::

:::mistake{title="Reading the top as the death rate"}
The top shows life expectancy and the number of elderly; death rates are shown by how quickly the bars narrow with age.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="ZIMSEC Paper 2 Section C"}
"Compare the population pyramids of Zimbabwe and Germany" needs comparative statements for the base, sides and top, with figures, then reasons.
:::

:::exam-tip{title="Cambridge Paper 2 (topic 6)"}
"Evaluate the impacts of an ageing population" needs both problems and opportunities and a judgement. A detailed specific example such as Japan strengthens the answer.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A pyramid shows age-sex structure: base (births), sides (deaths), top (life expectancy), bulges and notches (booms, migration, wars, epidemics).
- Dependency ratio = (0-14 + 65+) ÷ (15-64) × 100.
- Natural increase and net migration shape structure; out-migration of young adults narrows the 20-39 bars.
- Youthful populations (e.g. Zimbabwe) need schools and jobs but offer a future workforce; ageing populations (e.g. Germany, Japan) face pension and care costs and labour shortages.
- Japan has responded with later retirement, more women and older workers, childcare support, some immigration and automation.
:::
