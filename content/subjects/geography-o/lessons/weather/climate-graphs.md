## Introduction

A single day's weather tells you little about a place. To understand its **climate** you need records kept month after month, then summarised as averages. Climate statistics decide which crops a farmer in Hwange district can grow, when a tour operator at Victoria Falls expects visitors, and how big a dam must be to last through the dry season.

Geographers summarise climate in two ways: a **table of monthly figures** and a **climate graph**. Both appear in almost every exam, ZIMSEC and Cambridge alike. In this lesson you will calculate the key statistics, learn how a climate graph is built, and practise describing what one shows. You should already know how temperature and rainfall are measured.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Mean monthly temperature"}
The average temperature for a month: the mean of all the daily mean temperatures in that month. On climate tables it is usually an average over many years.
:::

:::definition{term="Annual temperature range"}
The difference between the mean temperature of the hottest month and that of the coolest month.
:::

:::definition{term="Total annual rainfall"}
The sum of the twelve monthly rainfall figures, in millimetres.
:::

:::definition{term="Climate graph"}
A graph that shows mean monthly temperature as a line (or points joined by a curve) and mean monthly rainfall as bars, on the same set of months.
:::

## Calculating climate statistics

:::keyfact{title="The formulae"}
- Mean daily temperature = (maximum + minimum) ÷ 2
- Mean monthly temperature = sum of daily means ÷ number of days
- Mean annual temperature = sum of the 12 monthly means ÷ 12
- Annual range = hottest month − coolest month
- Total annual rainfall = sum of the 12 monthly totals (never divide by 12 unless you are asked for the *mean monthly* rainfall)
:::

The table below is for a **fictional** station on the plateau of southern Africa.

| Month | J | F | M | A | M | J | J | A | S | O | N | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature (°C) | 21 | 21 | 20 | 18 | 15 | 13 | 13 | 15 | 19 | 21 | 22 | 21 |
| Rainfall (mm) | 190 | 170 | 90 | 30 | 10 | 2 | 0 | 1 | 8 | 30 | 95 | 180 |

:::example{title="Statistics for the fictional plateau station"}
1. **Hottest month**: November, 22 °C. **Coolest months**: June and July, 13 °C.
2. **Annual range** = 22 − 13 = 9 °C.
3. **Mean annual temperature** = (21 + 21 + 20 + 18 + 15 + 13 + 13 + 15 + 19 + 21 + 22 + 21) ÷ 12 = 219 ÷ 12 = 18.25 °C.
4. **Total annual rainfall** = 190 + 170 + 90 + 30 + 10 + 2 + 0 + 1 + 8 + 30 + 95 + 180 = 806 mm.
5. **Wettest month**: January, 190 mm. **Rainfall from November to March** = 95 + 180 + 190 + 170 + 90 = 725 mm, which is 725 ÷ 806 × 100 ≈ 90 % of the year's total.
:::

## Drawing a climate graph

Imagine a sheet of graph paper with the twelve months, J to D, spaced evenly along the bottom.

1. Draw **two vertical axes**: rainfall (mm) on one side and temperature (°C) on the other. Choose scales that fit the largest values and label both axes with units.
2. Draw the **rainfall as bars**, one for each month, rising from the bottom axis. They are usually shaded blue.
3. Plot the **temperature** for each month as a point in the **middle** of the month's column, then join the points with a smooth line (usually red).
4. Give the graph a **title** with the station's name (and its latitude, longitude and altitude if known).

On the fictional station's graph you would see tall bars from November to March, almost no bars from May to September, and a gentle temperature curve that dips in June and July.

## Describing a climate graph

When an exam asks you to **describe** the climate, use the data and quote figures with units. A useful checklist:

| Look at | What to say |
| --- | --- |
| Temperature | Hottest month and its value; coolest month and its value; the annual range |
| Rainfall | Total; the wet season months and the wettest month with its value; the dry months |
| Pattern | Whether rain falls all year or in one season, and whether that season is in the hot or cool months |

:::example{title="A description of the plateau station"}
"Temperatures are warm all year, from 13 °C in June and July to 22 °C in November, an annual range of 9 °C. The total rainfall is 806 mm, falling mainly from November to March (about 90 % of the total), with the wettest month January at 190 mm. May to September is almost dry, with 0 mm in July. The rain falls in the hot season."
:::

## Interpreting what the graph tells you

Climate graphs also let you **deduce** things about a place:

- **Hemisphere**: if the hottest months are December to February, the station is in the **southern** hemisphere; if they are June to August, it is in the **northern** hemisphere.
- **Distance from the equator**: a very small annual range (1-3 °C) with high temperatures all year suggests a place near the equator; a large range suggests higher latitudes or a continental interior.
- **Altitude**: a station in the tropics with moderate temperatures (for example a hottest month of only 22 °C) is probably on high ground, because temperature falls with height.
- **Climate type**: high temperatures and rain all year suggest an equatorial climate; a hot wet season and a cooler dry season suggest a tropical continental (savanna) climate, like most of Zimbabwe.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Averaging the rainfall"}
Annual rainfall is a **total**. Adding the twelve months and then dividing by 12 gives the mean monthly rainfall, which is a different statistic.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing without figures"}
"It is hot in summer and rainy" earns little. Quote the months, the values and the units: "the hottest month is November at 22 °C".
:::

:::mistake{title="Drawing temperature as bars"}
Temperature is a continuous line plotted in the middle of each month; rainfall is shown by bars. Mixing them up loses marks for the whole graph.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Read the scales carefully: the two vertical axes have different units and intervals. Use a ruler to read across from the top of a bar or a point on the line.
:::

:::exam-tip
"Describe" means give the pattern with data; "explain" means give reasons (for example, the dry winter is caused by high pressure and sinking air).
:::

:::exam-tip
In calculations write the working: "Range = 22 − 13 = 9 °C". A correct method can earn marks even if you slip on the arithmetic.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Annual range = hottest month − coolest month; mean annual temperature = sum of monthly means ÷ 12.
- Total annual rainfall is the sum of the monthly figures.
- A climate graph shows rainfall as bars and temperature as a line plotted in the middle of each month, with two labelled axes.
- Describe a climate with figures: highest and lowest temperatures, range, total rainfall, wet and dry seasons.
- The hottest months show the hemisphere; the range and temperature level suggest latitude, altitude and climate type.
:::
