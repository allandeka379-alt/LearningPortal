## Introduction

Fly from Harare to Kinshasa and you swap a dry winter for steamy, daily downpours. Fly on to Cape Town and the winter brings rain instead of sunshine. These differences follow a pattern across the globe. Geographers group places with similar temperature and rainfall into **climate types**, which helps to explain the vegetation, farming and ways of life of each region.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Climate type (climatic region)"}
A large area of the world with broadly similar temperature and rainfall patterns through the year.
:::

:::definition{term="Climate classification"}
A system for grouping climates, mainly by mean temperatures (hottest and coolest months, annual range) and by the amount and seasonal timing of rainfall. Natural vegetation is often used as a guide because it reflects climate.
:::

:::definition{term="Continentality"}
The effect of distance from the sea: inland places have hotter summers, colder winters and a larger annual range than coastal places at the same latitude.
:::

## The basis of classification

Climates are classified using:

1. **Temperature**: is it hot all year, hot with a cool season, or cold? What is the annual range?
2. **Rainfall**: total amount, and *when* it falls (all year, in summer, or in winter).
3. **Controls that produce the pattern**: latitude (sun angle and pressure belts), distance from the sea, ocean currents, altitude and prevailing winds.

A useful rule: as latitude increases, temperatures fall and the annual range increases. West and east coasts at the same latitude differ because of winds and ocean currents.

## The main climate types

| Climate | Approximate latitude | Temperature | Rainfall | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equatorial | 0-5° (up to about 10°) N and S | Hot all year, about 26-28 °C; annual range under about 3 °C | Heavy, every month; often over 2,000 mm; convectional storms most afternoons | Congo basin, Amazon basin, Indonesia |
| Tropical continental (savanna) | About 5-20° N and S | Hot; warmest just before the rains; range about 5-10 °C | Wet summer, dry winter; about 500-1,500 mm | Most of Zimbabwe, Zambia, northern Nigeria, Brazilian highlands |
| Tropical (hot) desert | About 15-30° N and S, often on west coasts | Very hot summers; large daily range; cold nights | Under about 250 mm, very unreliable | Sahara, Namib, Kalahari (semi-desert), Atacama |
| Tropical monsoon | South and South-East Asia | Hot; hottest just before the monsoon | Very heavy summer monsoon rain; dry winter | India, Bangladesh, Myanmar |
| Mediterranean | About 30-40° N and S, west coasts | Hot summers, mild winters | Wet winters (frontal rain), dry summers; about 400-900 mm | South-western Cape, North African coast, California, south-west Australia |
| Warm temperate east coast | About 25-35° N and S, east coasts | Hot, humid summers; mild winters | Rain all year, most in summer | KwaZulu-Natal coast, south-east China, south-east USA |
| Cool temperate (western margin) | About 40-60° N and S, west coasts | Mild winters, cool summers; small range | Rain all year from depressions | Britain, north-west Europe |
| Cool temperate continental | Interiors of large northern continents | Very cold winters, warm summers; very large range | Mainly summer; moderate | Central Canada, Siberia |
| Polar (tundra and ice cap) | Beyond about 65° | Warmest month below 10 °C; very long cold winters | Very low, mainly snow | Northern Canada, Greenland, Antarctica |

All figures are approximate guides; textbooks draw the boundaries slightly differently.

## Why the pattern exists

- **Equatorial**: the midday sun is high all year, so it is always hot; strong heating and the ITCZ give rising air and rain every month.
- **Savanna**: the ITCZ brings rain only when it moves over the region in summer; in winter subtropical high pressure brings a dry season.
- **Hot desert**: subtropical high pressure gives sinking, dry air all year; on west coasts cold ocean currents (such as the Benguela off Namibia) cool the air from below so it cannot rise to give rain.
- **Mediterranean**: in summer subtropical high pressure moves over the region (dry); in winter it moves towards the equator and depressions bring frontal rain.
- **Warm temperate east coast**: warm ocean currents and onshore winds bring moist air and rain to east coasts.
- **Cool temperate and polar**: low sun angle and long winter nights; westerly winds and depressions bring rain to west coasts.

| Month | J | F | M | A | M | J | J | A | S | O | N | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature (°C) | 21 | 21 | 20 | 18 | 15 | 13 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 16 | 18 | 20 |
| Rainfall (mm) | 12 | 15 | 18 | 45 | 85 | 95 | 90 | 75 | 40 | 30 | 15 | 12 |

1. Hottest months January and February (21 °C), coolest July (12 °C): range 9 °C.
2. Warm summer in December-February, so the station is in the southern hemisphere.

4. Hot dry summer + mild wet winter = **Mediterranean**, like the south-western Cape.
:::

## Climates of Africa

Africa is almost symmetrical about the equator:

1. **Equatorial** in the Congo basin and the coast of the Gulf of Guinea.
2. **Savanna** in broad belts north and south of it, including Zimbabwe.
3. **Hot desert** further from the equator: the Sahara in the north; the Namib and Kalahari in the south-west.
4. **Mediterranean** at the far north (Morocco to Tunisia) and far south-west (around Cape Town).
5. **Warm temperate east coast** along the KwaZulu-Natal coast.
6. **Mountain (highland) climates** where altitude lowers temperatures, as in the Ethiopian Highlands, East Africa and the Eastern Highlands of Zimbabwe.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Mixing up Mediterranean and savanna"}
Both have a dry season, but the Mediterranean dry season is in **summer** and savanna's is in **winter**. Check whether the rain falls in the hot or the cool months.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the hemisphere"}
Southern hemisphere stations are hottest around December-February. A climate graph with a July peak in rain might be savanna in the north or Mediterranean in the south: check the temperatures first.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing climates without figures"}
"Hot and wet" is too vague. Quote temperatures, range, total rainfall and the wettest months.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Paper 2 often gives a climate table and asks you to identify the climate, give two reasons and name a region where it is found. Work out range, total and seasonality first, then decide.
:::

:::exam-tip
For "explain the basis for classifying climates", mention both temperature and rainfall (amount and season) and that vegetation often reflects the climate.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Climates are classified mainly by temperature (level and range) and rainfall (amount and season).
- Equatorial: hot and wet all year. Savanna: wet summer, dry winter. Hot desert: very dry. Monsoon: very wet summer.
- Mediterranean: dry summer, wet winter. Warm temperate east coast: rain all year, mostly summer.
- Cool temperate: mild, rain all year. Continental: very large range. Polar: very cold, little precipitation.
- Africa's climates are arranged in belts north and south of the equatorial Congo basin.
:::
