## Introduction

A tonne of rock from a gold mine may contain only a few grams of gold. A tonne of platinum ore holds even less of the precious metals. Before minerals can be sold, the valuable part must be separated from the waste rock and purified. Each step adds value. A country that exports raw ore earns far less than one that exports refined metal or finished products, which is why Zimbabwe's government stresses **beneficiation**.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Mineral processing"}
The stages that turn mined ore into a saleable product: crushing, grinding, concentrating, smelting and refining.
:::

:::definition{term="Concentrate"}
The product of separating the valuable mineral from most of the waste rock; it has a much higher grade than the ore.
:::

:::definition{term="Smelting"}
Heating ore or concentrate to a very high temperature, often with a reducing agent such as coke, to melt out the metal.
:::

:::definition{term="Tailings"}
The finely ground waste left after the valuable mineral has been removed; usually stored behind a tailings dam.
:::

:::definition{term="Beneficiation (value addition)"}
Processing a mineral further within the country, beyond its raw state, before it is exported or used, so that more of its final value is earned locally.
:::

## How selected minerals are processed

### Gold

1. **Crushing:** jaw and cone crushers break the ore into small pieces.
2. **Milling:** rotating ball mills grind it into fine powder mixed with water (a slurry or pulp).
3. **Gravity concentration:** coarse gold is caught first because it is heavy.
4. **Leaching:** a weak cyanide solution dissolves the fine gold from the pulp; activated carbon absorbs the dissolved gold.
5. **Recovery:** gold is stripped from the carbon and deposited by electricity (electrowinning).
6. **Smelting:** the gold is melted into bars of impure bullion.
7. **Refining:** in Zimbabwe, bullion is refined at Fidelity Gold Refinery in Harare before sale.

### Platinum group metals

:::example{title="A diagram in words: from Great Dyke ore to platinum"}
Imagine a flow diagram running left to right.

1. **Underground mine** → ore is hoisted to the surface.
2. **Concentrator** → ore is crushed and milled; in **froth flotation** tanks, chemicals make the metal-bearing grains stick to air bubbles, which float off as a froth. The concentrate is only a small fraction of the ore's mass.
3. **Smelter** → the concentrate is melted in an electric furnace; the metals collect in a **matte**, and waste forms slag.
4. **Refinery** → the matte is separated into platinum, palladium, rhodium and other metals, plus nickel and copper. Much of Zimbabwe's platinum matte has been refined in South Africa; refining it in Zimbabwe is a beneficiation goal.
:::

### Chrome to ferrochrome

Chromite ore is mixed with **coke** (as a reducing agent) and fluxes and smelted in **electric arc furnaces**. The product, **ferrochrome** (an alloy of chrome and iron), is exported for making **stainless steel**. Zimbabwe's ferrochrome is produced mainly at Kwekwe, close to the chrome of the Great Dyke. Smelting uses very large amounts of electricity.

### Iron ore to steel

1. Iron ore, **coke** (from coal) and **limestone** are fed into the top of a **blast furnace**, and hot air is blown in at the bottom.
2. Carbon from the coke removes oxygen from the ore, leaving molten **pig iron**; limestone combines with impurities to form **slag**, which floats and is tapped off.
3. Pig iron is turned into **steel** by blowing oxygen through it to burn off excess carbon.

Zimbabwe's integrated steelworks at Redcliff, near Kwekwe, used local iron ore, Hwange coal and local limestone, but it stopped producing in the late 2000s.

### Copper (an African example)

:::example{title="Case study: Zambia's Copperbelt"}
Copper ore is crushed, milled and concentrated by froth flotation. The concentrate is smelted to produce impure **blister copper**, cast into anodes and **electrolytically refined**: in tanks of acid solution, pure copper is deposited on cathodes. The cathode copper is very pure and is used for electrical wire. Processing close to the mines lets Zambia export refined metal rather than bulky concentrate.
:::

### Diamonds

Diamond-bearing gravel or rock is crushed, then heavy minerals are separated by **dense medium separation**. Diamonds glow under **X-rays**, so X-ray sorters pick them out. Rough diamonds can then be **cut and polished**, which multiplies their value.

## Beneficiation: why it matters

1. **Higher export earnings:** refined metal or a finished product sells for many times the value of ore or concentrate.
2. **Jobs and skills:** smelters, refineries and factories employ people and train engineers and technicians.
3. **Linkages:** processing creates demand for power, transport, chemicals and engineering, and supplies local industries (for example steel for construction).
4. **Lower transport costs per unit of value:** processed products are lighter and less bulky than ore.
5. **Less dependence on raw-material prices**, which rise and fall sharply.

In 2022 the government banned the export of unprocessed lithium ore, so lithium miners built plants in Zimbabwe to produce concentrate before export.

## Challenges of beneficiation

- **Electricity:** smelters and refineries need large, reliable power, and power cuts have been common.
- **Capital:** refineries cost hundreds of millions of US dollars.
- **Technology and skills:** specialised equipment and trained staff are needed.
- **Markets:** buyers abroad may already have their own refineries; trade barriers may favour raw imports.
- **Scale:** a plant needs a large, steady supply of ore to be efficient.
- **Pollution:** smelting releases sulphur dioxide and dust; tailings and slag must be managed.

:::example{title="Worked example: value added (fictional prices)"}
A mine produces 10,000 tonnes of chrome ore worth US\$200 per tonne. It takes 2.5 tonnes of ore to make 1 tonne of ferrochrome, worth US\$1,400 per tonne.

1. Value as ore: 10,000 × 200 = **US\$2,000,000**.
2. Ferrochrome made: 10,000 ÷ 2.5 = 4,000 tonnes; value = 4,000 × 1,400 = **US\$5,600,000**.
3. Value added by smelting = 5,600,000 − 2,000,000 = **US\$3,600,000** (before the cost of power, coke and labour).
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing processing with beneficiation"}
All mined minerals need some processing. Beneficiation means doing **more** of that processing (and manufacturing) **inside the country** to keep more value at home.
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting the reducing agent"}
Smelting of chrome and iron ore needs **coke** (carbon) to remove oxygen from the ore; heat alone is not enough.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Processing turns ore into saleable products: crushing, milling, concentrating, smelting and refining.
- Gold: crushing, milling, cyanide leaching, smelting to bullion, refining. Platinum: concentrating by flotation, smelting to matte, refining.
- Chrome is smelted with coke into ferrochrome for stainless steel; iron ore, coke and limestone make pig iron and then steel.
- Beneficiation keeps more of a mineral's value in the country: more earnings, jobs, skills and linkages.
- It is held back by shortages of power and capital, technology, markets and pollution control.
:::
