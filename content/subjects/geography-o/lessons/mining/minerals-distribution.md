## Introduction

Zimbabwe is one of the most mineral-rich countries in Africa for its size. More than forty different minerals have been mined here, and towns such as Hwange, Zvishavane, Shurugwi, Bindura, Kwekwe and Kadoma grew up around mines. Minerals usually earn well over half of the country's export income (an approximate share that changes from year to year with world prices).

To understand mining you first need to know **what** is mined and **where** it is found. The pattern is not random: each mineral is linked to a particular type of rock, and those rocks are found in particular parts of the country.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Mineral"}
A naturally occurring, inorganic substance with a definite chemical composition, found in the Earth's crust (for example gold, chromite or quartz).
:::

:::definition{term="Ore"}
A rock that contains enough of a valuable mineral to be mined at a profit. Chromite is the ore of chrome; haematite is an ore of iron.
:::

:::definition{term="Mineral deposit"}
A concentration of a mineral in one place; it becomes an **ore body** when it can be mined profitably.
:::

:::definition{term="Mining"}
The extraction of minerals from the Earth's crust, at or below the surface.
:::

## Mineral groups

Minerals are grouped by what they are and how they are used.

| Group | Sub-group | Zimbabwean examples |
|---|---|---|
| Metallic | Precious metals (rare, high value) | Gold, silver, platinum and the other platinum group metals (PGMs) |
| Metallic | Ferrous (iron, and metals alloyed with iron to make steel) | Iron ore, chrome, nickel |
| Metallic | Base metals (non-ferrous, used in industry) | Copper |
| Non-metallic | Industrial minerals and gemstones | Asbestos, limestone, diamonds |
| Energy minerals | Fuels | Coal (and coal-bed methane) |

Diamonds are a **precious stone** (a gemstone), so you may also see them listed with the "precious" minerals. Gold, platinum and diamonds are the minerals usually called precious.

## Where minerals are found: three geological settings

:::example{title="A diagram in words: Zimbabwe's mineral belts"}
Imagine an outline map of Zimbabwe.

1. **Greenstone belts** are scattered patches of very old volcanic and sedimentary rock across the central watershed, for example around Kwekwe, Kadoma, Shurugwi, Gwanda, Bindura and Mazowe. Most of the country's **gold** mines lie on them.
2. **The Great Dyke** is a narrow band of igneous rock, about 550 km long and only a few kilometres wide, running roughly NNE to SSW through the middle of the country from near Mvurwi towards Zvishavane. It holds **platinum group metals, chrome and nickel**.
3. **Karoo sedimentary basins** lie in the low-lying north-west (the mid-Zambezi area around Hwange) and the south-east. Their sedimentary rocks contain **coal**.
:::

The table summarises where the main minerals are found.

| Mineral | Main areas (examples) | Geological setting or note |
|---|---|---|
| Gold | Kwekwe, Kadoma, Shurugwi, Gwanda, Bindura, Mazowe; alluvial gold in rivers such as the Mazowe | Greenstone belts and river gravels |
| Platinum group metals | Ngezi (near Chegutu), Shurugwi, Zvishavane | The Great Dyke |
| Chrome (chromite) | Along the Great Dyke, for example near Shurugwi and Mutorashanga | Great Dyke seams; smelted into ferrochrome at Kwekwe |
| Nickel | Bindura area | Greenstone rocks at Bindura; nickel is also recovered with platinum from the Great Dyke |
| Coal | Hwange | Karoo sedimentary rocks; feeds the Hwange power station |
| Diamonds | Marange (Chiadzwa) near Mutare; Murowa near Zvishavane | Alluvial and conglomerate deposits at Marange; a kimberlite pipe at Murowa |
| Lithium | Bikita (Masvingo Province); Arcadia (Goromonzi) | Pegmatite rocks |
| Iron ore | Buchwa (Mberengwa); Ripple Creek near Redcliff | Once supplied the steelworks at Redcliff |
| Copper | Mhangura area (Makonde district) | Many copper mines have closed |
| Asbestos | Zvishavane and Mashava | Production has largely stopped because of health risks and falling demand |

:::mistake{title="Putting coal on the Great Dyke"}
Coal forms from plant remains in **sedimentary** rock. The Great Dyke is **igneous**, so it cannot hold coal. Coal is found in the Karoo basins, especially at Hwange.
:::

## Why minerals matter to Zimbabwe

1. **Export earnings:** gold, platinum group metals, diamonds, chrome (as ferrochrome), nickel and lithium are sold abroad and earn foreign currency to pay for imports such as fuel and machinery.
2. **Employment:** large mines employ thousands of people, and hundreds of thousands more work in small-scale and artisanal mining, especially for gold.
3. **Government revenue:** mining companies pay royalties, taxes and licence fees.
4. **Raw materials for local industry:** coal for power and industry, limestone for cement, iron ore and coal for steel.
5. **Infrastructure and settlement:** roads, railways, power lines, schools and clinics were built to serve mines, and mining towns grew up.
6. **Linkages:** mines buy goods and services (transport, engineering, food, explosives), creating jobs in other sectors.

:::example{title="Case study: the Great Dyke"}
The Great Dyke is one of the world's largest known deposits of platinum group metals. Platinum mines at Ngezi, Shurugwi and Zvishavane extract ore underground and concentrate it before smelting. Chrome seams along the Dyke supply ferrochrome smelters at Kwekwe. Mining has brought roads, housing and services to the area, but the region also depends heavily on world platinum and chrome prices, which rise and fall.
:::

:::example{title="Worked example: share of export earnings (fictional data)"}
A fictional country earns US\$6,000 million from exports in one year, of which minerals earn US\$3,900 million.

1. Share from minerals = 3,900 ÷ 6,000 × 100 = **65%**.
2. Share from other exports = 100 − 65 = **35%**.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Confusing mineral and ore"}
Every ore contains minerals, but a mineral deposit is only an ore if it can be mined at a **profit**. A low-grade deposit far from transport may not be an ore today but could become one if prices rise.
:::

:::mistake{title="Listing mines without minerals"}
"Bindura is a mining town" earns little. Say "**nickel** and **gold** are mined around Bindura".
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="Paper 1 and Paper 2 Section B"}
Paper 1 items often ask you to match a mineral with its area (coal–Hwange, diamonds–Marange, platinum–Great Dyke, lithium–Bikita). In Paper 2, "describe the distribution" means say where minerals are concentrated and where they are absent (for example "gold is scattered along the greenstone belts of the central watershed; coal is confined to the north-west at Hwange"). "Explain the importance" needs a developed point with an example.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A mineral is a natural inorganic substance; an ore is a rock that can be mined at a profit.
- Groups: metallic (precious, ferrous, base), non-metallic and energy minerals.
- Gold is mainly in the greenstone belts; platinum, chrome and nickel along the Great Dyke; coal in the Karoo basins, especially Hwange.
- Diamonds are mined at Marange and Murowa, lithium at Bikita and Arcadia.
- Minerals earn most of Zimbabwe's exports, create jobs, pay taxes, supply industry and lead to the growth of towns and infrastructure.
:::
