## Introduction

Every time you buy airtime, ride a kombi, visit a clinic or send money with a mobile wallet, you use a **service industry**. Services now employ a large share of Zimbabwe's urban workers. One service, **tourism**, is special because it brings foreign visitors and foreign currency to the country.

Zimbabwe has world-class attractions: one of the largest waterfalls on Earth, the largest ancient stone city south of the Sahara, great herds of elephant and spectacular mountains. This lesson describes Zimbabwe's service and quaternary industries, its tourist attractions, why tourism matters and what holds it back.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Service (tertiary) industry"}
An industry that provides services rather than goods: retail, transport, banking, insurance, health, education, communications, government and tourism.
:::

:::definition{term="Quaternary industry"}
Knowledge-based services such as research and development, information technology and specialist consultancy.
:::

:::definition{term="Tourism"}
Travel to and stay in places away from home for leisure, business or other purposes, together with the services that support it.
:::

:::definition{term="Domestic tourism"}
Tourism by people within their own country, for example a family from Harare holidaying in Nyanga.
:::

:::definition{term="International tourism"}
Tourism by visitors from other countries; it earns foreign currency.
:::

## Service and quaternary industries in Zimbabwe

| Service | Examples |
|---|---|
| Retail and wholesale | Supermarkets, markets, tuck-shops, informal vendors |
| Transport | Road haulage, buses and kombis, National Railways of Zimbabwe, airlines and airports |
| Finance | Banks, insurance companies, microfinance, mobile money |
| Communications | Mobile networks, internet providers, broadcasting, postal services |
| Social services | Schools, colleges, universities, hospitals and clinics |
| Government | Ministries, councils, police |
| Tourism and hospitality | Hotels, lodges, safari operators, tour guides, restaurants |

**Quaternary** activities include research at universities and agricultural research stations, software development, mobile-money and banking systems, and data and consultancy services. They are concentrated in Harare and other large towns where skilled workers, universities and good communications are found.

## Zimbabwe's main tourist attractions

:::example{title="A diagram in words: Zimbabwe's tourist map"}
Imagine an outline map of Zimbabwe.

1. **North-west corner:** Victoria Falls on the Zambezi, with Hwange National Park to the south-east of it.
2. **Along the northern border:** Lake Kariba, and further down the Zambezi valley, Mana Pools National Park.
3. **South-west, near Bulawayo:** the Matobo Hills and the Khami ruins.
4. **South-centre, near Masvingo:** Great Zimbabwe and Lake Mutirikwi.
5. **South-east:** Gonarezhou National Park.
6. **Eastern border:** the Eastern Highlands (Nyanga, Bvumba, Chimanimani).
:::

| Attraction | What draws visitors |
|---|---|
| Victoria Falls (Mosi-oa-Tunya, "the smoke that thunders") | About 1.7 km wide and about 100 m high (approximate); World Heritage Site shared with Zambia; rainforest walks; bungee jumping, white-water rafting, sunset cruises on the Zambezi |
| Great Zimbabwe, near Masvingo | Huge dry-stone walls of the capital of a Shona state built between roughly the 11th and 15th centuries; the Great Enclosure and Hill Complex; the soapstone Zimbabwe Birds; World Heritage Site |
| Hwange National Park | Zimbabwe's largest national park; large herds of elephant, lions and many other species; game drives |
| Mana Pools National Park | Zambezi floodplain pools, elephants and other wildlife; canoe safaris and walking safaris; World Heritage Site |
| Matobo Hills, near Bulawayo | Granite kopjes and balancing rocks; ancient San rock paintings; rhino; World Heritage Site |
| Lake Kariba | Man-made lake behind Kariba dam; houseboats, tiger-fishing, Matusadona National Park on its shore |
| Eastern Highlands | Cool mountain scenery; Mount Nyangani (about 2,590 m, the highest point); waterfalls, hiking, trout fishing, botanical gardens |

## The importance of tourism

1. **Foreign currency** from international visitors pays for imports.
2. **Jobs** in hotels, lodges, transport, guiding, crafts and restaurants, many in rural areas with few other jobs.
3. **Multiplier effect:** tourist spending supports farmers, curio carvers, builders and transport operators.
4. **Infrastructure:** airports, roads, water and electricity built for tourism also serve local people.
5. **Conservation funding:** park fees and community schemes such as CAMPFIRE give wildlife a value, encouraging communities to protect it.
6. **Government revenue** from taxes, licences and park fees.
7. **Image and culture:** tourism promotes Zimbabwe internationally and helps preserve heritage sites and crafts.

:::example{title="Case study: Victoria Falls town"}
Victoria Falls town depends almost entirely on tourism. Hotels, lodges, adventure-activity companies, craft markets and restaurants employ most of the workforce. The international airport was upgraded in the 2010s to take larger aircraft. The KAZA visa lets visitors move between Zimbabwe and Zambia on a single visa, and the town co-hosted the UN World Tourism Organization General Assembly with Livingstone in Zambia in 2013. Problems include heavy dependence on foreign visitors, seasonal changes (the falls carry far less water late in the dry season), conflict between people and wildlife, and the fall in visitors whenever the country's image suffers.
:::

## Problems facing tourism and other services

| Problem | Effect |
|---|---|
| Economic instability and foreign-currency shortages | Rising prices; difficulty importing vehicles, equipment and supplies |
| Negative international image | Tourists choose neighbouring countries instead |
| Poor infrastructure | Potholed roads, few direct international flights, power and water cuts at hotels |
| Competition | Zambia, Botswana, South Africa and Namibia offer similar attractions |
| Poaching and loss of wildlife | Fewer animals to see; damage to reputation |
| Drought and climate change | Low water at Victoria Falls and Kariba; stressed wildlife |
| Seasonality and shocks | Few visitors in some months; collapse of travel during the COVID-19 pandemic |
| Loss of skilled staff | Trained chefs, guides, nurses, teachers and bankers emigrate |

## Possible solutions

1. **Marketing** abroad by the Zimbabwe Tourism Authority (ZTA) and through social media, to improve the country's image.
2. **Improving infrastructure:** airports, roads, reliable power (including solar at lodges) and water.
3. **Regional cooperation:** the KAZA visa and transfrontier conservation areas encourage multi-country trips.
4. **Promoting domestic tourism** with lower local rates, so the industry does not depend only on foreign visitors.
5. **Anti-poaching** patrols and community conservation such as CAMPFIRE.
6. **Diversifying attractions** (cultural, heritage, conference and adventure tourism) to spread visitors and seasons.
7. **Training and better pay** to keep skilled workers; stable economic policies to attract investment.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Listing attractions without describing them"}
"Hwange, Mana Pools, Kariba" earns little. Say **what** attracts tourists: "Hwange, Zimbabwe's largest national park, has large herds of elephant."
:::

:::mistake{title="Forgetting domestic tourism"}
Tourism is not only foreigners. Zimbabweans visiting Nyanga, Kariba or Great Zimbabwe are domestic tourists, and they help keep hotels open when foreign arrivals fall.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip{title="ZIMSEC Paper 1 and Paper 2 Section B"}
Paper 1 may ask you to match an attraction to its region. Paper 2 often asks you to "describe tourism in Zimbabwe and its importance" and to "describe the problems and suggest solutions". Pair every solution with the problem it solves, and use named places.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Service industries provide services (retail, transport, finance, communications, health, education, tourism); quaternary industries provide knowledge and ICT.
- Zimbabwe's attractions include Victoria Falls, Great Zimbabwe, Hwange, Mana Pools, Matobo, Lake Kariba and the Eastern Highlands.
- Tourism earns foreign currency, creates jobs, supports other sectors and funds conservation.
- Problems: economic instability, image, infrastructure, competition, poaching, drought, seasonality and skills loss.
- Solutions: marketing, infrastructure, regional cooperation (KAZA visa), domestic tourism, anti-poaching and diversification.
:::
