## Introduction

The grey granite of a kopje near Masvingo, the coal burned at Hwange, the limestone ground into cement and the marble of a polished floor are all rocks, but they formed in completely different ways. Knowing how a rock formed explains how it weathers, what landforms it produces, what minerals it may hold and what it can be used for.

All rocks belong to one of three groups: **igneous**, **sedimentary** and **metamorphic**. This lesson explains each group, the rock cycle that links them, and the rocks that build Zimbabwe.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Rock"}
A solid, natural material made of one or more minerals (or of organic remains), forming the Earth's crust.
:::

:::definition{term="Mineral"}
A naturally occurring substance with a definite chemical make-up and crystal structure, such as quartz, feldspar or mica.
:::

:::definition{term="Magma and lava"}
Magma is molten rock below the surface; when it reaches the surface it is called lava.
:::

:::definition{term="Strata"}
Layers (beds) of sedimentary rock laid down one on top of another; the singular is stratum.
:::

## Igneous rocks

Igneous rocks form when **magma or lava cools and solidifies**. Crystals grow as the melt cools, so the **rate of cooling controls crystal size**.

| Type | Where it cools | Rate of cooling | Crystals | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intrusive (plutonic) | Deep underground | Slow | Large, visible | Granite (quartz, feldspar, mica) |
| Intrusive, near the surface | In dykes and sills | Medium | Medium | Dolerite |
| Extrusive (volcanic) | At the surface as lava | Fast | Very small | Basalt |

Igneous rocks are usually **hard, crystalline and without layers or fossils**. Granite is light-coloured and coarse; basalt is dark and fine-grained.

## Sedimentary rocks

Sedimentary rocks form from **sediments** laid down in layers, usually in water, then **compacted** by the weight of layers above and **cemented** by minerals.

1. Older rocks are weathered and eroded.
2. Rivers, wind or the sea transport the fragments.
3. Fragments are deposited in layers on lake or sea floors.
4. Over long periods the layers are buried, compacted and cemented into rock.

| Origin | How it forms | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fragments of other rocks (clastic) | Sand, silt, clay or pebbles cemented together | Sandstone, shale, mudstone, conglomerate |
| Organic | Remains of plants or shells and skeletons | Coal (plants), most limestone (shells) |
| Chemical | Minerals precipitate from water | Rock salt, some limestone |

Sedimentary rocks have **layers (strata)**, may contain **fossils**, and are often softer and more porous than igneous rocks.

## Metamorphic rocks

Metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks are changed by **great heat and/or pressure** without melting, for example near a magma intrusion or deep in a mountain belt where plates collide. Minerals recrystallise and often line up in bands.

| Original rock | Metamorphic rock |
| --- | --- |
| Granite | Gneiss |
| Limestone | Marble |
| Sandstone | Quartzite |
| Shale or mudstone | Slate |

Metamorphic rocks are usually **hard and dense**, and many show **banding** or splitting into thin sheets (slate).

## The rock cycle in outline

:::example{title="Diagram in words: the rock cycle"}
Draw a circle with three boxes: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic.

1. **Igneous** rocks at the surface are weathered and eroded; the fragments are deposited and become **sedimentary** rock.
2. Sedimentary rock buried deeply is changed by heat and pressure into **metamorphic** rock.
3. Metamorphic rock heated further melts into **magma**, which cools into new **igneous** rock.
4. Short cuts exist: any rock can be weathered into sediment, and igneous rock can be metamorphosed directly.

Label the arrows with the processes: weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction and cementation, heat and pressure, melting, cooling.
:::

## Rocks of Zimbabwe

- **Granite country**: much of the plateau is underlain by ancient granites and gneisses. They weather into kopjes, dwalas and balancing rocks (Matobo, Epworth, Domboshava) and into sandy soils.
- **Greenstone belts**: narrower belts of old, partly metamorphosed volcanic and sedimentary rocks between the granites; most of Zimbabwe's gold mines are in these belts.
- **The Great Dyke**: a long igneous intrusion, about 550 km long and only a few kilometres wide, running roughly NNE-SSW across the country. It is rich in chrome, platinum and nickel.
- **Karoo rocks**: younger sedimentary rocks (sandstones, shales and coal) in the Zambezi valley and the south-east lowveld; the Hwange coalfield lies in them. Karoo basalt lavas also occur, and the Victoria Falls and its zigzag gorges are cut into basalt.
- Dark dolerite intrusions and pockets of limestone and dolomite occur in places, for example the dolomite of the Chinhoyi area, which contains caves.

:::example{title="Case study: uses of Zimbabwe's rocks"}
- **Granite**: building stone, crushed stone (aggregate) for roads and concrete, kerbs and monuments; dark igneous rock sold as "black granite" is quarried around Mutoko.
- **Coal** (sedimentary): fuel for the thermal power station and industry at Hwange.
- **Limestone**: cement, flux in iron and steel making, and agricultural lime for acid soils.
- **Sand and clay**: bricks and building.
- **Great Dyke rocks**: ores of chrome, platinum and nickel.
- **Basalt**: weathers into dark, fertile clay soils; crushed for road stone.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Thinking all hard rocks are igneous"}
Quartzite and marble are very hard but metamorphic. Look at how the rock formed, not only how hard it is.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up crystal size"}
Slow cooling gives large crystals (granite); fast cooling gives small crystals (basalt). Students often reverse this.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling coal a mineral rock formed from magma"}
Coal is an organic sedimentary rock formed from compressed plant remains, not from magma.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
"Describe how sedimentary rock forms" needs the sequence: weathering, transport, deposition in layers, compaction, cementation. One mark per stage.
:::

:::exam-tip
When asked to **classify** rocks, give the group and a reason: "marble is metamorphic because it is limestone changed by heat and pressure".
:::

:::exam-tip
Link rocks to landforms and resources: granite gives kopjes; the Great Dyke gives chrome and platinum; Karoo sediments give coal.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Igneous rocks cool from magma or lava: slow cooling gives large crystals (granite), fast cooling small crystals (basalt).
- Sedimentary rocks form from layers of sediment that are compacted and cemented; they have strata and may hold fossils (sandstone, shale, limestone, coal).
- Metamorphic rocks are existing rocks changed by heat and pressure (gneiss, marble, quartzite, slate).
- The rock cycle links the three groups through weathering, deposition, burial, heat, melting and cooling.
- Zimbabwe: granite country, gold-bearing greenstone belts, the Great Dyke (chrome, platinum, nickel) and Karoo sediments (coal) and basalts.
:::
