## Introduction

Travel by bus from Harare to Mutare and the land changes as you go: a broad, gently rolling surface around Harare and Marondera, then steep slopes, deep valleys and pine-covered mountains as you approach the Eastern Highlands. Travel north from Harare to Kariba instead and the road drops steeply down the Zambezi escarpment into a hot, low valley.

These shapes of the land are **landforms**. They control where rain falls, where rivers flow, where people farm, build towns and dams, and where tourists go. This lesson introduces the main landforms, the relief regions of Zimbabwe and the ways landforms benefit people. Later lessons explain *how* they form (weathering, rivers, wind, folding, faulting and volcanoes).

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Landform"}
A single natural feature of the Earth's surface with a recognisable shape, such as a hill, valley, plateau or escarpment.
:::

:::definition{term="Landscape"}
The whole scene of an area: several landforms together with the vegetation, rivers and human features on them. A landform is one part; a landscape is the combined view.
:::

:::definition{term="Relief"}
The differences in height and the shape of the land surface in an area: high or low, flat, gentle or steep.
:::

:::definition{term="Escarpment"}
A long, steep slope or cliff that separates higher land from lower land, for example the Zambezi escarpment between the plateau and the Zambezi valley.
:::

:::definition{term="Inselberg"}
An isolated, steep-sided hill of hard rock that rises abruptly from a flat plain (the word means "island mountain"). Many of Zimbabwe's granite hills are inselbergs.
:::

## The main landforms

| Landform | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mountain | High land rising steeply to a peak or ridge, well above the surrounding land | Nyangani (Zimbabwe), Kilimanjaro (Tanzania), the Himalayas |
| Plateau | A large area of high land with a fairly flat or gently rolling top, often bounded by steep edges | The Zimbabwean highveld, the East African plateau, the Deccan plateau in India |
| Plain | A large area of flat or gently undulating low land | The Mozambique coastal plain, the Ganges plain |
| Escarpment | A long steep slope separating high and low land | The Zambezi escarpment, the Drakensberg escarpment |
| Valley | A long low area between hills, usually with a river | The Zambezi valley, the Great Rift Valley |
| Hill | Raised land lower and less steep than a mountain | Kopjes around Harare and Masvingo |
| Inselberg | An isolated steep hill on a plain | Granite domes in Matabeleland and Masvingo |

There is no exact world rule for when a hill becomes a mountain; describe the height and steepness instead of arguing about the name.

## The relief regions of Zimbabwe

Most of Zimbabwe is a **plateau**. A broad ridge of high ground, the central **watershed**, runs roughly from south-west to north-east through Bulawayo, Gweru and Harare. Rivers on its north side flow to the Zambezi; rivers on its south side flow to the Limpopo and the Save.

Heights in the table are approximate; textbooks draw the boundaries slightly differently.

| Relief region | Approximate height | Where | Typical features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Highveld | Above about 1,200 m | The central watershed: Harare (about 1,500 m), Gweru, Bulawayo, Marondera | Gently rolling land, cooler climate, more reliable rain, main towns and commercial farms |
| Middleveld | About 900-1,200 m | Land on both sides of the highveld | Granite hills and kopjes, mixed farming, many communal areas |
| Lowveld | Below about 900 m | The Save-Limpopo lowlands in the south-east and the Zambezi valley in the north | Hot, dry, low and unreliable rain; irrigated sugar cane in the south-east; wildlife areas |
| Eastern Highlands | Mostly above about 1,500 m | Nyanga, Bvumba and Chimanimani along the Mozambique border | Mountains, steep slopes, the highest rainfall; Mount Nyangani, about 2,590 m, the highest point |

:::keyfact
Zimbabwe's highest point is Mount Nyangani (about 2,590 m) in the Eastern Highlands. The lowest point, at about 160 m, is where the Runde river joins the Save in the south-east Lowveld.
:::

:::example{title="Diagram in words: a cross-section from Kariba to Mutare"}
Imagine a cross-section drawn from north-west to south-east.

1. **Zambezi valley** (left): low land, below about 500 m, with Lake Kariba.
2. **Zambezi escarpment**: a steep rise of several hundred metres.
3. **Middleveld**: a gentle rise across the plateau, dotted with kopjes.
4. **Highveld** around Harare: the highest part of the plateau, gently rolling.
5. **Middleveld** again as the land falls gently eastwards.
6. **Eastern Highlands**: steep slopes rise to mountain peaks near Mutare.

Label each part with its approximate height and one land use, and you have a sketch that earns marks in a "describe the relief" question.
:::

## Benefits of landforms to people

Landforms are not just scenery; they are resources.

- **Mountains and highlands**: cooler, wetter climate for tea, coffee and timber plantations in the Eastern Highlands; sources of rivers; tourism (hiking in Nyanga and Chimanimani).
- **Plateaus**: a large area of gentle land that is easy to farm and build on; cooler and healthier than the low valleys, so the main towns grew on the highveld.
- **Plains and valleys**: flat land for irrigated farming and transport routes; fertile alluvial soils near rivers.
- **Gorges and narrow valleys**: good dam sites, such as the Kariba gorge on the Zambezi, giving water and hydro-electric power.
- **Escarpments and waterfalls**: tourism (Victoria Falls, about 100 m high), and viewpoints.
- **Hills and inselbergs**: building stone and quarrying, rock art and sacred sites, water catchment, grazing, tourism (the Matobo Hills are a World Heritage Site).

Landforms can also be **obstacles**: steep slopes make roads and railways expensive, erode easily and are hard to farm, and escarpments separate communities.

:::example{title="Case study: the Eastern Highlands"}
High altitude gives cooler temperatures and the highest rainfall in Zimbabwe, partly from moist south-east winds forced to rise over the mountains. This supports tea and coffee estates, pine and eucalyptus plantations, trout fishing, holiday resorts and national parks. The same steep slopes are prone to soil erosion and landslides, as the damage in Chimanimani during Cyclone Idai (March 2019) showed.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Using landform and landscape as the same word"}
A kopje is a landform. The view of kopjes, fields, a river and a village together is a landscape. Use "landscape" for the whole scene.
:::

:::mistake{title="Calling the highveld a mountain range"}
The highveld is a plateau: high, but broad and gently rolling, not steep and peaked.
:::

:::mistake{title="Quoting exact boundary heights as facts"}
Textbooks use different heights for the highveld, middleveld and lowveld boundaries. Write "about 1,200 m" and describe the relief instead of relying on one exact figure.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
ZIMSEC Paper 2 Section A often starts with **identify** or **describe** a landform from a map or description. Give its shape, height and slope, and use figures: "a steep-sided hill rising about 200 m above the plain".
:::

:::exam-tip
For "explain the benefits of landforms", link each landform to a **use** and a **reason**: "the Kariba gorge is a good dam site *because* the narrow, deep valley needs only a short wall to hold back a large lake".
:::

:::exam-tip
In Paper 1 multiple choice, read the heights carefully and remember that relief affects temperature: air temperature falls by roughly 6 to 6.5 °C for every 1,000 m of height gained, so the highlands are cooler than the valleys.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- A landform is one natural feature; a landscape is the whole scene made of several landforms and what is on them.
- Main landforms: mountains, plateaus, plains, escarpments, valleys, hills and inselbergs.
- Zimbabwe is mostly a plateau: highveld (above about 1,200 m), middleveld, lowveld (below about 900 m) and the Eastern Highlands, divided by a central watershed.
- Nyangani (about 2,590 m) is the highest point; the Runde-Save confluence (about 160 m) is the lowest.
- Landforms give farmland, water, dam sites, minerals, building stone and tourism, but steep landforms also hinder transport and farming.
:::
