## Introduction

A topographical map records how people use the land: fields on gentle slopes, plantations on high ground, mine dumps beside a railway siding, homesteads scattered across communal land, and shops and a police post clustered at a road junction. Map-work questions ask you to **identify** these uses and patterns from the symbols, and then **explain** them using the physical evidence on the same map (relief, drainage, soils implied by vegetation) and the human evidence (roads, railways, services).

The skill is always the same: **state what you see, give a grid reference, then link it to a reason shown on the map.**

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Land use"}
The purpose for which an area of land is used, such as cultivation, grazing, forestry, mining, settlement, transport, recreation or conservation.
:::

:::definition{term="Settlement pattern"}
The way buildings or homesteads are arranged across an area: grouped (nucleated), scattered (dispersed) or in a line (linear).
:::

:::definition{term="Settlement function"}
The main activity or service a settlement provides, such as mining, administration, trade, transport or tourism.
:::

:::definition{term="Drainage pattern"}
The shape formed by a river and its tributaries when seen from above.
:::

## Identifying land use and explaining it

| Land use | Map evidence | Usual reasons visible on the map |
|---|---|---|
| Cultivation | Field boundaries, "cultivation" labels, irrigation canals, centre pivots | Gentle slopes, nearby rivers or dams, roads to markets |
| Grazing / ranching | Open grassland or bush, dip tanks, cattle pens, boreholes | Drier or rockier land, too steep or too dry for crops |
| Forestry / plantations | Tree symbols in regular blocks, sawmills | High, steep or wet land (as in parts of the Eastern Highlands) |
| Mining | Mine symbols, dumps, shafts, quarries, sidings | Where the mineral is found; railway or road for transport |
| Recreation and conservation | National park or safari area boundaries, camps, viewpoints | Scenery, wildlife, steep land unsuitable for farming |

:::example{title="Linking land use to the map"}
"Cultivation is concentrated in square 3456 *because* the contours are widely spaced (gentle slopes) and a dam at 348 563 supplies water." This answer identifies, locates and explains using map evidence. "Farming happens because of good soil" earns little, because soil type is not shown on the map.
:::

## Settlement patterns and functions

- **Nucleated** settlements group buildings closely together, often at road junctions, bridging points, water sources or mines.
- **Dispersed** settlements are scattered homesteads, typical of communal and commercial farming land, where each household lives near its fields.
- **Linear** settlements stretch along a road, railway, river or valley floor.

Settlement **site** clues on a map include water supply (river, borehole), flat or gently sloping land, land above flood level, defence (hilltop) and shelter. Settlement **function** comes from the services shown: a settlement with many schools, clinics, police and a post office is a **service or administrative centre**; one surrounded by mine dumps and shafts is a **mining settlement**; one at a railway junction with sidings is a **transport centre**; one with hotels and camps near a scenic feature is a **tourist centre**.

## Drainage patterns

Imagine looking down on the rivers:

- **Dendritic**: tributaries join the main river at acute angles from many directions, like the branches of a tree. Common where the rock is of similar resistance throughout, such as on granite.
- **Trellis**: main streams run parallel, with short tributaries joining at nearly **right angles**. Found where bands of hard and soft rock alternate.
- **Radial**: rivers flow **outwards** in all directions from a central high point, such as a dome or a volcanic cone, like the spokes of a wheel.
- **Parallel**: rivers run roughly side by side down a uniform slope.

When describing drainage also mention density (many or few streams), permanent or seasonal rivers, marshes and dams.

## Transport networks and relief

Transport routes reveal relief. Roads and railways **follow valleys**, **avoid steep slopes and marshes**, **cross ridges at cols**, and **bridge rivers at narrow points**. On steep slopes roads zig-zag. Main roads link the largest settlements; minor roads and tracks link farms and villages to them. A railway with sidings at a mine shows that the mineral is bulky and heavy.

## Point, line and area data

Map features can be stored and analysed as three kinds of data (the same types used in GIS):

- **Point** data: features with a position only, such as a borehole, school or trigonometrical station.
- **Line** data: features with length, such as roads, railways, rivers and power lines.
- **Area (polygon)** data: features that cover space, such as a dam's surface, a forest, a farm or a national park.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Explaining with things not on the map"}
Soils, rainfall figures and history are rarely shown. Use evidence you can point to: contours, rivers, roads, symbols.
:::

:::mistake{title="Describing without locating"}
Every statement should carry a grid reference or a direction ("in the north-east").
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up trellis and dendritic"}
Look at the angle of the junctions: tree-like acute angles are dendritic; right-angled junctions between parallel streams are trellis.
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
For "describe the distribution of settlement" give the pattern (dispersed, nucleated, linear), where it is dense and where it is absent, each with a grid reference. For "explain", add the map evidence (relief, water, roads).
:::

:::exam-tip
In Paper 1, settlement function questions usually hinge on one symbol cluster. Find the services in the square named before choosing.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Identify land use from symbols and explain it with map evidence: relief, water, transport.
- Settlement patterns: nucleated, dispersed, linear; functions come from the services shown.
- Drainage patterns: dendritic (tree-like), trellis (right-angled), radial (outward from a high point), parallel.
- Routes follow valleys, cross ridges at cols and avoid steep and marshy land.
- Map features are point, line or area (polygon) data.
:::
