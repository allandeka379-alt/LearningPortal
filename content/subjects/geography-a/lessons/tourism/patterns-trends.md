## Introduction

Victoria Falls in March, when the Zambezi is in full flood, receives tourists from all over the world, while in the cooler months visitors travel to Hwange and Mana Pools to watch game. Tourism is one of the largest economic activities in the world and an important source of foreign currency and jobs in Zimbabwe. But where tourists come from and go to, when they travel, and what kind of holiday they take are not random: they follow patterns that can be described and explained. This lesson covers the patterns and trends of international and national tourism. You should already know the meaning of the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors, and the idea of foreign exchange.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Tourism"}
The movement of people to places outside their usual environment for leisure, business or other purposes, for less than a year, together with the activities and services that serve them.
:::

:::definition{term="International tourism"}
Travel between countries; a visitor from outside the destination country is an international tourist. **National (domestic) tourism** is travel by residents within their own country.
:::

:::definition{term="Seasonality"}
The regular variation in the number of tourists during the year, with peak seasons and low seasons.
:::

:::definition{term="Ecotourism"}
Responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and supports the well-being of local people.
:::

## Sources and destinations

**Sources** (generating regions) are mainly the wealthy countries with high incomes, long paid holidays and good transport: Europe, North America, East Asia and increasingly China and other emerging economies. **Destinations** (receiving regions) are those with attractions such as beaches, mountains, wildlife, culture and heritage, accessible by transport and judged safe. Europe receives the largest number of international arrivals, and France has long been the most visited country. According to the United Nations tourism agency, there were about 1.5 billion international tourist arrivals in 2019 (rounded), a number which fell very sharply in 2020 because of the COVID-19 pandemic and then recovered.

Within Africa, the main sources of tourists to southern Africa are neighbouring countries, especially South Africa, and long-haul markets such as Europe and North America. Zimbabwe's attractions include Victoria Falls, Hwange National Park, Mana Pools, Lake Kariba, the Eastern Highlands and heritage sites such as Great Zimbabwe, Khami and the Matobo Hills. Victoria Falls (1989), Mana Pools (1984), Great Zimbabwe (1986), Khami (1986) and the Matobo Hills (2003) are World Heritage Sites.

## Volume, value and seasonality

**Volume** is measured by the number of arrivals or visitor nights; **value** by tourism receipts (money spent by visitors) and by average spending per visitor. A destination can gain more from fewer visitors who spend more, as with high-end safaris.

Seasonality has physical and social causes. Climate decides when beaches or parks are pleasant; in southern Africa the dry season from about May to October is best for wildlife viewing, and Victoria Falls has peak water flow around March to May. School holidays, public holidays and business conferences create peaks at particular times. Low seasons cause unemployment in the industry and unused hotels, so destinations try to attract visitors in the off-peak months with lower prices, events and conferences.

### Worked examples

:::worked-solution[Seasonality from the monthly data]
1. Total arrivals: $38 + 36 + 42 + 52 + 58 + 55 + 70 + 77 + 72 + 65 + 44 + 51 = 660$ thousand.
2. Monthly mean: $660 \div 12 = 55$ thousand.
3. Peak month (August) compared with the mean: $77 \div 55 = 1.4$, so August is 40% above the monthly average.
4. Share in the three peak months (July–September): $(70 + 77 + 72) \div 660 \times 100 = 219 \div 660 \times 100 = 33.2\%$.
5. Description: arrivals are lowest in January and February, rise to a peak in July to September (dry season and holidays), and fall again.
:::

:::worked-solution[Value and growth]
The destination's visitors spent US\$198 million in the year, and arrivals rose from 660 thousand to 726 thousand the next year (illustrative data).

1. Average spending per arrival: $198\,000\,000 \div 660\,000 = 300$, so US\$300.
2. Growth in arrivals: $(726 - 660) \div 660 \times 100 = 10\%$.
:::

## Factors behind patterns

- **Transport and ICT:** cheaper flights, low-cost airlines, better roads and airports, and online booking have made travel easier.
- **Affordability:** rising incomes in source countries, paid leave, and exchange rates that make a destination cheap or costly.
- **Transnational corporations:** airlines, hotel chains and tour operators advertise, package and sell holidays and invest in resorts.
- **Government support:** marketing, visas (for example the KAZA UniVisa for visitors to both Zimbabwe and Zambia), security, protected areas and infrastructure.
- **Image and safety:** news about conflict, disease or political unrest can reduce arrivals quickly, as shown by the pandemic.

## Types of tourism

| Type | Example in or near Zimbabwe |
|---|---|
| Business (conferences, meetings) | conferences in Harare, Bulawayo and Victoria Falls |
| Recreational and sport | tiger-fishing on Lake Kariba, white-water rafting, golf |
| Medical | patients travelling to neighbouring countries or abroad for treatment |
| Ecotourism | walking safaris and community-based wildlife projects |
| Cultural and heritage | Great Zimbabwe, rock art in the Matobo Hills |

**Trends in Zimbabwe:** arrivals fell during periods of economic and political difficulty, and in the pandemic, and recovered as air links and markets reopened. The Zimbabwe Tourism Authority (ZTA) promotes the country, grades facilities and collects data. Growth areas include regional tourism, conferences, community and heritage tourism, and domestic tourism.

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Describing a graph without figures"}
Quote months or years and values with units, and state the highest, lowest and the trend.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing volume with value"}
Arrivals measure volume. Value is the money earned. A destination may receive fewer tourists but earn more.
:::

:::mistake{title="Giving only one reason for a pattern"}
Combine physical factors (climate, scenery, wildlife) with human ones (transport, price, safety, marketing).
:::

## Exam tips

:::exam-tip
Tested in ZIMSEC Paper 2 and Cambridge Paper 4 (option 13). *Describe* seasonality or arrivals using the highest, lowest, range and trend with units; *explain* patterns with physical and human factors; *compare* types of tourism; *suggest* ways of spreading demand through the year. Do not quote statistics you are not sure of.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Sources are wealthy, high-income regions; destinations offer attractions, access and safety.
- Volume is measured by arrivals, value by receipts and spending per visitor.
- Seasonality follows climate, holidays and events, and causes seasonal unemployment.
- Transport, ICT, affordability, transnational corporations and government support shape patterns.
- Types include business, recreational and sport, medical, ecotourism and cultural or heritage tourism.
- Zimbabwean arrivals respond to access, security, the economy and global shocks.
:::
