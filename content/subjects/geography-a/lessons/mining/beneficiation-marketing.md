## Introduction

Ore taken out of a mine is not ready to sell. A tonne of Great Dyke platinum ore, for instance, contains only a few grams of platinum group metals mixed with large amounts of waste rock. Before a metal can be used in a catalytic converter or a battery it has to be separated, concentrated and refined. Each stage raises the value of the product and, if done in Zimbabwe, keeps more jobs and income in the country. This lesson describes the main processing methods, explains how they are matched to different ores, and outlines the rules and market conditions that affect the sale of minerals. You should already know the main minerals of Zimbabwe and the steps of obtaining mining rights.

:::note{title="Check current law"}
Laws, export rules and policies change. The outline below is for understanding how the system works and should be verified against current legislation.
:::

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Beneficiation"}
Processing a mineral ore to increase its mineral content and value before it is sold, including concentrating, smelting, refining and manufacturing.
:::

:::definition{term="Ore grade"}
The amount of valuable mineral in an ore, expressed as a percentage or as grams per tonne.
:::

:::definition{term="Gangue"}
The worthless rock and minerals that occur with the valuable mineral in an ore.
:::

:::definition{term="Concentrate"}
The product left after much of the gangue has been removed, with a much higher grade than the original ore.
:::

## Stages and methods of processing

1. **Crushing and grinding (comminution).** Large rocks are broken in crushers and then ground in mills to a fine powder so that the valuable mineral grains are freed ("liberated") from the gangue. Grinding uses much electricity.
2. **Screening and drying.** Sieves separate material by size. Drying removes moisture so that material flows, is lighter to transport and can be treated by methods that need dry feed.
3. **Gravity separation.** Uses differences in density: heavy minerals settle or stay behind while light gangue is washed away. Examples are panning, sluice boxes, shaking tables, jigs and spirals. It is used for gold, tin and chromite, and is cheap, which is why small-scale miners use it.
4. **Magnetic separation.** Magnets pull out magnetic minerals such as magnetite (iron ore) from non-magnetic waste.
5. **Flotation.** The ground ore is mixed with water and chemicals; air bubbles carry the mineral grains to the surface as a froth, which is skimmed off. It is the main method for sulphide ores of copper, nickel and platinum group metals.
6. **Leaching.** For gold, a cyanide solution dissolves gold from finely ground ore, and carbon then captures it. Mercury, once used by artisanal miners, is dangerous to people and rivers and should be replaced by safer methods.
7. **Smelting and refining.** The concentrate is heated in a furnace so that the metal separates from the slag. Further refining by chemical or electrical methods gives a pure metal. Ferrochrome is made by smelting chromite in electric furnaces.
8. **Cutting and polishing (diamonds).** Rough diamonds are cut and polished to bring out their shine; a large part of the rough stone is lost, and the value rises steeply.

## Matching methods to ores

| Ore or mineral | Typical methods | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Gold in quartz reef | crush, grind, gravity, cyanide leaching, smelt into bars | gold is dense and dissolves in cyanide |
| Copper and nickel sulphides | crush, grind, flotation, smelt, refine | sulphide grains float easily |
| Platinum group metals (Great Dyke) | crush, grind, flotation, smelt, refine | metals are held in sulphide minerals |
| Chromite | crush, gravity separation, then smelting to ferrochrome | chromite is dense |
| Iron ore (magnetite) | crush, magnetic separation, smelt | magnetite is magnetic |
| Lithium (spodumene) | crush, grind, density or flotation to concentrate, then chemical conversion | produces a concentrate before chemicals |
| Diamonds | sort, then cut and polish | already a finished mineral |

## Selling minerals: rules and markets

Most minerals are sold through licensed channels. Gold is bought from small-scale miners by Fidelity Printers and Refiners, and diamonds and other precious stones are sold under the Precious Stones Trade Act and Kimberley Process certification. The Minerals Marketing Corporation of Zimbabwe (MMCZ) markets most other minerals and issues export documents. Sales inside Zimbabwe need licensed buyers and records, and exports need permits. Policies to encourage processing in the country include a ban on the export of raw lithium ore announced in December 2022. These details should be verified against current law.

**Market research** asks who wants the mineral, in what form, how much, and at what price. Demand follows the industries that use the mineral: lithium for batteries, platinum group metals for catalytic converters, chrome and nickel for stainless steel, gold for jewellery and investment. Prices on world markets rise and fall, and competing producers elsewhere affect them, so a mine must know its likely buyers, its costs and the risk of price changes. Selling a processed product, such as ferrochrome rather than chromite, usually earns more per tonne but needs power and capital.

### Worked examples

:::worked-solution[Copper concentrate]
A mine treats 1 000 t of ore of grade 2.0% copper. Flotation recovers 90% of the copper in a concentrate of grade 25% copper (illustrative figures).

1. Copper in the ore: $1\,000 \times 0.020 = 20$ t.
2. Copper recovered: $20 \times 0.90 = 18$ t.
3. Mass of concentrate: $18 \div 0.25 = 72$ t.
4. Concentration ratio: $1\,000 \div 72 = 13.9$, so the concentrate is about 14 times smaller than the ore, which cuts transport cost.
:::

:::worked-solution[Gold recovery]
A gold plant treats 500 t of ore of grade 4 g per tonne. Recovery is 85% and the gold sells at US\$65 per gram (illustrative figures).

1. Gold in the ore: $500 \times 4 = 2\,000$ g.
2. Gold recovered: $2\,000 \times 0.85 = 1\,700$ g.
3. Value: $1\,700 \times 65 = 110\,500$, so US\$110 500.
4. Gold lost in the tailings: $2\,000 - 1\,700 = 300$ g, so recovery methods that save even 5% more would add value.
:::

:::example{title="Choosing a route for a chromite mine"}
A mine produces lumpy chromite. Selling the crushed and gravity-separated ore to a buyer abroad needs little capital but earns a modest price. Smelting the ore to ferrochrome in Zimbabwe earns a higher price per tonne and creates jobs, but needs a smelter, reliable electricity and a market among steel makers. **Judgement:** smelting adds more value if power is secure and the demand for ferrochrome is strong, otherwise the lower-risk option is to sell the concentrated ore.
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Giving the method without the principle"}
Say what property the method uses: density for gravity separation, magnetism for magnetic separation, surface properties and air bubbles for flotation, heat for smelting.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing ore with concentrate and metal"}
Ore is the rock from the mine, concentrate is the product after removing much of the gangue, and metal is the refined product. Value rises at each step.
:::

:::mistake{title="Ignoring the market"}
Processing is worth doing only if there are buyers, power and capital. A good answer names the demand, price and cost conditions.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Beneficiation raises the grade and value of a mineral before sale.
- Methods include crushing, grinding, drying, gravity, magnetic separation, flotation, leaching, smelting, and cutting and polishing.
- Methods are matched to ores by density, magnetism and sulphide chemistry.
- Minerals are sold through licensed channels such as Fidelity, MMCZ and Kimberley-certified diamond sales.
- Market research examines users, prices, competitors and volatility.
- Processing in Zimbabwe adds value but needs power, capital, skills and demand.
:::
