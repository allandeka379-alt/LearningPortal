## Introduction

Dig a pit on the crest of a granite hill near Masvingo and you find pale, sandy soil only a few tens of centimetres deep. Walk down to the wet vlei at the bottom and the soil is dark, deep and sticky. Farmers know that these soils behave differently: the sandy soil drains quickly and needs frequent fertiliser, while the vlei soil stays wet and is used for dry-season gardens. Soil is a mixture of weathered rock, organic matter, water, air and living organisms, and it forms slowly. This lesson explains how soils form, how they change down a slope and how geographers measure their properties in the field. You should know the main weathering processes and what humus is.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Soil profile"}
A vertical section through the soil from the surface to the parent material, showing distinct layers called horizons.
:::

:::definition{term="Soil horizon"}
A layer of soil, roughly parallel to the surface, that differs from the layers above and below in colour, texture, structure or composition.
:::

:::definition{term="Soil catena"}
A sequence of soils down a slope, from the crest to the valley floor, which are linked by drainage and the movement of water and material but differ because of their position.
:::

:::definition{term="Soil texture"}
The relative proportions of sand (2.0–0.05 mm), silt (0.05–0.002 mm) and clay (below 0.002 mm) particles in the soil.
:::

## Soil-forming factors (Jenny's formula)

Jenny's formula states that a soil is a function of five factors: $S = f(cl, o, r, p, t)$.

1. **Climate (cl).** Rainfall and temperature control weathering, leaching and the rate of decay. Warm, wet climates produce deep, strongly leached soils; dry climates produce thin soils rich in salts.
2. **Organisms (o).** Vegetation supplies organic matter; bacteria, fungi, termites and earthworms break it down and mix the soil.
3. **Relief (r).** On steep slopes erosion removes soil, so profiles are thin; on gentle slopes and valley floors, soil is deeper and wetter.
4. **Parent material (p).** Granite weathers to sandy soils with little clay minerals; basalt and dolerite weather to clay-rich soils; limestone weathers to soils rich in calcium.
5. **Time (t).** Soils take hundreds to thousands of years to develop, so young soils are thin and old soils deep and more altered.

## The soil profile

| Horizon | Description |
|---|---|
| **O** | Surface layer of litter and decomposing plant remains (thin in dry savanna) |
| **A** (topsoil) | Dark, mixed with humus, rich in organic matter and life; roots are concentrated here |
| **E** | Pale layer from which clay, iron and nutrients have been washed out (eluviation); not always present |
| **B** (subsoil) | Accumulation of clay, iron or aluminium washed in from above (illuviation); often more compact and redder |
| **C** | Weathered, broken parent material |
| **R** | Solid, unweathered bedrock |

Water moving downwards removes soluble materials (**leaching**) and carries fine particles from the A horizon to the B horizon.

## The soil catena

On a granite slope in southern Africa a typical catena shows:

![Soil catena on a granite slope: topsoil depth increases from 20 cm at the crest through 35, 60 and 80 cm to 100 cm in the vlei, while clay rises from 10 to 65 percent and pH from 5.2 to 6.6, because water and fine particles move downslope and collect at the bottom.](media:diagrams/soil-catena-granite.png "Figure: soil depth, clay and pH down a granite catena (worked-example data; slope shape schematic)")

- **Crest and upper slope:** thin, sandy, well-drained soils; the finer particles are washed downslope; low in nutrients and organic matter.
- **Mid slope:** moderate depth and texture; a transfer zone where water and material move through.
- **Foot slope:** deeper soils with more clay and nutrients, because material washed from above accumulates and the water table is closer.
- **Valley floor (vlei or dambo):** deep, dark, clay-rich and often waterlogged soils with high organic matter, because poor drainage slows decay.

## Measuring soil characteristics

| Property | Method |
|---|---|
| **Texture** | Hand-feel test (gritty = sand, smooth = silt, sticky and plastic = clay) or sieving and settling, then plot on a texture triangle |
| **Structure** | Describe the shape of aggregates: crumb, granular, blocky, platy or prismatic |
| **pH** | pH kit or meter on a soil and water mixture; pH below 7 is acid, above 7 is alkaline |
| **Moisture** | Weigh fresh soil, dry it in an oven, reweigh; the loss is water |
| **Organic content** | Loss on ignition: heat dried soil strongly and measure the mass lost, or compare colour |
| **Colour** | Compare with a colour chart (for example Munsell); dark colours suggest humus, red colours iron oxides |
| **Depth** | Auger or measure the profile with a tape |

Take samples at the same depth and at systematic or random points, record the location and repeat measurements. Present the results in tables, graphs of properties down the catena, or a labelled sketch.

## Worked examples

:::worked-solution[Soil moisture and organic content]
A student weighs a fresh soil sample (120 g), dries it in an oven until the mass is constant (96 g), then burns a 50.0 g dry sample until it is 47.0 g (illustrative data).

1. Water lost: $120 - 96 = 24$ g.
2. Moisture as a percentage of the fresh mass: $24 \div 120 \times 100 = 20\%$.
3. Moisture as a percentage of the dry mass: $24 \div 96 \times 100 = 25\%$. State which method you use.
4. Organic matter by loss on ignition: $(50.0 - 47.0) \div 50.0 \times 100 = 6\%$.
:::

:::worked-solution[Interpreting a catena data set]
Illustrative data for a granite catena (data invented for this exercise).

| Position | Slope (°) | Topsoil depth (cm) | Sand (%) | Clay (%) | pH | Moisture (%) | Organic matter (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crest | 2 | 20 | 78 | 10 | 5.2 | 6 | 1.2 |
| Upper slope | 8 | 35 | 70 | 15 | 5.4 | 8 | 1.5 |
| Mid slope | 5 | 60 | 55 | 28 | 5.8 | 12 | 1.9 |
| Foot slope | 2 | 80 | 40 | 40 | 6.2 | 18 | 2.4 |
| Vlei | 0.5 | 100 | 20 | 65 | 6.6 | 34 | 5.8 |

1. Silt at the crest: $100 - 78 - 10 = 12\%$.
2. Clay increases from 10% to 65%: a difference of $65 - 10 = 55$ percentage points.
3. Moisture in the vlei compared with the crest: $34 \div 6 = 5.7$ times.
4. Mean pH: $(5.2 + 5.4 + 5.8 + 6.2 + 6.6) \div 5 = 5.8$.
5. Description: depth, clay, pH, moisture and organic matter all increase downslope, while sand decreases. Explanation: fine particles and nutrients are washed downslope, water collects at the bottom, and waterlogging slows decay so organic matter builds up.
:::

:::example{title="Describing and explaining a soil profile"}
A pit shows a dark grey topsoil 20 cm deep, a pale grey layer to 40 cm, then a reddish-brown layer with a higher clay content. **Describe:** the dark topsoil is rich in humus; the pale layer has lost clay and iron; the reddish-brown layer has gained clay and iron. **Explain:** water moving down leaches and carries fine particles out of the pale layer (eluviation) and deposits them in the layer beneath (illuviation).
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Giving only a list of factors"}
For "explain", link each factor to its effect: granite weathers to sandy soil because it contains much quartz, and warm rain increases the rate of weathering.
:::

:::mistake{title="Confusing eluviation with illuviation"}
Eluviation is the removal of material from a layer; illuviation is the deposition of that material in a lower layer.
:::

:::mistake{title="Taking the percentage of moisture from the wrong mass"}
Say whether moisture is calculated from the fresh mass or from the dry mass. They give different percentages.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Jenny's formula links soil to climate, organisms, relief, parent material and time.
- A profile has horizons: O (litter), A (topsoil), E (leached), B (accumulation), C (weathered rock) and R (bedrock).
- A catena links soils down a slope: sandy and thin at the crest, deeper and clay-rich with more organic matter in the vlei.
- Texture, structure, pH, moisture, organic content, colour and depth can all be measured in the field or simple laboratory.
- Moisture and organic content are percentages of a stated mass.
- Present data in tables and graphs and interpret by linking each change to drainage and the movement of material.
:::
