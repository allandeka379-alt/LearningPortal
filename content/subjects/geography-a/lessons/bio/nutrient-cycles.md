## Introduction

A farmer clears a patch of tropical forest, burns the trees and plants maize. The first harvest is excellent. By the third or fourth year yields have fallen, and she moves to a new patch. Why does fertile-looking forest land lose its fertility so quickly? The answer lies in how nutrients are stored and moved. Plants need nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and calcium, and ecosystems pass them round in cycles. This lesson covers the global cycles of carbon, nitrogen and oxygen, then the **Gersmehl model**, which shows the nutrient stores and flows of an ecosystem in a single diagram. You should know what photosynthesis, respiration and decomposition are.

## Key definitions

:::definition{term="Nutrient cycle"}
The movement of a chemical element between the living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) parts of an ecosystem, through stores and transfers.
:::

:::definition{term="Gersmehl diagram"}
A model with three circles, **biomass**, **litter** and **soil**, whose areas are proportional to the nutrient stored in each, joined by arrows whose widths are proportional to the nutrient transferred each year.
:::

:::definition{term="Nutrient flow and loss"}
Flows are the transfers between stores: uptake from the soil, litterfall and decomposition. Losses leave the system, mainly by **run-off** and **leaching** (drainage through the soil).
:::

## The global cycles

### The carbon cycle

Carbon is stored in the atmosphere (as carbon dioxide), in living things and soil organic matter, in the oceans, and in rocks and fossil fuels. Key transfers:

1. **Photosynthesis** takes carbon dioxide from the air into plants.
2. **Respiration** by plants, animals and decomposers returns it to the air.
3. **Decomposition** of dead matter releases carbon dioxide and builds soil organic matter.
4. **Combustion** of wood and fossil fuels returns carbon quickly to the air.
5. **Ocean exchange** dissolves carbon dioxide in the sea and releases it again.

Human activity has added carbon to the air by burning fossil fuels and clearing forests, so the atmospheric concentration has risen from about 280 ppm before industrialisation to above 420 ppm in recent years.

### The nitrogen cycle

The air is about 78% nitrogen, but plants cannot use the gas directly. The cycle converts it into forms they can take up.

1. **Nitrogen fixation:** nitrogen-fixing bacteria (such as *Rhizobium* in the root nodules of legumes), lightning and industrial processes convert nitrogen gas into ammonium or nitrates.
2. **Ammonification:** decomposers turn the nitrogen in dead matter and wastes into ammonium.
3. **Nitrification:** soil bacteria change ammonium into nitrite and then nitrate.
4. **Assimilation:** plants take up nitrate and ammonium and build proteins; animals eat the plants.
5. **Denitrification:** bacteria in waterlogged, oxygen-poor soils change nitrate back to nitrogen gas.
6. **Leaching** removes soluble nitrate in drainage water.

Farmers use the cycle when they rotate maize with groundnuts, cowpeas or soyabeans: legumes fix nitrogen that the next crop uses.

### The oxygen cycle

Oxygen makes up about 21% of the air. Photosynthesis releases it; respiration and combustion use it up; and some forms ozone high in the atmosphere. The cycle is closely linked to the carbon cycle: the two gases are exchanged in opposite directions.

## The Gersmehl model

The three compartments are **biomass (B)**, **litter (L)** and **soil (S)**. The circles show how much nutrient is stored; the arrows show the transfers: uptake from soil to biomass, litterfall from biomass to litter, decomposition from litter to soil, and losses by run-off and leaching.

### Comparing the ecosystems

| Feature | Tropical rainforest | Savanna |
|---|---|---|
| Biomass store | Very large (most nutrients are in the trees) | Medium |
| Litter store | Small, as litter decomposes in weeks | Moderate, because decomposition slows in the dry season |
| Soil store | Small | Larger |
| Litterfall and uptake | Large and fast | Smaller; seasonal |
| Loss by run-off and leaching | Large flows of rain, so leaching is high | Smaller; strong run-off during storms |

In the rainforest nutrients are cycled quickly: warmth and moisture decompose litter fast and roots take up the nutrients at once, before heavy rain can leach them. The soil is poor but the forest is rich. Nutrients in the savanna are held more in the soil and litter, so the cycle is slower.

### Effects of disturbance

- **Burning** converts litter and biomass to ash, a short-term addition of nutrients to the soil but much of the nitrogen is lost as gas and smoke, and ash can be blown or washed away.
- **Clearing and deforestation** remove the biomass store, so the nutrients that were held in the trees are lost or left in the thin soil. Without the roots to take them up, rain leaches them away. Raindrops also erode the bare soil. Soil fertility falls within a few years.
- **Sustainable alternatives** such as agroforestry, mulching and crop rotation keep a living store and protect the soil.

## Worked examples

:::worked-solution[Reading a Gersmehl data set]
Illustrative nutrient stores in two ecosystems, in kg per hectare (data invented for this exercise):

| Ecosystem | Biomass | Litter | Soil | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical rainforest | 600 | 15 | 120 | 735 |
| Savanna | 150 | 60 | 450 | 660 |

1. Rainforest biomass share: $600 \div 735 \times 100 = 82\%$.
2. Savanna biomass share: $150 \div 660 \times 100 = 23\%$.
3. Savanna soil share: $450 \div 660 \times 100 = 68\%$.
4. Interpretation: in the rainforest most nutrients are in the trees, so clearing removes most of the fertility. In the savanna most nutrients are in the soil, so the land can be cultivated for longer without losing them.
:::

:::worked-solution[Nutrient loss after clearing]
After a rainforest plot is cleared and cultivated for three years, the soil store falls from 120 to 70 kg per hectare (illustrative data).

1. Loss: $120 - 70 = 50$ kg per hectare.
2. As a percentage of the original soil store: $50 \div 120 \times 100 = 42\%$.
3. Explanation: with no tree roots to take up nutrients, heavy rain leaches them below the root zone, crops remove nutrients in the harvest, and bare soil is eroded.
:::

:::example{title="Describing a Gersmehl diagram in words"}
For tropical rainforest: "The biomass circle is very large, the litter circle is tiny and the soil circle is small. The arrows for uptake and litterfall are wide, showing rapid cycling, and the decomposition arrow from litter to soil is wide because litter decays fast. The loss arrows are moderate: heavy rain leaches nutrients but the roots recapture most of them."
:::

## Common mistakes

:::mistake{title="Saying rainforest soil is fertile because the forest is lush"}
The forest is rich because nutrients are held in the biomass and cycled quickly. The soil store itself is small, so once the trees are removed fertility is lost rapidly.
:::

:::mistake{title="Drawing circle size from the number of plants"}
In a Gersmehl diagram the area of each circle shows the amount of nutrient stored in that compartment, and arrow widths show the size of the yearly transfer.
:::

:::mistake{title="Mixing up fixation and nitrification"}
Fixation converts nitrogen gas into ammonium or nitrate; nitrification changes ammonium into nitrate by soil bacteria.
:::

## Summary

:::summary
- Nutrient cycles move elements between stores in biomass, litter, soil, air and water through uptake, litterfall, decomposition and loss.
- The carbon cycle involves photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion and ocean exchange; human use of fossil fuels and forests has raised atmospheric carbon dioxide.
- The nitrogen cycle includes fixation, ammonification, nitrification, assimilation, denitrification and leaching.
- In a Gersmehl diagram circle area shows the store and arrow width shows the annual flow.
- Rainforest has a large biomass store, small litter and soil stores and fast cycling; savanna has more nutrients in the soil and a slower cycle.
- Clearing and burning remove biomass and increase leaching and erosion, so fertility falls quickly.
:::
